In a nutshell
: From the moment I arrived, I felt completely relaxed and totally unrushed. Every detail is thought of, and every want and desire is attended to by some of the most amazing staff. The phrase “when you’re here, you're family,” is clichéd, but the folks at Il San Pietro mean it.
Eugene Shevertalov
Eugene Shevertalov
Rooms
The Deluxe Room was extremely generous in size and truly classic Amalfi coastal chic without feeling dated or overplayed. The majolica tile floors, Mediterranean coastal palette, and ceramics throughout the room made me feel like I was a world away from my NYC home. The views of the coastline and Positano in the near distance, seen from the private balcony, melted all my worries away. Speaking of, definitely book a room with a view of Positano—it’s beautiful day and night and well worth the price. You won’t regret it.
Eugene Shevertalov
Food and drink
For a small coastal hotel, the food and beverage options are top-notch. At check-in, my husband and I were greeted with a pair of freshly made Bellinis, which set the tone for the entire stay. The breakfast spread in the main restaurant was one of the best I’ve ever seen, with everything from bagels and lox to a huge selection of pastries. At least eight types of bread were available every morning, as was a yogurt station with too many toppings to count. And that was just the buffet—egg dishes were also available to order in any manner you could dream up. Lunch was served at the open-air seaside restaurant opposite the hotel’s private beach club, while aperitivos were best enjoyed on the cliffside patio before heading to dinner at the hotel or in town. The food was always on point, and the seafood tasted like it was caught that morning.
Amenities and experiences
The hotel strikes an amazing balance between spacious and intimate. It’s one of the few on the coast with an elevator that descends to its own private beach club by the sea. The La Prairie treatments at the spa gave me a reason to retreat out of the sun; the treatment menu includes facials, massages, manicures, and pedicures. But the showstopper is the hotel's boating program. Guests can take advantage of a complimentary two-hour outing on the hotel yacht, or charter one of the other hotel boats, like I did, for a full day of exploring the coast, swimming in hidden coves, and visiting the charming towns of the region.
Eugene Shevertalov
Eugene Shevertalov
What to do nearby
While there are many towns along the coast to explore, the most logical and closest is Positano itself. From the hotel, it’s reachable by either a 10-minute shuttle along the winding road or a complimentary boat that takes you right into the port, an amenity few hotels here can offer, and one I took advantage of often. I did plenty of eating and shopping while walking around the town’s narrow streets. There is also an amazing art gallery right in the center of town called Liquid Art System, where I made a rather large purchase that is currently taking up a prominent spot on my living room wall.
If you’re looking for something a bit more outdoorsy (I was not), carve out a morning for the Path of the Gods, a trail high above Positano with views stretching to Sorrento, or so I’m told.
Eugene Shevertalov
The backstory
Founder Carlino Cinque opened the hotel in 1970 with just 33 rooms, and it has never left the family since. After his death, his niece Virginia Attanasio and her brother Salvatore took the reins; Virginia's sons, Carlo and Vito, have since taken over. Virginia still lives on the property and personally oversees the flower arrangements. The hotel has maintained three generations of family stewardship by offering a truly personal and warm, almost family-like experience.
Insider tip
Even if you don’t play tennis, you have to swing the racket at least a few times on what I think is the world's prettiest
tennis court
. It’s situated at sea level and practically carved into the cliffside, offering a natural never-ending breeze to cool off the gameplay. If you’re more of an après-tennis player, you can do that too, sipping a cocktail and splitting your view between tennis and the sea—you can even pick up a sweatshirt from their gift shop with such a moniker printed across the chest.
Final considerations:
Family friendly
: Not particularly; this hotel has an age limit for children (ages 10 and up are allowed), and honestly, you probably wouldn’t want to bring them anyway.
Accessibility
: This region as a whole isn't the best for those with accessibility needs—steps are unavoidable here; call ahead for more information.
Wellness
: Spa, a small gym with sea views, yoga and Pilates classes, and a tennis court
