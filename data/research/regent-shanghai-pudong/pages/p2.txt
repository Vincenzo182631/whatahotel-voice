Reviewed by

Why book?
On
Shanghai
's Bund, there are few other hotels so close to the river and with unobstructed views as this one. Most hotels sit behind the main road, often thronged with tourists and traffic noise, but the Regent Shanghai on the Bund gives guests great views without the fuss. Even better, almost all have balconies, which is rare in town.
Set the scene
Rising 15 floors, this hotel is front and center on the Huangpu River’s western (Puxi) side with direct views of Pudong and its famous towering skyscrapers like the Oriental Pearl Tower. The entrance is set back from the road, and massive doors swing open automatically, leading guests into a small, windowless lobby.
The sit-down reception is swift, and the flower displays are out of this world. There is not much space here, but the live music and elegant furnishings make up for it. A stunning ten-foot-tall lacquer painting took six months to complete. Don’t be fooled by the small entryway; the position of the building lends itself to dedicating the floors above the lobby to the real scenery.
The backstory
What appears to be a modern building actually has quite a bit of local history. This was once The Seagull on the Bund hotel, dating back to the mid-1980s, before Shanghai’s boom lit up the river to what it is today. Today, it is the second Regent hotel from IHG in town and sits where the Suzhou River flows into the Huangpu. The hotel could not be any more perfectly positioned for great views. Historic elements of the former hotel remain today, including a grand piano and white stone, spiral staircase that rises up to the second-floor banquet rooms.
The rooms
They are large, thoughtfully laid out, and purposefully furnished to maximize the view. But, choose wisely as only half the rooms face the Huangpu River, and it would be a shame to check into what is one of the best-positioned hotels to look at a neighboring building.
Classic rooms are the standard and are divided into city-facing or Bund-view rooms, with the latter being the optimal choice. Burnished hardwood floors are in all with enormous beds facing outward towards the windows (no matter what your view). A quartet of gigantic pillows and a fluffy duvet cover them all, while the wireless phone charger and additional power outlets complete the picture.
Most exciting perhaps is how intuitive the lights are. There are lots of buttons, but one master switch handles them all, should you wish (why don’t more hotels do that?). A cream-hued sofa and olive green armchair also face the windows. In some rooms, a soaking tub is in the corner of the bedroom, as long as you don’t mind the seagulls or your companion having a closer look.
Bathrooms are also open-plan so that, you guessed it, they maximize the view. Clever translucent panels close off the shower and Japanese Toto toilet areas, although the closet space and double vanities are still open to the room. Perricone MD toiletries in large pump bottles are standard, but a bevy of extras like dental kits with the uber-popular Marvis toothpaste and small bottles of Perricone MD Vitamin E lotion prove to be thoughtful extras. Sinks on the vanity have slanted, green marble angles giving them a trendy look, and the faucet’s water spray is equivalent to a drenching rainfall shower (wide, strong, and incredibly impressive). I have never seen a sink quite like it.
The glass closets are not large enough to hold a suitcase, though. They end up remaining partially ajar, and there are no luggage racks, although you can call for one. But it is nice that the minibars contain comped refreshments like local beer, coconut water, soft drinks, and dark chocolate candy bars.
The hotel sits at an angle, so the views of the Bund change as you move down the hallway. Some have those prized postcard shots of the Oriental Pearl Tower, while others angle towards the Garden Bridge. Either is a good option, part of why people come to this hotel. The Seagull Suites offer similar Bund or Pudong views from the bed, and there is a Penthouse Suite with its own living room and private sauna.
There are balconies in almost every room, and there is something special about being directly on the river (not across the street like most other hotels) and watching the passing boats with the nighttime skyline view as the backdrop.
Food and drink
Harmonia is the second-floor, all-day dining venue. Its breakfast buffet has live cooking stations, complete with an area to watch handmade dumplings rolled a la minute. An outdoor terrace is available for dining in warmer weather, but the Bund views are visible from every inch of the restaurant.
I went for the traditional yangchun noodles at breakfast and an omelet, which did not disappoint. The attentive staff tried to persuade me into trying the signature lobster omelet or the “Regent waffle” with all kinds of toppings, but I passed to take advantage of the rest of the colorful spread.
Jin Lin Chinese restaurant on the third floor opens for lunch and dinner with a Cantonese menu. There’s also the Condé Boutique and lobby lounge, but there is no view (no windows at all). While the pastries and afternoon tea are nice, this would not be my pick to spend your time when the view just outside is so spectacular.
The spa
An indoor pool is open from morning to night with a lifeguard, sauna, and a few lounge chairs beneath enormous grass sculptures suspended from the ceiling. While it would have been nice to peer out over the Bund while swimming, the building’s design doesn’t allow for that. That’s something you can enjoy from your pillow if you book a Bund-facing room. The fitness center is always open and has a good selection of cardio and free weight equipment. A fridge stocks free bottled water, energy drinks, apples, and chilled towels.
The neighborhood/area
You are right on the Bund, just across the bridge from the main promenade and minutes from bustling Nanjing Road’s famous avenue of shops and restaurants. IHG nailed it when opening this as a Regent, and the full-frontal Pudong scenery with passing boats in the foreground is as Insta-worthy as it gets.
The service
I was immediately impressed with how poised the service staff is here. They encounter travelers from around the world, handling guest requests with patience and care. For example, when I went for breakfast, the dining room was quite warm (it was a cold and rainy day), but the server instantly called engineering to see if they could cool the space down after I explained it was like a sauna. And the executive chef and general manager were surveying the buffet to ensure it was never lacking for anything. Florists tended to lobby arrangements like they were performing horticultural surgery.
For families
This would not be my first choice for families with kids, but they are most certainly welcome. The breakfast buffet’s extensive sweets, candy, and chocolate fountain section is just one clear indication of that.
Anything left to mention?
The IHG One Rewards connection adds value for travelers wanting to redeem their points on a great room. You can also earn points during a stay, and top-tier Diamond members are treated to room upgrades, two drinks in the bar, and breakfast, too.
