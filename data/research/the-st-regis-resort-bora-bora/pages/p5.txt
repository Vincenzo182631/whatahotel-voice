Why book?
The screensaver-meets-overwater bungalow fantasy is brought to life on a private
motu
(or islet) in French Polynesia’s most storied locale.
Set the scene
Travelers looking to step directly into the vision of tropical beauty that Bora Bora has always manifested in their minds may find it hard to differentiate fantasy from real life at one of the largest and most spectacular resorts in French Polynesia. The St. Regis Bora Bora Resort unfolds across 44 acres of coconut palms, bougainvillea and pink and white flowering frangipani trees interrupted only by beaches, a protected snorkeling lagoon and 90 private overwater and land villas, many with Mt. Otemanu views.
The resort occupies its own private
motu
(islet) just 20 minutes by speedboat from Bora Bora’s airport. Almost all of the people you’ll bump into onsite are guests–and almost all clad in designer sunglasses and sundresses or dressed down in board shorts and bikinis, inevitably celebrating being newlywed or some kind of milestone anniversary or moment (that moment may also be just finally making it to Bora Bora). At every turn, smiling staff members greet you with a friendly “Iorana!” Every villa comes with a pair of beach cruiser-style bicycles with baskets for exploring the sprawling grounds. Boardwalks over the water linking villas are a particularly fun place to bike while spotting passing sea turtles and eagle rays below. Guests can stay active at the resort’s tennis courts, adjacent to a well-equipped Technogym fitness center that’s open around the clock.
The St. Regis’ piece de resistance, however, and unique in these islands, is the Lagoonarium–a private marine sanctuary ringing the spa’s private island where all resort guests can dip in to snorkel or swim with more than 120 species of fish protected within these crystal clear, sheltered waters.
The backstory
Among luxury hotel chains, the St. Regis, known for its butler service, stands apart in the service realm. And the St. Regis Resort Bora Bora keeps those traditions intact with impeccable details and carefully tailored moments throughout your vacation that live up to the brand’s reputation as well as French Polynesia’s warm tradition of hospitality.
The property first opened on one of Bora Bora’s most beautiful motus in 2006, when it immediately attracted holiday elite like Nicole Kidman and Keith Urban, who honeymooned there that same year. Justin Bieber has also vacationed at the resort on several more recent occasions.
Designed by Parisian architect Pierre Lacombe, the resort made the pages of Architectural Digest when it debuted. Lacombe is said to have spent an entire year diving into the culture and lifestyle of French Polynesia while designing the property as a village of sorts, with a peaceful and convivial feel. The pitched villa roofs reveal natural Pandanus and Asian hardwoods inside, essential island elements rendered with an idyllic balance of tradition and modernity.
Perched atop stilts and accessed via boardwalks on the open lagoon, overwater villas not tucked into the dense foliage on land are designed to feel like serene spaces within spaces, adding to the overall transporting effect.
The rooms
You’re staying in French Polynesia’s largest overwater villas here, with even the most diminutive among them clocking in at 1,550 square feet. There are more than 90 overwater and land villas spread across the property. And while they can feel somewhat dark inside at times, despite large sliding glass doors that give onto the deck and water, decor dominated by rich woods and woven thatch walls is brightened up with starched white linens. All eyes are inevitably drawn to the bright views glowing just beyond your private deck, which overlooks either the lagoon or ocean side of the resort’s beautiful surroundings or the beach. Villa bathrooms are enormous, with soaking tubs set into the floor, walk-in showers and luxe bath amenities from professional vegan and paraben-free haircare brand, Sachajuan.
Butlers are always just a WhatsApp text or email away to handle your ironing and unpacking (included), make dinner reservations or send a fresh robe after you’ve been lounging wet on your deck during a post-snorkeling session (ladders descend from the waterfront villas for direct lagoon access, but a jump for joy into the water is even more momentous). Morning coffee or tea delivered to your villa by your butler is also included. The most luxurious accommodations on site are the 13,000 square foot, three-bedroom Royal Estate Villa (essentially its own compound), and the two-bedroom overwater Royal Suite Villa, with a private suspended swimming pool with exceptional lagoon and Mt. Otemanu views.
Food and drink
With Asian, Italian, and American-meets-French Polynesian inspired restaurants onsite at the hotel, you can get a taste of the world without leaving the property. In the hour before sunset, outdoor seats at 727 Bar, with full-frontal Mt. Otemanu views, filled with guests splurging on pre-dinner cocktails and tapas. Breakfast and lunch play out beachfront at an open-air restaurant where the resort’s resident cat (Regis) will likely make his presence known with a tail curled around your ankle before being shooed away. For a unique cocktail, this St. Regis puts a spin on its classic Bloody Mary by adding a sweetening splash of Watermelon juice to delicious effect. At breakfast, be sure to grab a bowl of poisson cru from the lavish buffet–it’s Tahiti’s national dish, and the St. Regis’ version is particularly delicate and delicious. Guests arrive from Vaitape and other motu resorts for dinner at Lagoon. Perched on stilts with glass windows in the floor to view passing marine life and views of Mt. Otemanu, it’s widely lauded as one of the best restaurants in the South Pacific. Service begins with the passing of a Champagne cart and continues with course after course of French-Asian fare that’s nearly too pretty to eat. For a special breakfast at your villa, have it delivered aboard a traditional Polynesian canoe festooned with flowers.
The spa
The resort’s Iridium Spa stands apart from wellness areas at other luxury Bora Bora addresses. It’s set on a private island within the property’s grounds, accessed by wooden bridges from the main motu and surrounded by the Lagoonarium–a private sanctuary home to thousands of marine creatures including iridescent giant clams, protected Napoleon wrasse and parrot fish. The spa occupies its own villa with treatment rooms as well as separate sauna and steam rooms for men and women, each with an outdoor soaking tub and outdoor shower. The true pleasure before or after a treatment, however, comes in stepping out onto the spa’s serene beach and lounging in a chair or hammock under a palm or putting on a mask and fins for a snorkel in the Lagoonarium. Any treatment here can be enhanced by requesting the spa’s black pearl body scrub treatment (ask for it, as it’s not always on the spa menu), made with Tahitian black sand to exfoliate and tiare-infused oil from the islands’ favorite flowers to inject essential moisture. Complimentary wellness classes, including yoga, are offered on the spa beach throughout the week.
The neighborhood/area
You’re on a private motu here, which guests are delivered to aboard the resort’s comfortable private speed boats. Everything is immaculately landscaped, with views pulling your gaze to water on both sides of the islet–a sparkling sapphire and turquoise canvas that morphs and deepens in tone throughout the day. Over it all lords Mt. Otemanu, Bora Bora’s legendary peak (2,385 feet), and it’s a pleasure to watch clouds and rainbows come and go from its spire throughout the day. Guests returning from forays to Vaitape or adrenalin-fueled adventures scuba diving and sailing on the lagoon can often be heard exhaling a sigh of relief when they step foot once again back in this peaceful enclave.
The service
Service is where the St. Regis really shines And whether you’re looking to score a last minute opening at Bora Bora’s most coveted “mainland” table near Vaitape at Villa Mehana or arrange a bespoke sailing charter with the resort’s romance concierge for an impromptu proposal aboard a luxury yacht like Vitamin Sea, just ask and let the concierge desk work its magic. The resort also has a cultural ambassador who can share their experience growing up in these islands, teach you the art of making a flower lei or take you on a tour of the grounds and gardens to see all the edible and aesthetic beauty in bloom. Dining alone at a restaurant? Expect someone to arrive with a book or magazine to keep you company. Bike chain acting up? Bring it to the onsite “Bike Doctor” for a quick fix or swap. Craving something to cool down on the beach? Enter a beach boy in a pareo with a frozen treat just when you were wanting it most. The thoughtful touches go on and on. There’s even a free shuttle service by boat and shuttle bus to the main island’s town, Vaitape, offered every day but Sunday and holidays.
For families
While the bulk of the guests here are coupled up, depending on school holiday schedules you can expect to see a few (or more) families with young children biking the pathways of the St. Regis Bora Bora Resort, paddling the complimentary kayaks or SUPs into the lagoon and giggling over schools of minnows in the clear shallow waters of the resort’s beach. There are multiple pools onsite, including a family one (and another reserved for adults only that’s secreted away in a garden-like setting. To cut down on family dining costs, note that kids six and under eat for free here with 50 percent discounts on regular menu items for those between the ages of 7 and 12. Nannies and babysitters can be requested through the resort’s concierge if you’d like to enjoy an adults-only dinner or spa reprieve. The protected Lagoonarium is also a great spot for both young guests and anyone new to snorkeling to get comfortable with the gear and French Polynesia’s abundant marine life. Kids up to 12 years old should be sure to request the resort’s Ice Cream Passport, which they can use for unlimited ice creams and milkshakes at the beachfront bar throughout the day.
Eco effort
The resort’s Sustainable Development officer is charged with helping to preserve the island and lagoon’s natural beauty and cultural value, tapping local producers, fishermen, farmers and vendors for ingredients at its restaurants and spa, among other local outreach. Rainwater recovery technology is used on the property. And the only mode of transport for staff on the motu is via electric golf carts and bikes. Guests can get involved with eco efforts by participating in a coral cutting workshop at the Lagoonarium led by the resort’s marine biologists during which you’ll learn how to snip and attach coral fragments into an in-water garden that will later be moved to an established Bora Bora coral reef in need of some TLC..
Accessibility
Most of the resort is connected by boardwalks and ramps and is wheelchair accessible. Golf carts are always on standby to transport guests throughout.
Anything left to mention?
Scuba certified guests should not miss the chance to dive here. While other French Polynesian islands tend to steal the underwater spotlight among divers (the Tuamotu Archipelago, in particular), diving in the lagoon and ocean waters of Bora Bora comes with nearly guaranteed sightings of manta rays and squadrons of eagle rays, so it’s well worth gearing up.
