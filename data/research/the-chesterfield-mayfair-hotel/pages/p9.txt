# Local attractions

Mayfair is one of the most affluent districts of any city in the world. Mayfair's prime location within London means that Hyde Park, Buckingham Palace, Trafalgar Square, Oxford Street, Piccadilly Circus, and Leicester Square are all just a short stroll from The Chesterfield Mayfair. In addition to hosting an abundance of high-end residential properties, Mayfair is home to a great number of designer shops, restaurants, and luxury hotels. Its charming hidden gems, however, are sure to take visitors by surprise.

## Things to see & do

### Savile Row

The street from which the term “bespoke” originated has an unparalleled reputation for tailoring that stretches back more than 200 years, with Winston Churchill and King Charles III counted amongst its loyal customers.

Discover more

### Shepherd Market

One of the oldest parts of Mayfair, Shepherd Market is home to a variety of charming pubs, boutique shops, restaurants, and a cinema, just moments from The Chesterfield Mayfair.

Discover more

### The West End

Embrace the bright neon lights of Piccadilly Circus, sip fashionable cocktails in the chic bars of Soho, feast on the delights of Chinatown, or catch a matinée on theatre-filled Shaftesbury Avenue.

Discover more

### Buckingham Palace

When we think about unmissable London landmarks, few are more unmissable than Buckingham Palace. Built as far back as 1703, the Palace has since grown to be a point of convergence for national rejoicing and mourning.

Discover more

### Burlington Arcade

Originally built so that Lord Cavendish’s wife could shop in enclosed safety, it is still patrolled by beadles in top hats and frock coats today. Boasting the world’s finest retailers, Burlington Arcade is a true bastion of British luxury.

Discover more

### Shopping

The glittering shops on Bond Street, Regent Street, South Moulton Street, Piccadilly, and Oxford Street are just a few minutes away. World famous stores include Hamleys, Liberty’s and Fortnum & Mason, to name but a few.

Discover more

## Green spaces

### Grosvenor Square

This delightful space is the second largest square in London. Make the most of a sunny afternoon with an agreeable stroll around this leafy retreat, whilst paying your respects at the 11th September 2001 memorial garden.

Discover more

### Hyde Park

The largest of the four Royal Parks that populate London, the park is known for its rich political history. In recent times, it has served as a venue for large-scale concerts in the summer and, in the festive season, Hyde Park Winter Wonderland.

Discover more

### St James' Park

Stretching the length of the park is a lake populated by pelicans that have been a fixture since 1660. A park highlight is the Blue Bridge that crosses the lake, from which you can enjoy glorious views of neighbouring Buckingham Palace.

Discover more

## Galleries

### The Royal Academy

The Royal Academy of Arts, located in the heart of London's Mayfair district, stands as one of the world's foremost institutions for contemporary art and culture. Its diverse exhibitions and renowned collections make it a must-visit destination for art enthusiasts.

Discover more

### The National Gallery

A world-renowned art institution, housing an extensive collection of masterpieces spanning centuries and diverse artistic styles. Its iconic location in Trafalgar Square has made the gallery a cultural landmark.

Discover more

### The National Portrait Gallery

A captivating showcase of portraiture featuring a diverse array of individuals who have shaped British history and culture. The NPG offers a compelling exploration of artistic expression and the stories behind the faces.

Discover more

## Blue plaques

As you traverse Mayfair's streets, keep your eyes peeled for the little blue plaques that identify the notable people that made this area their home.

**Lord Horatio Nelson (1758-1805)****:** Immortalised by the commemoratory Nelson’s Column in Trafalgar Square, Lord Nelson was one of Britain’s greatest ever naval commanders, leading the nation on to win several key battles during the French Revolutionary and Napoleonic Wars.

**Florence Nightingale (1820-1910):** An icon of Victorian culture, her innovative nursing work significantly lowered death rates through the improvement of hygiene standards. You can find Florence Nightingale’s plaque at 10 South Street.

**P.G. Wodehouse (1881-1975):** One of Britain’s most beloved comic novelists, with his works read and revered all over the world. Wodehouse’s Jeeves character is now often considered the archetypal manservant.

**Jimi Hendrix (1942-1970):** One of the most innovative and iconic rock guitarists of all time, Hendrix spent much of the late 60s in the UK, and it was at The Samarkand Hotel, Notting Hill, that Hendrix died a tragically early death.
