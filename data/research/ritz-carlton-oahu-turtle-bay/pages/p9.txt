The Ritz Carlton O'ahu, Turtle Bay
Casual elegance in a serene surf paradise

Why book?
Located on the famed North Shore of Oahu, where surfers flock to catch some of the best waves in the world, this five-star resort offers direct access to tranquil beaches, nature trails, delicious island fare, and refined digs to come home to.
Set the scene
The open-air, natural-light-filled lobby, flanked by ocean views, is the perfect preamble to your Turtle Bay stay. You’ll get a glimpse the protected cove (where you’ll likely lounge and swim), the bar with the infinity water display (where you can grab a drink and listen to live music), and the surf (where you might catch some waves), all while walking the few short steps from the entrance to the check-in desk. While the property is five-star, the vibe is unpretentious, so you can lean into the North Shore come-as-you-are casual, or sport a designer dress and heels and feel equally comfortable. Head to the grassy area just past the pool to watch the sunset, and you’ll find a mix of honeymooners cozied up on the lawn, families dressed in matching aloha prints snapping group photos, local fishermen reeling in fresh catch, and of course, surfers riding the waves.
The backstory
Host Hotels & Resorts, Inc. bought and rebranded the property as The Ritz-Carlton O’ahu, Turtle Bay in 2024. But the hotel and the land have a long history here. Historically, the area was part of a ahupua’a, a traditional Hawaiian land division from the mountains to the ocean. There were taro patches and gardens planted with banana, sweet potato, sugar cane, noni, and more. Then, from 1893 to 1971, the land was home to the Kahuku Sugar Mill. In 1972, a hotel was opened as Del Webb’s Kuilima Resort Hotel & Country Club. It was intended to be a casino, but when the law to legalize casinos didn’t pass, the property was sold. It changed hands several times over the years, from Hyatt to Hilton to Benchmark Hospitality International to The Blackstone Group before becoming the Ritz-Carlton. Today, the resort’s Kuilima Farm grows many of the same fruits and vegetables that were cultivated as part of the traditional ahupua’a.
The rooms
Inspired by the natural elements of light, wind, water, waves, and the coastline at Turtle Bay, interior designer Dianna Wong chose to incorporate organic fabrics, natural hardwoods, and hand-woven carpets in the rooms. The color palette also reflects the natural surroundings: shades of red and orange similar to the soil and papayas, blues and greens found in the waves and plants, deep black and brown like the rugged coastline, and sandy tones seen on the beach. The result is sophisticated yet inviting—think elevated beach house with a dash of desert aesthetic. Local artworks, including pieces by Nick Kuchar and Abigail Romanchak, are also featured. The Ocean Bungalows accommodations, steps from the sand, with their own dedicated hosts and pools, are a popular choice—you get all the amenities of the luxury resort but with more privacy, a front porch, and a lawn with loungers and hammocks outside your door.
Food and drink
Island-style eats are available at several on-site restaurants. For casual fare, such as salads, burgers, and poke bowls, grab a seat at the poolside Sunset bar or Off the Lip lobby bar. Two other restaurants, Beach House by Roy Yamaguchi and Alaia, offer a more elevated menu, featuring locally-sourced produce, meats, and fresh catch. Guests also have the option to purchase access to the Club Lounge, which includes a buffet breakfast, lunch, afternoon tea, hors d’oeuvres, drinks, and desserts (including made-in-Hawaii ice cream sandwiches). All dining options at the Ritz-Carlton O’ahu, Turtle Bay have outdoor seating; Sunset bar gets you the closest to surf, the Club has a fantastic sunset view (and is also a great place to hang out while waiting to check-in or after you check-out), and Beach House is the spot if your heart is set on being right next to the sand.
The spa
Open-air treatment rooms allow the birdsong and breeze to flow through at the oceanside Nalu Spa, and the incorporation of organic, locally-sourced ingredients serves to deepen your connection to this special land. (If you want an unobstructed ocean view, reserve the hale, which means “house” in Hawaiian, when booking.) The rhythmic, wave-like motions of the lomilomi massage are sublime in this setting, where you can hear the actual waves rolling and breaking nearby—and you may even have a chance to experience a traditional oli (Hawaiian chant), as I did. My massage therapist was Keola, a Native Hawaiian from the island of Moloka’i, where he started learning healing practices from his kupuna (elders) at a young age. Don’t miss the bath scrub made of island-grown ingredients, including rosemary and other medicinal plants from the property’s farm, available for your post-treatment shower.
The neighborhood/area
The North Shore is beloved for its natural beauty and slower pace. Directly around the hotel, you can walk the nature trails, take a horseback ride, and visit quiet coves and beaches for sunbathing, snorkeling, and swimming. About 20 minutes away, in Haleiwa town, you’ll find restaurants, boutiques, and food trucks, but don’t expect much nightlife. Things shut down fairly early up here, with just a handful of places, like Haleiwa Joe’s, open until 12 a.m.
The service
Service here reflects the location more than the brand. It’s generally friendly, but more laid-back and less of the formal, highly attentive service you might typically expect from a Ritz-Carlton.
For families
With an abundance of outdoor and cultural activities available here—in addition to several on-site eateries, horse stables, and a golf course—families have plenty of options to keep the kids entertained. The variety of room styles and configurations, some with connecting rooms, means you can choose accommodations that best fit your family.
Eco effort
The property is home to over 70 species of birds and thousands of native plants and trees. Protected wildlife can also be found here, including green sea turtles, Hawaiian monk seals, and humpback whales. The on-site Kulima Farm supplies some ingredients for the hotel’s restaurants and also offers tours, where visitors can learn about plants that are culturally significant in Hawaii. The farm’s larger goal is to promote agricultural education and support local farmers, to help move Hawaii away from its dependency on imported foods. Refillable in-room toiletries, guest recycling, and electric car charging stations also contribute to lowering the property’s footprint.
Accessibility
This hotel has prioritized accessibility, offering features such as elevators, accessible parking, hearing- and mobility-accessible rooms, and accessible entrances to the business center, fitness center, restaurants, and pool.
Anything left to mention?
Be warned: It may be difficult to go to any old gym after you’ve worked out in this fitness center, where the walls are windows with incredible views of the ocean (and surfers, whales, and magical sunsets) directly in front of you.
Take some time to check out the information in the “Our Ahupua’a” cultural walkway (just before you reach the spa and fitness center) and the “Legends Artwork” (on the right side as you enter the walkway). You can learn a lot about the land and Hawaiian history and culture here.
