Anantara New York Palace Budapest Hotel
Old school glamour with just the right amount of modern convenience.

Best for:

Location:

Can’t-miss feature:
The vast Italian marble atrium with a glass pyramid roof
In a nutshell
: Like staying inside a fancy Belle Époque cake, the luxury here comes from your surroundings, the spa treatments, and staying in one of the most photographed landmarks in Budapest.
Matt Charlton
Matt Charlton
Rooms
There are two sections to the hotel. The older section, housed in the original building, has rooms which open onto the atrium, giving you
that
view every time you step out. The rooms—though grand, indulgently decorated, and well appointed—are slightly older, showing signs of wear and needing tech updates.
Better then to stay in the newer block, where the same opulent materials are applied (textured wallpapers, marble bathrooms, plush textiles) only in a way more befitting of a 2026 guest—such as less wasted space, more light, integrated tech, and better outward views over the Budapest rooftops.
Matt Charlton
Matt Charlton
Food and drink
This is very much pinned around the opulent, dazzling White Salon and Deepwater breakfast room. Both are attached to the New York Café, which sees long tourist queues in the street from early in the morning. As a resident however, you get to skip these, and sit in the more intimate seating towards the back, albeit with a few wayward iPhones pointed at you as you masticate your dumplings.
You can reserve a White Salon balcony spot for a unique breakfast experience—traditional pastries, home-baked bread, continental favorites, and a personal waiter. In the evenings, you can take to the same tables for traditional Hungarian fayre with a modern twist—I had the chicken paprikash with Hungarian dumplings and cucumber salad, which was very good.
Sadly my experience of the room service was comically chaotic, with the forgetting of side dishes, dessert, and drinks, but the staff member dealing with me was incredibly helpful. It seems the app they encourage you to use in the rooms is a tad glitchy.
Amenities and experiences
I’ve experienced my fair share of hotel massages, and The Signature Massage was one of the best I’ve had—incredibly sympathetic to any old or new injuries and just the right amount of pressure and ceremony. This was preceded by a foot ritual and followed by a recline in the atmospheric tea lounge. I thoroughly recommended it; it seemed to cure my jet lag too.
Though I didn't take this tour, I was very intrigued by the four-hour ‘Budapest in a VW Van’ offering, which pretty much does exactly what it says, and would definitely be one for the history and architecture buffs.
What to do nearby
There is everything to do nearby. You are in the center of things here, and
Budapest
—not only steeped in every era of history, but rich and vibrant with its current culture—takes at least four days to take a proper bite out of. The Great Boulevard on which the hotel is situated is incredibly busy, but the noise doesn’t seem to make it to the rooms.
You’re about a 15-minute walk from the House of Terro—well worth a visit to learn about Hungary’s tumultuous 20th century—a 10-minute walk to the buzzy Jewish quarter, and just two stops on the metro from Blaha Lujza tér to Deák tér. There are plenty of cafés, mini supermarkets, ruin bars, and restaurants nearby, albeit pointed directly at tourists, so it’s a good idea to ask the very knowledgeable concierge if you want more of a local flavor.
Matt Charlton
The backstory
And what a backstory. The walls of this building have endured almost every significant historical event of Europe’s 20th century. Originally opened in 1894 as the lavish European headquarters for New York Life Insurance Company (hence Lady Liberty at the apex of the café entrance), it was designed by renowned Hungarian architect Alajos Hauszmann, blending Renaissance, Baroque, Gothic, and Art Nouveau styles.
Initially, the first two floors housed offices, with upper levels serving as residences for employees. Damaged during both World Wars, it later became a sports shop and tourism office during communism. Unloved and neglected at the close of the 20th Century, it reopened as a luxury hotel in 2006, and joined Anantara Hotels, Resorts, and Spas in 2020.
Insider tip
To experience the atrium at its best, the Afternoon Tea Ritual feels like a very good idea. Whereas usually you are just passing through this impressive space—either to an adventure, dinner, or reception—this offering gives you the opportunity to be in the space and drink it all in. Literally.
As an
English person
, I found the tea to be the most important feature, of course, but you are then thoroughly spoiled, with a tiered beautiful presentation of treats such as finger sandwiches, delicious scones, cheese polenta, choux buns, chocolate cake, and bonbons, all the while basically sitting inside a tiered wedding cake of a building.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes—kids are welcome in the restaurant and there are connecting suites/ rooms. Pet friendly too.
Accessibility:
The hotel offers step-free entrances, elevators to all floors, and designated accessible rooms with wide doors (32-inch opening).
Wellness:
There is a very attractive and well-equipped gym stacked with Technogym equipment, and some cool design features such as arched mirrors and down lighting. Spa, sauna, basement leisure pool, steam room, and rainforest shower are all present and correct.
