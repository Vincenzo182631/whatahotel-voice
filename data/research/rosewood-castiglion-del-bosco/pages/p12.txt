Reviewed by

How did it strike you on arrival?
This hotel is the sprawling Tuscan estate you've always dreamed of staying in. The property is spread over 5,000 acres but manages to feel intimate, with 42 stylishly decorated suites and 11 villas.
Nice. What’s the crowd like?
You're likely to find slick city-dwellers and international jetsetters taking a break in the countryside.
The good stuff: Tell us about your room.
The suites and villas are individually designed in a rustic-chic style, some with four-poster beds, others with block printed textiles. They definitely err on the side of the lavish, featuring antiques and modern technology. If money were no object, I'd check into one of the sumptuous villas and never leave. Each one comes with a private heated pool, though some feature wood-burning pizza ovens, a home theater, or tennis court.
What was most memorable—or heartbreaking—about your stay?
The sprawling estate is full of Tuscan pleasures, including the on-site Brunello di Montalcino winery, an 18-hole golf course, a cooking school where you can learn to make Tuscan dishes with organic ingredients from the garden, a spa with luscious treatments, and an infinity pool. Oh, and you can organize truffle-hunting excursions and helicopter transfers, naturally.
Bottom line: worth it?
The price is steep. But if it's true that you get what you pay for, a stay here is worth it.
