Why book?
The Lodge at Torrey Pines feels like stepping back into a bygone era. It’s campy meets California Craftsman luxury lodge with all the bells and whistles: a destination spa, two locally beloved restaurants, and a front-row seat to one of the world’s best golf courses that make this So Cal retreat, dare we say, an all-around hole in one.
Set the scene
We wouldn’t fault you for being slightly confused when you pull into the circular driveway of The Lodge at Torrey Pines and are greeted by doormen in traditional Scottish kilts. It’s part of the many charms of this 20th-century California Craftsman–inspired lodge. Inside, rich amber-hued stained glass, hand-carved woodwork, and Arts and Crafts–style lanterns cast a warm glow across the lobby. Oversized fireplaces and intimate nooks create the kind of atmosphere that makes you want to sink into a leather chair with a good book and a generous pour of California Caymus.
The Lodge is a big bucket list destination for golfers, most of whom plan their entire stay around getting to play the North and South Courses at the world-famous Torrey Pines Golf Course right next door. In addition to the golf set, you’ll find a steady mix of business and conference travelers, older couples who appreciate a low-key luxury getaway that involves a leisurely dinner at the on-site fine-dining institution A.R Valentien, as well as families and multi-gen groups who come to take advantage of the on-site amenities and close proximity to the hiking and walking trails at Torrey Pines State Beach.
The backstory
The hotel first opened in 1961 as a 74-room inn catering largely to golfers and travelers drawn to the dramatic bluffs overlooking this once-wild stretch of southern California coastline. The inn was purchased by the San Diego-based Evans family (founders of Evans Hotels) in 1955, who continue to own and operate the hotel to date. In 2000, the hotel was closed for a two-year renovation, during which time most of the original buildings were demolished. The Lodge at Torrey Pines reopened in 2002 as an homage to the early California Craftsman architecture popular in Southern California in the early 1900s. What you’ll find today is the same post-and-beam construction, stained glass windows, dark wood, clinker bricks, and stone facades. Even the lighting is a nod to the early Arts and Crafts–style lighting popular at that time, which continues to give the lodge the look and feel of a private estate rather than a full-service luxury hotel.
The rooms
There are 170 spacious rooms and suites, all of which are kitted out in a distinctly California Craftsman aesthetic. If you’re looking for bright, minimalist interiors with floor-to-ceiling windows and plenty of natural light, this is not that place. Rooms feature lots of dark wood, Tiffany-style stained-glass lamps, Craftsman leather chairs, hefty wooden armoires, and cozy ottomans that give the whole space a little feel of an upscale summer camp, meshed with a historic SoCal lodge. Fireplaces feature prominently in many rooms, adding to that cozy summer camp feeling, especially on foggy coastal San Diego mornings. Bathrooms are also extremely generous in size and feature deep soaking tubs and tilework that mirrors the Arts and Crafts design ethos. For the best views, the Palisade Suites offer private patios and sweeping sight lines over the Torrey Pines Golf Course and the Pacific Ocean in the distance.
Food and drink
Two words: Drugstore Hamburger. The Grill is the Lodge’s casual, open-air patio restaurant and the spot for all casual fare you could hope for, done extremely well. The locally beloved Drugstore Burger with onion rings and fries is the perfect antidote after a round of golf or a hike through Torrey Pines State Park. The Grill also leans into wood-fired dishes, hearty salads, and an easygoing cocktail list that features San Diego craft beers and California wines.
A.R. Valentien is the Lodge’s all-day restaurant and transforms into one of San Diego’s most respected fine-dining rooms come dinner service. The menu skews seasonal, with an emphasis on farm-to-table sourcing and produce from Southern California growers. Standouts include the Liberty Farms duck confit, the house-made charcuterie, and the platter of pickled vegetables with Dijon mustard, all prepared simply and in keeping with the overall Craftsman-era setting.
The Spa
The Lodge’s spa incorporates a bevy of botanical-inspired treatments. There are 12 treatment rooms, a dedicated steam and sauna area, and a heated outdoor pool framed by cabanas and loungers, ideal for unwinding pre-or post-treatment. There’s also a separate oversized swimming pool with a new pool bar called Judson’s, where you can order bites, beverages, and beers. Bonus points, you can also order the Grill’s famous pharmacy burger here, too.
If you’re coming straight off the golf course or a long hike, the CBD Therapeutic Massage or the sports massage are the move. Both are tailored to sore muscles, with the latter incorporating a Theragun to loosen tight joints and get you back out on the links the next day.
The neighborhood/area
Proximity to the award-winning Torrey Pines Golf Course is really the big draw here. The Lodge sits directly next to the course, and guests of the Lodge are automatically guaranteed a tee time on the North Course if they book a package through the hotel. The Lodge is also within walking distance of Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve, which offers miles of beautiful hiking trails.
The service
It’s as close to a country-club hotel vibe as they come, and the service (minus the country-club pretension) definitely reflects that. The staff here is polished and exceedingly friendly, and the concierges are pros at navigating Torrey Pines tee times, trail conditions, and restaurant reservations. The kilted doormen and valets run like clockwork, always appearing right when you need them. It’s attentive, thoughtful, and refreshingly unstuffy, and unlike a snooty country club, this is the kind of place you actually want to join.
For families
The Lodge is very family-friendly. The hotel offers a Learn at The Lodge program for kids, with four- and eight-hour options, along with a range of activities, lunch, snacks, and even a guided study area to help kids with their schoolwork. As far as amenities on the property, the croquet lawn, access to nearby Torry Pines State Park, along with scavenger hunts for kids, and a complimentary in-room camp set up.
Accessibility
There are ADA-compliant rooms across the property, ranging from Signature Rooms to Palisade Suites. The hotel offers ramps and an ADA-friendly resort map.
Anything left to mention?
The hotel sits just between La Jolla to the south and Del Mar to the north. While there are plenty of activities both on the property and off to keep you occupied, outside of the golf course and Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve, there’s not much within easy walking distance, so it’s best to plan accordingly.
