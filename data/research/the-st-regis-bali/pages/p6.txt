The St. Regis Bali Resort
A classically luxurious beachfront escape in the resort town of Nusa Dua, with all the tropical island amenities a heart could desire.

Reviewed by
Kathryn Romeyn
Best for:
Families, couples and special occasions
Location:
Right on the beach in Nusa Dua, Bali
Can’t-miss feature:
The expansive meandering saltwater lagoon pool, which knits together the entire resort with its jewel-like sparkle.
In a nutshell:
There’s nothing minimalist about this regal beachfront resort that sprawls over 22 luxuriant acres and offers its guests extremely generous suites and pool villas, exhaustive amenities and culinary indulgences, and privileged, easy access to the sugary sand.
Rooms
Like the resort as a whole, all the 124 accommodations—81 suites and 43 villas—are graciously grand, with the smallest suites stretching over nearly 1,000 square feet. Marble bathrooms and oversized tubs are de rigueur in every room type, and they’re all festooned with deluxe textiles, feel-good rugs, glitzy chandeliers and elegant examples of Balinese craftsmanship, from wood carving to gilded headdresses worn by traditional dancers. Guests seeking seclusion can find it in a variety of room types, from St. Regis Pool Suites to one and two bedroom villas and residences, larger still at three and four bedrooms, all with private pools and tucked into the resort’s mature tropical flora. I found my 6,500 square foot (indoor and out) The Strand Villa a dream when traveling with my young children since it was mere steps from the beach and had its own private pool with a waterfall, a cushioned gazebo and an ample grassy backyard where my girls played with the toys delivered by our personable butler alongside coloring books and soft stuffed bears.
Food and drink
If I didn’t know any better I’d think the mission of the resort’s F&B team was to plump up its guests, the culinary offerings are so lavish and decadent. It starts at the international restaurant Boneka, which is inspired by Indonesia’s
wayang
shadow puppet theater, and serves a bountiful breakfast buffet as well as a la carte dinners and an even more extravagant Sunday brunch buffet. Dulang serves up local Balinese flavors in a charming gazebo setting, and there are a pair of bars—one uber-sophisticated and perched high and the other a swim-up down by the beach. At the whitewashed seaside fine-dining boîte Kayuputi and elsewhere the delicious food is not only for adults but also the smallest guests—my children dug eagerly into wholesome dishes with vibrant veggies at each outlet. While a babysitter watched them, I indulged in a degustation dinner that included velvety crab soup with laksa spices, ginger flower, shimeji mushrooms, and a plump crab dumpling as well as multiple luscious, textural desserts.
Amenities and experiences
The salty shore out front—arranged with comfy lounge chairs and umbrellas—is a beautiful place to wade and splash, but the resort’s nearly 40,000-square-foot saltwater lagoon pool gives the actual ocean a serious run for its money. Bright turquoise and crystal clear, it meanders through the verdant nine-acre resort creating intimate little pockets and shady coves. It’s not a Bali holiday without a massage, and the 30,000-square-foot Iridium Spa delivers on the promise of profoundly curative, intuitive rubdowns and elaborate pampering rituals. After a relaxing herbal foot bath, I relished every minute of one in a bed beside my toddler who promptly fell into a Sleeping Beauty–worthy slumber at her masseuse’s touch. We didn’t make it to the nightly Champagne sabrage, but it’s a beloved tradition featuring the local form of a sword called
kris
plus live dance or jazz. The resort offers a lot for kids, and our family’s most memorable moments included an adorable tiny tea party in our villa with more colorful sweets than Halloween, a wagon of books and a teepee filled with stuffies, and a peaceful pony ride along the water’s edge.
What to do nearby
In the beach resort mecca of Nusa Dua, in the southeast of Bali, The St. Regis Bali Resort is one of the best-located properties for quick, gridlock-free transfers to and from the airport. In this region, soft, sandy beaches are abundant, not only directly in front of the resort but on both sides of it, and surfers can find some excellent, challenging breaks. Neighboring resorts provide dozens of additional dining options, and there are modest locally owned
warungs
for casual Indonesian fare, too. For a dose of Balinese culture, Uluwatu Temple—one of the island’s most iconic for its clifftop architecture, sunset vistas and nightly
kecak
fire dance spectacle—is some 30-45 minutes by car. A day visit to Uluwatu is worthwhile as well, for even more world-class meals plus postcard beaches, surf lessons, yoga classes, shopping and spa circuits.
Insider tip
Plan your Saturday around indulging in the brunch at Kayuputi, which is as abundant a gastronomic affair as I’ve ever experienced—with a sea view to match—and kept me sated for just about 24 hours. Delicacies from the sea and mountains included a Sakoshi oyster topped with kimchi and salted lime sorbet, beautiful yellowfin tuna tartare, and wagyu D-rump with lobster vol-au-vent, fermented onion hollandaise and truffle jus—all washed down with Champagne and St. Regis signature Bloody Marys, naturally.
Final considerations
Family friendly:
Extremely, there’s a kids club and loads of amenities and activities for little ones.
Accessibility:
Yes, there are wheelchair-accessible room types and the resort has elevators, lifts and ramps.
Wellness:
Iridium Spa is a temple of calm with 12 treatment rooms, hydrotherapy and comprehensive treatments—and it’s open late, until 11 p.m.
Loyalty group:
Marriott Bonvoy
