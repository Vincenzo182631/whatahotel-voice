Reviewed by
Todd Plummer
Why did this hotel catch your attention? What's the vibe?
Waikiki can get a bad rap for its impersonal mega-resorts, but gems like Halepuna prove that, yes, there are still enjoyable places to stay here. For some reason, this property seems to attract fewer families with rambunctious kids, so the atmosphere is also more serene than you might find elsewhere.
What's the backstory?
The interiors are by world-renowned Champalimaud Design, known for its broad array of hotel projects from
Raffles Singapore
to Troutbeck in the
Hudson Valley
.
Tell us all about the accommodations. Any tips on what to book?
My (entry-level) room was on the smaller side—something I would expect from a hotel in Manhattan perhaps, but not in Hawaii. Otherwise, the decor was perfectly charming. And hey, when you come to Hawaii, how much time are you spending in your hotel room anyway?
Is there a charge for Wi-Fi?
No charge, and no issues.
Drinking and dining—what are we looking at?
The Halekulani Bakery & Restaurant adjacent to the lobby was adequate, but forgettable. There's a selection of coffee, pastries and breads, and all-day dining, but it's really the kind of place you stop for a quick refuel before continuing on with your day.
And the service?
The staff are wonderful. Once a week there is a cute Manager's Wine Reception with complimentary wine and light bites hosted by the General Manager and the management team—they really value getting to know individual guests, despite the hotel's size (nearly 300 rooms).
What type of travelers will you find here?
Honeymooners, couples, friends on group trips—basically everyone except families with kids.
What about the neighborhood? Does the hotel fit in, make itself part of the scene?
The hotel blends in with many of the larger Waikiki hotels in the area. It's located just two minutes from the beach.
Is there anything you'd change?
The rooms could be a bit bigger. Otherwise, the hotel does a good job of creating an oasis in the heart of
busy Waikiki
.
Any other hotel features worth noting?
There's no resort fee, which is a welcome change for a hotel of this size and with its breadth of amenities (I appreciated their daily selection of fitness classes). Another nice touch was the free admission to some of Oahu's most popular museums, including the Bishop Museum, the Honolulu Museum of Art, and the Manoa Heritage Center.
Bottom line: Worth it? Why?
If you want Waikiki without the kids, this is the way to go.
