Rosewood Phnom Penh
A cloud-busting, world-class stay, boasting zen vibes, elegant rooms, and two of the best restaurants in the city.

Best for:
Wellness fans, young couples, or business travelers chasing more than a white-walled room with a free pen.
Location:
Perfectly positioned on Monivong Boulevard, in central Phnom Penh, with skyscraper views that take in the best of the city.
Can't-miss feature:
The crow's-nest rooftop bar, Sora (yes, Japanese for sky, yet Khmer for… alcohol) is well worth the ear-popping elevator ride, especially at dusk, when that remarkable Cambodian sunset shifts to a honey gold.
In a nutshell:
An elevated, elegant stay in keeping with the rest of the Rosewood collection's high levels of quality, offering a crisp, chic retreat from the city's bustle. Inside, there's a well-equipped spa and indoor pool, though the real draw is the phenomenal food across six distinct venues.
Jonnie Bayfield
Rooms
A skyscraper hotel never looked so chic. The 175 rooms (including 37 suites) boast striking panoramas over the city, possessing the air of an art gallery filled with beds rather than beds surrounded by art. Designed by BAR studio, these pan-Asian-inspired spaces exude a quiet confidence, breaking free of monastic simplicity to incorporate a touch of luxury. Navigating the robust network of elevators can feel isolating to some, but these rooms are a perfect retreat from the close-knit Cambodian street life.
Inside, they are homey yet design-led: dark wood furniture and muted grays peppered by bright wall art and unique ceramics inspired by local traditions. Spacious bathrooms feature dual sinks and integrated bathtubs gazing over the cityscape. While entry-level rooms offer solid value, there is much pleasure in splashing out on a Grand Deluxe Suite for its cozy corner sofa and panoramic views. Best of all, bedside-controlled blackout blinds ensure each day begins with a grand reveal of that immense, waking city.
Jonnie Bayfield
Jonnie Bayfield
Food and drink
For my money, the F&B is where this hotel truly stands apart. Across six venues, there's something for all moods, well-enjoyed by my local Khmer friends too. The Living Room invites casual brunches and afternoon teas that drift into evening aperitifs. As for the heavy hitters, Cuts is the steakhouse, dealing in artisanal meats cooked to perfection alongside starters that offer flashes of Khmer style. Better still is Iza, an elevated Japanese izakaya fused with the aesthetics of a sultry Tokyo jazz bar—all low-hanging tangerine bulbs and the irresistible scent of an open robata grill. The menu is refined yet playful; the Mango Daifuku was a work of art.
Brasserie Louis serves a fine breakfast spread featuring fresh yogurt worth the room rate alone. Additionally, there is a Chinese restaurant, Zhan Liang, and Sora, the sky bar renowned for the city's best views and mischievous cocktails. The anime- and Lego-themed drinks are fun, though the curry-flavored one was a bridge too far for me.
Amenities and experiences
The Rosewood's spa, Sense, overlooks the skyline and has interestingly developed a menu of Lost Remedies that bring ancient Khmer healing techniques into contemporary wellness practices. It is well worth handing yourself over to the expert hands here. The indoor pool is a blessing at the height of the dry season, alongside a well-served fitness studio, sauna, steam room, and relaxation lounge. Beyond wellness, art is taken seriously, with a rotating gallery of Cambodian artists adorning the walls. Guests can also partake in a morning monk blessing at the Wat Ounalom temple, and the concierge will bend over backwards to arrange private city tours.
What to do nearby
Situated in an upmarket business district within the Vattanac Capital Tower, the hotel sits above a small shopping mall with designer boutiques. Naturally, there is only so much Burberry anyone can take, so embark upon the two-minute walk to Exchange Square Mall for diverse food outlets and a solid cinema. Luckily, as is the nature of tuk-tuk-filled Phnom Penh, every major sight—from the Royal Palace to the National Museum—is well within a short ride via the Grab app (Southeast Asia’s version of Uber).
Insider tip
I'll be honest—in this line of work, I stay in innumerable hotel rooms playing host to stacks of randomly ordered coffee-table Taschen tomes that go as unnoticed as the tragically outmoded in-room shoe mitt. At the Rosewood Phnom Penh, however, these meaty art books have clearly been selected with purpose. Take a morning coffee and flick through the photojournalism documenting much of Cambodia's fascinating, often brutal history.
Final considerations
Family friendly:
Though not strictly adults-only, the vibe doesn't scream kid-friendly; however, a Rosewood Explorers service with tailored activities for little ones is on offer.
Accessibility:
Like any sky hotel, accessibility is reliant on elevators, though corridors are wide throughout for wheelchair access.
