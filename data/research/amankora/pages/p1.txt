### Banya Spa House

Set amid Paro’s pine forest, the Banya Spa House draws on Eastern European and Scandinavian traditions with oak- or birch-leaf bundles used to massage, stimulate and exfoliate, and an outdoor terrace with a cold plunge for taking in views of the river and a hilltop monastery.

### Hammam Spa House

At Amankora Paro, the Hammam Spa House is a peaceful escape with a double treatment room, spacious relaxation area, cold plunge, outdoor pool, hot bath and steam room – all rooted in traditions that reach back to the Roman Empire and live on in Turkey and Morocco today.

### Himalayan Hot Stone Bath

Inspired by a tradition, where river stones are heated and lowered into the bath to release beneficial minerals, while local Khempa herbs infuse the water with their healing properties.

### Yoga Classes

Enjoy a complimentary morning breathwork and _asana_ (yoga poses) session in the lodge’s studio, hosted by an experienced teacher and suitable for all abilities.

### Forest and Fortress Barbecue Dinner

Dine in a lush forest clearing or ceremonial tent. A traditional dance performance or musical ensemble can be arranged alongisde a classic Bhutanese feast or barbecue.

### Log Cabin Recovery Lunch

Conclude the hike to Bhutan’s holiest pilgrimage site with a rejuvenating lunch in the shadows of the Tiger’s Nest at Amankora’s atmospheric Log Cabin, inviting reflection on the morning’s adventures.

### Farmers Market Tours

Paro’s local markets offer unique insight into the daily life and culture of Bhutan. A kaleidoscope of colours, sounds and aromas, stalls are laden with ingredients from nearby farms showcasing the region’s agricultural diversity.

### Kitchen Garden Activities

A dedicated team member, together with the lodge’s gardener, leads an informative tour, showcasing Amankora’s commitment to using home-grown ingredients that reflect the country's humble heritage.

### Hike to Taktsang (Tiger's Nest)

Embark on the exhilarating four-hour hike to the Tiger’s Nest, the iconic 7th-century monastery that clings to the mountainside, 900 metres above the valley floor.

### Light 108 Butter Lamps

Take part in the symbolic lighting of 108 butter lamps at one of Bhutan's oldest temples, Kyichu Lhakhang, which dates to the 7th century.
