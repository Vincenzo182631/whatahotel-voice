# Welcome to Matild Palace, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Budapest

## An icon returns. Your story begins.

Matild Palace, our luxury hotel in Budapest, welcomes guests with a distinctive ambiance representative of its Hungarian heritage and UNESCO World Heritage Site status. Built during the Belle Époque era...See More in 1902, the palace was developed under the patronage of Her Imperial and Royal Highness Maria Klotild of Saxe-Coburg and Gotha as the social hub of Budapest. She dreamed of the twin palaces at the very heart of Budapest greeting those who enter Elisabeth Bridge as a graceful lady. Today, this hotel in Budapest city center offers global explorers regal luxury through 111 tastefully furnished rooms, many overlooking the Danube River, 19 suites including the Crown Tower Suite and Maria Klotild Royal Suite, remarkable history, and a refined gastronomic destination by Wolfgang Puck and COVA. ### Matild Palace's Journey

For over a century, Matild Palace has provided Budapest’s residents and visitors with an iconic place to socialize, full of splendor, elegance, and alluring stories. From its golden age days in the early 20th century to the present day, Matild Palace has been a place where people come together to be seen and find solace in its unique atmosphere.

### From the Budapest Memoirs to Re-Birth

Spanning two World Wars and a revolution, this is the incredible journey of Matild Palace’s restoration: from the Budapest memoirs to the opening of Matild Palace, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Budapest in 2021.

### A Belle Époque Masterpiece Opening in 1902

Matild Palace’s story begins in the Belle Époque, when Duchess Maria Klotild envisioned twin Baroque palaces - crafted by architects Giergl and Korb - as a grand gateway to Pest. Known as the Klotild Palaces, they became a social hub, welcoming all with their majestic beauty and timeless elegance.

### Where Hungarian Artists Found Inspiration

Matild Palace has long been a cultural beacon, alive with cafés, shops, and galleries. At its heart was the iconic Belvárosi Kávéház, a Belle Époque haven for artists and thinkers. Among them was Gyula Krúdy, who found inspiration here for his famed character, Sindbad, as ideas flowed freely in the vibrant café culture.
