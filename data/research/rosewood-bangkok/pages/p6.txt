Reviewed by

Why book?
Thai hospitality harmonizes beautifully with Rosewood’s hyper-polished service style at this art-filled glass tower that presides over Bangkok’s central business and shopping district.
Set the scene
Just two sharp turns from bustling Ploenchit Road is all it takes to find the driveway to Rosewood Bangkok, so neatly tucked away that its very position seems to shield guests from even the Thai capital’s sweltering heat. At least that’s what I thought when I arrived on a muggy May day and immediately felt a sense of calm as valets relieved me of my luggage and offered me a bottle of water. An elevator swept me up to the higher floors where the lobby sits next to the hotel’s all-day restaurant Lakorn. Draped across its splendid couches were predominantly Asian guests: aunties wearing vintage Vuitton gossiping over tea, polo-shirted parents and toddlers on holiday, bleach-blond influencers with tripods and selfie sticks, wedding-goers in their
CRA
finery. Such a scene was familiar to me: I grew up in Southeast Asia, and these grand lobby restaurants that act as living rooms for the city’s well-heeled have always made me feel right at home.
After check-in (swift and gracious, many “kop kun ka/kaps” were exchanged), I was escorted by a friendly guest manager to the 24th floor. Upon entering my room, I felt my shoulders drop instantly. Perhaps I was put at ease by the welcome gifts waiting for me—a bottle of Thai milk tea, chilling in an ice bucket like a bottle of Krug; a sweet confection in the shape of a bergamot orange underneath a glass cloche; a pillow embroidered with my initials, “M.O.” Maybe it was the floor-to-ceiling windows with sweeping views of Bangkok’s dense Lumpini commercial district that gave the impression of arriving onto a higher plane. But ultimately I think it was the attentiveness—the hotel’s high-caliber service warmed by Thai hospitality—that told me I would be well cared for at the Rosewood Bangkok.
The backstory
After the wellness-focused
Rosewood Phuket
, née 2017, the Hong Kong–based brand’s second outpost in Thailand arrived in 2019 with a splash. The purpose-built, 30-story tower that the hotel (and the hotel alone) calls home cuts a striking figure in the Bangkok skyline, shaped as it is after the wai, a Thai gesture of greeting made by pressing one’s palms together—a feat accomplished by Kohn Pedersen Fox, the New York–based architectural firm that counts among its projects the
Atlantis The Royal
in Dubai and Japan’s Otemachi Tower, home of the
Aman Tokyo
. The local cultural influence extends to the building’s well considered interiors: Modern sculptures and paintings by living Thai artists, and subtle design nods to the country’s silk-making history and garments worn by traditional Thai dancers, delighted me at every turn.
As far as specs go, the Rosewood Bangkok has 158 rooms, including 20 unique suites and 3 “signature houses” (penthouse keys with private pools and terraces), and 4 dining outlets. The event spaces easily accommodate business meetings and celebrations; on the weekend I stayed, there was a “small” wedding of about 150 guests.
The rooms
I stayed in one of the hotel’s Premier Rooms, which are corner units with a king bed, living area, and separate bathroom. Clocking in at about 500 square feet, the place was more than comfortably sized, with floor-to-ceiling windows for easy wake-ups with the sun. (Worry not, as the user-friendly controls at the bedside draw the shades swiftly.) The view overlooks Ploenchit Road, though I couldn’t see much at ground level from the 24th floor; more interesting to watch were the hotel patrons swimming in the floating infinity pool of The Okura Prestige, across the street from the Rosewood.
The room’s color palette was your typical five-star contemporary: dark hardwood floors and creamy gray carpet, white and black marble surfaces, brass fixtures and copper pendant lights, and splashes of blue and celadon. The winning details were a bronze sculpture of a goldfish next to the television; a blooming orchid on the desk; the bowl of clementines next to a Sonos speaker on the bedside table; and, on the loveseat, a cozy wool throw blanket with a pattern that resembled that of Thai silks.
In the bath (more marble) were a freestanding tub and a separate rainfall shower stall; it had a bench as well, perfect for any accessibility needs. The bathrobes were Frette, the hair dryer was Dyson, and the cotton swabs were kept in a cute ceramic jar. And a moment for the stellar minibar, generously stocked with local Thai goods: liquor from Phuket’s Chalong Bay, distilled from Thai sugarcane; a bone china teapot and teacups by Thai company Royal Porcelain; and a green tea of Assam leaves grown in the hills of Chiang Rai in northern Thailand, prepared by the brand Chaidim exclusively for the Rosewood Bangkok.
But my favorite part about the room is not guaranteed to every guest: the experience of waking up gently one night to the sound of rain on the glass walls, and watching the night wash away the day’s heat and smog from the comfort of my bed.
Food and drink
There are three main dining options at the Rosewood Bangkok. The lobby restaurant, Lakorn, was where I ate most of my meals. The menu features Thailand’s four culinary regions by spotlighting indigenous agriculture and teaming up with local farmers through its Partners in Provenance program. The breakfast buffet was notable, a visual and gastronomic feast, with fresh fruits, pastries, and a rainbow of juices; the made-to-order items were even more stellar—the khao mun gai (poached chicken rice) is sublime.
Go upstairs to find Nan Bei, where a massive menu of Chinese cuisine—nan and bei mean “south” and “north” respectively in Mandarin—overwhelmed me and my dining companion one evening, such was its embarrassment of riches. (During the day, set lunch and dim sum menus make the choosing less arduous.) Open kitchens showcase racks and racks of Peking ducks in the middle of swanky, Art Deco–inspired dining rooms within dining rooms that seem to go on forever. Hot tip: Whether at Nan Bei or Lakorn, grab a reservation at eight o’clock or later for something like a private meal; everyone else in the kingdom tends to take dinner very early.
Another handful of floors up is Lennon’s, a cocktail bar with libations named after 20th-century hits. If you don’t know what to have, pull the record from your menu—made to look like sleeves—have the team play it, and whatever song that comes on the speakers is your drink of choice. (I got “Diamonds Are a Girl’s Best Friend,” a sort of negroni with absinthe.) If the tunes aren’t to your liking, you have your pick from the bar’s vinyl collection, 6,000 records strong.
The spa
The entire sixth floor of the Rosewood Bangkok belongs to the spa (official name: Sense, A Rosewood Spa) and its many treatment suites, in one of which I had the best massage of my year thus far. Thai massage techniques are, in my view, the pinnacle of the craft; thanks to my flawless spa therapist, who used her hands, forearms, and elbows to work out my neck and back, I probably grew an inch in height that afternoon. As with the interior design and in the culinary concepts, the wellness program is an area in which the hotel incorporates the local culture and a sense of place extremely well.
To keep up fitness routines, the gym comes stocked with Technogym machines and plenty of natural light. Just outside is the indoor-outdoor saltwater pool, which also looks out onto the city below. As beautiful as the setup is, I should say that it was smaller than I had expected. When I went for a swim, there were a handful of families with children present, and though they weren’t exactly commandeering the pool, I didn’t feel like there was much square footage left to share. So I instead got a juice from G&O, the hotel’s poolside café and its fourth dining option, and sat on a day lounger to relax—not a bad option, given the pool area’s elevated perch on the ninth floor.
The neighborhood/area
I must immediately tell you that one of the major highlights of the Rosewood Bangkok’s position in the central neighborhood of Lumpini is its direct access to the Phloen Chit stop on the city’s skytrain transit system. This makes sightseeing a breeze, as the line links the property to tourist sites like the shopping mall CentralWorld, the aquarium Sea Life Bangkok Ocean World, the Jim Thompson House Museum (a must for any fan of fashion or Thai silks), the Victory Monument, and more. Though you don’t have to ride the train to take advantage of this access: These covered elevated walkways are also helpful in exploring the area on foot, connected as they are to the points of interest along Ploenchit Road like malls and temples.
That is all to say that the hotel is right in the thick of the tourist strip, alongside a clutch of the city’s five-starriest names: all within a mango’s throw of each other are the Rosewood, Ritz-Carlton, St. Regis, Waldorf Astoria, Anantara, Kempinski, Park Hyatt, and the new Aman, to name a few. Also within walking distance are the Siam Area, a bustling shopping district; and Lumpini Park and Benchakitti Park, perfect for morning runs. For other tourist must-sees like Chinatown, you'll need to commute, call a car, or tap the Rosewood’s concierge to set you up with one of the hotel’s cultural tours.
The service
At the Rosewood Bangkok, the team is a mix of local talent and seasoned managers imported from Rosewood properties elsewhere. They all operate at a high caliber, where the starch is softened by that famous Thai hospitality (the country is nicknamed “the Land of Smiles”). They remember names, press buttons for guests at the elevator banks, turn tables over quickly at breakfast, and happily fetch booster seats for little princes and princesses. One morning at Lakorn, when I ordered an egg white omelet, a server remembered that I’d mentioned I’m lactose intolerant and asked if I wanted the cottage cheese on the side. As with the hotel’s design, the staff succeeds in paying attention to the details.
For families
Families are very much part of the, well, family at Rosewood Bangkok. The staff are well prepared to handle kids, from providing high-chairs to having lifeguards on duty at the pool. And with many larger room categories available, families of all sizes will find a place to bed down that fits their needs.
