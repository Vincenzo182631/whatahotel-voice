Rosewood Schloss Fuschl
Rosewood’s second hotel in Austria combines the best lake view in the country with plenty of history.

Reviewed by

Why book?
First and foremost: the best lake views in all of
Austria
. The Rosewood Schloss Fuschl is located on the eastern shore of Lake Fuschl and from its terrace you can gaze over 2.5 miles to the opposite side. The majestic Alps rise up to the left and right.
Set the scene
Lake Fuschl is located in the Salzkammergut region, known for its numerous lakes, rivers, forests and limestone mountains. It is a dream for travelers seeking peace and quiet: the shores of Lake Fuschl are barely developed. If you set off on the approximately seven miles circular hiking trail, you will pass idyllic cow and sheep meadows a few miles in. If you start missing the hustle and bustle of city life, you still don’t have to travel far. Salzburg is only 15 miles away and it offers a wide range of cultural attractions, from Mozart’s birthplace to a wonderful Museum of Modern Art. Rosewood Schloss Fuschl provides individual transfers at any time of the day—or night.
The backstory
Fuschl Castle was built around 1461 as a hunting lodge under the Salzburg Prince Archbishop Sigmund l. von Gleink-Volkersdorf. It made its way to fame early on as a place of magnificent hunts and festivals. After World War II, it served as a home for US soldiers. A hotel was opened onsite for the first time in 1947. Now Rosewood has extensively restored the castle and modernized its interior as well as the newer parts of the building, in which the majority of the rooms, suites, the spa, and the lobby are located.
The rooms
Rosewood Schloss Fuschl comprises 98 rooms, including 42 suites. Visitors opting for accommodation in one of the newer parts of the building can expect first class comfort with a charming touch: sandstone brown wood flooring, forest green sofas and bathrooms with lime gray marble floors and walls. Those who prefer the old castle with its massive, partly lopsided walls will get excited about the fact that no two rooms are alike. The historic building is listed with its preservation in mind. Sounds charming? It definitely is: One of the bathrooms is tiny enough to not even fit the smallest sink. This is why interior designers ultimately went for a salad bowl from nearby Gmundner Keramik Manufaktur. Rosewood Fuschl’s Sissi suite can easily be considered a highlight with a freestanding bathtub, a century-old wooden wardrobe, and fluttering fabric panels around the bed (the modern interpretation of a royal four-poster bed). The six in-house chalets at Rosewood Schloss Fuschl are just as impressive: They are all directly located by the lake and have their own fireplace and butler.
Food and drink
Regional cuisine takes the spotlight in many places—Julian Schwamberger lives it. The chef and director of culinary at Rosewood Schloss Fuschl owns six culinary sites: Schloss Restaurant, Schloss Bar, See Terrasse and See Club (which includes a DJ and beach club vibes), Vinothek, and a Sissi Tee Salon. Local delicacies are served in all of them: Leberkäse, Schnitzel, Kaiserschmarrn, or Black Forest gateau. Local suppliers provide the ingredients. If the food starts to weigh on you, lighter options, especially plenty of fish and other seafood, are available as well—naturally from waters that are “on the doorstep,” including Lake Fuschl itself.
Spa
Rosewood is famous for its Asaya Spas, tranquil areas where body and mind come together. The Asaya Spa at Rosewood Schloss Fuschl is next level. It includes an indoor pool, an outdoor infinity pool, a winter garden and gym: all areas overlooking the peaceful lake. If this doesn't promote you into a zen-like state, check out the three saunas and a steam bath. Biohacking fans are offered a wide range of Biogena products at the spa. The leading supplement provider is one of the hotels’ regional connections: Their headquarters are located in Salzburg.
The service
Upscale and informal—anything else would feel inappropriate here. It is located amidst nature, with every corner exuding nativeness, and the service takes care to make the local population as well as guests feel welcome in the luxury castle. “We want to create a place people feel connected to—whether they’re arriving from afar or just around the corner,” says managing director Andreas Pade.
For families
Anyone who has ever checked into a Rosewood with children knows how much effort is made: Toys in the rooms can be considered standard, as well as individual meal requests. At the Rosewood Fuschl, a whole range of activities are also included: Fancy a trip to the nearby salt mines for an exploration? Or sliding down a 1.3-mile long summer toboggan run? Baking bread on a mountain pasture? Kayaking or SUPing on the lake? Not a problem. Rosewood Schloss Fuschl not only qualifies as a hotel, but rather an entire resort.
Eco effort
Regional cuisine is one thing. Rosewood Schloss Fuschl takes things a step further: During its restoration, care was taken to work with as many local trades as possible. For the furniture and decorations, many pieces were bought at local flea markets. There are vases, plates and stools everywhere, which appear to have been assembled especially for Rosewood Schloss Fuschl when in reality they are second-hand pieces. The typical Austrian wooden chairs with carved hearts in their backrests count as a prime example for this. You can check them out in the wine shop. Each one is unique and has a past life.
Accessibility
Old walls and new building regulations can be a challenge. That's why there is no elevator in the castle. If you want to go down to the lake, you should be able to walk well and manage a few stairs. Barrier-free living is possible in the newer parts of the building.
Anything left to mention?
The art! According to Rosewood Schloss Fuschl, it owns the world's largest collection of Old Masters outside a museum. This was already part of the former hotel business, but at the time was hidden away in the guest rooms. Today, over 100 works by artists like François Boucher, Giulio Carpioni, Henri Strésor, and Jacob Marrel are spread across the corridors, restaurants and bars visible to all visitors—the tried and tested, with a fresh twist. Like everything else at Rosewood Schloss Fuschl.
