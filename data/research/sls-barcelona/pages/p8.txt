The first European outpost from the SLS brand promises everything, and it delivers.

Reviewed by
María Ángeles Cano
“A waterfront wonderland” is how the new SLS
Barcelona
describes itself—words that could also be applied to the city that the hotel calls home. While there were no white rabbits or mad hatters in attendance at the grand opening, guests may feel a little like Alice as they discover the many surprises that SLS has included in its European debut.
Why book?
The better question may be “why not book?” once you realize all that this unique hotel offers. The design and staff exude an unrestrained luxury, the views of the Mediterranean provide a constant and wonderful backdrop, and the cuisine at its restaurants will have you wanting to dine at them for multiple meals. But perhaps the most appealing aspect of the hotel is how it combines an inviting and comfortable ambiance with the amenities of an urban resort.
The backstory
SLS hotels and resorts around the world have placed the brand at the top of any list of today’s luxury hospitality options. While different hotel groups bring their own unique approaches to luxury, SLS opts for one that is lively, carefree, and even sometimes theatrical, in the best sense of the word. Its hotels set out to attract attention, and they succeed. Its openings in destinations including Beverly Hills, Miami, the Bahamas, Cancun, Dubai, and Buenos Aires have been acclaimed, and now it’s Barcelona’s turn.
At the heart of the SLS take on luxury is a philosophy centered on lifestyle, which is why creating opportunities to socialize within the hotel is a key element of their properties. This trend can be seen in details such as the choice of music, which is more than just a background accompaniment and is instead central to creating a certain atmosphere, and in the many cozy spaces in its common areas that invite guests to linger.
The neighborhood
The new SLS is located in the heart of Barcelona’s Port Fòrum, moored on the shore of the sea like a grand yacht—although given its size perhaps a more accurate comparison would be to a cruise ship. Although it’s not an area frequented by many locals, SLS’s strategy to turn it into a new destination within Barcelona includes a collection of top restaurants, a cocktail bar that we expect will be the subject of much buzz, and a rooftop that aims to be the envy of the city. The area is also not too far from the popular Poblenou neighborhood and it’s easily accessible from the city center, both by tram and by subway.
While the SLS is filled with reasons to stay on property, the surrounding area has some attractions too making the hotel’s location a major draw. With direct access to the port, guests can rent catamarans and private boats for excursions, but they aren’t necessary if you simply want to spend a day at the beach, which is only 200 yards from the hotel and rarely crowded. The Parc del Fòrum, where the popular Primavera Sound festival is held (the hotel is an official partner), is also just a few steps away.
There are ambitious plans for this part of Barcelona including the creation of new green areas while the possibility of constructing a beach club is also being discussed. One new project already underway is Tres Xemeneies, which can be seen from the hotel’s rooftop. The architectural landmark, built as a power plant in the 1970s, will be transformed into a new home for creative industries, technological research, and digital experiences. As projects unfold, each one points to this area between Barcelona and Sant Adrià de Besòs becoming a new center of urban life.
The hotel
SLS Barcelona is the life of the party, that charming guest that everyone wants to hang out with, noted for their overwhelming charisma and infectious good mood. This makes it very difficult to leave once you walk through the hotel’s doors and start exploring all it offers. In the lobby, your eyes will be drawn to the pink neon lights that illuminate what looks like a path guiding Alice to Wonderland (it actually leads to the port). That bright hallway hypnotizes every guest and offers an understandably popular Instagram opportunity (one that we took advantage of ourselves).
The common areas and rooms have the playful and theatrical attitude that is typical of many Rockwell Group projects. The hotel’s interior design eschews minimalism and instead embraces the idea that more is more. This is a hotel for those who want to “see and be seen,” in the words of General Manager Ignacio Rodríguez, not for those who prefer to keep it all low-key. Extravagance and mischief reign here from the silver walls of the reception area to the enormous chandelier hanging at an unusual angle in the lobby and even to the floors of the elevators. The design of this SLS is addictive and leaves you wanting more.
The first floor leads to the pool with its views of the port. There’s a huge statue of a rubber duck which is the brand’s logo—you’ll also find it on the pins that employees wear on their uniforms. On the same floor, you’ll find the fitness center which, far from offering only the bare essentials to keep you in shape during your vacation, is instead a paradise for fitness-minded travelers. It’s flooded with natural light and has a multitude of machines, a spinning studio, and a wellness manager who can answer any questions and provide assistance. Plus, it’s open 24 hours a day.
As the saying goes, good things come to those who wait and the hotel will include a spa that will open its doors at the end of 2025. It will go beyond many hotel spa offerings and be an immersive experience that will transport you far from Barcelona. You’ll even be able to choose between different types of rain when you soak in the pool. Details regarding the treatments and other services will be unveiled in the coming months.
A feature that can be enjoyed now, the Grand Ballroom is one of the places where SLS has done the most to create a uniquely luxurious atmosphere. Any meeting or event held in the 8,600-square-foot space with room for up to 600 people will set the bar high. From its red curtains to other delightful details, the theatrical setting provides a backdrop for unforgettable events.
The rooms
When you finally make it to your room—getting lost in the SLS is easy, and fun—you’ll find the design is more restrained but even here there are theatrical and whimsical touches: avant-garde lamps, Venetian mirrors, surprising paintings, and even the curtain tiebacks are unusual. The crown jewel in rooms is the minibar—a small, mirrored bar topped by a circular shelf. You can imagine yourself as a mysterious character from a film, drinking a martini in your bathrobe.
Somehow the design by b720 Fermín Vázquez Arquitectos has managed to ensure that all 471 rooms in the hotel have balconies. These outdoor refuges allow guests to breathe in the sea air, admire the city, and, in some cases, look down at the scene by the pool. Among the rooms, 70 feature additional luxuries like a bathtub next to the balcony, a separate kitchen and dining room, and especially spacious private terraces.
Gastronomy
To do justice to the culinary offerings at SLS Barcelona, we would need much more space than the few paragraphs in this hotel review. There are a total of six stellar venues and Juan Gómez, culinary director, is responsible for assuring that they offer something for everyone, and at every hour of the day and evening.
We’ll start with the first restaurant you’ll encounter when you arrive at the hotel, Deluxe. It’s located in the lobby and its elegant bar is a tempting place to enjoy a few cocktails. There’s often a DJ and guests scattered at tables in the space. Here, appetizers, desserts, and other light snacks are served, varying according to the time of day. You’ll find homemade granola, poached eggs, and vegan burritos in the mornings, and steak tartare, katsu sandwiches, and plantains with lobster in the evenings.
Deluxe’s strong point is its cocktails. Their names are as original as their ingredients. Choose from the Not Fancy Paloma (white rum, lime, grapefruit, and bergamot liqueur), The Night Passenger (400 Conejos mezcal, passion fruit, lemongrass, lemon, and vanilla), and Breakfast with Diamonds (Stolichnaya, yuzu, chamomile, lemon, egg white, and Veuve Clicquot), and you can’t go wrong.
One floor up, next to the pool, Coral is a bar/restaurant where every season feels like summer. Its light dishes and refreshing drinks are best when enjoyed on sunny days with your feet in the water. Some must-try dishes include melon infused with mojito, green apple tabbouleh, chicken quesadilla, and coconut ice cream for dessert. Coral is the only restaurant at the SLS that is exclusively for hotel guests.
Now to move on to the main course: SLS’s flagship restaurants, Lora and L’Anxova Divina, both designed by
AvroKO
. Located off of the lobby, Lora takes you on a journey along the Mediterranean coast and the gastronomic magic of its villages. It is, in the restaurant’s words, “a journey from Barcelona to Beirut,” incorporating influences from North Africa. The elegant and enormous space (which will serve breakfast too) welcomes you with an open kitchen and there’s a wood-fired oven at the back of the room. It’s not only strikingly beautiful but also produces some of the most delicious breads in the world.
Justin Collinge, the executive chef at Lora, has a passion for cooking that is evident as soon as you start talking to him and hear his recommendations. We followed his suggestions and the result was a dining experience as exciting as he had promised. You can order anything from Lora’s menu and be confident it will be delicious, but if you want more guidance, we suggest starting with some of the dips like muhammara (roasted red peppers, chili, walnuts, and pomegranate molasses) and, my absolute favorite, spinach
borani
(with caramelized onions, yogurt, feta cheese, and pistachios), along with some pita and kubaneh (a brioche-style bread). Standouts among the main courses include sea bass with chermoula and grilled octopus with hamoosta sauce. The jewel in the restaurant’s crown, is an exquisite raw sea bass with muscat vinaigrette, orange, and pickled fennel. It alone would justify a trip to Barcelona. We haven't forgotten about dessert: the blood orange sorbet is incredible and the
bougatsa
(a crispy filo pastry with custard and vanilla ice cream) is a perfect conclusion to a meal.
The next stop is L’Anxova Divina. At the end of a brightly lit hallway, a fantastic and colorful universe awaits. The restaurant celebrates Catalan cuisine and executive chef Oriol Lagé leads diners on their culinary journeys here. His love affair with local cuisine is reflected in everything from his careful selection of products and ingredients to the menu that pays homage to tapas culture and regional flavors. The design of its more informal spaces is inspired by one of Barcelona’s most famous sons, the architect Antoni Gaudí.
We recommend ordering a selection of tapas so you can try as many as your appetite will allow. Among the options are classics such as a Spanish potato and onion omelet, patatas bravas, fried pork snout with adobo dressing, Perol’s botifarra sausage and toasted brioche with butter, anchovy picadillo, and fried capers. Alongside garden produce, rice dishes, and cured meats, which always taste better when accompanied by one of their breads such as coca bread with tomato, there are classic Catalan casseroles and stews like the delicious beef fricassee with mushrooms, arbequina olive tapenade, and sage oil. Choosing between desserts is no easy task, but two good options are the Garrotxa cheesecake and a traditional favorite, bread with olive oil, chocolate, and salt.
Finally, the last stop as you make your way up the hotel is Cósmico, the rooftop bar which promises to be an exciting addition. (It wasn’t open at the time of our visit.) Its menu will include cocktails and floating trays of sushi that are intended to be enjoyed next to, or even in, the pool. It will be accessible directly from the street without having to go through the lobby, and we expect its sunset views and live DJs will make it a favorite of both visitors to Barcelona and locals too.
Drinks
If you’ve been keeping count, you’ll know that we still haven’t covered all six gastronomic spaces. Kyara deserves a special mention of its own. This bar on the fifth floor of the hotel has emerged as one of the SLS’s biggest attractions. The famous mixology duo Monica Berg and Alex Kratena have launched their first project in Spain, and they did it in grand style. Their names will be familiar to many followers of cocktail culture from their award-winning Tayēr + Elementary bar in London, and now they aim to continue their success with this new postmodern cocktail bar.
Their approach to cocktails here draws inspiration from the world of perfumes, as they translate the principles of creating fragrances to the world of beverages. Unusual ingredients are used to make both traditional cocktails and inspired new creations. The space has a retro-futuristic interior design incorporating avant-garde details in keeping with the theme that this is an unusual laboratory of refreshment.
Sustainability
In this hotel that aspires to be on the cutting edge of hospitality, sustainability is a central priority. To this end, the SLS has used sustainable, healthy, and responsibly sourced materials and has adopted many measures to reduce energy usage, carbon output, and water consumption such as solar panels and the purchase of green energy, choosing native and drought-resistant plants for the landscaping, and making every effort possible to reduce waste including by eliminating single-use plastics, using keys made from recycled wood, and implementing a recycling program. When it comes to sustainability, no aspect of the building or its surroundings has been overlooked.
Accessibility
All of the hotel was designed to be accessible to guests with limited mobility.
Anything else to add?
With the third season of
The White Lotus
still fresh in our minds, you may notice the multitude of monkeys incorporated into the hotel's décor, transporting us to the famous hotel in Thailand from the series. Finding the monkeys hidden in the SLS Barcelona—in the paintings, in the decorative elements, and even in the form of lamps—can be an amusing pastime at the hotel. Go see if you can find them all!
