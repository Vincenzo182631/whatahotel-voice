Reviewed by
Ramsey Qubein
Why book?
This refined oasis stands head and shoulders above the mostly chain-link competition in Sandton thanks to its lush garden surroundings, top-tier service, and African-inspired decor that delivers a sense of place that is hard to match.
Set the scene
Frenetic Sandton bustles with traffic, office and residential towers, and commercial enterprises. But once you enter the gates of the Saxon Hotel, Villas and Spa, it’s another world. This is the kind of hotel where local socialites gather, and in-the-know business guests on an expense account intermingle. You’re far more likely to see private vans from first-class operators like Micato Safaris here than the big tour buses that pull into other Sandton hotels. While children are not a focus, families will be perfectly happy here, too.
The backstory
Once the late real estate billionaire Douy Steyn’s family home, this sprawling property became a hotel in 2000 and has hosted some of the world’s top dignitaries. The celebrated guest list includes big names like Oprah Winfrey, Bill Clinton, Princes William and Harry, and perhaps the most famous, Nelson Mandela, who penned his autobiography “Long Walk to ” here.
Climb the pair of curving staircases in the reception area to explore the walls of interesting regional art, photography, and statues. It helps tell the story of the hotel and many of its notable guests.
The rooms
The rooms are divided between the main building and the luxury villas, accessible via an alfresco bridge. They showcase contemporary interiors with plank floors topped with area rugs and duvet-wrapped beds. Most overlook the gardens, but the biggest pops of color come from the original African accent fabrics on pillows and artwork on the walls or shelves. Open-plan baths are bright with dual vanities, soaking tubs, and separate showers.
The rooms that overlook the pool are among the most appealing. Don’t be shy about checking out the minibar as its contents are part of the room rate.
There are three standalone villas with central living rooms and guest rooms that branch off of different hallways. These can be booked as individual rooms or entire units for large groups or those who want maximum privacy. The top-tier suites come with dining rooms, kitchens, and butler service to attend to every need.
Food and drink
While there are great choices in the neighborhood, most guests seem to cocoon themselves here, and why not? Qunu, the main restaurant, overlooks the pool and gardens from interior and outdoor dining spaces. Sunday brunch is a local favorite, and the wine-tasting menus make their mark with impressive selections, many from the Western Cape.
Vegan and vegetarian taste buds are well-sated (the chef has his own on-site garden), but it is the eclectic carnivorous selection that earns the highest accolades. You can tuck into everything from ostrich and duck to venison and Wagyu beef filet here.
Don’t miss breakfast, which is served in the piano lounge and included in the rates. Later, lunch and afternoon tea often fill the tables with locals or guests seeking live music and a great way to wind down the workday. The Olive Bar opens to tables on the patio in warmer months and has a menu of sharing plates to pair with a drink.
The spa
Saxon Spa stays busy with more local business than guests, thanks to its highly regarded massage and treatment menu. After a long flight, this is the place to catch a break by the fireplace with an herbal elixir and a good rubdown or a dip in one of the whirlpools. For those who can chill on their own, the cushioned lounge chairs by the guest-only pool are the ultimate in serenity (for no extra charge).
The neighborhood/area
Sandhurst is a mix of swanky residences and quiet streets away from the center of busy Sandton. Arriving at the hotel’s guarded main driveway is calming in its own way because you feel like you have left the city entirely. There is no traffic noise here, and that’s what has made the Saxon a true getaway.
The service
Service staff here operate to the highest standards. A job at the Saxon is highly coveted, and guests end up on the receiving end of creme de la creme service in Sandton (if not Johannesburg as a whole).
For families
Kids won’t find much to entertain them here, and the pool is more a quiet refuge than a place to perfect the ultimate cannonball splash.
Eco effort
The hotel is marching along a path to zero-landfill waste, helped in part by reductions in single-use plastics and paper materials, plus a swift recycling program. A pair of rainwater reservoirs helps keep its gardens so green while reducing water and energy use.
Accessibility
Wheelchair accessibility is in full swing here with elevators, ramps, and a subset of guest rooms at the ready.
Anything left to mention?
As a member of The Leading Hotels of the World, anyone who joins its free loyalty program receives special perks like late checkout considerations.
If you’re lucky, you can twist the concierge’s arm to show you the most famous suite (and desk) where Mandela wrote his famous book. Otherwise, the impressive collection of art and photos details important periods of the former president’s life throughout the hotel.
