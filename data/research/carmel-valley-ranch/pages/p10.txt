Carmel Valley Ranch
A taste of farm life with the service of a resort and stellar valley views that offer plenty of room to roam.

Reviewed by

Best for:
Anyone who tends not to sit still for very long and enjoys being outside
Location:
The foothills of Monterey’s Santa Lucia Mountains, about eight miles inland from the Pacific Ocean
Can’t-miss feature:
All the wide-open spaces—get out and frolic
In a nutshell
: It’s fun! Set on 500 stunning acres, Carmel Valley Ranch is a smile-inducing hub of activity. From birding adventures to cooking classes and splashing about in a trio of swimming pools, the list of possibilities goes on and on.
Rooms
All of the 179 accommodations at Carmel Valley Ranch are suites ranging from studios to four bedrooms. Want a fireplace? Every suite has one. Fancy a deck? You’re covered. With plenty of leather and wood furnishings, suites can skew a tad on the darker side (especially in the evenings), but they have a comforting warmth that makes settling in oh so easy.
Swaths of lavender erupt on resort grounds every summer, and it’s showcased in custom-made toiletries. Room views vary from the valley and golf course to vineyards and stretches of oak trees that filter the view of The Lodge and the adjacent parking lot, so if you have your heart set on a specific vista, take care when booking. After many stays, the Valley Suites are my favorite. They’re a bit further from The Lodge pool and restaurant, but I enjoy the disconnect. And traversing the aerial pathway above the roadway is kind of fun.
Food and drink
Menus at signature restaurant Valley Kitchen showcase fresh, local ingredients—many of which are grown or produced at the resort. Think goat cheese ricotta, made using milk from the Carmel Valley Ranch herd, topped with honeycomb from the resort’s apiary. Fancy a cocktail? The Lavender Mule pairs housemade, cold-pressed lavender syrup with The Botanist gin. If you'd rather wine, the resort boasts a four-acre working vineyard—depending on where you are sitting, you might be able to see the vines—so a glass of the estate-grown Pinot Noir is another good bet.
Be sure to save room for dessert. Pastry chef Molly Coen won the first season of Food Network’s
The Ultimate Baking Championship
with creations such as Campfire S’mores, made with chocolate mousse, milk chocolate Chantilly, honey cinnamon cake, torched marshmallow, and graham “soil.” Coen is also responsible for the resort’s breakfast pastries. I passed on a croissant in favor of a double-chocolate muffin with Oreo cookies and have no regrets. The Hen-House Egg White Burrito, with queso fresco, baby spinach, potato, salsa, and guacamole, was a lighter favorite.
Amenities and experiences
You’ll receive a list of scheduled activities at check-in; peruse it or risk missing out. Daily activities range from guided hikes and yoga to archery lessons and picnics with Pablo and Paco, a pair of resident miniature donkeys. If the blinds are up at the on-site creamery, stop by the window to watch the cheesemaker in action. Along with chickens and a salt house, the organic garden is home to a rainbow of veggies, flowers, and honeybees—suiting up and stepping into the apiary was a highlight of my stay. If you can find the time, there are multiple pools, tennis and pickleball courts, fitness classes, and an 18-hole Pete Dye–designed golf course and driving range. Pampered pooches lucky enough to visit the resort are spoiled with a seemingly never-ending supply of biscuits, pup cups, and a dedicated menu (available at the restaurant and via room service) to call their own.
What to do nearby
Carmel Valley Ranch makes a strong case for you to simply stay put and enjoy the expansive property, but the Monterey Peninsula offers a wealth of memorable things to see and do. Known for its jaw-dropping coastline and Monterey Cypress trees, Point Lobos State Natural Reserve is just shy of a nine-mile drive. Charming Carmel-by-the-Sea is equally as close. Give yourself about a half-hour to make the trip to the Monterey Bay Aquarium and historic Cannery Row. Oenophiles will adore Carmel Valley; there are more than 20 tasting rooms and wineries in the area.
The backstory
Originally opened as a private golf club in 1981, Carmel Valley Ranch became a resort in 1987. Hyatt heir John Pritzker purchased the property in 2009. From the lavender and vineyards to the apiary, creamery, and corral, he’s credited with shaping the resort that folks experience today.
Insider tip
Lavender lovers, don’t sleep on the in-room bubble bath. It’s an easy way to sneak in some me time first thing in the morning or at the end of the day (or both). Bottles are generous, so you’ll have enough for a few luxurious soaks at home too.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes, it’s great to be a kid at Carmel Valley Ranch!
Accessibility:
There are seven suites adapted for guests with mobility disabilities. The property is spread out; paths can be hilly, but they are paved, and there are
accessible features
throughout.
Wellness:
Along with expected massage and facial offerings, you’ll find sleep therapy and hypnosis on the menu at Spa Aiyana.
Loyalty group/hotel brand:
World of Hyatt
