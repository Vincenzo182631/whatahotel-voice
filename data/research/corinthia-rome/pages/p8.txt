Corinthia Rome
This latest Corinthia has given Rome back a part of its heritage, with sleek, modern design in the former offices of the Bank of Italy.

Why book?
The new five-star luxury hotel in
Rome
has many stories to tell, but equally fascinating are the stories that are playing out today. In the Viride and Piazzetta restaurants, diners enjoy delicious dishes from one of the country’s star chefs, while the collection of art, objects, and books speaks to Italian culture and design. In the words of managing director Danilo Zucchetti: “The Corinthia in Rome is a perfect example of what we call a ‘grand boutique’: an intimate property with the services of a grand hotel.”
Set the scene
The new Corinthia is the 23rd five-star luxury hotel in Rome. Second only to London, Rome has seen the most high-end hotel openings of any European city in the last five years. There’s a fierce competition in the Italian capital to see who can open the most beautiful, most original, most innovative, and most locally authentic hotel. The Corinthia definitely does well when it comes to location, facing the Palazzo Montecitorio, the seat of the Italian parliament, and near the Via del Corso. Look out your window, and you can imagine the politicians at work; go down to the bar at Viride (the fine-dining restaurant) or Piazzetta (the more casual option), and you may cross paths with one of Italy’s political leaders.
The palace—with its austere and elegant façade, generous spaces, high ceilings, and beautiful terraces—has been effectively preserved. Eagerly awaited in the city, the first Italian property from Corinthia has opted for classic hospitality, emphasising comfort and discretion. With just 60 rooms, it is easy to find a quiet, private corner, perhaps in the library or the spa.
The backstory
Until 1961, the 1920s building served as the offices of the Bank of Italy. After changing names and uses several times in the decades leading up to 2000, it was then closed for years until it was acquired, in 2019, by David and Simon Rueben, who wanted to turn it into Italy’s first Corinthia hotel. During its transformation, the building was carefully restored to its original state, informed by extensive historical research. The project uncovered exquisite mosaics, stuccoes, cornices, painted ceilings, and marble details. The centerpiece is the Sala del Consiglio on the piano nobile, with frescoes by Giulio Bargellini on the theme of prosperity. The ceiling is decorated with representations of Italian coins from the Etruscans to the early 20th century, the floor consists of colorful geometric mosaics, and gazing down on the scene are busts of various iconic Italian figures, from Marco Polo to Alessandro Volta.
The rooms
The 60 rooms have some common denominators: lots of space, plenty of light, and marble bathrooms. Then, as one moves up through the different categories, there are more precious inlays and mosaics, beautiful wall coverings, larger lounges, and other extras until you arrive at the four themed suites with private terraces overlooking the roofs and domes of Rome. From the Arte Suite, you can see the Vatican, while the Theodoli Heritage Suite, on the first floor, incorporates the Sala de Consiglio, where the bank’s board of directors would meet, as its living room. All rooms include Dyson hairdryers, a steamer to freshen clothes, a minibar, and lots of books on art, culture, and history (in room 406, where I stayed, I counted 83 books in total).
Food and drink
At the helm of the hotel’s food and beverage offerings is Carlo Cracco, one of Italy’s most famous chefs, thanks to his appearances on the television programs
MasterChef
and
The
Dinner Club
. In addition to television fame, Cracco has also long been a pioneer when it comes to contemporary Italian haute cuisine, with innovative dishes such as marinated egg and caramelized Russian salad served as a starter at Viride, the fine dining restaurant with 28 seats and a 10-course tasting menu. Then there is the Piazzetta bistro that serves classic Roman dishes, such as tonnarelli cacio e pepe and a rigatoni “carbonara” style (which is much lighter than traditional carbonara dishes). Breakfast doesn’t include the gargantuan spread with anything and everything, which you'll find at many hotels. Instead, there’s just one table with cereals and another for fruit and juices. Everything else is brought to your table, from a basket of breads and croissants to a long list of egg dishes. I recommend the soft-boiled egg with Parmigiano Reggiano and guanciale. Even the snacks and drinks at the Ocra bar were created under the direction of the multitasking Cracco. Rest assured that here too, under the gold-leaf ceiling, you may be seated next to luminaries of Rome’s cultural and political scenes.
The spa
Press the -2 button in the elevator, and you’ll soon find yourself in an inviting urban retreat located in the former bank vault. Now, instead of holding gold and jewels, it’s a repository of wisdom on how to live well and feel good, inspired by the rituals of the ancient Romans. There’s a tepidarium, calidarium, and frigidarium, while a path leads from two baths at different temperatures to a hammam, sauna, ice fountain, and then beds of heated black stones where you can recline and relax. Two lines have been chosen for massages and beauty treatments: the Italian brand Seed to Skin, which uses natural Tuscan botanicals, and the anti-aging line 111Skin, developed by plastic surgeon Yannis Alexandrides.
The neighborhood/area
It’s hard to imagine a better location in Rome. Located between the Tiber, the Pincio terrace, and the Spanish Steps, Campo Marzio is a central historic district with a concentration of landmarks and elegant shopping streets, as well as many small independent and artisan boutiques.
The service
There is one thing about Rome that always puts every visitor in a good mood: the friendliness of the city’s residents. At the Corinthia, they greet you with spontaneous smiles and always keep an attentive eye turned your way, diligent about assuring that every guest is happy. This is true of the doormen, the front desk, the restaurant staff, housekeeping, and everyone else you’ll encounter. Their pride in working at the Corinthia translates into an atmosphere that’s warm and welcoming.
Eco effort
The hotel has applied for its LEED assessment following the adoption of a number of sustainability and energy-saving measures during the property’s renovation and current operations.
Accessibility
Every effort has been made to make public areas accessible, though the listed nature of the building presents some challenges. There are four guestrooms specifically designed for those with mobility, hearing, or visual impairments.
Anything left to mention?
We recommend taking at least one tour of the property while looking up—the Corinthia’s ceilings deserve to be admired. Stucco, gold leaf, and frescoes now decorate them; some details are delicate, and others are bold. Also, take time to note the many Italian products and sources of inspiration found at the property. To create a hotel that is a celebration of Italy was one of Corinthia’s main goals, and it’s reflected in the names of the hotel’s different spaces, the Italian wines in the minibar, and the Lazio producers who supply the restaurant with its ingredients. An inside tip: we have been told that Corinthia has two more projects on the way in Italy—one on Lake Como, the other in Puglia. I'm eagerly anticipating both.
