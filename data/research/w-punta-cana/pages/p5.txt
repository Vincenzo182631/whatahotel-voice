W Punta Cana, Adult All-Inclusive: First In
The W Hotel’s first foray into the world of all-inclusives.

Reviewed by
Jessica Sulima
Why book?
To settle with ease on Punta Cana’s lush Uvero Alto, knowing that everything—rooms, dining, experiences—will be sorted under high standards.
Set the scene
I’ve been to an all-inclusive resort twice in my life—once, on a family vacation when I was too young to have a palate, and again, during college spring break, when I was old enough to know better. When I think about the concept of an
all-inclusive
, a predictable color story comes to mind: white sand beach, green palm tree, blue pool, yellow piña colada. But I understand that all-inclusives have experienced a resurgence these last few years, appealing to travelers (like myself) who usually come back from a vacation saying they need a vacation from their vacation.
We’re burnt out
, it seems, and we want a respectable entity like the Marriott to tell us how to chill. And that’s how I ended up, mostly horizontal, at the W Punta Cana for three days in September.
The one-hour drive from Punta Cana International Airport was a procession of walled-in resorts, but there was something decidedly open—and especially chic—about the entrance to this one, emblazoned with that trademark “W.” Green plants unfurled from high, wood-crated ceilings, water trickled down like rain from a towering waterfall, and the property seemed to unfold via airy tunnels. Though the resort was only at about 25% capacity when I arrived, I could see the general lay of the adults-only land: business-y types, married couples, and a group of friends, presumably in their 30s.
The friendly front desk staff prompted me to download the Marriott Experiences app, from which I could view dining options, access a map of the resort, and check out daily programming, which included everything from Tabata HIIT workouts to Dominican cacao tastings. I promptly made myself at home on the WET Deck, which featured a 260-foot infinity pool mirroring the Atlantic shoreline.
The backstory
This is the W Hotel’s first foray into the world of all-inclusives. Construction began on the site in 2023, and the resort officially opened its doors in June of this year. The minimalist structure, designed by architect Pino Violante of
Zanobia Arquitectura
, is horseshoe-shaped, which gives it a communal feel, but there are pockets that call for more private moments as well, like the palm-shaded Chill Pool (where you can listen to lo-fi music and request a spritz from the swim-up bar). The natural surroundings are highlighted by way of blue reflection ponds and elevated walking paths that preserve the endemic plants. Native materials, like Caballero white marble from the north coast, run throughout the property, giving it a sandy, neutral appearance. I was curious about the pyramid-like structures that dot the premises, and was pleased to discover that they serve a sustainable purpose: concealing a network of service pathways that enhance energy efficiency.
The rooms
The W Punta Cana boasts some of the largest entry-level rooms in the Dominican Republic, each measuring 700 square feet and featuring a bathtub and king-size bed. Beyond the standard offerings, you can opt for rooms with everything from private plunge pools and ocean views to outdoor showers and direct beach access.
I was lucky enough to stay in a two-bedroom Oasis Suite, which was so big I did numbers on my step count getting from one point to another. Neutral interiors—from the brown, midcentury-modern cabinetry to the sleek slate floors—were accented by rugs and decorative pillows that came in pops of red, orange, and blue. While the design was certainly understated, there was a touch of whimsy in the bulbous light fixtures and swirly, contemporary art pieces. The suite came equipped with a family-style dining table, kitchenette, massive sectional couch, and—best of all—a terrace featuring a private infinity pool that looked out into the ocean. I will say, though, that not every view was perfect: One of the bedrooms faced a plot of somewhat undeveloped land—what the resort refers to as the “Tropical View.” According to the team at W Hotels, this will eventually get covered with a canopy of green as vegetation continues to grow.
Food and drink
Perhaps the easiest way to distinguish a run-of-the-mill all-inclusive from a luxury one is the food and beverage options. I was curious to see whether or not the dishes were going to be reheatable, buffet-esque offerings repackaged on a selection menu. But they tasted, in fact, made-to-order, and all coming out piping hot (except for the fresh ceviche, of course). From my five-course Caribbean meal at Scena to the nori tempura bites at Noodle Bar, I was impressed by the attention to detail (think ingredients like hand-fermented hibiscus flowers and seaweed-infused butters) and the fact that each of the four restaurants I tried had a distinct cocktail menu.
There are 12 food and drink outposts on the property (though not all were open when I visited). I would describe the cuisine I tasted as bold and experimental, and as such, sometimes you’ll get hits and sometimes you’ll get misses. There were moments when I preferred to forego the foams and emulsions for something much simpler, and dishes like the done-to-perfection Dominican fried chicken and plantains served on “Colmadito Night” at The Plaza really hit the spot. As someone who is adamant about avoiding food waste, I was pleased to see that portions were reasonable, a far cry from the excess normally associated with all-inclusives. Perhaps my favorite feature—and the most luxurious, might I add—was the 24-hour in-room dining menu. It was a delight to order fresh fish for lunch when I didn’t feel like leaving my room, or a cheeky plate of chicken fingers when I was hungry in between meals.
The spa
The two-story, 4,000-square-foot Away Spa features 10 treatment rooms, several pools (including hot pools and cold plunges), a beauty salon, a Himalayan salt sauna, a steam room, and a fully-equipped, 24-hour gym. Hydrotherapy experiences are included with all treatments, though these are not part of the all-inclusive package. The treatments, which range from body work to facials, make use of local products including turmeric, arnica, and the single-pressed moringa seed oil, often referred to on the island as “miracle oil.”
I opted for the Herbal Healing Body and Face treatment, which involved an exfoliation session, a deep-tissue massage featuring the moringa oil, and an express facial. I appreciated how the spa menu was flexible with add-ons, and I was able to easily supplement my facial with extractions. Before my treatment, I had a fun time engaging with the sauna and steam rooms, and one of the lovely spa assistants talked me into a boot camp-style session of 40-second back-to-back intervals in the cold and hot pools. The entire experience was the highlight of my stay, and I left the spa somehow feeling both relaxed and energized.
The neighborhood/area
The Uvero Alto region is a more secluded stretch of Punta Cana, famous for its laid-back beaches. With the Cordillera Mountains looming in the background, there are plenty of outdoor excursions to embark upon, which you can book through the resort’s Adventure Center. These include everything from mountain buggy rides and cenote dives to private sails and visits to national parks—all within a 45-minute drive. There are also private tours bookable via the hotel’s agents that offer a snapshot of Dominican culture, like mamajuana tastings at a nearby ranch.
The service
There was always someone there to hand me a towel when I sat down on a sun lounger or point me in the right direction whenever I looked lost walking through the resort. You could tell the staff were excited to be a part of this new enterprise, and I interacted with a number of employees—from the barista who remembered my coffee order at the Outpost Café to the hype guy performing poolside magic tricks with a deck of cards—whose spirit was contagious. I was assigned a “personal butler” (a service offered when booking Supreme or Oasis suites) who was able to assist me via WhatsApp with anything I needed, from dinner reservations to the delivery of a hot water kettle after dinner. It seemed almost silly to need an assistant at a resort where meal times and activities are already scheduled on an app, but I will not lie, I enjoyed it when Algenis earnestly asked me how I was feeling, in a manner that suggested he actually cared. It made the experience all the more personal.
Eco effort
As mentioned above, the property is designed to take maximum advantage of the environment, from the marine breeze to natural light. It makes use of photovoltaic solar systems, natural ventilation, and rooftop insulation with local gravel. Native landscaping calls for lower maintenance, thus reducing water use.
Accessibility
The whole property is wheelchair accessible, with elevators throughout. Six room styles are fully ADA compliant, and four are hearing-impaired-adapted.
Anything left to mention?
At the time of my visit, there was one guest room building that had not yet opened, estimated to be completed in October. The beach, like many in the Caribbean and Mexico, suffered from a bit of a sargassum problem, but this is likely to clear up once hurricane season ends in November.
