Grand Hyatt Singapore
For chic comfort in the heart of Singapore’s main shopping area

Set the scene
This longstanding, always-buzzy hotel’s central location has turned it into a community institution—it’s a popular hangout for office and family gatherings (its superb halal buffet restaurant, Straits Kitchen, is a big draw) and wedding celebrations. The sprawling spa and fitness center, which is open to non-guests, is a go-to for executives working nearby and the ladies-who-lunch. And Brix, the basement nightclub, has long attracted a crowd looking for live music and a good time. The hotel’s cavernous marbled lobby, with its wooden columns and iconic spiral staircase, often doubles as a photo spot, too.
The backstory
One of
Singapore
’s earliest five-star stays, the Grand Hyatt Singapore—owned by Brunei’s sovereign wealth fund—held the distinction of being Southeast Asia’s tallest hotel when it opened as the 22-story Hyatt Regency in 1971. It was also the Hyatt group’s largest hotel at the time, with 700 rooms. In 2024, it underwent its most extensive renovation yet, its interiors stripped to the bone then resurrected under the care of design director Yohei Akao of the award-winning design firm Strickland, and renowned landscape architects Grant Associates. The makeover has transformed the hotel into a green oasis in one of the country’s busiest, most built-up areas, with pockets of lush landscaping woven throughout almost all of its spaces.
The rooms
This isn’t your run-of-the-mill, corporate Hyatt. The rooms are decidedly chic, with polished dark wood fittings and modernist chairs that design buffs will be tempted to cart home. The soft furnishings are dressed in elegant tones of spring green; the mirrors and ceilings accented with intricately woven rattan. Lead-in category rooms start from a spacious 450-square-foot and come with smart comforts like double entrances for the bathroom, and a daybed in an alcove to lounge in.
Food and drink
The crown jewel in the hotel’s culinary repertoire is Le Pristine, a stunning, art installation-like outpost of the avant-garde, Michelin-starred Antwerp restaurant, which also has a branch in Tokyo. But really, you’ll get a tasty meal or tipple anywhere here—this is one hotel that even food-obsessed locals keep returning to for meals, whether it’s Straits Kitchen’s wide-ranging buffet, hearty Italian plates at Pete’s Place, or delicious grilled meats and seafood at the poolside bistro Oasis. Tip: if you’re not up for a full meal at Le Pristine, you can order snacks from the restaurant to Martini Bar.
The spa
The award-winning Damai Spa has long been one of Singapore’s top-rated. Post-renovation, it boasts even more state-of-the-art offerings, like the signature, science-backed Recovery treatment, developed in-house, that combines advanced heat therapy, light therapy, and acupressure massages to brilliantly combat jet lag. For longtime fans of the spa, popular classics like the Damai massage with warm oil remain. Meanwhile, Damai House, with its mind-body-therapy wall, is a space for functional movement classes—that are free for guests—from vertical yoga to tai chi.
The neighborhood/area
A stay here puts you right in the action of Singapore’s main shopping belt. The streets swirl with fashionable folk and tourists. Next door, to your left, is Singapore’s oldest department store, Tangs, one of the best places to pick up local souvenirs. On your right, a couple of doors down, you’ll find Far East Plaza, a haven for used designer goods (particularly watches and bags), and one of the most reasonably priced places to eat in the stretch. Across the road, buskers play to crowds in the evenings. Everywhere you go, there is something to buy or watch or do.
The service
It really feels like the staff are looking out for you here, whether it’s by discreetly moving you to a more private seat at the cafe when one frees up without you asking, or offering to swap out your veggies for fries when they learn you’re not a fan of greens. All of which may have something to do with the hotel’s employee wellbeing program—this is the first Hyatt in the world to have a wellbeing director overseeing staff wellness.
For families
The daybeds in the rooms can be converted to beds that sleep two kids below the age of 12. The sprawling new club lounge has play areas for children.
Eco effort
Even though this is a structure that’s over 50-years-old, its environmental features are state-of-the-art. The hotel’s owners took the opportunity to build in sustainable systems when the building was gutted during its latest renovation, which means it now has its own bespoke water bottling plant; a waste recycling vacuum system developed in-house that’s been so successful, other Hyatts have adopted it—and a clutch of other industry-leading green features.
Accessibility
The hotel is set up for accessibility, with accessible rooms, a network of ramps, and wide corridors.
Anything left to mention?
One of the loveliest spaces in the hotel is the lush Garden Oasis on the fifth floor, with its winding paths, banyan trees, and butterflies. It’s part of the Orchard Road Butterfly Trail, a green-corridor initiative developed in partnership with the national parks board and the Nature Society Singapore, and is filled with nectar-bearing plants that support around 50 species of butterflies and other pollinators.
