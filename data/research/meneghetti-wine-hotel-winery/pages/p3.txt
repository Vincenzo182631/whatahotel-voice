### A village in Stone

In the historic center of Bale, centuries unfold through narrow cobbled streets and arched passageways shaped by Venetian influence. The Gothic-Renaissance Soardo-Bembo Castle anchors the town with quiet nobility, while small galleries, local ateliers and intimate squares give it a living, contemporary rhythm. Just beyond the stone walls, olive groves and open countryside stretch toward the sea, inviting slow walks and unhurried exploration. In Bale, heritage is not displayed, it is inhabited, creating an atmosphere that feels authentic, intimate and deeply connected to place.

### Art of Truffle Hunting

In the quiet forests of inland Istria, one of the world’s most prized delicacies lies hidden beneath ancient oak and hornbeam trees. In the company of seasoned local truffle hunters and their impeccably trained dogs, the search unfolds as a slow and sensory ritual, guided by intuition, patience and deep knowledge of the land. The experience concludes in an intimate countryside setting, where freshly unearthed truffle is delicately prepared and served at its peak. Rare, seasonal and profoundly rooted in place, it offers a glimpse into Istria’s most treasured traditions.

### The Art of the Rovinj

Behind the ancient stone walls of Rovinj, a world of narrow passageways and hidden perspectives reveals itself gradually. Polished steps lead upward toward the hilltop Church of St. Euphemia, while discreet galleries, artisan studios and intimate terraces unfold along the way. Between centuries-old houses, sudden openings frame uninterrupted views of the Adriatic, reminding you how closely the town lives with the sea. In Rovinj’s Old Town, every turn feels intentional, every detail preserved, creating an atmosphere that is at once storied, elegant and quietly captivating.

### The Amphitheatre by the Sea

In the heart of Pula rises the magnificent Arena, one of the world’s best-preserved Roman amphitheatres. Built in the 1st century, it once hosted gladiatorial contests before tens of thousands of spectators and still commands awe today.

Beyond the Arena, ancient temples, triumphal arches, and elegant squares reveal layers of Roman and Austro-Hungarian heritage. Pula offers grandeur and atmosphere in equal measure - a city where history stands monumental against the Istrian sky.

### Stone, Salt and Sky

At the southern tip of Istria, Cape Kamenjak reveals a landscape shaped by wind and sea. Rugged cliffs rise above crystalline waters, while hidden coves invite quiet swims in untouched surroundings.

Coastal trails wind through wild herbs and low Mediterranean shrubs, opening to sweeping Adriatic views. Here, nature feels elemental and unfiltered. Stone beneath your feet, salt in the air, sky stretching endlessly overhead. It is Istria in its purest, most powerful form.

### Grožnjan - Town of Living Art

Grožnjan is a small medieval town transformed by creativity. Within its ancient stone walls, cobbled streets lead to intimate galleries, artist studios, and music spaces that give the town its distinctive cultural rhythm. Especially in summer, concerts and workshops fill the air with energy, drawing visitors from across Europe.

Browse handmade crafts, regional artworks, and small shops offering olive oils and truffle delicacies. Pause at the Church of St. Vid or enjoy panoramic views over vineyards and olive groves that stretch toward the horizon. In Grožnjan, history and artistic spirit come together in a setting that feels both authentic and quietly inspiring.

### The Hilltop of Motovun

Perched high above the Mirna River valley, Motovun rises behind its medieval stone walls, overlooking vineyards and truffle-rich forests that define this part of Istria. Inside the old town, narrow cobbled streets lead past Romanesque and Gothic details, small galleries, and quiet squares where time seems to slow. From the ramparts, panoramic views unfold across rolling hills, especially striking at sunset when the landscape turns warm and golden.

Motovun is also a place of deep-rooted culinary tradition. The surrounding forests hide the region’s prized truffles, available in local taverns and paired with Istrian wines. Here, history, gastronomy, and sweeping scenery come together in a setting that feels both timeless and intimate.

### Beyond the Shoreline

Slip away from the mainland and into the quiet vastness of the Adriatic along the coast of Istria. A privately appointed yacht awaits, its course shaped entirely around your pace and preferences. Cruise past limestone cliffs and hidden inlets, anchor in translucent waters far from view, and linger where the horizon seems endless. The day unfolds without urgency, guided by sun, sea and subtle indulgence. Beyond the shoreline lies a sense of freedom reserved for those who seek privacy, space and the rare luxury of time entirely their own.

### Across the Brijuni Archipelago

Just off the western coast of Istria lies the serene archipelago of Brijuni National Park, where unspoiled nature and layered history exist in rare harmony. Explore the islands by private boat or guided tour, passing Roman ruins, lush parkland and quiet coves edged by crystalline water. Once a favored retreat of aristocracy and statesmen, Brijuni still carries an air of quiet distinction. Time seems to slow here, inviting you to move gently through landscapes shaped by centuries and preserved in remarkable stillness.

### Istria’s Hidden Forts

Step back in time and explore the ancient military fortresses scattered across Istria, where history whispers through stone walls and panoramic views stretch over rolling hills and the sparkling Adriatic. Wander through centuries-old bastions, imagine the lives of soldiers long gone, and uncover hidden corners that tell tales of strategic battles and coastal defense. Each fortress offers a unique window into Istria’s rich past, blending fascinating history with breathtaking scenery - a perfect experience for curious travelers eager to connect with the region’s heritage.
