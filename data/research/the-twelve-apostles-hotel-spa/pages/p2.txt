# Our hotel

The only property located on the spectacular coastal road that links Cape Town and Camps Bay with Llundudno Beach and Hout Bay beyond, The Twelve Apostles is the perfect place to watch the sun set into the deep blue Atlantic, dine on fine international cuisine or relax in the hotel’s multi award-winning spa.

1721

The first member of the van Breda family came to the Cape in 1721 and over the years the family acquired large tracks of land.

1836

Lord Charles Somerset, Governor of The Cape, gave Michael van Breda a piece of land at Oudekraal in 1836 on which to build his own hunting lodge.

1929

In 1929, Jack van Breda designed a handsome house, after the style of Sir Herbert Baker, with two big white frontal gables and a thatched roof. It took two years to build.

1954

Van Breda sold the farm on 28th January 1954 to Sir Henry Philip Price, Chairman of a well-known London tailoring establishment for the price of £60,000. Price issued instructions for the homestead, and more than one acre of ground (portion three) was separated from the remainder of the estate.

1955

In 1955 Emily Bolton purchased portion three of the farm, on which the house stood, from Sir Henry. Bolton, a rich English spinster, lived here with her lover, Mr Butcher. Bolton and Butcher, both regarded as recluses, led strange, solitary lives and the house fell into disarray.

1968

The Wiehahn family bought the surrounding Oudekraal Farm from Sir Henry’s estate on 28 May 1968.

1986

In 1986 Mel Miller, who operated an advertising agency called The White House, bought the house on auction for R405 000.

1992

In 1992 Miller sold the house to the developer Stephen Jones for R3.1 million, who decided, amidst great controversy, to build a R40 million hotel and conference centre. Stephen Jones sold the hotel to Business Bank, which was then, along with its assets, sold to PSG Bank.

2002

In March 2002 the hotel became part of the family-owned Red Carnation Hotels and was re-named The Twelve Apostles Hotel and Spa. Within just five years the property had been renovated. Around the hotel, alien plant species were removed and extensive indigenous gardens were cultivated.

2014

The Twelve Apostles won ‘South Africa’s Leading Spa Resort’ in the 2014 World Travel Awards, while Travel + Leisure named it one of the ‘Most Romantic Hotels in Cape Town’, and voted it amongst the ‘World’s Best 500 Hotels’.

# The power of passion

"Opened in 2002, The Twelve Apostles has undergone a number of incarnations, from a private home, to advertising agency, to hotel. My own journey with Red Carnation began in 2001 in London and in 2014 I was appointed as General Manager at The Twelve Apostles.

I am proud to have witnessed the growth and achievements of our team and I’m equally proud of the role the hotel plays in the local community. Our location in the environs of a national park demands diligent sustainability practices. We have impressive water-saving initiatives, a system for the management of waste and energy consumption, and have eliminated single-use plastics from our operations.

For as long as I’ve worked at The Twelve Apostles, it never ceases to delight me. Our hotel is close enough to Cape Town but far enough away from the ‘madding crowd’ to make your visit seem like a retreat. The V&A Waterfront, Camps Bay beaches, the historic Constantia Wine Route and Hout Bay are all within easy reach.

Our location is exceptional – wedged between mountain and sea – our design is incredible, and our guest experiences are so diverse. Experiences range from exhilarating helicopter flights and mountain walks, to High Tea by the Sea or a treatment in an outdoor mountain spa gazebo. Our views of the Atlantic Ocean are unequalled anywhere else in the city.

We look forward to welcoming you to our little slice of paradise very soon."

**\- Michael Nel, Managing Director**
