Best for:

Location:

The rooftop bar and terrace Pen Top for only-in-New-York views
In a nutshell
: Fresh off a substantial redesign nearly 40 years after its opening in 1988, the Peninsula New York remains a fine expression of the hotelier’s attentive style of hospitality and graceful design codes. Though not as breathtakingly palatial as its forebears in Hong Kong (the first-born) and Manila (the second), this third child is nonetheless manor-like, quietly confident, and a total oasis. There’s nothing quite like the ability to escape the touristy hubbub of Fifth Avenue and dip into your plush room at the Pen to make you feel like you’ve got it all in hand.
Matt Ortile
Matt Ortile
Rooms
I stayed at the Peninsula New York soon after the property unveiled its latest renovation in the fall of 2024. All 219 keys, from the base rooms to the top suites, have gotten a makeover courtesy of Manhattan’s
Bill Rooney Studio
. The guest rooms have this pleasant and airy quality, the one endemic to all Peninsula hotels; the brand is committed to giving its guests respite from the chaos of their central urban locations (Tsim Sha Tsui in Hong Kong, Makati in Manila, Wilshire and Santa Monica in Beverly Hills, etc.). Everything in my Grand Luxe Room was easy on the eyes: pale walls, contemporary art in soft blues, and a mix of dark and white marble surfaces. Above the bed was a bas-relief sculptural design element in white that looked rather like feathers on a bird’s wing. It was illuminated by elegant mood lighting from behind the headboard; inspired, I eventually went home to
Brooklyn
and stuck a strip of LED lights behind my own headboard. It’s been a hit with guests. Mine, I mean.
Food and drink
The one word to describe dining at the Peninsula New York is “intimate.” The seat counts at each of the four food and beverage venues are small—I’d guess no more than 40 at the largest—but this only contributes to the hotel’s exclusive, if-you-know-you-know vibe. Just off the lobby is the hotel’s classy
Gotham Lounge
, where full dinner is served; find at the grand piano masterful artists tickling the keys on Thursday, Friday, and Saturday nights.
Clement Restaurant
does something-for-everyone American fare for breakfast and lunch. The adjoining
Bar at Clement,
slinky and moody, has a specialty cocktail menu and an array of small bites. They also list what I like to call “New York Happy Meals:” a pairing of a cocktail (Johnnie Walker Blue Label, Cynar, and red vermouth) and a burger (wagyu beef, cheddar from New York state, fries); and Grey Goose martinis with bumps of Petrossian caviar. Business lunches are de rigeur on weekdays; on weekends, you’ll mostly find tourists here. Maybe we locals are missing out. The view from the 23rd-floor rooftop bar and terrace
Pen Top,
is, well, top.
Amenities and experiences
One thing particularly fun about Peninsula Hotels is the brand’s commitment to having an in-house fleet of custom cars at their properties, from the Rolls-Royce and Bentley rides at the Hong Kong mothership to the Manila location’s souped-up luxury Jeepney (an air-conditioned version of the popular and characterful Jeeps that Manileños use for public transportation). At the New York hotel, the cars are bespoke BMWs, bookable based on availability, and MINI Cooper S Clubmans, which chauffeurs use to take guests on three-hour tours of the city (complimentary for those who are staying in suites). After a day of exploring, relaxation is within easy reach at the three-story
Peninsula Spa & Wellness Center
. Treatments range from facials using
Biologique Recherche
products to “baby moon” spa journeys. The pool is large and surrounded by windows that let in plenty of natural light; the same is true of the fitness center, featuring Technogym machines and a team of personal trainers who can help perfect your form. All this to say, the Peninsula New York does what most five-star hotels set out to do: take care of you.
What to do nearby
The main selling point of the Peninsula New York, I think, is its location. It’s ideal for first-timers since it’s just steps away from the classic stops like
Central Park
, Rockefeller Center, and the luxury fashion flagships along Fifth Avenue (
Harry Winston
jewelers is right across the street). It’s also right off two convenient subway stations, though I imagine if you’re staying here, you’d prefer to have the valets out front call you a car.
Other recommendations for the area, all within walking distance: beloved Polo Bar is down the street, on 55th Street; the Museum of Modern Art is around the corner, on 53rd Street; and underrated St. Thomas Church, on the corner of Fifth Avenue and 53rd, in all its Gothic glory. All told, the beating heart of the city has moved downtown, but the Pen’s position is a good Midtown midpoint between obligations in the
Upper East Side
(business or otherwise—there’s the Met, of course) and attractions a touch further south, like Broadway.
The backstory
Opened in 1988 as the third flag of Hong Kong-founded hotel company
Peninsula Hotels
(after Hong Kong in 1928 and Manila in 1976), Peninsula New York sits in the space once occupied by the Gotham Hotel, founded in 1905. It was one of the earliest “skyscraper” hotels in the city, and originally meant to be a residential hotel for, as the
The
New York Times
once put it, “those New Yorkers who were tired of maintaining a city house.” Other past lives included stints as the Nova-Park Gotham Hotel and Hotel Maxim’s de Paris—understandable, given the inarguable Beaux-Arts opulence to the Landmark building, with its limestone carvings, copper cornices, and Doric columns.
I do have to say that, being a big fan of the signature and stately Peninsula lobbies in Hong Kong and Manila, with their practically cavernous size and tables occupied by well-heeled locals sipping afternoon tea, I expected something similar at the New York location. But here, the two staircases that lead from the marble foyer up to the check-in desks, restaurants, and bars have their own grandeur, something more restrained but still lavish. A twinkling crystal chandelier doesn’t strictly classify as ‘quiet luxury,’ for which I am glad.
Insider tip
The Sunday buffet at Clement is appointment brunching in the city, ne plus ultra (starting at $115 per person, practically a steal). The Saturday spread is great too, served a la carte. Both days boast live music, which my own stepfather loves and has come to expect at every Peninsula property he visits. At the Gotham Bar, ask the team for Conrad, cheerful and gracious, who can suggest a martini for your mood. And with the hotel’s “Peninsula Time” program, you can check-in as early as 6:00 a.m. on arrival day, and check-out is as late as 10:00 p.m. on the day of departure, which makes it perfect for out-of-towners getting in way early or having a red-eye out from JFK.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes, the hotel can accommodate families nicely; ask the reservations team for connecting rooms
Accessibility:
Yes, you can book accessible rooms via the
Peninsula New York site
; call the reservations team to determine which accessible room is best for you
Wellness:
Yes, the spa, pool, and fitness center at the Peninsula New York are some of the best in Manhattan; grab an elliptical by the window and watch the city below go by
Loyalty group:
None, Peninsula Hotels does not have a traditional loyalty program
