San Ysidro Ranch
Steeped in history and oozing with modern elegance, San Ysidro Ranch beckons guests with its stunning surroundings and year-round appeal.

Why book?
Don't worry about the weather or the season when booking a room here. Any time of year is a good time to visit San Ysidro Ranch. Rain or shine, this iconic property will envelop you with its elevated dining, stellar wine collection, and generous accommodations.
Set the scene
As charming as it is, don't plan on seeing much of the surrounding Montecito and Santa Barbara when you're staying at San Ysidro Ranch. From the moment you pull into the lavender-lined road leading to the hotel's guard gate, it's apparent you're someplace special and exclusive. Of course, the tony beach town of
Santa Barbara
—and its even tonier enclave of Montecito—is known as a prime summer spot, but a stay at San Ysidro Ranch is about so much more than going to the beach and exploring the local wineries and wine-tasting rooms that the region is also known for. The property's 550 acres have just 38 bungalow-style cottages, each with their own bespoke decor and unique layouts, as well as private outdoor sunken spas. Once you're here, you'll want to settle in. When not in your room, lounge by the pool, practice your short game on the property's own golf-putting course, wander the gardens—just relax. Should you wish to venture out, walk outside the gates and you're in a Nancy Myers backdrop of Spanish Revival–style estates—but you can see those anytime. A stay at the property is a splurge—soak up every minute of it.
The backstory
Ty Warner spared no expense when he purchased San Ysidro Ranch in 2000. The local billionaire also owns the nearby Four Seasons Biltmore, Santa Barbara (temporarily closed) and its adjacent Coral Casino private club, The Montecito Club, as well as Las Ventanas al Paraiso, A Rosewood Resort, in Los Cabos and the Four Seasons Hotel New York (temporarily closed)—all fine properties, but the independent nature of San Ysidro Ranch truly makes it shine. Artfully lit in-room libraries, hand-painted wallpaper in the bathrooms that even lines the ceilings of the showers, authentic antiques, and museum-quality art are just some of the elements he added to the property, which dates back to 1892. Over the years, the property has hosted everyone from Winston Churchill to Audrey Hepburn, as well as Jacqueline and John F. Kennedy for their honeymoon. Today, it's known as the ultimate Southern California hideaway for food and wine connoisseurs, as well as those who enjoy luxuriating in a quiet setting.
The rooms
The property's dated website hardly does it justice. Ignore how it might make the cottages feel underwhelming. In person, each is jaw-droppingly stunning, complete with four-poster beds, fireplaces, outdoor showers, and private backyard gardens, some with hot tubs. Upon check in, you'll be guided to your cottage, where you can park directly in front. Each is named after a variety of flora (Lotus, Fig, Honeysuckle, etc) and is also marked with each guest's last name on a plaque with wooden letters. No two are alike, and there's no "bad" option. In fact, guests typically grow so attached to certain rooms and book them specifically upon return that the property avoids presuming guests want to be upgraded. Rooms start with the modest Daisy cottage, featuring 775 square feet of space and extend to the 1,450-square-foot Acacia with its separate living and sleeping spaces and outdoor sunken hot tub. Rooms like the 1,550-square-foot Laurel are set along the San Ysidro Creek, which is lovely for those who like to take in the sounds of flowing water. For those who really want to splurge, there's the two-bedroom, 6,000-square-foot Warner cottage, complete with its own 33-foot-long pool and outdoor barbeque (a cottage chef is available upon request). Upon check in, ask a hotel employee to point out the unique antiques, textiles, and furnishings in your cottage, as there's typically a story about each one.
Food and drink
In recent years, Warner has focused on elevating the property's dining experience, adding a food and beverage director who previously oversaw Michelin-starred restaurants at the nearby Rosewood Miramar Beach and Napa Valley’s Auberge du Soleil, and a wine director who hails from SingleThread Farms. At the signature restaurant, The Stonehouse, menus aren’t edgy, but they do feature some of the finest seasonal ingredients, including many from the Ranch’s own organic garden. Ask for a table at the end of the terrace for sunset views over the water. When it comes to imbibing, prepare yourself for one of the most comprehensive wine lists in the country, which includes everything from reasonably priced local producers up to a recently acquired collection of Château d'Yquem, which includes 138 vintages from 1811 to 2008. There’s also a 14,000-bottle wine cellar with a 70-vintage vertical collection of Château Petrus dating back to 1945. Wine not your thing? How about a taste from the only bottle of Glenturret 50-year-old scotch in the US or one of their other ultra-rare spirits like the Balvenie Chapter 4 Collection or the Macallan 1824 Series. And, as of this year, Warner has produced his own rare spirit, with 111 bottles of The Ty Warner Reserve, a special-edition blanco tequila made with Código 1530.
The spa
With its acres of dreamy gardens, the property is always a popular destination for weddings, which are limited in number so as not to disturb hotel guests, and the spa is designed to accommodate bridal parties extremely well with its full separate salon. Regular guests will also enjoy the range of therapies using oils made from the property's own lavender, rosemary, and lemons, as well as Yon-Ka and oxygen facials, and rejuvenating massages.
The neighborhood/area
Even the most experienced luxury travelers will likely melt at the surrounds. Once off the freeway, the six-minute drive up San Ysidro Road will pass through some of the most stunning homes in all of California, as well as the quaint shopping areas of Coast Village Road and East Valley Road. Consider skipping the gym and instead go for a walk through the neighborhood and marvel at the well-maintained historic estates, many of which date back to the early 1900s. Locally owned boutiques abound, but for something truly one-of-a-kind, pop into William Laman and explore the revered antiques dealer's hand-picked treasures or to Wendy Foster Upstairs for curated home goods from across the globe.
The service
If you're not on the list (meaning a registered hotel guest or have a reservation at the restaurant), you're likely not going to get through the gates. But, once you're in, there's no pretense. The staff is warm, attentive, and will go out of their way to make you feel at home. The intimate nature of the resort allows the staff to get to know each guest so that their stay can be totally customized for whatever their interests are, whether it's exploring the wine cellar on a private tour, hiking through the adjacent trails, or getting access to the area's golf clubs.
For families
The resort allows children, but it's not the kind of place where other guests will appreciate screaming little ones running through its ample lawns. There's no kids club, no dedicated high-tech video game room... if your kid is the one screeching and splashing in the pool, expect to feel the death glares emanating from behind the sunglasses of the other guests. You might have a better time if you leave the kids at home. Dogs, however...should a four-legged child be a part of your family, the staff will not only greet them at check in with hand-made Fois gras dog treats, but your fur baby will have its own bed, food bowl, TY dog toy, and giant biscuit with its name spelled out in frosting in your room. There's even a dedicated in-room and poolside dog menu, so Fido never feels left out.
Eco effort
There’s such a long list of eco-minded efforts at the property, many of which aren’t immediately obvious to hotel guests. Among them: the property participates in Hotels for Trees, which plants a new tree each time a guests opts to skip the daily room cleaning; there are electric car charging stations next to each of the 38 bungalows, and four at the restaurant; all used feather duvets and pillows are sent to animal shelters and repurposed for dog bedding; an advanced Rain Bird watering system operates based on evapotranspiration.
Accessibility
Although the hillside property is sloped, there aren't many stairs to worry about, and access ramps exist where needed.
Anything left to mention?
Checking the weather for Santa Barbara ahead of time can be misleading, as San Ysidro Ranch seems to live in its own microclimate. Just a few minutes away, directly on the coast, it might be chilly and overcast, but the resort—located in the hills at a slight elevation, which often means it's above the fog—could be a perfect 74 degrees and sunny. If you're not sure what to pack or plan ahead for based on weather, call the property for a more accurate weather report.
