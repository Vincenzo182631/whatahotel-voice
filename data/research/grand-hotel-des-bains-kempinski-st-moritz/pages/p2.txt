# Welcome to St. Moritz

The spectacular mountain setting with urban glamour in the peaceful surroundings of the Swiss Alps

### Welcome to St. Moritz

Welcome to St. Moritz, the glimmering jewel in Switzerland's crown, where luxury, nature and cultural richness coexist harmoniously. This mesmerising Alpine destination beckons with its invigorating blend of world-class winter sports, high-end fashion, delectable gastronomy, vibrant art scene and breathtaking landscapes.

#### Breathtaking Natural Beauty

Discover the radiant charm of St. Moritz, enveloped by the majestic Alpine peaks and serenaded by serene lakes. Here, the sun shines for an astounding 322 days a year, offering enchanting vistas and an exceptional climate. Whether it's the snowy panorama during winter or the lush green landscapes of summer, the ethereal beauty of St. Moritz is a year-round spectacle.

#### Discover the Most Beautiful Natural Attractions Around St. Moritz

##### **1\. Lake St. Moritz**

A jewel of the Engadin Valley, Lake St. Moritz is a must-see for any traveller. Its shimmering waters provide a perfect backdrop for a leisurely stroll or a more adventurous sailing excursion. During the winter, the frozen lake becomes a playground for polo and horse racing, creating a unique spectacle against the snow-covered mountains.

##### **2\. Piz Nair**

For those seeking panoramic views of the Alps, a trip to Piz Nair is essential. Accessible by cable car, this mountain peak offers breathtaking vistas that stretch as far as the eye can see. Whether you're skiing down its slopes or enjoying a meal at the summit restaurant, the views from Piz Nair are unforgettable.

##### **3\. Morteratsch Glacier**

A short drive from St. Moritz lies the Morteratsch Glacier, one of the most accessible glaciers in the Alps. A well-marked trail leads you through a stunning valley, offering views of the glacier's icy expanse. This natural wonder is a testament to the raw, untamed beauty of the region and is a poignant reminder of our planet's changing climate.

##### **4\. Val Roseg**

For a tranquil escape, head to Val Roseg, a picturesque valley that offers serene hiking trails and abundant wildlife. The lush greenery and the gentle sound of the river make it an ideal spot for picnicking or simply soaking in the natural surroundings. In winter, the valley transforms into a snowy wonderland, accessible by horse-drawn sleighs.

##### **5\. Engadin's National Park**

A short distance from St. Moritz, Engadin's National Park is Switzerland's oldest national park and a haven for biodiversity. With over 80 kilometres of hiking trails, visitors can explore diverse landscapes ranging from alpine meadows to dense forests, all while spotting native wildlife like ibexes and marmots.

#### World-Class Winter Sports

As the birthplace of winter tourism, St. Moritz is renowned for its premier skiing and snowboarding experiences. Home to the iconic Cresta Run and an array of winter sporting events, St. Moritz promises exhilarating thrills for both novices and seasoned athletes.

#### Prime Location

Centrally poised in the heart of Upper Engadin within the Swiss canton of Graubünden, St. Moritz is a unique mountain escape perched at an altitude of 1,800 metres above sea level. This luxury destination is conveniently accessible by road, rail and even private jet via the Engadine Airport in Samedan, making it a perfect choice for 'luxury travel to the Swiss Alps'.

#### Alpine Climate

St. Moritz boasts a remarkable Alpine climate characterised by over 300 days of sunshine and mild, dry weather, a feature that has made it a popular 'year-round Swiss resort'. Day and night, as well as seasonal temperature variations, are part of the charm, along with the refreshing Maloja wind, a boon for windsurfers and sailors alike.

#### Currency

The local currency, the Swiss Franc (CHF), is referred to as 'Stutz' by locals, adding to the region's character. The minor unit of this currency is the 'Rappen'.

#### Multilingual Heritage

Reflecting its cultural diversity, Graubünden's official languages include German, Italian and Romansh, the traditional Engadine language. The latter is still taught in schools and widely spoken by locals, adding to the region's rich linguistic heritage.

#### Dress Code

Whether you're planning to explore the 'winter wonderland of St. Moritz' or enjoy the 'summer Alpine adventures', remember that the Alpine climate influences the dress code. Winter requires warm jackets, gloves and hats, while summer calls for light jackets and sunglasses. Evening events often follow the 'dress to impress' motto, though formal attire isn't always mandatory.
