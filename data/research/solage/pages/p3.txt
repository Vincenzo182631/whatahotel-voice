Reviewed by
Laura Ratliff
Why book?
This design-forward Napa resort trades wine-country formality for private cottage living, a lively pool scene, and one of Calistoga’s best wellness setups.
Set the scene
At Solage, you’re pretty firmly in Calistoga territory—this is less polished than the center of Napa Valley, but far more relaxed. The property unfolds like a series of modern farmhouse neighborhoods: low-slung, whitewashed buildings, gravel paths, and landscaping that feels mature and slightly wild rather than overly manicured.
The crowd here is affluent but still casual; you can expect well-dressed couples on long weekends, small groups doing a wine-and-spa rotation, and a handful of design-minded travelers who care as much about the look of the place as the wine list. By mid-afternoon, most guests have migrated to the pool, cocktails in hand, and the energy shifts from quiet retreat to something more social.
The backstory
Part of the Auberge Resorts Collection, Solage has been a fixture in Napa for almost 20 years, offering a more contemporary alternative to the area’s classic vineyard estates. Where others lean traditional, Solage built its identity around wellness, open space, and a more relaxed interpretation of luxury.
A large-scale renovation around five years ago updated guest rooms, expanded dining, and refreshed the pool and public areas, making it one of Napa’s more modern, design-conscious resorts.
The rooms
Everything here is built around standalone cottages, which immediately sets it apart. Even entry-level studios feel incredibly private, each with its own patio. Suites, especially the Orchard and Estate categories, read more like full residences.
Design is minimal, with neutral palettes, clean lines, and high-quality materials. It may come across as less visually striking than some of the newer, sexier competitors, but that’s not necessarily a bad thing. Solage is undoubtedly comfortable and functional in a way that works for longer stays.
The real standout is the indoor-outdoor flow. Many rooms include outdoor showers, fire pits, and generous garden space, making it easy to spend as much time outside as in. Details like fireplaces and oversized bathrooms add to the sense of space, even if some finishes (particularly in showers) feel more standard than you might expect at this price point.
Worth noting: Minibars are fully stocked but entirely chargeable, which can feel slightly out of step.
Food and drink
Solbar is the anchor of the dining scene here, and true to Napa, it’s seasonal, locally driven, and consistently strong, especially for dinner. Dishes like tuna tartare and risotto tend to outperform expectations, even if the atmosphere can feel a little flat on some nights.
Picobar is the resort’s newest addition and is undoubtedly where things get more fun. Set poolside, it leans into modern Mexican flavors with a Napa twist—tostadas, tacos, and mezcal-heavy cocktails are far more compelling than typical resort fare.
Savvy guests—and diners—will treat the hotel like a progression; enjoy casual lunches and cocktails at Picobar, then something more structured at Solbar.
The spa
You’re in Calistoga after all, so wellness is a core part of the experience. Spa Solage delivers. The Bathhouse, fed by Calistoga’s geothermal springs, is the highlight, with multiple hydrotherapy pools, a steam room, sauna, and cold plunge.
It’s one of Napa’s more extensive setups, and importantly, it’s actually usable rather than just decorative. Guests can move through different pools and treatment circuits, making it easy to spend hours here without it feeling repetitive.
The neighborhood/area
Calistoga is Napa’s quieter end. It’s walkable, slightly scruffier, and less scene-driven than Yountville or downtown Napa, but that’s absolutely part of the appeal. You’re close to smaller wineries and local institutions, and the town itself is easy to dip in and out of without committing to a full itinerary.
The service
Service is generally strong but not flawless. (It’s a regional thing.) Staff are friendly and attentive around the pool and across the property, but there can be occasional lapses—particularly at arrival, where check-in delays have been known to happen. Once settled, though, the experience smooths out, and the team does a good job maintaining a relaxed but polished tone.
Accessibility
The spread-out, mostly single-story design makes navigation relatively straightforward, and accessible accommodations are available upon request.
Anything left to mention?
The pool is the heart of the hotel—and it defines the experience. It’s lively, well-serviced, and beautifully set, but not particularly quiet. If you’re looking for total seclusion, you’ll want to lean on your private outdoor space or the spa.
