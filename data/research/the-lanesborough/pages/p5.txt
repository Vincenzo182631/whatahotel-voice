Why book?
An 18th-century aristocratic hunting lodge reborn as a
Regency-era
hotel, The Lanesborough stands apart with its five-star service and royal pedigree, including a private entrance reserved for the British Royal Family.
Set the scene
Commanding a stately perch between Hyde Park and
Buckingham Palace
, The Lanesborough announces its regal ambitions from the moment of arrival, when bowler-hatted bellmen whisk away luggage with impressive speed and ceremony. If the VIP treatment goes to your head, a look around the hotel’s gilded sitting rooms and glittering dining spaces quickly restores perspective: fellow guests often include Hollywood celebrities and Saudi royals. This is London in full high-society mode, so you’ll want to pack accordingly.
The backstory
Long before luxury boutiques and embassies claimed Knightsbridge, the area was a pastoral patchwork of open fields and marshy lowlands. It was here that Anglo-Irish nobleman James Lane built his hunting-lodge-cum-country mansion, where he resided until his death in 1779. The grand property later took on a radically different role as the state-of-the-art St George's Hospital, rebuilt in 1825 by architect William Wilkins, the mind behind London’s National Gallery. Its final transformation came in 1991, when the grand Regency-era structure was reborn as a luxury hotel, followed by a lavish 2014 restoration involving more than 300 plasterers, embroiderers, and crystal specialists, among other artisans. Fun fact: more than 42,000 gold leaf sheets were used for the gilding on the ceilings alone. Since 2015, the hotel has been a jewel of the Oetker Collection, whose portfolio includes icons such as Le Bristol Paris and the Hotel du Cap-Eden-Roc in Antibes.
The rooms
The hotel’s 93 rooms and suites channel Regency splendor, dressed in period color palettes with canopy beds, ornate plasterwork, sparkling chandeliers, and playful flourishes of Chinoiserie (think lacquered lamps and polished mahogany furniture). The maximalist fantasy is the vision of the late great interior designer Alberto Pinto, who worked with British designers to create furnishings and materials that were 95 percent bespoke for the hotel. Yet for all the old-world drama, the rooms are built for the needs of the modern traveler. The Lanesborough remains the only hotel in London to offer 24-hour butler service in every room, summonable at the tap of an iPad that also controls lighting, curtains, and more. Even the most “entry-level” accommodations feel over-the-top, with Hyde Park views and marble bathrooms outfitted with heated floors and high-tech Japanese toilets. For the full royal treatment, book the seven-bedroom Royal Suite overlooking the Buckingham Palace Gardens or the 3,907-square-foot Presidential Suite, styled like a 19th-century mansion with marble columns, elaborate bas-reliefs, and space for up to 14 guests.
Food and drink
The flagship restaurant, The Lanesborough Grill, ranks among the most beautiful dining rooms I’ve ever seen, set beneath a soaring glass dome and anchored by crystal chandeliers, stately columns, and tasseled baby-blue banquettes. It’s a fittingly elegant stage for Michelin-acclaimed chef Shay Cooper’s quintessentially British cooking (think coronation crab salad, beef Wellington, and Dover sole) as well as the hotel’s daily “Bridgerton”-inspired afternoon tea. One of the most extravagant tea services in the city, it’s a parade of delicate finger sandwiches, house-baked scones, and signature sweets presented on silver stands, accompanied by a vast selection of classic and custom-blend teas. For a different kind of afternoon indulgence, the atmospheric Library Bar is among London’s most highly regarded cocktail dens, celebrated for its trove of fine whiskies and cognacs, some dating back to 1770. French-born bar manager Mickael Perron presides over the room, shaking cocktails like the Lilibet, a blend of Earl Grey–infused gin, bergamot, white cacao liqueur, and English Chardonnay.
The spa
Tucked away from the hotel’s main areas is the luxurious Lanesborough Club & Spa, an urban oasis widely recognized as one of the city's most exclusive wellness addresses. Guests and a small community of local members enter a subterranean sanctuary of saunas, steam rooms, rain showers, a hydrotherapy pool, and serene treatment suites offering everything from exosome facials and pelvic-floor toning therapy to detox IV drips. The spa made history as the first in the UK to carry Tata Harper products and also features the Royal Fern skincare line, the plant-based Ground Wellbeing range, the Cotswolds-born ila brand, and non-toxic nail care by L’Atelier Green Paris. Complementing the treatment menu is a sleek, state-of-the-art fitness center, where daily small-group classes span HIIT, boxing, TRX conditioning, and reformer Pilates. Rounding out the experience is the spa’s own restaurant, serving clean, all-day dishes perfect for refueling on the go.
The neighborhood/area
It’s hard to beat The Lanesborough’s location, rising grandly above Hyde Park Corner and placing guests within easy reach of Buckingham Palace, Harrods, and cultural heavyweights like the Serpentine Galleries, the Victoria and Albert Museum, and the Natural History Museum. This being one of London’s most affluent pockets, the vibe is upscale and residential yet still conveniently located to enjoy the best of Central London.
The service
Service here is unmistakably white-glove, and the world-class concierge is no exception. Whether you’re angling for a private meet-and-greet with the cast of a West End production or hoping to tour the city’s buzziest exhibition with a curator as your guide, the team is ready to make it happen. For everything else, the hotel’s 24-hour butler service steps in, handling tasks like unpacking and pressing your wardrobe, arranging fresh flowers, or stocking up your room’s fully tricked-out minibar with bespoke add-ons.
For families
Young guests receive the same white-glove treatment through The Lanesborough’s Little VIP Club, a thoughtfully curated program of family-friendly experiences that includes mocktail-making classes, horseback riding and carriage rides through Hyde Park, in-room treasure hunts, and even a Little Butler Bootcamp, where children learn how to brew the perfect cup of tea or set a table to royal standards. The hotel has also partnered with local events planner Notorious Kids to transform guest rooms into whimsical play spaces, complete with teddy-bear picnics, balloon-filled teepees, and other made-to-order fantasies.
Eco effort
The hotel supports a range of sustainability initiatives, from electric and hybrid house cars and locally sourced British produce to LED lighting, zero single-use plastic toiletries, and a charity partnership with Heartburn Cancer UK.
Accessibility
A selection of ground-floor, wheelchair-accessible rooms are equipped with emergency alarm systems and adapted bathrooms featuring raised toilets, grab rails, adjustable showerheads, and roll-in shower cubicles. A public accessible restroom is also available on the ground floor.
Anything left to mention?
Keep an eye out for the hotel’s adorable (and hypoallergenic!) resident Siberian Forest cat, Lilibet — named after Queen Elizabeth II — who can often be spotted padding through the shadowy corners of The Library Bar or into the adjoining Withdrawing Room.
