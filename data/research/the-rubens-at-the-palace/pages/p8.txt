# Local attractions

The Rubens' prime location in London Victoria, opposite the Royal Mews of Buckingham Palace, lends itself to theatregoers, park strollers, and shoppers alike. With Westminster just round the corner, those looking for cultural sites will find the Houses of Parliament, Big Ben, the London Eye, and Buckingham Palace a short stroll away. Hyde Park, Green Park, and St James’s Park provide the area with various leafy retreats. Victoria’s Cardinal Place plays host to high street brands, bars, restaurants, a cinema, and a rooftop garden.

## Places to see

### Eccleston Yards

A courtyard comprising of shops, restaurants, and wellness hubs brimming with delightful discoveries and independent spirit. Their Sunday market lends itself perfectly to leisurely explorations.

### Buckingham Palace

When we think about unmissable London landmarks, few, if any, are more unmissable than Buckingham Palace. With the core of today’s site built as far back as 1703, the Palace has since grown to be a point of convergence for the national rejoicing or mourning of the British people.

Discover more

### Westminster Cathedral

A short stroll from The Rubens, Westminster Cathedral is known for being the largest Catholic church in the UK. Take some time to soak in the majesty of one of the UK’s few neo-Byzantine masterpieces.

## Green Spaces

### St James' Park

Stretching the length of the park is a lake populated by pelicans that have been a fixture since a Russian ambassador gifted a pair to Charles II in 1660. A park highlight is the Blue Bridge that crosses the lake, from which you can enjoy glorious views of neighbouring Buckingham Palace.

Discover more

### Hyde Park

This is the largest of the four Royal Parks that populate London. Established as a hunting ground by King Henry VIII in 1536, the park is known for its rich political history. In recent times, it has served as a venue for large-scale concerts in the summer and, in the festive season, Hyde Park Winter Wonderland.

Discover more

### Battersea Park

Just a short trip across to the other side of the Thames lies an inner-city oasis. Cricket pitches, cycling routes, an adventure playground and a children’s zoo make a visit fun for the whole family. Blessed with a lake, boating is a fun option during the summer months and a number of cafes offer welcome refreshments.

Discover more

## The Royal Mews

The Royal Mews provides a unique and intimate glimpse into the royal stables and carriages. Visitors can explore the historic vehicles, from the dazzling Gold State Coach to the sleek Diamond Jubilee State Coach, and witness the care and craftsmanship that goes into maintaining the royal transport, offering an enchanting experience beyond the gates of Buckingham Palace.

Discover more

## Blue plaques

As you traverse Victoria's streets, keep your eyes peeled for the little blue plaques that identify the notable people that made this area their home.

**Mary Shelley (1797-1851):** The novelist who wrote the Gothic classic, Frankenstein. She also edited the works of her husband, Romantic poet Percy Shelley. You can find Mary Shelley's plaque at 24 Chester Square.

**Matthew Arnold (1822-1888)** – A poet and cultural critic who also worked as a school inspector, his lyric, Dover Beach, is now looked on as a key exemplar of Victorian poetry. You can find Matthew Arnold's plaque at 2 Chester Square.

**Joseph Conrad (1857-1924)** – A Polish-British writer who used his novels to examine imperialism, colonialism, and the human psyche more broadly. You can find Joseph Conrad's plaque at 17 Gillingham Street.

**Ian Fleming (1908-1964)** – The creator of the iconic James Bond spy novels is perhaps less known for having also written Chitty Chitty Bang Bang. You can find Ian Fleming's plaque at 22 Ebury Street.
