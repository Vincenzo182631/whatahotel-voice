Hotel Grande Bretagne, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Athens
An Athenian classic, whose resplendent interiors have presided over the city’s star-studded hospitality scene for 150 years.

Why book?
For a prime location, old-school glam, and a room with a view of the Acropolis, look no further.
Set the scene
Presiding over central Syntagma Square, the Grande Bretagne is a dependable constant in a city that has evolved and expanded over more centuries than most. While the hectic energy and roaring traffic of downtown
Athens
can be overwhelming, inside these wallpapered walls and glittering halls everything runs smoothly, and nothing is too much trouble.
The backstory
As intrinsically linked to the city’s identity, history, and high society as The Savoy in London or The Plaza in New York, the GB has hosted everyone from Maria Callas to Jodie Foster and Jean-Paul Gaultier since opening in 1874. Founder Efstathios Lampsas, a cook at the royal court who trained in Paris, had the chutzpah to introduce a truly grand hotel to Athens at a time when tourism infrastructure was almost non-existent. Originally, the hotel had 35 rooms and two bathrooms with no running water. (A bath cost more than an overnight stay). The first hotel with electricity in Athens, and the first fully air-conditioned hotel in Europe, during World War II the Grande Bretagne went from hosting parties and balls to serving as the HQ for the royal family, the government, and Allied Forces. The hotel was demolished in 1957 to make way for bigger, grander premises on the same site, opposite what was then the royal palace and is now the Greek Parliament. Now part of Marriot’s The Luxury Collection, the hotel retains its historic cachet.
The rooms
Supremely comfy, with no gimmicky surprises — just proper, old-school amenities, the plumpest beds and (16 types of) pillows, and gleaming bathrooms decked in mottled marble. Décor is a classic cocktail of swagged drapes, richly patterned carpets, inlaid cabinets, and lacquered lamps. Even the technology is reassuringly old-fashioned, with no confusing switches or fiddly shower settings. The most coveted rooms have Parthenon views (which come at a high premium), but the quieter rooms overlooking the courtyard gardens have a cozy charm of their own.
Food and drink
You could order breakfast in your room, but then you’d miss the Parthenon views served alongside your feta and tomato scrambled eggs or spanakopita. The bustling breakfast scene at GB Roof Garden transforms into a rather more formal affair for sunset Negronis and business or birthday dinners (with a smart dress code strictly imposed). Executive chef Asterios Koustoudis has overseen the elevated Mediterranean menu here for years. Lately, he has introduced more local ingredients to refined comfort food such as risotto with zucchini and creamy
tsalafouti
cheese or grilled turbot with scallops wrapped in seasonal greens and an emulsion of red peppers from Florina. Afternoon tea in the lobby-level winter garden has a fairy tale ambience, and there’s poolside souvlaki on the seventh floor or wood-fired pizzas served beneath the olive trees in the atrium for a more casual lunch. But the best seats in the house are the weathered leather stools at Alexander’s Bar, a local institution where the bowtied bartenders mix a pitch-perfect Martini.
The spa
A favorite with local ladies of a certain class, the spa is much about keeping up appearances (with its hairdresser and nail salon) as unwinding in sedate style. Scented steam rooms clad in amethyst tiles are a welcome prelude to transformational treatments by some of the best therapists in Athens (ask for Mara). Rubs and scrubs are powerfully restorative and facials using ESPA and 111 Skin products have instant effects.
The neighborhood/area
It doesn’t get more central or metropolitan than this. Bang on Syntagma (Constitution) Square, overlooking the Hellenic Parliament and balmy National Garden, it’s just blocks away from the designer shops along Voukourestiou Street, the Museum Mile of Vassilis Sofias Avenue, and the warren of rooftop bars, ethnic eats, and independent boutiques in the scrappier Historic Triangle.
The service
Gracious, prompt, and polished. Plentiful staff are ever-present and always obliging, without being obsequious. This is the kind of hotel where staff remember your name and stick around for years, welcoming repeat guests with their favorite drink or regular breakfast order.
For families
Any budding Eloise will feel right at home here. Despite the opulent surroundings, kids are warmly welcomed everywhere, even in the indoor spa pool. The outdoor pool is the perfect sweetener after a morning of schlepping around the ruins, and the pool deck is transformed into an open-air movie theater during the summer. While there aren’t any special facilities for families beyond the obvious (kids’ menus, highchairs, baby cots and rollaway beds, babysitting on demand), little treats for kids abound. Suites are the best bet for families, as classic rooms are pretty snug.
Eco effort
Green Key certified. Energy-efficient heating and lighting systems, reduced use of plastic and paper, and local procurement wherever possible form the backbone of the hotel’s sustainability policy.
Accessibility
There are a handful of fully accessible rooms. The main entrance and large elevators are designed to accommodate wheelchairs.
Anything left to mention?
The concierge desk is exceptionally on the ball, but suites come with their own butler, on call around the clock for anything from unpacking your holiday outfits to booking behind-the-scenes tours and bespoke dinners.
