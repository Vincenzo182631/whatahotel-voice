First In: La Bahia Hotel & Spa
Santa Cruz finally gets a hotel with a luxe take on its enduring surf-centric culture.

Why book?
This is the hotel Santa Cruz has been longing for. Cosmopolitan with a sprinkle of upscale coastal bliss, La Bahia Hotel & Spa offers a new perspective on a weekend getaway to a beach town long known (and loved) for its bohemian vibes.
Set the scene
The
Spanish
Mediterranean façade and meticulously laid brick herringbone driveway feels just right for its iconic beachfront location; it also keeps what’s waiting inside a delightful surprise. When you step in the lobby, it feels a bit more like a home—okay, palatial estate—than a hotel. La Bahia’s muse and inspiration is a well-traveled lady affectionately referred to as Aunt Elva. As you explore, you’ll glimpse all the artwork, trinkets, and tchotchkes she collected from around the world and brought back to her mansion in Santa Cruz. There’s everything from Italian marble on the bar top to dangling Moroccan pendant lamps and fragile Asian vases.
Calvin and Hobbes
fans (who isn’t?) will want to peruse the collection of coffee table books. At first glance, the scene is strangely eclectic, but the more you dig in, the more cohesive and comforting it feels.
With black and white striped chaise loungers, sunny yellow and white umbrellas, and bright pink towels, the pool area gives off legendary European
beach club
vibes. Servers deliver palomas and Aperol Spritzes bejeweled with La Bahia branded cocktail umbrellas. Primo views of the surf, cabanas, fire pits, and an infinity hot tub, provide even more reasons to linger longer.
I spent two nights at La Bahia Hotel & Spa less than a week after the property welcomed its first overnight guests. While La Bahia has the potential to put Santa Cruz on the map for the discerning traveler, during my visit, it was clear there were lots of locals checking the property out, sipping cocktails in the lobby, and dining on the pair of patios overlooking the ocean.
The backstory
The hotel building may be new, but its roots date back nearly a century to when architect and UC Berkeley professor William C. Hays designed a community of oceanfront apartments and a hotel. Thanks to its Spanish Mediterranean design, striking bell tower, and enviable beachfront location steps from the Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk, it became a city landmark. In the early 1940s, the United States Navy used the hotel and apartments as a convalescent hospital for wounded troops. After the war, the property was sold a number of times throughout the decades and lost its luster. A developer from
Chicago
pursued the construction of a new hotel at the site, but backed out before securing all of the necessary approvals. That’s when Ensemble Hospitality, owner of nearby Dream Inn, stepped in and brought the plan for Santa Cruz’s first luxury hotel to fruition.
The rooms
The 155 guest rooms and suites bubble with personality and a cheery elegance. Just like the lobby and other public spaces, they feature a refreshing mash-up of color and design elements. There are more than two dozen room types to choose from, but the majority feature an ocean view. I stayed in the spacious, 757-square-foot Cowell Suite with a trio of outdoor balconies, the largest of which looked directly at the sparkling Pacific and was outfitted with a gas fire pit, cushy furnishings, and a dining area. There are smile-inducing touches around every corner, but the headboard portraying Santa Cruz in a dreamscape (floating ship included) is a signature piece in all accommodations. The vibrant floral wallpaper and bone inlay mirror combination in the half bath is a showstopper. (In a time where all too many hotel bathrooms are beige and boring, it’s a splashy scheme that might inspire you to take a risk when you redecorate at home.) A few other pearls found in all accommodations: hand-carved bathroom vanities, wood beaded tassels that serve as do-not-disturb signs, and bedroom slippers that you’ll actually want to take home. (Staff hope you take them too.)
Food and drink
From locally caught fish to strawberries and artichokes plucked from nearby fields, the property’s signature restaurant High Tide and casual counterpart, Low Tide Bar & Grill, both take full advantage of the bounty of their backyard. While the latter serves an all-day menu that focuses on artisan pizzas and sharables such as surf tacos (using locally made tortillas), the dinner menu at High Tide skews Pacific Rim. Creative standouts for me included the vegetarian spring rolls with daikon, carrots, and a pineapple black bean sauce, miso-glazed butterfish atop forbidden rice, and an agnolotti with chili oil and cotija. I ordered from an a la carte menu, but a prix fixe offering is said to be in the works. The lobby bar, Pearl serves a fun lineup of craft cocktails. Ask for the Soda & Popcorn, a glamorous twist on a rum and Coke with brandy, rum, Tempus Fugit Crème de Banane, lime, and Mexican Coca Cola topped with caramel popcorn. Plunge bar and eatery sustains guests who can’t bear the thought of leaving the pool. Although the presentation longs for a glow-up, offerings such as a Niman Ranch Kobe burger, garlic parmesan-topped French fries, and catch of the day ceviche fit the bill.
The spa
Is one of a kind. With four indoor and four outdoor treatment rooms, The Spa at La Bahia takes up a good chunk of the sixth floor. Its rooftop Zen Deck dominates primo rooftop real estate, delivering arguably the most mesmerizing ocean views on the property. Decompress while lounging in cushy daybeds, swaying in hanging chairs, or basking in a sauna overlooking the sea. All treatments, from body scrubs and facials to massages, are inspired by the Monterey Bay ecosystem and marine environment. I recommend the La Paz de La Bahia; it’s a full-body massage, herbal foot treatment, and facial all rolled into one 80-minute stretch of total relaxation. Multi-instrumental sound baths with sound bowls, harp, harmonium, and more are also an option.
The neighborhood/area
The spirit of summer lives year-round in this quirky beach town, said to be the birthplace of mainland surfing. La Bahia Hotel & Spa faces Main Beach, with the Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk⎯the oldest amusement park in California⎯to the left and the Santa Cruz Wharf to the right. It’s a 1.5-mile stroll (overlooking the ocean nearly the entire stretch) to the Mark Abbott Memorial Lighthouse, the lighthouse illustrated on the headboards behind every bed at La Bahia. The beloved landmark overlooks surfing hotspot Steamer Lane and is home to the Santa Cruz Surfing Museum. For those who prefer to roll along the coast, the hotel has two Lincoln vehicles (a Lincoln Navigator and hybrid Lincoln Nautilus) that guests can take out for test drives and to explore the area.
Set on the northern shores of Monterey Bay, Santa Cruz is about 80 miles south of
San Francisco
. If you’re flying into Northern California, San Jose Mineta International Airport (SJC) is approximately a 30-mile drive and the closest major airport. It’s about double the distance to reach San Francisco International Airport (SFO), but you might find more flight availability.
The service
Attentive but not at all stuffy or formal. Staff are all smiles, happy to be welcoming guests and willing to go the extra mile to make your stay a memorable one. Beach butlers stake out sought-after spots in the sand for guests, setting up retro-inspired reclining chairs, umbrellas, cooler bags, and more. From the microfiber cloth left by housekeeping alongside my sunglasses, to the easy-release key ring the valet attendant added to my car key fob to the monogrammed pillows on my bed (A playful touch for guests staying in signature suites.), kudos to the entire hotel team for paying attention to seemingly little but meaningful details.
For families
Families are welcome. At check-in, young guests are invited to dive into a treasure chest teeming with plush and colorful sea life stuffed animals. Kids’ menus are available throughout the property. That said, I think tweens and teens will likely have more fun reveling in the social media-friendly décor. Selfishly, I would leave little ones at home and return with my husband or a group of girlfriends.
Eco effort
While the hotel is a new build, the soon-to-be century-old bell tower was restored. (The bell rings from time to time to celebrate milestones such as the hotel’s ribbon cutting.) Clay roof tiles from the original hotel and apartments were removed and reused atop the new structure. Single-use plastics have been almost entirely eliminated from guest rooms. Water is provided in recyclable aluminum bottles, and filtered water stations are conveniently placed throughout the property. The hotel features four living roofs.
Accessibility
La Bahia Hotel & Spa is ADA-compliant and strives to be accessible to all. Accommodations for guests with mobility issues, as well as hearing and visual impairments, are spread across various room categories, including suites.
Anything left to mention?
Guest experiences are unique and robust. Surf enthusiasts can hit the waves for a lesson with Santa Cruz local and pro surfer, Richie Schmidt, then take home a custom-made surfboard, while coffee lovers can opt for a behind-the-scenes tour with nearby specialty coffee company Cat & Cloud. Sunset cruises and guided bike rides to Natural Bridges State Beach can also be arranged.
La Bahia goes out of its way to partner with its Santa Cruz neighbors. Shoppers who pop into the hotel boutique (almost hidden in the corner of the lobby steps from reception) will find exclusive jewelry pieces by local maker Desert Moon Design and artisan chocolate by nearby Ashby Confections. (La Bahia also has a fun limited-edition travel bag collab with ALOHA Collection.)
Lastly, don’t forget to simply relax and enjoy the views of the surf and sand. The sounds of barking sea lions in the distance. It’s Santa Cruz at its best.
