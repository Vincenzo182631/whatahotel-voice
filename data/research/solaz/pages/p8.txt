Solaz, a Luxury Collection Resort, Los Cabos
“Transformative travel” is the buzzword at this contemporary oceanfront resort.

Why book?
Solaz is an oceanfront sanctuary where the desert meets the sea, and every detail—from the sculptural suites to the spa rituals—feels designed to restore and inspire.
Set the scene
Along the Los Cabos corridor, where the desert spills into the Sea of Cortez, Solaz feels like the sleekest address on this stretch of sand. While its sculptural architecture turns heads from the highway, inside it’s all plunge-pool terraces and a buzzing scene where honeymooners sip mezcal margaritas next to design-savvy couples in linen and Loewe sunglasses. More than anything, Solaz is built into the land itself, embedded in the desert and coastline, so it feels like part of the landscape.
The backstory
Part of Marriott’s Luxury Collection, Solaz opened in 2019 as a design-driven tribute to Baja. Created by Mexico City’s Javier Sordo Madaleno Bringas, its stone-and-glass structures are built low into the desert terrain, with commissioned Mexican art and native landscaping. The property is anchored in four pillars of design: art, architecture, landscape, and wellness.
The rooms
Rooms at Solaz feel like a continuation of the surrounding vistas—minimalist but warm, with pale stone floors, huanacaxtle wood, and floor-to-ceiling glass that frames the Sea of Cortez. Private terraces aren’t just for plunge pools; they come with sandy patches and cacti that make each one feel like a pocket beach of its own. The look is sculptural and pared-back, but it works, keeping the focus on the jaw-dropping setting. Oceanfront suites are the standouts for uninterrupted views, while multi-bedroom residences near the pools give families a bit more breathing room. Even the standard rooms feel generous, but the upper levels edge ahead with their cinematic sightlines.
Food and drink
Food at Solaz feels less like hotel dining and more like a Baja tasting tour. Al Pairo, a Michelin-recognized restaurant, is the heart of it, and the ceviche alone is worth the flight. Down by the beach, Mako keeps things casual but dialed-in with just-caught seafood and wood-grilled steaks, while Riccio di Mare shifts the mood entirely with handmade pastas, briny crudo, and seafood risotto that would feel right at home on the Amalfi Coast. For guests of villas and residences, resort-provided chefs are available to cook meals right at your accommodation for privacy that doesn’t sacrifice on quality.
The spa
The Ojo de Liebre Spa is all about slowing down. Try the thalassotherapy circuit, the only one in the region, using seawater treatments alongside hot and cold plunge pools and aromatherapy and chromotherapy showers to mimic the healing qualities of the sea.
The neighborhood/area
Solaz sits right on the Los Cabos corridor, perfectly positioned between Cabo San Lucas and San José del Cabo. Stay here and you’re close enough to dip into San José’s galleries and Thursday night art walks or Cabo’s nightlife and marina scene, but far enough away to feel removed from the bustle.
The service
Staff at Solaz remember names and favorite drinks without turning it into theater, and there’s a sense that nothing is too much trouble, but it’s delivered with a casual ease rather than white gloves.
For families
Solaz works surprisingly well for families, thanks to its multi-bedroom residences that give parents and kids room to spread out—without losing the resort’s sleek design vibe. These units are perfect for multigenerational families and come with kitchens, dining areas, and plenty of outdoor space, making them feel more like modern beachfront apartments than hotel rooms. Plus, younger kids and teens have dedicated club spaces to enjoy.
Eco effort
While not the most hardcore green resort in Baja, Solaz was designed to blend into the desert terrain rather than dominate it, with more than 150 endemic plants woven into the landscape to cut down on water use.
Accessibility
Solaz was designed with accessibility in mind, and the resort meets ADA standards with ramps, elevators, and wide pathways that flow through the property’s low-slung architecture. Public spaces—including restaurants, pools, and the spa—are all reachable without stairs, so guests with mobility needs don’t feel cut off from the property’s main draws.
Anything left to mention?
Don’t miss the resort’s on-site museum, a thoughtful space that traces Baja’s natural history and culture through artifacts and exhibits. It’s an unexpected addition, and kids can even enjoy a night in the museum during high season, featuring a themed dinner, a movie, and an assortment of snacks.
