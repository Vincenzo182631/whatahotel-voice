Thompson Nashville
The hippest design hotel in Nashville's already hip Gulch neighborhood.

Reviewed by
Margaret Littman
Why book?
The Thompson Nashville is a sleek, but not sterile, high-rise in the heart of The Gulch, supported by an ethos of quiet luxury. Everything is first-rate, but in an understated way that makes your experience, not the hotel, the star.
Set the scene
The Thompson Nashville rises 12 stories above the intersection between 11th Avenue South and 12th Avenue South (Nashville’s idea of a grid for street planning is squishy at best). Through the windows of the glass edifice, you’ll see tourists headed to the famous Station Inn or checking out the neighborhood’s murals, and locals headed to work or to the adjacent Marsh House restaurant, which cantilevers off the side. The hotel crowd is well-heeled. These are folks who regularly wear Atelier Savas leather boots; they didn’t buy cowboy boots on Amazon before their trip.
The backstory
One of the first luxury hotels in The Gulch neighborhood, the Thompson Nashville was the brand’s first hotel in the South. It was owned by a group of developers, including AJ Capital Partners and MarketStreet Enterprises, who are responsible for many of Music City’s high-profile projects. The brand was acquired by Hyatt in 2018. Thompson Nashville retains its boutique vibe, now with the benefits of being part of an international hotel brand.
The rooms
Hotel decor is midcentury-modern–inspired, which means you’ll be treated to minimalist interiors with lots of natural wood and neutral colors, like wood floors with patterned area rugs and sliding barn doors. Every room at the Thompson has floor-to-ceiling windows, which allow the views of the city to be the focal point, taking in the lights, people, and energy of Music City. The deep bathtubs are perfect for soaking in after a night on the town. When you need some shut-eye, close the thick curtains and drift into darkness. If you want to splurge, check out the nearly 13,000-square-foot penthouse.
Food and drink
Marsh House is one of those restaurants where locals know to make a reservation. It’s renowned for its sustainably harvested seafood and legit Southern recipes served in a space that feels special but not formal. Weekend brunch is popular, as is weekday happy hour, which includes deals on drinks and oysters. Upstairs, L.A. Jackson is a rooftop bar also popular with locals, thanks to its cocktail menu, DJ scene, and great views. Guest room mini bars are stocked with local treats and ingredients, including Tennessee spirits. You can use the cocktail shaker in your room to craft your own perfect drink if you don’t want to head upstairs.
The neighborhood/area
For nearly a century, The Gulch was an industrial section of town where the railroad ran. Businesses that supported trains and depended on trains did their heavy lifting here. More recent development has changed the urban landscape in the last few decades, creating a walkable neighborhood with boutiques, restaurants, and nightlife, all near downtown. Over the years, many of the walls of the new high-rises have become canvases: The Gulch is one of the best places to take a mural tour in Nashville—the famous wings are just across the street from the Thompson.
The service
The Thompson is known, most of all, for its discretion. It’s a place where celebrities like to stay when they come to Nashville on tour (the Obamas stayed here when Michelle performed her
Becoming
book event at the Ryman). So, you know that means the staff accommodates all needs and requests with aplomb. If you like your luxury low-key, this is the place for you. Like any hotel that caters to your entire crew, the Thompson Nashville is also pet-friendly.
For families
Kids stay free at the Thompson Nashville, and guests can request cribs if necessary. There are also connecting rooms available for older children or multi-generational families.
Eco effort
The entire Gulch neighborhood is LEED certified. Thompson Nashville has some sustainable elements, including the lobby floor, which is made from reclaimed wood from the neighborhood.
Accessibility
ADA standards are met, with accessible parking, room access, fitness center access, and ample-sized elevators. Closed captioning decoders and assistive listening devices are available.
Anything left to mention?
If you wake up bleary-eyed, the first-floor Killebrew Coffee will help give you a jolt, but expect a line as locals in the neighborhood love to get their caffeine fix here, too.
