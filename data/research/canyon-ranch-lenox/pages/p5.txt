Canyon Ranch Lenox
An adults-only, all-inclusive wellness resort in the Berkshires with 100,000 square feet of spa and wellness facilities.

Reviewed by
Lauren Dana Ellman
Why book?
Perched on 120 acres of wooded land just outside of Lenox, Massachusetts, once you arrive at Canyon Ranch Lenox, there’s little reason to leave. The property runs on a steady schedule of daily classes and treatments that guests can engage with as fully—or selectively—as they choose. The massive spa and fitness facilities feature a continuous flow of offerings, avoiding the sense of scarcity you often feel at smaller destination spas.
Set the scene
Canyon Ranch Lenox takes wellness seriously: This is a destination guests head to for a weekend (or week) of rest and relaxation. The grounds here are anchored by Bellefontaine Mansion (which is home to the on-site restaurant), built in 1897. Newer buildings are connected to it by long, glass-enclosed corridors that extend outwards into the surrounding area. These corridors allow guests to move between rooms, dining spaces, the spa, and fitness facilities without going outdoors—especially useful in winter—and double as informal galleries lined with work by regional artists.
Most guests arrive alone or in pairs, many of them for a repeat stay. Spa robes and slippers are rarely seen outside treatment rooms; instead, people dress for comfort—leggings, knit sweaters, walking shoes. Phones stay mostly out of sight, only making an appearance to briefly check schedules in the app. Most things—meals, bookings, scheduling—are handled through the app ahead of time, which I appreciated since that meant all I had to do was simply show up (or not). That said, there is a guest services desk within the spa and fitness complex, with staff on hand to assist as needed. Dining and daily resort credits are included, so you’re not constantly reaching for your wallet either.
The backstory
Canyon Ranch Lenox opened in 1989, the brand's second property after its original Arizona location. It has since been followed by Canyon Ranch resorts in Las Vegas, Nevada, and Fort Worth, Texas. The Berkshires location was developed around Bellefontaine Mansion, a 1897 estate inspired by the Petit Trianon at Versailles that later served as a Jesuit seminary. While the mansion anchors the property visually, most functional spaces—including the spa, fitness facilities, demonstration kitchen, and teaching areas—were added later and spread across the grounds. The resort reflects Canyon Ranch’s long-standing model of integrating fitness, spa services, nutrition-focused dining, and expert-led programming into a relaxing hotel stay.
The rooms
Canyon Ranch Lenox has 126 rooms and suites, restrained in tone and largely consistent in layout. Interiors are understated and neutral, though the amount of natural light stands out; large windows look out onto trees and landscaped grounds. With little decoration to pull focus inward, attention stays on the view. Rooms are used less as places to linger and more as spots to sleep and unwind before bed after a full schedule of classes and treatments.
Food and drink
Silverleaf, the main dining room, serves breakfast, lunch, and dinner in a bright, sun-drenched space that overlooks the landscaped grounds. The menu mixes tasty favorites with wellness-driven plates—think rooibos tea-cured salmon (a personal favorite, which I happily piled high onto fresh-baked challah bread in the mornings), blue crab and lobster cakes, and chicken tinga tacos alongside innovative options like an Okinawan sweet potato cappuccino soup—a vivid purple purée topped with lavender foam and finished with ube powder. Alcohol is available at dinner for an additional charge, but plays a minor role overall and is absent from daytime meals.
Culinary Rebel is the informal alternative eatery, open throughout the day for coffee, lighter meals, and snacks. It’s one of the few places on the property where guests linger, often settling in by the fireplace between classes for straight-from-the-oven pizza (it's hard to resist seconds), Mediterranean-inspired comfort dishes, and grab-and-go options. It tends to be the most animated food space on-site, though still subdued by resort standards.
Lastly, the Demo Kitchen operates as both a dining destination and a teaching space, hosting lunch-and-learn sessions and hands-on cooking classes focused on technique and practicality.
The spa
Canyon Ranch Lenox’s spa and wellness facilities span roughly 100,000 square feet and sit within an integrated spa and fitness complex. Treatment rooms, locker facilities, saunas, steam rooms, whirlpools, and other thermal amenities are housed under one roof, allowing guests to move easily between appointments and shared spaces throughout the day. The treatment menu is extensive, with more than 150 offerings ranging from standard massages and bodywork to therapies drawn from both Eastern and Western traditions.
Most guests book multiple services and spread them out over their stay rather than stacking everything into one afternoon; the spa is so ingrained in the experience that it doesn’t feel like a special-occasion stop. Each day before my treatments, I found myself settled into a chaise lounge by a window overlooking the pool for what felt like hours but was, in reality, only a few quiet minutes. As a self-proclaimed spa snob, I was impressed less by the aesthetics of the locker rooms and relaxation lounges (which, like the guest rooms, are fairly understated) and more by the quality of the treatments themselves. I particularly enjoyed my custom Biologique Recherche facial, which incorporated gentle lymphatic drainage and massage techniques. Afterward, my skin looked noticeably glowier, and my features felt subtly more defined. The Hydrating Body Bar Massage was another standout, which used the Kate McLeod Body Stone—a solid moisturizer—to soothe and rehydrate my parched winter skin.
The neighborhood/area
The property is about one mile from downtown Lenox, close enough that the town’s compact, picturesque center feels accessible but far enough removed that it rarely figures into a guest’s day. Lenox itself is walkable and quaint, with small shops, cafés, and restaurants clustered around a few blocks, set against wooded hills and quiet roads. Cultural landmarks such as
Tanglewood
and
The Mount
are a short drive away and especially appealing in summer, though many guests remain on the property once they arrive.
The service
During check-in, guests meet with a staff member in the lobby to review the schedule and a property map. Classes, dining spaces, and treatment areas are pointed out on paper rather than in person. A tote with a few basics, including a reusable water bottle, is handed over before guests head off on their own. After that initial orientation, staff stay mostly out of sight, appearing briefly outside classrooms and inside treatment areas to check names, open doors, and keep things moving with little conversation. As someone who cringes at the thought of small talk, this didn’t bother me; that said, others may prefer a bit more guidance or direction—both literally and figuratively (with its winding corridors and sprawling grounds, the property can feel like a maze, especially if you're a first-timer).
Eco effort
Reusable water bottles are issued at check-in, with refill stations throughout the property.
Accessibility
Canyon Ranch Lenox offers accessible rooms, and most public spaces—including the dining areas, spa, and fitness facilities—are reachable without stairs. The property is spread out, but the main buildings are connected by indoor corridors and elevators, allowing guests to move between spaces without going outside, even in winter.
Anything left to mention?
At first, the range of classes and treatments can feel overwhelming, but how you book matters. Some guests plan day by day, paying for individual services, while others arrive with a package already in place. The standard
Stay Your Way
option includes a daily resort credit—$200 per person, per night—that can be used toward spa treatments and fitness services. Other packages are more prescriptive, grouping services around a specific focus, including promotions like
All You Can Spa
, which allows for unlimited treatments.
Beyond standard classes, Canyon Ranch also offers
themed packages, retreats, and expert-led events
for an additional cost. Private sessions, specialized consultations, and select activities are priced à la carte unless bundled into one of the aforementioned packages or retreats.
