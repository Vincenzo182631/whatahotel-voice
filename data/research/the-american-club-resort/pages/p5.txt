Why book?
Known among Midwesterners for its rich history, golf courses, and day spa, the American Club makes this town in eastern Wisconsin a destination in its own right.
Set the scene
The American Club comprises a number of buildings sprinkled throughout the town that the Kohler family—yes, of Kohler plumbing fame—built in the early 1900s. Originally built to support the Kohler factory and house its workers, the town today is a mix (roughly half and half, according to a Kohler representative) of Kohler employees and other locals who have made this Olmsted Brothers–designed community their home. You’ll find a good number of Wisconsinites streaming through the lobby at the American Club, with visitors driving in from Milwaukee, Green Bay, and beyond for rounds of golf and spa treatments. On the weekends, the crowd gets a bit further reaching. One thing’s consistent, though: Tennis skirts, khaki slacks, golf shirts, and country club visors are the unofficial uniform in these parts, until evening falls and dinner jackets and crew-neck sweaters drape around shoulders.
The backstory
The Kohler family built the building that houses American Club in 1918, well before it was intended to operate as a resort. It originally functioned as a dormitory for the immigrant labor force that came to work in the Kohler factory. In 1981, after decades of sitting out of use, the American Club was reopened by Herb Kohler, the grandson of company founder John Michael Kohler, as a hotel. A constellation of spaces have been added since: The Carriage House, just a few steps from the American Club, has 55 rooms and gives guests staying in the building unlimited access to the Kohler Waters Spa and other amenities. A shuttle ride away are the Whistling Straits and Blackwolf Run golf courses and River Wildlife, a 500-acre outdoor recreation area with walking trails, canoeing, fishing, and a quintessential hunting lodge.
The rooms
While you’ll find the occasional offering that feels distinctly of this decade—hot sculpt yoga classes and private cabins for rent with decor reminiscent of the latest West Elm catalogue, for starters—the overall aesthetic of the resort is most akin to your grandfather’s country club. You know—the spot that hasn’t changed in years, and that’s exactly what he loves about it. There’s a weight to the interiors, with wooden sleigh beds, patterned taupe carpets, and hefty window treatments in the rooms. The art in most spaces is Americana 101: Oil paintings of horse-drawn carriages, rich landscapes, and vintage black and white photos. But you will notice a Peloton plopped in the corner of your room if you’re staying in the Carriage House (which you’ll want to, so you can use the spa at your leisure and enjoy perks like free workout classes).
Food and drink
The Immigrant—a maze of rooms with stone walls, oil portraits, and a battalion of cutlery on each table—is The American Club’s fine-dining establishment. There’s a 50-page wine list, and a tasting menu to splurge on; foie gras, sea scallops, and a dry aged ribeye were part of the seven-course experience during a recent visit. Unexpectedly delightful was the River Wildlife Restaurant for lunch. In the thick of the woods, past the Blackwolf Run golf course, the log cabin is a cozy hideaway with rustic classics that feel right at home in the setting: a pheasant BLT sandwich, an autumn harvest salad, and a crunchy, golden grilled cheese that oozes Wisconsin cheddar. If you do golf at Blackwolf Run, make sure to swing by the putting course at the Baths at the end of the day for a well-earned Transfusion cocktail, fantastic dusk views, and feel-good music playing through the speakers (they take requests).
The spa
The Kohler Waters Spa explains why Kohler got into hospitality in the first place. The three-level experience, stretching across the ground level, basement, and rooftop of the Carriage House, is a spa designed to showcase Kohler’s best showerheads, bath tubs, and more. There’s a small pool, for example, with a waterfall that gently massages the necks and shoulders of bathers with admirable water pressure. The signature treatments emphasize water as well—like the hydrotherapy “massage,” in which guests receive a minty scrub while lying on a massage table, as warm water from a number of Kohler showerheads rain down from above for a full 50 minutes. The spa can get crowded, so the hotel restricts access to overnight guests on the weekends, but treatments can still book up months in advance—even on weekdays. Plan ahead.
The neighborhood/area
You’ll see plenty of the town of Kohler while shuttling from one building to another, but that’s more than enough sightseeing, given that the Kohler-owned properties are the jewel in the crown here. It takes a minute to get used to the fact that you walk through a parking lot to get from The American Club’s main building to the next-door Carriage House, or that the workout classes on offer (which are fantastic, by the way) are at a Yoga on the Lake, a yoga studio in the Kohler-owned Shops at Woodlake near the local high school. The neighborhoods you’ll pass through are quaint, though, and each arm of the resort really is a destination of its own.
The service
While there’s a definite country club-atmosphere, it’s a friendly country club—staffers are always smiling, eager to help, and you feel you can let your hair down a little bit. Some staffers though, like golf pro Matt at Blackwolf Run, clearly go above the call of duty to show guests a good time.
Accessibility
Shuttles make it easy for all guests to get from A to B, but are also essential for taking advantage of the property’s many spaces. When considering the American Club over the Carriage House for accommodations, it’s worth noting that, while both have elevators, the American Club has stairs separating most spaces that some travelers may need to actively pay attention to and navigate around—the Carriage House has a simpler floor plan that may be easier for those unable to use stairs.
Anything left to mention?
The Greenhouse, a stained-glass structure that Herb Kohler found in Europe and brought back to the garden of the American Club, is one of the most beautiful spaces on the property. Pop in for coffee, pastries, or a scoop of ice cream (from 3 to 5 p.m. daily, teas are complimentary).
