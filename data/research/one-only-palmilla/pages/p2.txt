Reviewed by
Jen Murphy
Why did this hotel catch your attention? What's the vibe?
You honestly feel as if you've stepped onto a movie set. The hacienda-style architecture and old chapel make you feel like you're in a colonial pueblo—especially when there's a pop-up craft market or it's a holiday, like day of the dead, when the chapel is decorated and there might be a mariachi band playing. There's an authenticity and elegance that you just don't find in newer properties in Cabo. The way the beach seems to spill down into the rocky sea makes you feel like you're at the end of the Earth. And there always seems to be that light ocean mist in the air that makes it seem like you're in a dream.
What's the backstory?
Back in the 1950s, Baja was like the wild west. Don Abelardo Rodriguez, son of the Mexican president, spotted this plot of land from helicopter and turned it into a private home and eventually the region's first resort. The original 15-room home could only be reached by helicopter or yacht (the Transpeninsular Highway wasn't built until 1970) and the seclusion attracted Hollywood stars like John Wayne and Lucille Ball. Today the hotel has played host to celebs like Oprah and Jennifer Aniston. One&Only acquired the property in 2004. The hotel was hit hard by Hurricane Odile in 2014, but the management team used the disaster as an opportunity to reinvent the property and it reopened in 2015 with revamped rooms, an improved spa and new culinary offerings.
Tell us all about the accommodations. Any tips on what to book?
I stayed in an Ocean View Junior Suite with a Terrace. It felt like it could have been in a home rather than a hotel due to the detail of the art and furniture. I was so obsessed with the Revolta Rivolta Carmignani linens that I asked the staff where to buy them. And there was a unique scent in the room that I was told was rooted in agave. Every morning I'd have coffee on the daybed of my balcony and watch the waves crash in the ocean. The bathroom was super spacious—I loved the pebble floor of the shower—it was like a reiki treatment for my feet. I liked how much light there was in the room and how the terrace was shaded by palms. The terrace also felt private so you didn't have to worry about neighbors. The furnishings felt handcrafted: steel studded wood doors and intricately carved wooden frames around mirrors. I'm not a tech person, and the TV and air conditioning and lights were all easy for me to use. I thought I'd want an oceanfront suite but actually really enjoyed the ocean view, and it was a quick walk to the pool and restaurant. An oceanfront with a plunge pool would be my splurge.
Is there a charge for Wi-Fi?
No charge. Great quality.
Drinking and dining—what are we looking at?
Seared is an experience. I'm usually not a foie person, but his seared foie with ginger and caramelized mango is to die for. Total decadence—sweet and savory with a little kick. I'm not a steak person, but my friend said the filet mignon was exceptional. When I'm on a beach vacation I tend to eat light and the Mexican-Japanese menu at Suviche is what I want to eat every night. The seafood is either flown in from Japan that day or caught right off the shores of Baja that day. Whatever the chef recommends in terms of sashimi is great. I appreciate that this is a super luxe hotel but that it has fun and offers casual experiences as well as fine dining. I ordered the seared fish tacos three days in a row for lunch at Agua—the addition of mint and jicama and a citrus chipotle aioli made them seriously addictive. Thank god they charge for quac, because if the bowl was bottomless I would have been in big trouble. I liked that Agua offered a ton of vegetarian options, and the restaurant also had a fantastic hearts of palm and papaya salad. Breakfast is not included but Agua offered everything and anything you could. I loved the daily changing smoothies and juices and caramelized onion scramble with Oaxaca cheese. The mobile margarita bar in an an old VW van was an Instagram hit - and served a pretty perfect cocktail. I'd go back to Suviche and Seared for dinner if I were at another resort.
And the service?
I felt as if I was a family member returning home. This had been my second visit but in the span of four to five years. I was given the same butler, Sariel Castro, and he remembered that I had been training for a marathon my last visit. His father and grandfather had both been butlers there. The check in and check out were seamless, and Sariel showed me the room which was refreshingly intuitive (no searching for light switches or outlets). I also just love that they offer complimentary 10-minute foot massages by the pool and have all of the amenities you could wish for from free sunscreen and face mist to palm hand fans. A staffer even came by and cleaned my sunglasses when I took a dip in the pool. The hotel can organize incredible marine life/snorkeling excursions to Cabo Pulmo, and they work with Palegic Fleet, an eco-sensitive outfitter. They can also arrange an overnight yacht charter for guests.
What type of travelers will you find here?
Prince Harry and Meghan Markle. They're global citizens who are curious and intentional. They like to have fun and be active and adventurous but appreciate great service and fantastic food and wine. The women are wearing Sandro coverups and sundresses and then men are in Vilebrequin swim trunks. They're reading Vanity Fair poolside and talking about everything from the political debates to a bespoke tailor they just discovered on a trip to Sicily. There are more books and magazines out around the pool than iPhones and iPads. The yoga mats are full by sunrise, and a good chunk of guests are still out surfing at sunset.
What about the neighborhood? Does the hotel fit in, make itself part of the scene?
The hotel is in San Jose del Cabo. There is a major boom in hotels in Cabo San Lucas and San Jose del Cabo, and I think what sets One&Only apart is that it doesn't try to be part of the scene. It's discreetly its own scene. It has an air of sophistication that feels like you're in the Cabo of another era. It's about a 10- to 12-minute drive from San José del Cabo, which is nice because you can go for the Thursday night art walks or the staff can curate gallery tours. Compared to other hotels this feels much more discreet.
Is there anything you'd change?
They may have changed this, but I hated that there were plastic bottles by the pool and in the spa/fitness area.
Any other hotel features worth noting?
They have one of the only swimmable beaches in Cabo, which is a huge draw and you can also surf right off the beach. The 22,000-foot spa is truly impressive, but I think the temazcal experience really stands apart...they'll even bring in a shaman. Both times I arrived I was welcome with homemade mango popsicles, a fun touch that reminds you that luxury does not have to be stuffy.
Bottom line: Worth it? Why?
Absolutely. The plot of real estate is simply unrivaled in terms of its privacy and swimmable ocean access. The mix of old world glamour and service with modern, fun touches make this the definition of modern day luxury.
