Mauna Lani, Auberge Collection
A major renovation of a storied icon on the Big Island’s sunny Kona-Kohala coast

Reviewed by
Megan Spurrell
What's the vibe here?
As at most Hawaiian hotels, the staff at Mauna Lani greets guests with a breezy "aloha", a lei, and a swarm of smiling people ready to grab your luggage and offer fresh jetlag-curing juice. But the real treat is when you step into the expansive open air lobby, tastefully done with warm cedar detailing. A wooden outrigger in the center of the lobby, mounted Hawaiian artifacts, and murals by contemporary local artists nod to the destination without overdoing it—and these elements feel totally natural beside modern furniture and a neutral palette.
What's the backstory?
Even though it is one of Hawaii's most iconic properties, the Mauna Lani has gone through a series of changes over the years, thanks to various owners and visions. Notably, it was a beloved hangout for Elvis Presley and his friends in the 70's—and some of the same staff from that era are still around to share their stories—though it's always drawn a mix of families, honeymooners, and friends looking for a
Big Island
getaway. Auberge Collection purchased the place in May 2018, and has since invested more than $200 million dollars in renovations prior to the January 15, 2020 reopening. It was the glow up Mauna Lani needed to get back to its former glory.
How were the rooms?
We stayed in a suite on the top floor—meaning gorgeous views and tons of space. The room itself is done up in clean, modern neutrals: Think crisp white linens, jute decorations above the bed, and just a few pops of green and blue. The subtlety feels appropriate given that the view from every balcony takes center stage.
A friend stayed on the side facing the fish ponds, which makes for an incredible, unobstructed sunset each night. However, keep in mind that the pools and private event suite (with a huge outdoor patio just a couple floors down) are all on that side, and the noise travels a bit. I'd bet the side facing the CanoeHouse restaurant, or the waterfront, would be preferable for anyone getting to bed by 9 or 10 when the pools, bar, and event space shut down.
Is there a charge for Wi-Fi?
Wi-Fi is complimentary (thank goodness).
What's the story on drinking and dining?
HaLani is a casual open air spot serving breakfast, lunch, and dinner right by the pools. Breakfast was my favorite, with a ton on offer: from acai bowls to fresh fruit plates to a hearty ahi eggs Benedict. Lunch gets a bit more fusion-y, with delicious poke on the menu. Dinners currently lean Mediterranean (think olive plates, hot stews, and chorizo on top of ahi sashimi). Luckily, CanoeHouse doesn't disappoint. Helmed by chef Matt Raso (formerly the executive chef at Nobu Dallas, Honolulu, and Miami), it offers fresh seafood and farm-to-table produce with Hawaiian and Japanese influences. In other words, it's exactly what you want to be eating in Hawaii. Everything was delicious, from the abalone starter to the cannot-miss teriyaki chicken (seriously).
And the service?
Check-in and check-out was breezy, with plenty of staff at the concierge desk ready to assist. But if there are excursions you have your heart set on make sure to bring that up over email before you touch down.
Who stays here?
The hotel, like other Auberge Collection, feels like a handful of well-heeled West Coasters were scooped up and dropped in. The vibe is laid back—meaning you can stroll up to CanoeHouse in flip flops or dress for dinner—yet distinctly sophisticated, and there's no doubt it attracts discerning travelers.
What about the area? What's here?
The hotel is a 40-minute drive from Kona airport, on a serene slice of the Kona-Kohala coast. The benefit? You're far from traffic, any semblance of kitsch, and the sound of airplanes. But if you find yourself wanting to check out a local coffee shop, curious about a dive bar, or needing to pick up something you can't find on property, you'll need to hop in a car and drive. The Fairmount is next door (they have a Hertz rent-a-car), but it's a good half-hour from the
Four Season Hualalei
.
Anything you'd change?
Given that the idea is to stick around the resort, we'd love to see more in the food and beverage program that makes up for local haunts you need to drive for: for example, the New York-style deli in the market would be better replaced with a poke or seafood bar, like most Hawaiian supermarkets have, and the Mediterranean-focus on HaLani's dinner menu could be scaled back. Hopefully the gym, which is loaded with great equipment, will get a workout class schedule up and running, too.
Any other tips?
Make sure to spend time getting to know the employees who really form the heart and soul of the place. Speaking with Danny Akaka, who leads the living culture program, Pii Leaha, who oversees the turtle conservation program, or any of the guys working with Bullet Obra, who runs the surf shack, is a surefire way to learn everything you've ever wanted to know about Hawaiian culture—without even having to leave the property. Find them, and your trip will no doubt be the better for it.
What's the bottom line?
The renovated Mauna Lani promises an updated space and stay that maintains its ties to ancient Hawaiian culture. For first-time Hawaii visitors and seasoned vets, it's a comfortable yet connected way to experience the Big Island (also, arguably the best island for gorgeous nature and fewer crowds, without sacrificing amenities or accessibility).
