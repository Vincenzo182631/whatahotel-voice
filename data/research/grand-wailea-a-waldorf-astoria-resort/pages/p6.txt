Grand Wailea, A Waldorf Astoria Resort
A newly renovated Maui icon where the largest spa in Hawaii, the island's first Nobu, and a museum-grade art collection sit steps from Wailea Beach.

Reviewed by
Pallavi Kumar
Best for:
Families, multigenerational groups, and big celebrations (bachelorette weekends included)
Location:
Right on Wailea Beach on Maui's southwestern coast, spread across 40 acres of tropical gardens
Can't-miss feature:
Kilolani Spa, the largest spa in Hawaiʻi at 50,000 square feet, where bespoke treatments and therapies change with the phases of the Hawaiian Moon Calendar
In a nutshell:
A grand, art-filled 1990s icon, freshly renovated, now with a Nobu and one of the grandest spas I have ever been to. This is the kind of place families return to year after year, until the kids who grew up on waterslides come back with kids of their own. It is enormous and it is lively, but there is real calm here if you know where to find it.
Pallavi Kumar
Rooms
Puja Kumar
When you think of a lush Hawaiian resort, this is the place that comes to mind. There are 795 rooms here, including 58 suites, and every one of them has a private lanai (a Hawaiian balcony or veranda) with either a garden or an ocean view. The extensive 2023 renovation features pale woods, woven textures, and Hawaiian touches that feel natural and warm. My sister and I were in Maui for the week celebrating her birthday, and we saved Grand Wailea for the end. We stayed in Napua, the resort-within-a-resort on the private upper floors, in a two-queen room, and it felt like its own private world. The 100 Napua rooms come with access to two private lounges serving breakfast, midday bites, and an evening cocktail hour, plus a chef's station and live music on the eighth floor. It's a unique space, open and airy above the resort, and the dedicated concierge team made it extra special with their advice and insight on all our questions. If wellness is your love like it is mine, look at booking one of the 11 wellness rooms, which come with Therabody kit and daily access to the spa's Hydrotherapy Gardens.
Food and drink
The real star is Nobu, the first in Hawaiʻi, which opened in early 2025 in a soaring Rockwell Group–designed space. It’s over 13,000 square feet of indoor and open-air dining, striking murals, and views over the resort's famous fountains to the ocean beyond. The heart of it is the sushi counter, a beautiful bar where chefs work meticulously right in front of you, and where the eight-course Signature Omakase is served. We dined on the iconic miso black cod (an absolute must), too many orders of the tuna tacos, beautifully fresh salmon and soft shell crab sushi, and the savory wagyu dumplings that are now a firm favorite, all merrily washed down with lychee martinis and sake.
Pallavi Kumar
Humuhumunukunukuāpuaʻa, quite the mouthful and named after Hawaiʻi's official state fish, is the resort's signature seafood restaurant, and a dreamy one. It sits over a lagoon in a thatched wooden pavilion that is perfect for sunset dinners (and Instagram). We had the softest ube brioche with lilikoʻi butter and a hearty spiny lobster coconut tom kha with clams, mussels, shrimp, and maitake in a ginger chimichurri broth, which we split, and it was the perfect comfort food after an afternoon swimming in the ocean.
Pallavi Kumar
Nightcaps and fried favorites are at Botero Lounge in the heart of the atrium, surrounded by the magnificent reclining bronze Botero figures. And my sister dragged me to Loulu, the café-market with an Erewhon collaboration and Cosmic Bliss soft-serve, a lovely afternoon fix for those of us on the East Coast without an Erewhon.
Amenities and experiences
There is a lot to do here. Wailea Canyon Activity Pool is a world of its own, with seven waterslides, a lazy river, caves, and a swim-up Grotto Bar, and between that and a daily calendar of cultural activities, families are well looked after. The adults-only Hibiscus Pool is where we went for quiet after zipping through the slides like kids. But I am a wellness person through and through, and Kilolani Spa is my kind of heaven. It is the largest spa in Hawaiʻi at 50,000 square feet, with 40 treatment rooms including meditation rooms, a full salon, and a wonderful hammam, and most magically, the menu shifts with the Hawaiian Moon Calendar every 10 days. It starts at the door, where we were led into a dark alcove called the portal to set an intention with water infused with sea salt, then handed a beautiful printed robe and led toward the gardens. The spa is a beautiful oasis away from the bustle of the rest of the hotel, with even the treatment rooms with views of the grounds.
The newest addition is a first-of-its-kind partnership with Moon Juice, the LA apothecary founded by Amanda Chantal Bacon, which launched this February with a menu of moon-aligned facials and wellness elixirs. The Moon Juice HydraFacial I had was refreshing and thorough, with Milk Cleanse, Plump Jelly, Cosmic Cream, and a pearl and honey finish, and I glowed for days. We spent the afternoon sipping ginger tea in the open-air Reflection Lounge, with breathtaking views of the property and the ocean, then floated through the Hydrotherapy Gardens, from the salt water vitality pool to the hammam, the cold plunges, and the MindSync lounge chairs. For a place this vast, it is wonderfully serene.
Pallavi Kumar
What to do nearby
Pallavi Kumar
Honestly, you will not want to leave. The resort is like a mini city and the concierge team can set up anything from a Haleakalā bike descent to a boat charter. But Maui deserves a few days of your time. My sister and I drove the Road to Hana in a convertible (red, of course), top down, music up, laughing our way around the bends past waterfalls and views that look photoshopped. A warning for anyone who gets car sick: come armed with ginger chews. I was definitely feeling it on the windier stretches, but every view was worth it.
We also took a boat out to snorkel at Molokini Crater, and watching the towering rugged landscape rise from the water feels like slipping back into prehistoric time. I had my heart set on a majestic sea turtle and we didn't see one, but the fish were colorful, and the snorkeling itself was dramatic and meditative, forcing us to slow down, breathe slowly and enjoy all the natural beauty around us. On our last morning in Hawaii, we took one final dip in the ocean before the spa. Standing in the shallows, I closed my eyes, thanked the water and the universe for bringing me to this magical, healing place, and when I opened them a giant turtle had swum up a foot in front of me. It winked, I swear it, and swam away. Thank you, Maui.
The backstory
Grand Wailea opened in 1991 as the Grand Hyatt Wailea, the passion project of Japanese developer and art collector Takeshi Sekiguchi, who spent an estimated $600 million on it, around $40 million of that on art. The nine bronze Boteros in the atrium, commissioned in 1989 and now the most valuable pieces in the collection, sit right where you can touch them, alongside works by Fernand Léger and a long list of Hawaiʻi-based artists. The property is now under Hilton's Waldorf Astoria, fresh from a multiyear renovation that ended with Kilolani Spa and Nobu. The weekly art tour is free for all guests and worth the hour.
Pallavi Kumar
Insider tip
The spa treatments and Hydrotherapy Gardens are the things I would fly back for. Access comes with any treatment, or as a two-hour pass, though two hours are not nearly enough. On the way in we were handed little pots of Hawaiian botanical scrub, which we rubbed over ourselves in the steam room until the whole place smelled like the islands, then rinsed off under a bucket of ice cold water, an invigorating rush before drifting into the vitality saltwater pool to float. Repeated until blissful. If wellness is the point of your trip, book one of the 11 wellness rooms, which come with daily access to the gardens for your whole stay. And there is so much more than facials: signature rituals like Helu Pō and ʻAwa Relaxation run up to two hours, mixing lava rock massage, native botanicals, and mindfulness, and change with the moon, so each visit offers something new and different.

Family friendly
: Yes, very—kids will love the Keiki Club, 4D virtual reality game room, and activity pool with seven waterslides, a lazy river, and a Tarzan rope swing
Accessibility
