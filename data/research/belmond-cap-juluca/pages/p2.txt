Cap Juluca, a Belmond Hotel
A slice of paradise in Anguilla with rainbow skies, lush palms, and bone-white Moorish architecture.

Best for:
One-of-a-kind romantic getaways
Location:
Maundays Bay, a crescent-shaped cove on Anguilla
Can’t-miss feature:
The nightly sabrage ceremonies in the airy Main House, where a select number of guests get to saber a bottle of bubbly to ceremonially commence the start of a great night—every night. Over pours, the team also shares stories about Anguilla’s history. The trick is to get there at 5:55 p.m. if you want to do the sabering…and believe me, you’re going to want to do the sabering.
In a nutshell
: Spending your day on a pristine Caribbean beach, swimming in turquoise water, and dining at premier culinary spots just steps away from your private, breezy villa is a beautiful thing. Even better is finding peace in true seclusion (while being entirely cared for) and remembering how fun life can be at the bottom of a rum punch. Being lulled into a deep sleep by the sound of lapping waves and a gentle, sugary ocean breeze—all of the above and more can be found at Cap Juluca.
Bridget Knowles
Bridget Knowles
Rooms
Between swaying palms and an impeccable stretch of beach is a cluster of white Greco-Moorish-style villas that feel like a haven. They offer the most romantic setting on a Caribbean stage—68 rooms and 45 suites of total solitude and 24-hour villa host services. Stays can range from a deluxe beachfront experience to an intimate casita with a private pool to a five-bedroom signature villa, though the quality never changes—it's always elevated and exclusive.
Bridget Knowles
Bridget Knowles
Food and drink
The best restaurants in Anguilla are found at Cap Juluca. Even the locals know to come here for special occasions. Uchu, with its poolside location, is all about the ambiance. The Cap Shack, a toes-in-the-sand spot, is on the opposite side of the cove and a must for midday shenanigans. It goes without saying that you should order the lobster roll and rum punch here.
Pimms is the fine dining option; it is, without argument, one of the most romantic spots in the world. You can't help but fall in love with someone—and Anguilla—here, under the sky full of stars juxtaposed by the glimmering cerulean water. Cip’s by Cipriani is on-site as well, named after their iconic sister hotel, which is definitive of the experience: high-end alfresco dining set to the tune of live music.
Bridget Knowles
Bridget Knowles
Amenities and experiences
A picture-perfect infinity pool and your own dedicated chaise loungers reserved on the powder-soft sand of
Maundays Bay
are just the beginning. The property offers a dedicated spa inspired by Anguilla’s founding Arawak tribe, serviced by Guerlain. There's also every classic Caribbean activity you can imagine—snorkeling, horseback riding on the beach, sunset sailing, and water skiing—in fact, if you think of something new, I know they’ll find a way to do it for you.
What to do nearby
Having a dedicated host assigned to your room means you have unlimited contact with someone who is going to know the island like the back of their hand—and to not use that? That’s a huge missed opportunity.
Maurey was my host during my stay and was not only extremely helpful in curating a list of some of the best off-the-beaten-path bars (
Elvis Beach Bar
is the island's resounding “must”), but was also so authentic in the advice he gave that I ran into him at those very same bars that Saturday night. Anguilla is an island of sprawling roads with pastel homes dotted between palm trees, and slices of turquoise water that appear when you weave between them. Most of all, though, it’s an island of people who love their home and want to share that story with you. So when you get offered rum punch over a chat, you take that rum punch and let them lead the way.
The backstory
Arawak explorers from South America were the first to discover the island, naming the land Malliouhana, meaning “arrow-headed sea serpent.” Cap Juluca’s owners, Sue and Robin Ricketts, in homage to its founders, named the property “Juluca” after the Arawak rainbow spirit, one that promises the sun will always be there to spread color across the bay. This energy perpetuates through the guests and the property, intangible but, of course, felt.
Bridget Knowles
Bridget Knowles
Insider tip
As you weave through the property—be it on a chauffeured golf cart, private beach buggies, or bike—you'll pass palm tree after palm tree adorned with plaques of family and couples’ names. They’re palm trees guests have individually dedicated to the property to show their love and gratitude, guests who have been coming here for 20, 30, or more years. It’s a testament to how uniquely amazing this place is—so good you can’t do it once, twice, or three times. You keep coming back.
It’s also essential that you find beloved Julucan George "I-Davis" Davis—dubbed “
The Coconut Man
"—while you’re here. He’ll pick you a fresh coconut and chop it right in front of you to drink. You didn’t hear it from me, and you certainly didn’t hear it from Davis, but the only way to drink these fresh coconuts is to go to Uchu and ask for a rum shot inside. Also, if Big Jim is your host for beach service, you're in great hands. When he tells you it's better to order the bucket of beers instead of singles—you listen. For economic reasons, of course.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes! There is a kids club called the Explorer’s Club & Nursery that will keep anyone 4–12 busy with a full roster of educational and interactive activities. All restaurants also cater to a child's palate.
Accessibility:
Yes; there are specially adapted rooms available upon request.
Wellness:
There’s a dedicated spa inspired by
Anguilla’s Arawak tribe
and serviced by Guerlain. It’s a really enchanting space with a hearty offering of time-honored wellness rituals for total nourishment and relaxation, each inspired by authentic Anguillan island living.
Loyalty group:
LVMH, the world’s leading luxury goods group, which isn’t lost in translation here.
