Alila Mayakoba
Latin America’s first Alila property is perfect for those seeking pampered wellness and authentic Mexican connection.

Reviewed by
Heather Greenwood Davis
Why book?
For a pampered, well-being focused stay that is intimately connected to the history and culture of the Yucatan peninsula.
Set the scene
Opened in February 2026, the newest addition to the stunning 620-acre Mayakoba complex adds a charming, boutique-like option to the sun-drenched coastline. About 40 minutes from Cancún airport, the hotel has a mix of guests including young families (though you won’t find kids clubs or entertainment hosts), and oozes potential for solo travelers and couples who want a low-key wellness getaway. Guests can wander between all four of the Mayakoba properties (
Fairmont Mayakoba
,
Rosewood Mayakoba
, and
Banyan Tree Mayakoba
are the others), enjoying room-charging privileges throughout. Access to El Camaleón golf course, Mayakoba Dive & Water Sports Center, and Mayakoba Sports Club offer options for a more active stay.
The backstory
Alila was introduced under the Hyatt Hotels umbrella in 2019 and has properties across Asia and the US. (
World of Hyatt members
have benefits here.) Alila Mayakoba is the brand’s first foray into Latin America and the Caribbean and is managed by RLH Properties. The Alila ethos focuses on three pillars: sustainability, location, and wellness. This location (an Andaz property until late last year), set on 60 acres of mangrove, coastal dunes, and jungle, is perfectly poised to become a flagship for the brand.
Signs of Alila’s commitment to cultural experiences begins on arrival when, along with a cold towel and welcome drink, guests are offered a local clay and Melipona honey hand treatment meant to introduce the Mayan tradition of the sacred zero—a spiritual reset. Throughout my stay, gentle touches at the spa (cleansing ceremonies and local teas) and the option to add on one of four signature Alila Moments highlighted traditional Yucatan healers and Mayan cosmology. It’s another route to connecting guests to the spirituality of the stunning location.
The rooms
The property’s transformation included reducing the number of rooms and increasing room size. Guests can now choose from Lagoon area and Beach area rooms (there are several categories of each) that start at 538 square feet. About 40% of inventory is suites, which start at 710 square feet.
The Beach area tends to be a bit more energetic than the Lagoon side, thanks in part to the number of young families who seem to prefer this location. Still, I found serenity in my beachfront oceanview suite, which felt more like an apartment than a hotel room. I found myself yearning for more time in my living room, complete with books and clay pottery on the shelves. My bedroom included a walk-in closet, and separate shower and toilet areas. I had two beach-view balconies—one with a plunge pool and pair of chairs, and the other with comfy outdoor seating from which I could watch sunrise meditators in the comfort of my pajamas.
For an even more tranquil stay, choose a room in the Lagoon area; perfect for those who want to make the glass-walled gym or spa enclave a central part of their stay. (Buildings 10 and 12 keep you closest.) The Chef’s Atelier—a cooking class and dining option for a maximum of 12 guests per session—is on this side too.
All rooms and suites lean into the neutral colors of sand and taupe, but textures (woven lamp shades, and carved stone wall hangings ) add some personality.
Food and drink
At the time of my visit, three dining options were available to try. Chef Michael Grau—whose culinary approach mirrors Alila's, with a commitment to local ingredients and historical customs—oversees each menu. Ninguno Taqueria (serving breakfast and lunch) and Alisio Beach Club (open for lunch and dinner) are beachside neighbors with extensive menus that range from chilaquiles and avocado toast in the morning to whole seasoned fish and grilled octopus at night.
On the Lagoon side, Casa Amate, a four-room imagination of a traditional explorer’s family home, is worth a reservation. The restaurant—decorated with “family photos,” art, and maps—is a cozy dining experience featuring a mix of tables and banquettes. Don’t miss the Mexican Crispy Avocado (a whole fried avocado served with a blackberry tequila sauce). A fourth restaurant, Na Cocina Local, opened after my visit. Across the property, robust cocktail and mocktail menus showcase fresh fruits and local liquors.
The spa
Set on the Lagoon side of the property, the two-story, six-suite oasis offers a host of treatments all inspired by the Mayan calendar and ancient wellness techniques. My Ak’bal (“night”) treatment, for example, included a Copal smudging ceremony to cleanse the air before a guided breath work massage. On arrival, spa guests choose from local herbs (rosemary and lemongrass among them) and sip a tea from their selection before descending to treatment areas.
All hotel guests have complimentary access to the hydro circuit (though a reservation is required) which includes a steam room, dry sauna, and cold plunge. A co-ed outdoor hot tub, Temazcal, and yoga deck round out the spa offerings.
The neighborhood/area
The Mayakoba complex is lush. Green foliage, tropical birds, and critters like coati and raccoons (you’ll need to keep doors closed or risk a closer encounter than you may desire) abound. El Pueblito, an on-site replica Mexican village, has a gorgeous church popular for destination weddings, shops, an arcade, and restaurants.
Complimentary on-site activities offer other ways to explore your location, from a scenic, winding, 45-minute mangrove tour via electric boat to cruiser bikes perfect for exploring the 3.73 mile-long nature trail with potential stops at an on-site cenote and a butterfly garden.
Off-site, popular nearby spots include Tulum (about a 90-minute drive), the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, and UNESCO World Heritage Site Chichén Itzá.
The service
Every room on the property has two dedicated Resort Hosts. Available via WhatsApp, they perform a range of functions from golf cart transportation to restaurant reservations. I was impressed by how information is shared between staff. One of my fellow travelers had a shellfish allergy and it was noted without prompting at every meal. Thoughtful evening turndown amenities appear alongside your carefully placed bedside slippers each evening. The bug spray and sunscreen in your room mean you can leave the big bottles at home.
For families
During my stay, which coincided with spring break, most guests had children with them. Chic moms in wide-brimmed hats, dark glasses, and breezy
sarongs
seemed comfortable nibbling breakfast and sipping green drinks while watching their kids play in the pool. There are no kids camps or dedicated counselors, and it's likely that the number of families here will fluctuate according to holiday schedules.
Eco effort
In guest rooms, glass bottles of purified water, in-room air-conditioning sensors, and locally sourced mini-bar items are intentional choices. The resort also touts solar panels, recycling plants, and ongoing efforts to protect local flora and fauna.
Accessibility
There are nine accessible rooms and suites across room categories that offer wheelchair-accessible entrances and terraces, roll-in rain showers, and lowered door handles, peepholes, and light switches. Restaurants, spa, and fitness facilities all offer accessible entry.
Anything left to mention?
Don’t miss the opportunity to participate in an Alila Moment—which are signature rituals rooted in Mayan beliefs. At Alila Mayakoba, Alejandro Ortiz, the hotel’s global wellbeing operations officer, has curated four rituals that celebrate earth, air, fire, and sea. I sampled the sunrise Earth and Clay ceremony, a combination of a meditative local clay application to my face and body, followed by a copal cleansing and sea bath, all set to live ancestral instrumentation. (Alila Moments are available at an additional cost ranging from $620–$2,200 for up to two people.)
