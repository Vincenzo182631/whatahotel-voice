La Casa de la Playa by Xcaret
An adults-only Riviera Maya retreat that redefines the all-inclusive experience.

Reviewed by
Lissa Poirot
Best for:
Couples, honeymooners, luxury travelers who want an intimate resort with plenty to do inside and out
Location:
On a secluded stretch of the Riviera Maya coastline, about 45 minutes south of Cancún International Airport and just outside Playa del Carmen
Can’t-miss feature:
The extraordinary oceanfront suites, where private plunge pools, built-in jellyfish aquariums, and handcrafted Mexican design make leaving your room surprisingly difficult
In a Nutshell:
La Casa de la Playa is an adults-only hideaway that redefines the all-inclusive experience through exceptional design, deeply personal service, and exclusive access to the best of Grupo Xcaret. I came expecting a theme park hotel and left convinced it’s one of the Riviera Maya’s finest.
Lissa Poirot
Rooms
Each suite spans more than 1,000 square feet, but lofty ceilings and thoughtful design make them feel even larger. The interiors celebrate contemporary Mexican craftsmanship through warm woods, hand-carved stone, natural fibers, and artisan-made furnishings that feel luxurious without being overdesigned. Sliding Caribbean shutters disappear into the walls, opening the dressing area to the same sweeping sea views as the bedroom, and the room’s centerpiece is an oversized winged heart sculpture, handcrafted in vibrant chaquira beads by Wixárika artisans, providing a striking tribute to Mexico’s artistic heritage. The outdoor space offers a large plunge pool, hammock, oversized daybed, and soaking tub where butlers prepare custom baths with your choice of honey- or mint-infused products. And then there are the in-room jellyfish. Illuminated by a blue glow, the translucent creatures seem to fly rather than swim, drifting weightlessly through the built-in aquarium in the wall beside the bed.
Lissa Poirot
Food and drink
I never once felt like I was dining at an all-inclusive, where overabundance can often outshine flavor. Lunch at Tuch de Luna set the tone immediately. Helmed by Chef Martha Ortiz and staffed entirely by women, the menu is described as a “love letter to Mexico,” and it shows in dishes that celebrate indigenous ingredients, from her signature Pink Mole to vibrant ceviches. Dinner at Xal was refined enough to rival many standalone fine-dining restaurants. The multi-course tasting menu weaves together the flavors of Mexico, the Philippines, and the Basque Country with dishes like grilled beef cheek with herbs. Guests also have access to all 21 restaurants by Hotel Xcaret Mexico and Hotel Xcaret Arte if they desire a change in pace.
The moments between meals, I found myself slipping into the underground cava to choose a bottle of wine to enjoy back on my terrace, stopping by the 24-hour chocolatería for handcrafted chocolates, and sampling small-batch tequilas at the Bodega, which, like the wine cellar, never closes. It felt like having the keys to a well-stocked beach house.
Lissa Poirot
Lissa Poirot
Amenities and experiences
If you can tear yourself away from your suite, the rooftop infinity pool offers uninterrupted Caribbean views, while the other, lower glass-walled infinity pool projects over the sea, creating the sensation of swimming straight into the horizon. Although the resort sits directly on the water, don’t come expecting a long, walkable beach. Instead, the shoreline is made up of rocky outcroppings, making the pools—and the views—the true focal point.
The surprise is that all of this comes with access to much more beyond the property. As the most exclusive hotel in the Grupo Xcaret collection, La Casa de la Playa has plenty of thoughtfully curated and immersive experiences, such as candlelit dinners inside cenotes after the Grupo Xcaret parks have closed and private expeditions through underground caves.
What to do nearby
One of the benefits of staying at La Casa de la Playa is that admission to all of Grupo Xcaret’s parks and tours is included, along with perks such as skip-the-line access, priority transportation, and exclusive experiences. If it’s your first visit, start with Xcaret Park, where underground rivers, wildlife encounters, and cultural exhibits offer an immersive introduction to Mexico. Adventure seekers should make time for Xplor’s zip lines and underground cave system, while Xenses provides a playful take on nature complete with optical illusions. Just don’t make the mistake of thinking you must visit the parks to get value from your stay. La Casa de la Playa is a rare resort where staying in can be every bit as rewarding as heading out, and where an afternoon spent by your plunge pool may become the highlight of your trip.
The backstory
Opened in 2021, La Casa de la Playa was conceived as the most intimate and luxurious expression of the Grupo Xcaret brand. Rather than building another large beachfront resort, founder Miguel Quintana Pali envisioned a boutique retreat where architecture, art, nature, and Mexican culture would take center stage. He entrusted that vision to his son, architect David Quintana, who filled the property with handcrafted works by Mexican artisans, native materials, and contemporary design rooted in its surroundings. During my stay, I watched Pali arrive by yacht at the hotel’s private dock and later learned that La Casa de la Playa is more than a resort; it’s also his home.
Insider tip
While nearly every dining experience at Grupo Xcaret is included in your stay, there’s one exception. You’ll have to reserve a table separately at HA’, the intimate tasting menu restaurant by Carlos Gaytán, the first Mexican-born chef to earn a Michelin star. The nine-course menu reimagines Mexican cuisine through refined French technique. Depending on the season, dishes might include wagyu tartare with hoja santa, a tetela filled with escamoles, or locally sourced seafood, each paired with Mexican wines selected to complement the menu. Reservations are limited and often book up before guests arrive, so ask the concierge to secure a table as soon as your stay is confirmed.
Lissa Poirot
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
No, adults only (ages 16+)
Accessibility:
Yes; there are accessible accommodations and public spaces.
Wellness:
Muluk Spa is relatively compact with serene treatment rooms illuminated by flickering candle-like lights. The hydrotherapy pool didn’t wow me, however.
