Nobu Hotel Marbella
A full tilt dive into Marbella's glamour with all the perks of a stay at one of the area’s choicest resorts.

Reviewed by
Charley Ward
Why book?
For an A-lister worthy break featuring tennis, sunbathing on the Costa del Sol’s most glamorous beach, Los Angeles’s favorite sashimi, cucumber mezcal Palomas, a 24-carat gold infused facial, and a suitably ritzy night out.
Set the scene
Celeb-y restaurant-turned-hospitality behemoth Nobu and starry Marbella were always going to make natural bedfellows. The brand’s Spanish outpost forms part of the sprawling
Puente Romano resort
that takes over the city’s buzziest stretch of coastline like its own self-contained beach town. There are plenty of families at
Puente Romano
, but Nobu’s reputation makes its particular enclave a magnet for the young, the flashy, and the never (ever) underdressed. It feels like a naughtier little sister within the breezy Mediterranean environs, where orange petals collect as colorful snowdrifts on cobbled streets lined with lime-washed Andalusian villas, the odd hidden pool, a great spa, and glut of restaurants and cafés. And that's before you even see the sea. Everyone rubs shoulders comfortably, but it’s the Nobu guests taking over the adults-only pool and queuing up outside Coya Club in the central courtyard, from where Latin beats emanate through the evenings on weekends.
The backstory
Nobu co-founder Chef Nobu Matshuhisa won a fan in actor Robert De Niro for his original Los Angeles restaurant, and the pair worked together to open the first Nobu in Tribeca, New York in 1994. It quickly became the place to be for an A-List contingent—the likes of the Kardashians, Biebers, Hadids, and Rihanna are often papped there, while Drake, Kanye, and Doja Cat have all name-checked the brand in their lyrics—and this celebrity cachet has helped the brand grow enormously. There are now over 50 Nobu restaurants and 13 hotels worldwide, with more to come, including one just-announced for Manchester, UK at a later date.
The rooms
Mediterranean at the front, Japanese(ish) at the back. There’s a slickness in keeping with the brand’s restaurant schemes, with Eastern-leaning brown marble bathrooms, minimal lines and lots of wood, while linen furnishings and a scattering of cushions bring you back to the Med. Balconies showcase views over the resort’s rooftops or, if you’re lucky, across the sea. Families can combine multiple suites, and party people will be assured by the larger rooms, which can sleep up to nine guests.
The food and drink
With around 20 bars and restaurants across the resort, really, the concept is “more is better”—at which it succeeds. A highlight is rustic chic beach bar Chiringuito, which serves up good vibes and excellent food to guests in slinky resortwear—think lobster salads, sea bass shawarmas, and an improbably delicious sharing steak despite my dining partner’s tedious insistence on it being well-done; surely the highest praise for a chef. Staying guests have priority at namesake restaurant Nobu, where you'll find its signature Japanese-come-Peruvian menu, developed during founder Chef Matsuhisa’s time in Lima. The honey-glazed miso black cod is the most popular dish, and there's seasonally changing omakase and a solid cocktail and sake list. We enjoyed all of the chef's tasting menu, but the basil gin Martini, scallops, and sashimi are the standouts to order.
The spa
Six Senses leads the spa which, like everything here, has everything you could possibly need and also that which you probably don’t. That is to say: Visiting practitioners in the past have included spiritualists and aura readers, you can have an ‘intra-oral’ massage whereby your cheeks are plumped from the inside by a latex gloved-up masseuse, or choose to wrap your entire body in a pearl-infused hot sheet. Again—more is better, although less adventerous guests can always elect for a bog-standard deep tissue or foot massage. The hammam is vibey, with herbal and regular steam rooms and sauna, hot-and-cold therapy showers, and various hydrotherapy pools, secreted behind a bubbling waterfall wall. New in 2025 were three personalized yogic programs designed to tackle common issues around sleep, immune systems, weight loss, and muscle-building.
The neighborhood
Marbella’s glitzy Golden Mile is an affluent stretch of coastline spanning from the city’s edge to the Puerto Banús marina, with its monied crowd, clutch of beautiful beaches, swish restaurants, and luxury resorts just minutes from the cobbled streets of Old Town. But there’s little reason to leave the resort, should you not have a yacht to catch—between the pool and the beach bars, more restaurants you can shake a stick at, morning reformer class and afternoon tennis, this is a stop-and-flop (if you like) dream.
The service
Efficient, if a little impersonal.
For families
La Casita Kids Club runs a daily activities program, including tennis coaching, art masterclasses, and cooking lessons (including Nobu sushi workshops), along with beach trips and the likes of treasure hunts, escape room games, and magic shows. Serving guests aged 13 to 18, the Teen Spirit Lounge has a poolside diner, and games to practice including pool, chess, and backgammon. There’s also a team of private tutors trained in both UK and global curriculums for primary and secondary school-aged children.
Eco effort
Good. The hotel has an ‘A’ energy efficiency rating, electrics are powered by 100 percent renewable energy and 90 percent of boiler systems by biofuels. In-room amenities are made from sustainably packaged and sourced bamboo, and a filtered water system has replaced single-use plastic bottles. A local farmer tends the hotel’s own organic market garden and priority is given to local and regional suppliers with a sustainable and/or ethical focus. The resort donates to multiple charities, consistently raising around €100,000 annually for charities including World Vision, The Children’s Trust, Triple A Animal Welfare, and Spanish cancer charity AECC.
Accessibility
There are some ramps throughout the resort, but a lot of rooms are only accessible via stairs. See our round up of the
best hotels in Marbella
for alternative suggestions.
Anything left to mention?
Book spa treatments in advance to avoid disappointment; I missed out despite multiple valiant efforts across the weekend.
