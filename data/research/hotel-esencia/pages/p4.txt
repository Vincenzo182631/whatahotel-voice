Hotel Esencia
This former villa of an Italian duchess is now your private jungle getaway, 30 minutes from hip Tulum.

Reviewed by
Mary Lussiana
Start us off with an overview: What's it like being here?
Every fashion photographer, model, chat-show host, and film director ends up here, on a perfect lick of beach, a short hop from the main drag of frisky boho Tulum. It's a quiet, stylish, supercool escape that hits just the right note. You feel hidden from the world, searching for a small, hardly signposted entrance, and then a thatched lodge where the outside world stops and only guests get to go through and onto the hotel.
What's the backstory on this place?
Originally a private hideaway holiday home for an Italian Duchess, it became a hotel in 2005 but was snapped by current owner, entrepreneur, and art collector Kevin Wendle in 2014 (and remains an independent hotel). An American with contacts in the fashion and arts world, he set about inviting friends and marketing by word of mouth, as well as adding rooms and sprinkling the renewed interiors with art and antiques. Famous clients include Jason Wu, who got married there and has designed a capsule collection inspired by (and available at) the hotel; Bella Hadid, who did a
Vogue
cover there; and Aerin Lauder, who launched her perfume there this year.
Tell us about your room.
I loved my room. It was an Ocean Suite, number 703, which was in the main house looking straight out to sea. It was large, with shiny white floors and a tranquil feel. A geometric colorful rug on the bed, a red sofa seat with red and white striped cushions on the wide terrace, from where I could watch the waves roll in. The bed was comfortable, the light switches user-friendly. The bathroom had double basins and a huge shower and a door out to the terrace. I would come back to this one; although some of the new beach suites looked lovely, I liked being on the first floor in the main house.
Food and drink: what are our options?
The food is the one thing that they haven’t got quite right in my mind. The food is good, but not at the same level as the rest of the hotel. The presentation needs work and the menu doesn’t have a clear profile. The new restaurant Mistura debuted this year and it is set under a beautiful palapa that curves right out onto the beach. I had delicious guacamole and tacos, and then a catch of the day wrapped in banana leaf. There are ceviches and tiraditos with the menu put together by Dimitris Katrivesis, but it seems he is no longer involved. Currently being built is a restaurant, which I think will add to the culinary variety and have the right vibe. Breakfast is included—try the soft scrambled eggs with jalapeño pepper served with warm tortillas. The Beach Bar, I loved. Wooden with dim candlelight, right on the edge of the beach, and stairs up to a bench for two. You can have your margarita within the sound of the sea.
How did you find the service?
Service was excellent. I particularly liked having a morning green tea brought to me at 7 a.m. each day (you are asked on arrival what you would like, when, in the morning). The staff were all very friendly. I thought Lupita in the spa was excellent, and Mr. Smith, who took me out snorkeling on the catamaran, was knowledgeable and great to be with.
What kind of people stay here?
Affluent cognoscenti. The fashion crowd and those who want to fall off the radar for their holiday. This is all about discretion and solace. Books will be intelligent, music could be the blues. They will be well traveled.
What about the neighborhood the hotel is in—anything of note nearby?
It doesn’t fit in and doesn’t want to fit in; this is an escape from the outside world. I think guests would rarely go out of here, for they won’t find somewhere of the same quality nearby. Other hotels in a nearby radius are bigger and resort-like, while this feels like a home—your private home.
What's the one thing you'd change?
The food.
Anything we missed?
The Spa is magical, with a limited amount of people allowed in at any one time. Several hot tubs are hidden away in niches, all under a soaring thatched palapa. Treatments draw on the local Mayan heritage and ingredients from the garden. And the pools—one for children, one of equal size for adults—are lovely. Right by the beach, they are bordered by lush jungle.
Bottom line: worth it, and why?
For some, a hotel is simply a place to lay their head; for me, it is an experience to bottle and place in my mind to enjoy time and again. This place is one of those hard-to-define spots where you will always want to come back hoping it hasn’t changed, feeling proprietorial about it. I felt looked after here, traveling alone yet surrounded by care. I loved the lack of signing bills, enhancing that home-away-from-home feeling. Worth every penny.
