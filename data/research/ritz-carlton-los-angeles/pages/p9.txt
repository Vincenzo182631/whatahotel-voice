The Ritz-Carlton, Los Angeles
A Ritz with a boutique-y feel

Reviewed by
Celeste Moure
Best for:
Power couples in town for a show or game, solo travelers looking for city energy without the Sunset Strip vibe, and anyone who knows DTLA is the place to be.
Location:
Downtown Los Angeles within the L.A. LIVE entertainment complex, and steps from Crypto Arena and the GRAMMY Museum.
Can't-miss feature:
A rooftop pool on the 26th floor with jaw-dropping views of downtown, framed by a recently renovated Art Deco aesthetic that feels fresh.
In a nutshell:
The Ritz-Carlton renovated its downtown tower and managed to do something tricky: made a corporate hotel feel considered. The thoughtful Art Deco renovation references old Hollywood without the pastiche, and you're steps from plenty of culture, entertainment venues, and dining options.
Rooms
The 123 rooms and 14 suites sit on the upper floors in muted jewel tones and velvet, with the kind of metalwork and etched mirrors that could feel overdone but don't. The furniture is spare enough that the design details land rather than overwhelm. You get Italian linens, Diptyque products, solid televisions with streaming—all the luxe boxes checked. But the rooms read as refined rather than fussy. Book high if you can; the views of downtown and the arena district are spectacular.
Food and drink
On the 24th floor there are three concepts under one roof: Sendero is the main restaurant, Corteza is the bar and the place for smaller plates (think empanadas, ceviche and arepas), and Leña is the Latin influenced steakhouse. There’s also the Agave Library, a cozy spot where you'd want to sit for a while and enjoy a drink (or three). If you want to bypass the 24th floor entirely, the Club Lounge on the upper floors does wine in the evening and coffee in the morning.
Amenities and experiences
The pool on the 26th floor has the kind of vantage point that makes downtown look less like a place you're stuck in and more like a place worth being. The spa takes itself seriously: nine treatment rooms, a couples' suite (actually intimate for a hotel property), signature treatments alongside the standards. A spa concierge will build custom programs, and your serums, eye masks, and creams await for you in the mini fridge placed in your bathroom. The fitness center is compact but complete.
What to do nearby
The arena is your literal backyard: Lakers, Clippers, concerts depending on the season. The GRAMMY Museum is a walk. There's a 14-screen cinema, bowling, a nightclub. The Arts District—galleries, independent restaurants, vintage shops, cafes that aren't trying—is maybe 15 minutes on foot.
The backstory
This was a functional corporate hotel before the renovation. Designer Gensler took the bones and reinterpreted the Art Deco language that actually defined Los Angeles: the cinema glamour, the fashion moment, the swagger of the 1920s.
Insider tip
The pool in the late afternoon, around 4 or 6 p.m. depending on the time of year, is a moment when downtown light turns gold without being dramatic—being there is a non-negotiable. The hotel has also partnered on a series of ultra-luxury experiences that are truly inventive: Dinner for Two on the 50-Yard Line at SoFi Stadium is the kind of thing that sounds absurd until you're sitting there; and the guided tour of the Historic Flower Market with a bouquet-making workshop after is pure LA. There's also a chocolate sculpture class with a chef named François that skews indulgent but works if you're the type who goes for that. My favorite is the beauty fridge: there’s a consultation with the spa manager prior to arrival, and a customized skincare routine delivered to your room in a cute little fridge.
Final Considerations
Family friendly:
Not really the vibe. L.A. LIVE brings adult crowds; older kids might enjoy the GRAMMY Museum nearby and the cinema, but this isn't a resort-pool situation.
