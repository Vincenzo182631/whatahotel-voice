### Beautiful Property and Excellent Food, but Service and Operations Do Not Match the Resort’s Level

We stayed at Alila Villas Uluwatu for three nights and had been very excited about the experience. The property itself is spectacular: distinctive architecture, a spacious and private one-bedroom pool villa, beautiful grounds, and a breathtaking ocean setting. We also enjoyed the food very much at both breakfast and dinner.

Unfortunately, the service, maintenance, and attention to detail were surprisingly inconsistent for a resort of this caliber and price point.

Excellent dinner service, but a very different breakfast experience

Dinner was one of the highlights of our stay. The staff were warm, attentive, and quickly remembered us. They also understood proper meal pacing, checking whether we had finished a course and asking when we would like the next one served. The service felt personal and polished.

Breakfast was almost the opposite.

The food itself was very good, but service often felt disorganized and understaffed. Staff did not always remember what we had ordered or what was still outstanding. On one occasion, a staff member recommended something we had literally just eaten.

It could also be surprisingly difficult to get anyone’s attention. Dirty dishes remained on our table even as staff walked past, and we sometimes had to ask for plates to be cleared simply to make room.

Several breakfast dishes would sometimes arrive together, crowding the table and getting cold while we tried to eat everything. The contrast with dinner was striking: at dinner, the meal was paced around the guest; at breakfast, it often felt as though the entire order was simply being delivered at once.

Even our departure reinforced this impression. We had informed the front desk that we needed a buggy at 6:30 AM for an early departure. That morning, we were ready early and called at approximately 6:10 AM. The buggy came, we checked out, and by 6:30 our luggage was loaded into our waiting Grab.

However, the takeaway pastry box and coffee the hotel had kindly offered us were still not ready. We waited another 10 minutes and even said we were prepared to leave without them, but staff asked us to wait.

The issue was not the 10-minute wait or the complimentary food. The resort already knew our departure time, and after our 6:10 call there was ample opportunity to alert the restaurant. It was another small example of the lack of anticipation and coordination we had experienced at breakfast.

Four different cabana explanations in two days

Our experience at the main pool was the most frustrating part of the stay.

There are only four cabanas, along with a limited number of shaded loungers. Before making a complaint, we specifically confirmed with Host Service that the cabanas operate on a first-come, first-served basis.

On our first morning, we went to the pool around 6:30 AM for sunrise and claimed a cabana. The pool staff member told us we could use it only until noon because someone else had it afterward. That was completely fine with us.

We went to breakfast, but when we returned, the cabana had already been given to other guests. Staff had to locate those guests in the pool and ask them to move their belongings. We treated it as an unfortunate human error and moved on.

The following morning, we again claimed the same cabana around 6:30 AM. A different staff member even placed a bag there for us. After breakfast, we returned, spent some time there and had drinks, then went back to our villa to change. Housekeeping was servicing the villa, which delayed us, and we returned to the pool approximately 45 minutes later.

Our cabana had been given away again.

We were then given several explanations: because of high occupancy, an unattended cabana could not be held for more than one hour, and unattended drinks had to be removed for hygiene reasons.

The problem was not having a time limit. With only four cabanas, that would be perfectly reasonable. The problem was that within two days we had heard several versions of how the cabanas worked: first come, first served; available only until noon because someone else had it later; a one-hour unattended limit during high season; and the hygiene explanation.

If the rule is one hour unattended, simply tell guests when they claim a cabana and apply it consistently. We would have happily followed it. In our case, however, we had been away for only about 45 minutes.

After the incident, we used the pool and later sat on an unshaded lounger. Staff subsequently moved us into an available cabana, which we appreciated.

About 2.5 hours after the incident, an F&B manager came to speak with us personally. She apologized and explained the one-hour rule during high season. We told her our main concern was simply that the resort needed one clear policy so guests could follow it. She then kindly offered to make our dinner complimentary as an apology.

We appreciated the gesture, but when we finished dinner and were told there was no charge, we insisted on paying.

We were not looking for compensation. We wanted the resort to correct the operational problem so future guests would not experience the same thing.

Maintenance problems and lack of follow-through

We also noticed maintenance issues throughout the property.

At night, each villa entrance has two ground lights, including one illuminating the villa number. We noticed many villas with only one functioning light, often with the villa-number illumination out, as well as non-functioning lights around steps and pathways.

Our villa had the same problem.

We reported it on our first night and were told it had been reported, but nothing was fixed. On our second night, we had to report it again. Only then was it addressed, although we were initially told that the part needed for the villa-number illumination was unavailable and would not be repaired before our departure.

The following morning, Housekeeping unexpectedly brought us snacks and drinks. We asked whether this was something offered to everyone and were told it was specifically for us. We later contacted Host Service to thank the hotel and ask why, and learned that it was a special amenity because of the lighting issue. We appreciated the gesture.

On our final evening, however, we continued to see many non-functioning lights at other villas, steps, and pathways.

When we returned after dinner, the head of Housekeeping was waiting outside our villa after turndown service and spent some time speaking with us about our stay, which we appreciated.

At the same time, we noticed that our villa-number illumination had finally been installed. Ironically, the replacement number was enormous - at least twice the size of those at the other villas - which we actually found funny.

We appreciated that ours was ultimately fixed. But after walking past many similar lights that remained out elsewhere, the maintenance felt reactive rather than preventive: the problem we repeatedly raised was fixed, while the same issues remained visible around the property.

A beautiful villa with some surprisingly impractical details

The villa itself was beautiful, but some of the lighting controls were surprisingly impractical.

There is essentially one master switch near the entrance controlling the main lights, with no equivalent master control at the bedside. Some decorative lamps also need to be switched off individually. More surprisingly, once the master switch is off, there is no independently usable switch for the bathroom lights.

On two mornings, I woke around 5:30 AM to get ready for sunrise while my partner was still sleeping. With the master lighting off, the bathroom was dark. Apart from the tiny light from the Toto toilet, I ended up using my phone flashlight to navigate the villa and bathroom rather than turning on the main lights.

These may sound like small details, but at a luxury resort, small details matter.

Management made genuine efforts - but service recovery is not enough

We do want to acknowledge that once our concerns reached management, several people made genuine efforts to address them. We received the special amenity following the lighting issue; the F&B manager personally came to speak with us, apologized, and offered dinner; the head of Housekeeping took time to discuss our stay; and our villa-number lighting was ultimately repaired.

We appreciated those efforts.

But service recovery is not a substitute for consistent service in the first place.

We were never expecting absolute perfection. Mistakes happen everywhere. At this level, however, we expect professionalism, consistency, anticipation, attention to detail, and proper follow-through.

During a stay of only three nights, we repeatedly found ourselves managing things guests should not have to manage: trying to get breakfast staff’s attention, asking for dishes to be cleared, keeping track of what food had or had not arrived, dealing with the same cabana problem on two consecutive mornings involving two different staff members, trying to understand multiple versions of the cabana policy, reporting the same maintenance problem twice, and finally waiting for takeaway breakfast while our luggage and Grab were already ready to leave.

That is a lot of unnecessary friction for such a short stay.

At this level, it is often the smallest details that reveal how seriously a resort approaches hospitality. Luxury is not only beautiful architecture, private pools, and spectacular views. It is also what happens quietly behind the scenes: noticing when a plate needs clearing, remembering what a guest has already ordered, pacing a meal properly, communicating one clear pool policy, following up on a maintenance request the first time, and having a takeaway breakfast ready when a guest’s departure time is already known.

The dinner team demonstrated this beautifully. They remembered us, watched the pace of our meal, and asked when we wanted the next course. That kind of anticipation makes service feel effortless to the guest.

Unfortunately, too often during this stay, we felt that we had to manage or follow up on the service ourselves.

That is especially disappointing because Alila Villas Uluwatu has the ingredients to be exceptional. The architecture is extraordinary, our villa was beautiful, the setting is spectacular, the food was very good, and some members of the team provided genuinely excellent service.

But at this price point, guests are paying for more than architecture, a private pool, and an ocean view. They are paying for consistency, anticipation, preventive maintenance, clear procedures, and attention to detail.

We arrived very excited to experience Alila Villas Uluwatu. We left appreciating the beauty of the property, the excellent food, and some of the people we met, but deeply disappointed by the inconsistency of the overall operation.

Unfortunately, we would not return.
