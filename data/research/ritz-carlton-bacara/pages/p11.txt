The Ritz-Carlton Bacara, Santa Barbara
A luxury Santa Barbara Property with unmatched views

Why book?
As a sprawling, 78-acre Spanish-style resort set on a dramatic coastal bluff near Santa Barbara, Bacara offers something for nearly every traveler. Plus, a top-to-bottom renovation completed in mid-2025 means it’s newly polished.
Set the scene
The Ritz-Carlton Bacara fully embraces the Mediterranean-like climate and easygoing vibe of California’s Central Coast. It’s upscale, yet casual. Even during the holiday season, when I stayed, the look was relaxed and resort-forward: linen sets, leather sandals, and crisp, unfussy resort wear at dinner. The crowd is a blend of multigenerational families, celebratory couples, and friends weekending from Los Angeles and San Francisco. As a destination resort, after check-in, there’s no need to leave the property if you don’t want to. You’ll fit right in by dining al fresco on sunset-facing terraces or by the pool beneath red-and-white striped umbrellas.
The backstory
Originally opened in 2000, Bacara was conceived as a grand Mediterranean-style coastal resort on land stewarded by the Chumash people. (The native presence is acknowledged through thoughtful design details and interpretive placards across the property.) Built in Spanish Colonial Revival style, common to this stretch of California, the hotel features stucco facades, arched doorways, and hand-painted tilework. Now part of the Ritz-Carlton portfolio, Bacara completed a multi-million-dollar renovation in July 2025 that touched nearly every corner of the resort. Led by interior design firm Meyer Davis, the updates introduce more local art and Chumash motifs, reimagined food and beverage venues, and expanded outdoor spaces, giving the property an even stronger sense of place.
The rooms
The 358-key hotel is spread across nearly a dozen buildings, ranging from 450-square-foot king guest rooms to two ultra-swanky signature Ritz-Carlton Suites, each spanning roughly 2,000 square feet. Regardless of room type, the design is cozy and residential, combining intricately woven textiles and handcrafted details drawn from Santa Barbara’s Mission history and Chumash heritage. Many rooms face towards the pool or garden courtyards, but the best accommodations have dramatic cliffside ocean views. Families and small groups will want to consider booking a one-bedroom suite, which features a sectional sleeper sofa in the living area and a second full bathroom. The main bedroom and bath include a four-poster king mahogany bed, with dual vanities and a deep soaking tub. Plush Frette robes and slippers, alongside Diptyque bath products, complete the experience.
Food and drink
Three full-service restaurants and bars, plus a Club Lounge, mean there’s no shortage of options at Bacara. Lulio is the most compelling of the bunch, serving Mediterranean-inspired cuisine that highlights local, in-season California produce. At dinner, don’t miss the vine-ripened tomatoes or BBQ lamb ribs, seasoned in Balkan spices and paired with citrus labneh and tursu pickles. The newest on-site restaurant, Marisella by chef Danny Grant of Maple Hospitality Group, delivers an upscale, Italian-meets-California menu, though service felt uneven during my visit. Pro-tip: Guests may also want to consider Club Lounge access. While Club-level rooms typically cost a few hundred dollars more per night, the upgrade includes a full day of dining at the lounge, from breakfast and light lunch to afternoon tea, hors d'oeuvres, desserts, and cordials. It’s a potentially strong value for families and small groups sharing a room.
The spa
The four-story spa spans 42,000 square feet and includes 36 individual treatment rooms, a redwood sauna, a eucalyptus steam room, and a whirlpool hot tub. It’s also home to the adults-only pool framed by black-and-white striped umbrellas. And a top-floor rooftop provides sweeping views of the surrounding landscape and the Pacific.
The neighborhood/area
Located on an idyllic and quiet stretch of California coastline often referred to as the “American Riviera,” the Ritz-Carlton Bacara sits about 12 miles north of downtown Santa Barbara. Just next door, though not affiliated with the resort, is the ocean-facing Sandpiper Golf Club. Nature is abundant at your doorstep, and Santa Barbara’s shops, restaurants, and broader scene are roughly a 20-minute drive away.
For families
Beyond its two family-friendly pools, nature-focused programming is a standout at Bacara. Travelers of all ages can explore nearby Haskell’s Beach with a resident naturalist, join guided eco-hikes through the Gaviota Coast foothills, or take part in birding and butterfly bike-and-hike adventures. Parents of younger kids, particularly those ages 5 to 12, are well covered thanks to the Ritz Kids Club, which offers a rotating lineup of activities, including cooking classes, scavenger hunts, and arts and crafts.
Accessibility
A range of accessible accommodations is available, including rooms with roll-in showers and tubs equipped with grab bars. Hearing accessible rooms have visual alarms, strobe lights in both the bedroom and bathroom, and portable hearing kits are available upon request. While the property is set on a hill, guests with disabilities can make use of the resort’s on-demand jitney cart service to move around the property.
Anything left to mention?
The hotel’s complimentary electric bike amenity is a perfect excuse to cruise over to the nearby Ellwood Mesa Butterfly Preserve with views of the Pacific. Also, if you’re into racquet sports, there’s a world-class Cliff Drysdale tennis facility on-site. Guests even get a 30-minute group tennis clinic with a pro, as part of the resort fee.
