French interior designer Jacques Garcia has transformed the Hotel des Indes, irrevocably associated with the Dutch royal family since it opened in 1881 and one of the grandest old hotels in Europe, from frumpy to fabulous with a shrewd and sexy makeover which makes the old gal hip without compromising the over-the-top Old World atmosphere that’s always been the ballast of its considerable charm. Garcia’s ubiquity may make him a little déjà vu in France, but his Dutch project is a success that goes beyond just a shake n’bake freshening up. Think a brilliant, bone-deep renovation that succeeds with major improvements—a superb new health club with indoor pool, and hundreds of sassy little details—the painted motif of tulips in the halls and the faux wooden tulip vase on a shelf next to the door to each room that bears the room number, heated slate floors and solid marble sinks in the bathrooms, the perfect lighting in all of the wonderfully high-ceilinged rooms, and one of the most opulent if intimate lobby-restaurants in the world—a huge bronze chandelier dominates the circular cutaway between the lounge below and mezzanine meeting rooms, the lighting is low through fringed Bordeaux silk shades, and all steps are muffled by thick Brussels carpets. Upstairs, solid old-fashioned good taste holds sway; rooms are done with mahogany-finished armoires and desks that reference miscellaneous colonial styles and color schemes are dusky and Victorian—camel, mauve, olive, and taupe. Run by Paris chef Michel Rostang, the restaurant is excellent, doing great dishes like Sea Bass with tiny Dutch shrimp, and there’s a nice list of wines by the glass. Afternoon tea pulls the local crème de la crème (diplomats and royals) to eat la crème de la crème, or cream-filled pastries and delicious little sandwiches. Now that it has this charming hotel, along with a flock of superb restaurants just around the corner (try Maxime’s), and one of the best small museums in Europe, the Mauritshuis with its Rembrandts and Vermeers, the Hague, always overshadowed by Amsterdam, is coming on strong as the new must-do destination in Holland.
