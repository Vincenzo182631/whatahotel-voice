Mondrian Mexico City Condesa
A property that somehow manages to capture the metropolis’ thriving art scene, historical past, and modern cosmopolitan vibes.

Why book?
An indefatigable energy exists at the Mondrian Mexico City Condesa property that somehow manages to capture the metropolis’ thriving art scene, historical past, and modern cosmopolitan vibes all within its walls.
Set the scene
You wouldn’t even know you were resting your head in the bustling capital of a huge North American state with nearly 22 million residents from your vantage point at the Mondrian
Mexico City
Condesa. The 183-room hotel is located in what some say is the most charming neighborhood in Mexico City, La Condesa, known for its leafy, tree-lined streets, nods to Art Deco architecture, wide sidewalks that encourage leisurely walking, and a slate of public parks and playgrounds where you’ll see millennials with leashed dogs, artsy ex-pats, and young families. Built within a mixed-used edifice constructed around the midcentury, the Mondrian opens with a reception area that is a prelude to the entire property’s aesthetic: chic, metallic finishes and plush velvet seating, and playful wholesomeness, thanks to two swinging benches suspended by heavy rope.
The backstory
It’s only fitting that parent company Ennismore finally lands in Mexico City with its arts-driven, innovative brand Mondrian. Despite lavish labels like SLS and Delano in its portfolio, Ennismore decided Mondrian would be the first of its hospitality branches to venture to Mexico City–home of cultural icons like Frida Kahlo and Luis Barragán known for their progressive thinking–when it opened in December 2022. It’s all part of the company’s bigger initiative of reaching more markets: When Ennismore merged with Accor in 2020, it became one of the world’s fastest growing lifestyle hospitality organizations. Company founder Sharan Pasricha’s hospitality ambitions have even been recognized by British sovereignty with his award as MBE in 2022.
The rooms
When you spot the huge, multicolored Ara Starck mural that greets every guest in each room, you know you've arrived at the Mondrian Mexico City Condesa. In truth, it’s hard to miss, and upon deeper inspection of the French-born artist’s work, guests begin to see how the wall-length piece featuring aqueducts painted in bold primary and secondary colors is a genuflection at the history of Mexico City and this neighborhood, which is home to one of the city’s remaining aqueducts built by the Aztec and Spanish colonists. Rooms are on the smaller side with soft goods and furnishings that display restrained design in an effort to not steal the thunder of the Starck mural. Bleach-white linens contrast against ash brown wood furnishings, and a hipster-approved Marshall bluetooth speaker with vintage feels adorns every room. Bold colors pop out in various fixtures, including a fire-engine red bedside lamp. With only nine floors, the hotel gives appropriate views of the La Condesa neighborhood that is neither flashy or humble, but a broad understanding that this residential area is best enjoyed by those who appreciate the same foundational pillar of understated elegance.
Food and drink
The hotel is home to two dining concepts, The Flower Shop, an all-in-one floral boutique and gift shop that also serves pastries, coffees, and wine with counter service; and the tapas-styled La Terraza, located on the ground floor courtyard.
The neighborhood/area
The hotel’s close proximity to a huge concentration of well-frequented, independently run restaurants and stores in the La Condesa and the adjacent Roma neighborhoods make it an easy sell to city travelers who like their adventures full of easy urban conveniences. Walk, run or bike around the area – either way, it’s all navigable. If you do find yourself meandering the neighborhood, restaurants such as MeroToro (considered one of
Latin America’s 50 Best Restaurants
) are not too far away.
Whereas the designer-laden area of the nearby Polanco district might be considered the Upper East Side of Mexico City, La Condesa is akin to Williamsburg, well, without the brownstones and skinny jeans. The sprawling urban park Parque México is a few blocks away, and offers a glimpse of landscape architecture full of ponds, covered benches, fountains, and even a skate park where youths go to ollie into a therapeutic mindset.
The service
Mexico is a land resplendent in service-oriented, hospitable people, and the Mondrian somehow picked the best of them. The multilingual front desk and concierge teams are as genuine as they come with easy smiles and gentle “Buenos días.” With so many well-recognized restaurants within walking distance of the hotel, the concierge team is quick to make recommendations, including some highly touted taco spots that are a few blocks away.
For families
Mexico City itself is an extremely family-friendly destination, so it makes sense that the Mondrian extends that same feel within its walls. While the property isn’t kid-focused or kid-centric, it’s pretty kid-friendly for such a swanky spot. I stayed with my two kids (6 and 4 years old), and they received some fun amenities, like a complimentary piñata filled with local candies and treats, and in-room cookie decorating kits. The hotel even had a bottle of tear-free shampoo waiting for us in the kids’ bathroom.
Accessibility
I found the rooms and common areas to be extremely accessible. I traveled with two kids who both insisted on being carted around in their respective strollers, and the main lobby met accessibility standards with its ramp and sensored sliding double-door entrance (however, there is a separate door from the street to the reception desk that has one step; easily avoidable if you move an additional 20 feet to the double-door entrance). Plus, at every entrance, including elevators, there was a staff member positioned to open the doors for guests.
Anything left to mention?
For good or bad, at times I forgot that I was physically in another country during my stay at the Mondrian: Everything is so easy and convenient. There are staff members whose English made me want to ditch the grade-school Spanish that I had been relying on for a couple of decades (and been polishing for weeks with Duolingo). And perhaps that’s what today’s Mexico City is all about: a global destination that recognizes that, no matter where we come from, we can just co-exist over tacos and mezcal.
