Why book?
Quirky maximalist meets contemporary design touches and a primo Polanco location make this early-aughts hotel worth checking in to for a fun night or two in CDMX.
Set the scene
If
A Night at the Roxbury
were a hotel, W Mexico City would be it. Like the cult late-’90s classic, there’s a silliness and self-aware nostalgia baked into the stay here, which is especially refreshing in an age when so many hotels look and feel like they’ve been carbon copied from one to the next.
True to its early-aughts roots, the W doesn’t take itself too seriously. The décor is unapologetically theatrical. Think: Dimly lit public spaces infused with bright pops of color, neon backlighting, and abstract, conversation-starting furniture shaped as oversized busts repurposed as lobby seating. The Living Room bar has an undeniable energy and reinforces the hotel’s identity as a lifestyle hotel. For some travelers, this mix can feel a little chaotic, and it’s anything but one-size-fits-all. That is very much on brand for the W. This isn’t a hotel trying to please everyone. While the crowd skews young, the hotel also attracts business travelers craving a post-conference buzz. It’s luxury, but with a wink. And maybe a head bop to
What Is Love
before a night at the club, too.
The backstory
The W Mexico City made its CDMX debut in 2003 and was the first W Hotel in Latin America and one of the earliest W’s to open outside of the U.S. The opening coincides with the peak of W hotel openings worldwide at the time. Over the years, the brand's cachet has ebbed and flowed. In 2016, the W Mexico City renovated its guestrooms, meeting spaces, and added the restaurant J by Jose Andres, which has since closed and been replaced by the restaurant 25 Dos. Today, this Marriott-owned property continues to maintain a loyal following of brand- and point-savvy Marriott Bonvoy travelers eager to collect or cash in on points. You’ll also find groups of friends and bachelor and bachelorette parties looking for a party spot in a great location in the heart of Polanco.
The rooms
Housed in a 25-story high-rise, W Mexico City has 237 guest rooms and suites. Accommodations range from deluxe, superior, and premier to urban, and two penthouses, the latter of which are a statement stay that seem better suited for entertaining than sleeping. Every room features fun and quirky touches, from an oversized mural by Mexican artist Ivan Aguirre to an infinity mirror installation over the bed filled with traditional Mexican crafts called alebrijes and miniature toys.
Rooms lean into the brand’s signature high-energy aesthetic while remaining extremely functional. Expect smart, mood-setting lighting within easy reach of the bed, neon accents that frame the W’s famously comfortable bed, a tech-forward setup that feels intuitive, and lightning-fast Wi-Fi. Bathrooms offer floor-to-ceiling black-and-white tile and benefit from natural light, adding an unexpected touch of polish to the otherwise playful design. Insider tip: opt for a corner ‘Urban’ room on a higher floor for the most dramatic views over Polanco and beyond.
Food and drink
25 Dos serves as the property's breakfast and lunch restaurant and offers a mix of traditional and modern Mexican fare. Don’t miss the chilaquiles. Trivvu is a trendy brunch and cocktail terrace that’s popular amongst locals on the weekends and serves a regular Sunday brunch called Drunch. Living Room Bar is the heart and soul of the hotel and the spot for pre- and post-night cocktails.
The spa
On the 4th floor of the hotel, the AWAY Spa offers a guided
temazcal
, an ancient pre-Hispanic ritual that’s considered both a blessing and a form of detachment from negative energy. It’s an experience not for the faint of heart, and this igloo-shaped house of heat is a unique amenity in an urban hotel setting. The spa also offers a small heated whirlpool, sauna, and a full-service gym with Technogym equipment.
The neighborhood/area
Perhaps the biggest draw of all is the hotel's location. It’s very conveniently located next to Lincoln Park in Polanco. Within easy walking distance to Avenida Presidente Masaryk, which is known for its high-end boutiques as well as two of the city’s two Michelin-starred restaurants, including Enrique Olvera’s Pujol and Chef Jorge Vallejo and Alejandra Flores Quintonil, it’s one of Mexico City’s nicest areas to stay and stroll.
The service
The service is friendly, but checking in can be slow depending on the time of day. The hotel seems to prioritize travelers with Marriott Bonvoy status. Even if you don’t have brand loyalty, it’s always worth asking for an upgrade, as they seem willing to accommodate guests, subject to availability.
For families
Many rooms can be connected, making this a decent option for families. The convenient location in Polanco also makes this attractive to families, though some guests might not appreciate the noise and club-like atmosphere in the common spaces and restaurants, especially on weekends.
Accessibility
ADA-accessible rooms with roll-in showers are available here.
Anything left to mention?
While this is technically considered a five-star hotel, you might notice some wear and tear and service issues that make that rating feel a bit unjustified. We wouldn’t be surprised if there’s another renovation planned in the near future.
