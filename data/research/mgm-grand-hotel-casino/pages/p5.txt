MGM Grand Hotel & Casino
Massive suites, a huge entertainment lineup, ever-refreshing dining options, and newly renovated rooms and suites keep this historic megaresort feeling current.

Reviewed by
Andrea Bennett
Best for:
Groups, first-timers, convention-goers, and anyone who wants a big pool scene and lots of dining and nightlife without ever leaving the property.
Location:
South Strip, across from New York–New York with easy access to T-Mobile Arena and a stop on the
Las Vegas
Monorail.
Can’t-miss feature:
The range of room products—from approachable, fully refreshed main-tower rooms to Skylofts’ two-story suites with butlers and airport transfers—plus the lazy-river pool complex.
In a nutshell:
A classic, very large Vegas resort that now has the updated room product to back up its restaurants, shows, pool decks, and clubs.
Rooms
Skylofts is the fantasy: 51 two-level lofts on floors 29 and 30, reached via private elevator and Sky Lobby, with double-height Strip views, residential living and dining spaces, and 24-hour butler service. Airport transfers (often in a Rolls-Royce Ghost), pre-stocked pantries, and in-loft dining make them feel like sky apartments more than suites. The main tower’s 3,969 rooms and suites finished a $300 million Gensler-led renovation in November 2025, adding 111 new suites and bringing the suite total to more than 750; expect warm walnut and champagne tones, graphic carpets, big smart TVs, better storage, and brighter baths with walk-in showers. Studio Tower’s 700 rooms, redone in 2022, feel like midcentury desert glam with sun-faded hues and compact but smart layouts close to the Strip entrance.
Food and drink
You could spend days eating here. Joël Robuchon still anchors the fine-dining end with elaborate tasting menus, flanked by Morimoto Las Vegas, Tom Colicchio’s Craftsteak, and other chef-branded rooms that cover everything from wagyu to robata and French-inflected tasting menus. Casual options run from the Grand Buffet to sports bars and grab-and-go, and Netflix Bites adds a newer, pop-culture angle with dishes pulled from the streamer’s food and travel shows. Skylofts guests can have butlers orchestrate in-loft meals drawing from several of these restaurants; everyone else can lean on an extensive room-service menu when the idea of crossing the casino again is a bridge too far.
Amenities and experiences
The five-acre pool complex, complete with lazy river, is one of MGM Grand’s major selling points, especially for groups who want cabanas, DJs, and plenty of space to spread out. Wet Republic layers on a dedicated daylife scene with big-name DJs and a higher-energy crowd. Inside, there is a big, busy casino; multiple theaters that host everything from long-running productions to rotating headliners; and Hakkasan Nightclub, which remains one of the city’s marquee multi-level clubs. Stay Well rooms, a sizable fitness center, spa options, and convention space round out the “small city” feel.
What to do nearby
From MGM Grand you are well placed for the southern Strip. Pedestrian bridges put you within easy walking distance of New York–New York, Excalibur, and the T-Mobile Arena corridor, so sports and concert nights are especially convenient. The attached monorail stop gives you a relatively stress-free way to bounce to mid-Strip, the Convention Center, or the north end without gambling on rideshare traffic. If you want a quieter change of scene, The Signature at MGM Grand sits just behind the main resort with condo-style suites and is connected by interior walkways.
The backstory
When MGM Grand opened in 1993, it leaned hard into a Wizard of Oz theme and family-friendly positioning, all wrapped in emerald glass and fronted by a gigantic lion. Over the decades it has steadily shifted toward a more adult, entertainment- and dining-driven identity, adding Skylofts, Stay Well floors, and major nightlife to compete with newer properties. The 2022 Studio Tower refresh and 2025 completion of the $300 million main-tower room and suite renovation are the latest milestones in that evolution.
Insider tip
If you want the newest hardware without the loft price tag, specifically book one of the renovated main-tower or Studio Tower rooms—look for language calling out “renovated” or “Studio Tower” when you reserve. For a blowout trip, check Skylofts midweek or during off-peak periods, when rates can drop well below headline numbers, and use the butler team to line up in-loft dinners so you can enjoy the resort’s restaurant lineup without leaving your view.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes—especially in the main and Studio towers, with the lazy river, casual dining, and proximity to arena events, though nightlife is ever-present.
Accessibility:
Strong; there are multiple accessible room types plus ramps, elevators, and monorail access across the spread-out campus.
Wellness:
Better than you would expect from a party-forward resort, thanks to Stay Well rooms, a large fitness center, spa services, and all that pool time.
Loyalty group:
Part of MGM Rewards, useful if you plan to earn and redeem across other MGM Resorts properties.
