Il San Corrado di Noto
Once the private residence of a prince, now a refined getaway for well-heeled, food-obsessed travelers.

Reviewed by
Rosalyn Wikeley
Why book
For extraordinary food and languorous afternoons of Etna rosé with a hot, citrus-infused breeze.
Set the scene
Smart green gates suggesting a noble abode edge open and the car rasps up a track. To its left: Val di Noto views of ancient olive and citrus groves interrupted only by a neat tennis court. At its peak, a contemporary spin on a princely residence crowns the hill. A step inside is a Narnia-style step into a different realm, one carved with contemporary flair from Modican stone. Shoulders lower to a courtyard fountain’s trickle, the adagio clack of Italian shoes that have paced these archways for centuries, and the warm greeting of
Il San Corrado’s
adopted canine mascots.
As seasoned travelers, Corrado’s owners’ affinity for
Aman hotels
reveals itself amid the pools sliced from muted stone in cubic chunks, the orderly rows of banana leaves and palms, and spaces and rooms resplendent in their restraint. Rather than detract from the masseria’s noble history, the sharp design is a masterclass in reconciliation—where ancient, near-sacred structures are gently eased into modern relevancy by architect Corrado Papa. Or, in the case of the family’s private 19th-century chapel, are left untouched and congruous with new additions. Archways, lined with Noto artist Sergio Fiornentino’s abstract spins on classicism, slice the pure,
Sicilian
daylight into celestial stepping stones, while an orchid-clad library showcasing Helmut Newton prints is a smooth, shady refuge from the midday sun.
Sun loungers and rooms line two enormous pools, one so long it errs on illusion, the other, a vast inviting oasis dotted with Champagne buckets and
Balinese
teak furniture. The clean, symmetrical choreography is interrupted only by the odd flush of green or a bronzed Italian rounding off a siesta with an abrupt dip in the pool. Time hits differently at Il San Corrado, amid the olive groves and light-filled courtyards. The day seems to move in these vignettes, building a dreamlike montage. And before you know it, it’s time to spruce up for supper.
The backstory
This ivory stone beauty was once the private residence of Prince Nicolac, who earned his noble stripes from gallant acts on the battlefield. Suffering the millstone fate of most aristocrats, he reluctantly sold off chunks of the estate, as did his successors, to keep it afloat. In 2015, the remaining 99 acres of the masseria were bought by a vending machine tycoon from
Northern Italy
. He embarked on an assiduous three-year renovation with Corrado Papa, only to fling open the doors before the world went into lockdown. Subsequently, it’s still bathing in that fresh curiosity and buzz of a new-to-the-scene hotel, despite being open for a few years.
The rooms
26
suites
and eight pool villas tow the stylishly subdued line of the communal spaces—all chipped and carved by local artisans from Modican stone and Guatemalan marble. Some overlook the main pool (the deluxe suites), while the premium suites, with cavernous ceilings of the original Sicilian masseria, spill out onto the long pool’s terrace. All are dominated by preposterously comfortable four-poster beds, draped in mosquito netting and positioned for theatrical views of a pink-streaked Sicilian sky. A leopard-print cushion strikes an unexpectedly kitsch note amid the consistent sandy hues of the walk-in-wardrobe, the open-plan bathroom and terraces at either end of the room. And aside from the barista-grade coffee machine and silver tray of traditional Sicilian pastries, the smooth marble bath, placed in the room like a contemporary sculpture, is a standout.
Scattered amongst the olive groves in cubic form, the pool villas mirror the main hotel suites in all design matters, just with pools and an immense level of privacy. Golf carts can be summoned at all hours, though refreshingly, the walk to the main hotel is not too laborious…pleasant even following one or two brioche con gelato.
Food and drink
In polished Principe di Belludia, a window peels back an orderly kitchen where chefs cook plates of red mallet on a bed of tomatoes and duck fusilli sprinkled in truffles with alchemic precision. The amuse bouche is pure foodie rapture—divine morsels of slow-cooked sea bass, beef carpaccio and foie gras, and paper-thin courgette and ink crisps. Impeccably dressed Europeans drench homemade bread (baked from ancient wheat) in rich Chiaramonte olive oil while perusing the extensive wine menu—Sicilian Tasca d’Almerita sparkling wine followed by a Planeta from Etna, perhaps. Breakfast shares this refined spot, though it tones down the formalities, with cold cuts, Sicilian cheeses, fruit, and traditional sweet pastries spread along the wine cellar. Pace yourself. A menu of Colazione Siciliana con brioche (iced granita with brioche), French toast with Etna berries, and vegetable caponata with Capitelli ham soon follow. The wellness brigade will be relieved to discover turmeric and ginger mocktails and egg white omelets amid the sweet granita and almond cake.
CasaPasta is a more relaxed, poolside affair—if “express” lobsters and oysters dispensed as readily as espressos qualify as relaxed. Chefs dance around an enormous pizza oven and outdoor grill lined with lamb chops and sizzling rib eyes. The objective here is to set a familial tone, where seafood platters, sausage tagliatelle, and barbecued squid are whipped up on-site and passed around the table. I’d love to know the family that eases into supper with a mandolin player and tender veal amuse bouche.
The spa
Carved from travertine marble and teak woods, the spa is a cozy refuge with a sauna, steam room, and (for a courageous few) a cold-water bucket shower. Skilled, nimble fingers work chemical-free Sodashi products on weary muscles during full body massages and facials. Rather unusual for an Italian hotel, the Technogym-adorned fitness center is no subterranean afterthought, but enjoys a privileged spot overlooking the vast main pool.
The area
Only 20 minutes from Noto—the “cradle of baroque” that rarely fails to seduce visitors with its honey-hued limestone architecture, an abundance of opulent churches, and imposing Roman Catholic cathedral—Il San Corrado is well-placed to explore the faded, blistered, and bonkers splendor of the baroque South East. A complimentary shuttle runs to both Noto and the hotel’s private beach club—a groomed patch of blonde sand with a polished, white-washed spin on the humble beach shack. And while the surrounding
UNESCO World Heritage Site
, Val di Noto, with its rich, varied cultural tapestry dating back to 664 BC, is fascinating to explore, Il San Corrado’s rural location allows guests to explore the region at their own pace.
The service
Wine glasses are flipped onto tables with the shock factor of a magician, requests are seen to as briskly as towels are whipped on sun loungers, and no stint in the library is without the offer of a cocktail or sparkling water. You get the picture.
For families
The villas afford families the privacy they crave while clearly separating the child-friendly areas from the more adult spots. While the hotel has curated a raft of activities for broods, Il San Corrado is geared towards the grown-ups, particularly in the communal spaces.
Eco effort
While the now-expected eco-trappings are present—the bamboo slippers and toothbrushes, the recycled water bottles—a three-year renovation essentially facilitated a sustainable rewiring of the entire hotel. Solar panels have been artfully fitted (you’d never notice them), with their own energy storage systems and the hotel uses its own well and water recycling system to water the gardens.
Accessibility
There are no stairs in this hotel, and while the stone labyrinth is good for wheelchairs, the surrounding paths are uneven.
Anything else to mention?
Il San Corrado organizes excursions anchored in the region’s traditions and rhythms, such as exploring hidden villages in the Hyblaean Mountains with a local guide or truffle hunting. Back at the hotel, outdoor cinema evenings and yoga classes abound.
