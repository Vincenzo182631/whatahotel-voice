Reviewed by
Lee Cobaj
Why book?
A bold hotel that channels Tsim Sha Tsui’s gritty cool, Mondrian Hong Kong blends an art-driven aesthetic with knockout harbor views and a healthy social pulse. It’s a design hotel that actually feels plugged into its neighborhood—energetic, expressive, and a little edgy.
Set the scene
Arriving here feels like slipping from one world into another. Outside: the hum of scooters, the blink of neon, the classic red taxi chorus of Hong Kongi. Inside: a glassy lobby opens with a two-story-high mosaic of underwater dancers, while an orbital chandelier casts ribbons of light across the room. The guests match the mood—design-forward, globally minded, a mix of streetwear and legacy couture—lending the space a sense of relaxed swagger.
The backstory
Opened in March 2024, Mondrian Hong Kong is the brand’s first outpost in Greater China. While the name nods to the Dutch painter, the hospitality group traces its roots to Los Angeles, where its West Hollywood hotel helped define the brand’s penchant for avant-garde interiors, arts, and culture. In Hong Kong, that DNA plays out inside a tall and skinny tower with triangular windows that previously housed the long-running Panorama Hotel.
Amsterdam-based designer Karin Krautgartner and her studio, byKK, completely transformed the previously run-of-the-mill decor with unapologetically bold and saturated hues, retro-futuristic furniture, a refreshingly riveting art collection, and clever room layouts designed to maximize the Victoria Harbour views.
The rooms
About 70% of the 324 rooms and suites face Victoria Harbour, with the rest gazing over Kowloon’s wonderfully tangled streets. Request a high floor for the best views—the upper floors lift you above the rooftops and away from neighboring towers.
Rooms start at a compact 226 square feet, but thoughtful design makes them feel larger: lots of natural light, bold hits of crimson and mustard, custom graphic rugs, washi-paper lamps, and playful details that give each space real personality. For extra space, book one of the 12 Harbour Suites. These 527-square-foot retreats come with 180-degree views, a separate lounge, a second bathroom, and a deep-soaking tub.
Food and drink
The Mondrian’s dramatic sense of style continues in its dining spaces. On the 39th floor, Carna by Dario Cecchini—led by the poetry-reciting, bear-hug-giving Tuscan butcher who starred on
Chef’s Table
—unfolds like a glam Roman palazzo, accented by a glowing dome and fresco-like stained glass artwork. Designed by local star Joyce Wang, the space is fully immersive, branching into two arms—one with a bar and romantic two-tops by the windows, the other with curved columns, ornate booths, and a gorgeous private dining room. The menu is all about nose-to-tail cooking, including highlights like rare cuts grilled over charcoal, hand-chopped tartare mixed tableside, and beautifully cooked fish paired with Italian wines and postcard-worthy harbor views.
One floor down, Avoca—a lounge and workspace by day, cocktail bar by night—channels a totally different mood with its stark white walls, velvet banquettes dressed in jewel tones, rounded chairs, and wrap-around vistas. The cocktails riff on Hong Kong flavors—try the Lap Yuk Old Fashioned with its Chinese bacon-washed bourbon—and as the night goes on, DJs turn the space into a buzzy, high-rise party.
The spa
There’s no on-site spa or pool—something to note if you’re visiting during the height of summer. Mondrian’s more about cultural events, ranging from DJ sets to art installations and experimental performances in the rooftop garden.
The neighborhood/area
This is Tsim Sha Tsui at its most eclectic: in the immediate vicinity, it feels like a throwback to 1980s Hong Kong with hole-in-the-wall tailors and street food vendors, eyewear stores, foot massage parlors, and dive bars, plus decidedly modern additions like ramen joints and bubble tea cafes. A short walk south brings you to the harborfront promenade, where you'll find the K11 Museum shopping-slash-art complex, plus major landmarks like the Hong Kong Museum of Art, Avenue of the Stars, and the Star Ferry to Central. Just a few steps from the hotel is The Corner Shop—Mondrian’s quirky sliver of an art gallery—that’s usually hosting a pop-up exhibition by a local artist.
The service
The staff is young, international, and genuinely enthusiastic, quick to point you toward under-the-radar boutiques or their favorite cha chaan teng. And their uniforms—if you can even call them that—fit right into the hotel’s stylish vibes. Designed by Hongkonger Frances Yim, of Three Pound Void, the ensembles channel chic urban streetwear inspired by the city’s industrial craftsmanship.
For families / Best for
Though design lovers and creative types would enjoy a stay here with their kids, families are not the target audience. With its high-end dining, cultural programming, and relatively snug rooms, the Mondrian seems most suitable for young creatives, couples, and solo travelers who want to be immersed in Kowloon’s gritty energy with the bonus of easy access to an MTR station, the waterfront, and some of the city’s best shopping.
Eco effort
Sustainability is threaded throughout: In-room water purifiers replace bottled water, smart lighting and blinds cut energy use, and paperless check-in and digital newspapers reduce waste. You’ll also find in-room recycling, a strong focus on supporting local creatives, and notable F&B initiatives, from Carna’s nose-to-tail menu to Avoca’s zero-waste cocktails.
Accessibility
The hotel is fully accessible, with elevators to every floor and select rooms fitted with adapted bathrooms and wheelchair-friendly layouts.
Anything left to mention?
We love the little details: a mirrored pedestal serves as the mini bar, a lacquered Chinese cabinet holds snacks and amenities, in-room artwork echoes Hong Kong’s landscapes, films, and craft heritage, and whimsical underwater murals course through the corridors. All in all, it's high design grounded by a playful joie de vivre that makes perfect sense for Hong Kong.
