Belmond Reid's Palace
A distinctively salmon pink hotel that has been luring smart travelers for over 125 years

Reviewed by

Set the scene
Step inside the pink palace (you can spot the salmony pastel shade every so often on the 20-minute drive from Funchal airport), and a fleet of friendly staff are there to welcome you. On the craggy cliffs above the Atlantic Ocean, an air of history is woven into the fabric of the hotel. Interiors see its British heritage splashed throughout—soft whites and blues and chunky marble mixed with Portuguese tiles and vintage photographs of a cigar-smoking Winston Churchill tapping away on his typewriter (he spent many months here before and after his second stint as PM). Find newlyweds, young
families
, and long-time Belmond fans out on the terrace amidst a subtropical breeze—Madeira is closer to Africa than mainland Portugal—with a fruity glass of
Poncha
.
The backstory
Picture this: guests who arrive by boat are carried up the cliff in hammocks by strapping porters. This was the dream of young Scotsman and hotel founder, William Reid. Since then, the hotel has been luring in a smart set of travelers (back in the day, it was
the
place for European royalty) for over 125 years. Passing the reins to his sons, and with the help of the Madeira-winemaking Blandy family, they turned the hotel into its legendary status today. Belmond took over in 1996, giving the palace a fresh makeover while keeping the essence and charm of the place, attracting their global cult following to their island hospitality.
The rooms
The majority of rooms have kept the shape and form of the original building, but even the smallest room can easily fit a couple and an overly packed suitcase. Nab a sparkly sea-facing room if you can; they come with balconies, so the sounds of the sea are your wake-up call. More charmingly comfortable than state-of-the-art, rooms are decked out in soft creams, clean whites, and powder blues. Beds are extra-large with crisp linens, while bathrooms are equipped with tubs and showers and delicious soaps from Portuguese brand Claus Porto.
Food and drink
Spoiled for choice—from relaxed poolside munching or terrace top Italian feasting to Michelin-starred dining. Fill up at breakfast—a daily changing detox juice and the most delicious spread of subtropical fruits from the island, plus all the pasteis de nata you can eat. Overstuffed club sandwiches or Caesar salads can be brought down to the ocean-level saltwater pool (via the iconic cliff-hugging lift; be wary of the steep steps carved into the rock), but save room for supper. Villa Cipriani has some of the best coastal views over Reid’s, plus they dish out Italian plates—prosciutto and melon, seafood linguine, beef tagliata—that feel straight out of
Venice
. The Michelin-starred William Restaurant keeps things formal yet simple with modern takes using seasonal local ingredients. Classic afternoon tea is held on the black-and-white checkered terrace—secure a spot in advance with the front desk as it’s normally fully booked with outside guests.
The spa
The ocean is your
spa
(we suggest staking out a bed down by the ocean first thing in the morning, and there’s a ladder and a diving board that lead right into the water), but for a bit more R&R book in a Reid’s Palace regional treatment that uses the best of local ingredients—aloe vera balsam and grapeseed oil combined with the aromas of flora from the garden and scents from the sea. There’s also a quaint but fully stocked gym that overlooks Funchal harbor and the ocean below.
The area
Funchal’s old town is about a 10-minute taxi ride away. If you can manage to pull yourself away, Casal da Penha dishes out the best garlic shrimp on the south side of the island while Gavião Novo catches the freshest fish of the day. For more exploring, speak to the concierge and have them help plan a sunrise trip up Pico do Arieiro, Madeira's third-highest peak; a boat ride to spot dolphins and whales, or a hike along one of the island's many
levadas
. At the north of the island, there are lava rock pools worth soothing all aches and pains in.
The service
Belmond knows a thing or two about pampering. Staff are friendly and local and go above and beyond to please.
Eco effort
The hotel works to use as many local ingredients from the island at their restaurants. Plus, the hotel promotes wild and natural subtropical growth in its gardens (book a garden tour to learn all about their tree and flower collection).
Accessibility for those with mobility impairments
There is one accessible room and a few ramps throughout the hotel, but do note that some elevators are too small for wheelchair access.
