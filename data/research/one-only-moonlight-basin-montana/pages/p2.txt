One&Only Moonlight Basin: First Guest
One&Only's United States debut rings in a new era for the ski resort.

Why book?
For the next generation of the modern ski resort, where alpine adventure can be enjoyed year-round.

Set the scene
Big Sky
isn’t Aspen or Vail, and it’s not trying to be. Often, with ski vacations, it feels like there are only two options: the underwhelming rustic or the ostentatious and over-the top, with very little in between. One&Only Moonlight Basin manages to find that middle ground, offering an all around premium experience without the air of exclusivity; instead, the atmosphere is one of quiet luxury. My first impression of the hotel, which I remarked on immediately upon arrival, was that James Bond—and his closet of Bogner snowsuits—would feel right at home here. Come cocktail hour, expect parka-donning guests straight off the slopes enjoying apré-ski cocktails by the fire, as well as a stylish set wrapped in silk and cashmere, perhaps recovering from a long day at the spa. Whether you opt to wear your ski bib and boots or a fur coat and heels, you won’t be out of place, and there’s always a choice of casual and elevated dining options at your fingertips.
The backstory
This is One&Only’s first US hotel and the brand’s first alpine resort. The Dubai-based brand currently operates 14 ultra-luxury resorts across Europe, Africa, Asia, and North America, with more on the way (including another US location in New York’s Hudson Valley, slated to open in 2028). Not one resort is the same, but what underpins them all is a focus on exceptional service, represented by the signature One&Only hand-on-heart greeting that guests receive regardless of where in the world they are staying. The Montana opening not only represents the brand’s debut in the American market, but into the outdoor adventure space as well—which requires a very specialized type of hospitality expertise. Thanks to the property’s kick-ass recreation team led by local outdoorsman extraordinaire Matt Jackson, you’d think One&Only has been in the luxury alpine game for a very, very long time.
The private cabins each have an outdoor deck with a private fire pit and hot tub.
Courtesy One&Only
The rooms
The resort, designed by architect Olson Kundig, spans three main guest buildings with 73 rooms and suites, in addition to 19 freestanding cabins and private homes. The entire hotel feels very residential: Even the smallest of the rooms, which start at 721 square feet, feature a fireplace and private balcony, and bathrooms with soaking tubs and separate showers. I stayed in one of the dozen or so standalone cabins (including studio, one-bedroom, and two-bedroom options), ideal for couples, friends, or families looking for a little more seclusion and immersion in nature. They offer the best of both worlds—you get the privacy and space of a rental home in addition to seamless access to all the resort's various amenities, as well as a personal host. Each morning, I was able to enjoy a quiet cup of coffee by the fire pit on my cabin's back deck and each evening, I decompressed from the day's activities in the private hot tub. Not too shabby.
Food and drink
There are three main restaurants and two bars (which also serve small bites). Starting off with dining, you’ve got The Landing at the Sky Lodge, Wildwood in the main lodge, and Akira Back. The hotels’ best dishes are served at the latter, led by the eponymous Michelin-starred Chef Akira Back, a celebrity culinary disruptor and ex-professional snowboarder known for his creative takes on Japanese-Korean fusion. The menu, ranging from prime wagyu and dry-aged beef to fresh seafood and sushi, is exactly the type of food I want after a long day of skiing, and is a welcome departure from American ski resorts' obsession with grease and carbs. However, sometimes you really just need a beer and a burger, and for that there is no better place to be than The Landing, whose delicious bison patty and beer boot are far and above your run-of-the-mill ski lodge cafeteria meal. Like at all One&Only resorts, an extravagant breakfast buffet is served every morning at Wildwood, where I enjoyed a daily yogurt bowl or chia pudding, with a coffee and pastry. (Wildwood also serves lunch and dinner.) The hidden gem of the f&b portfolio is Moonshack, a speakeasy-style cabin with live music and poker, a whisky bar, and a lengthy menu of 25 cigars. Back in the main lodge, Dear Josephine is a fashionable cocktail bar with moody lighting and as always, dramatic mountain views as far as the eye can see.

The spa
You can leave your jet lag and altitude sickness at the door when you step inside the hotel’s 17,000-square-foot wellness sanctuary, which includes an indoor-outdoor lap pool, heated onsen soaking tub, cold plunge, steam room, and sauna. Whether you’re recovering from a long day on the mountain or a long flight across the Atlantic, the spa offers a combination of high-tech and conventional remedies, from hot-and-cold contrast therapy to an “oxygen bar.” The spa’s second floor is home to an expansive fitness center (personal training and group fitness classes available), and there’s a tennis court and outdoor yoga deck just outside. Massage and facial services run the gamut; I got the 120-minute Cryo-Detox Ritual, which included a full-body exfoliation, warm herbal clay wrap, targeted cryotherapy, and trigger-point massage that noticeably helped relieve my lower back pain. Facials use a combination of Augustinus Bader and AUTEUR products.
The neighborhood/area
The hotel is situated on 240 secluded acres in Moonlight Basin, a 8,000-acre community stretching from Lone Peak towards the Madison River Valley. The area, which is part of the Greater Yellowstone ecosystem, links two vast sections of protected wilderness in southwestern Montana’s Rocky Mountains. This means the landscape provides critical migration routes for species like elk, mule deer, bears, moose, and bobcats—thus allowing for exceptional wildlife sightings in the spring and fall that rival Yellowstone National Park. We spotted moose several times during my stay in early November (once from my cabin’s hot tub!). When we saw another on our drive back from dinner, a staff member remarked that people wait all day at Yellowstone for experiences like that, but here they can happen naturally.
Guests have direct access to Big Sky Mountain via the One&Only gondola.
Courtesy One&Only
At 5,850-skiable acres with lift access, Big Sky is one of the largest ski resorts in North America with terrain for all skill levels. Combined with the fact that the mountain is still comparably under the radar, you’ll barely ever find yourself waiting in a lift line or weaving around crowded trails. This winter also marks the culmination of a massive 10-year development project, meaning visitors can now enjoy $150 million worth of tech upgrades and infrastructure improvements (including the world’s longest 8-seat high-speed chairlift). All's to say that if you’re checking in to take advantage of some of the best skiing in North America, you’re in the right place. That said, if you plan an off-peak visit during the warmer months or quieter shoulder seasons, you’ll likely hear a local refrain that’s common among Western mountain towns like this one:
The winter brings ya, but it’s the summer that keeps ya!
It’s a sentiment that underlies the hotel’s vast range of all-season outdoor programming—led by an impressively knowledgeable recreation team—including excursions such as fly fishing on the Madison River (about an hour drive away), golfing at the famed Jack Nicklaus course nearby, and day trips to Yellowstone National Park.
Guests have direct access to Big Sky Mountain via the One&Only Sky Lodge, the hotel’s home base for snow sports. Here, the ski concierge can prepare and store all your gear for you, arrange Salomon demo rentals, and even tune equipment with their world-class Wintersteiger Jupiter machine (the same one used by World Cup racers). This is also where The Landing restaurant is located, the place to be for aprés ski drinks and bites. From the Sky Lodge, you can take the One&Only Gondola to the Madison Base area—a heated 5-minute ride that then connects you to the rest of the mountain. If downhill isn’t your speed, you can opt to explore the 15 miles of trails (most of which are groomed daily) onsite that are open for snowshoeing, fat tire biking, and cross-country Nordic skiing.
The service
Perhaps the hotel’s most impressive perk is that every guest, no matter their room category, is paired with an on-property “host” who can do everything from whisk you between buildings on a side-by-side ATV, organize dining reservations, book activities, and more. They are always available and responsive via text or phone call, but will often appear in the flesh the very moment you realize you need them. This theme of anticipating guests’ needs also lent itself to thoughtful surprises, like a hot bath waiting for me upon returning from a chilly afternoon of fly fishing, or a fanny pack with hand warmers and Kleenex left in my cabin before an upcoming hike. From the restaurant staff to the highly-skilled outdoors recreation team, everyone here shares an impressive attention to detail that allows them to meet guests’ needs in a personalized way.
For families
Half of the hotel’s rooms were designed so they can be connected to other rooms to form a family suite. For extra-special occasions, the entire third floor of the main lodge can be booked out by large groups, including access to a private elevator, six rooms, and a so-called “party suite” specifically designed for raucous entertainment, with 360 degrees of mountain views. For guests with children, the “KidsOnly club” offers special outdoor programming for young adventurers.
Eco effort
The restaurants sources many of its ingredients from local farmers, fishers, and ranchers, including wild-caught trout from
Native Fish Keepers
, harvested by Montana's Salish and Kootenai Tribes, fresh eggs from
Straight From the Farm
, and bison meat from
North Bridger Bison
, an family-run, environmentally friendly local ranch. Much of the resort's art was also sourced from the local community (curated by
Echo Arts
in Bozeman) and pays homage to the area's rich cattle herding history.
Accessibility
Staff are always available to shuttle guests between the resort's many buildings in a fleet of off-road side-by-side vehicles. The resort also offers several wheelchair accessible guest rooms and suites.
Anything left to mention?
An absolute highlight of my stay was the time we spent at the hotel’s private observatory, where a local astronomer can lead stargazing sessions for guests upon request. Their advanced PlaneWave telescope is one of the best in the world, and even if the clouds do end up obscuring your view, the astronomer's incredible storytelling will transport you to the cosmos—come here every opportunity you get.
