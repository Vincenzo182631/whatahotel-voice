The Ballantyne, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Charlotte
If you want the trappings of a resort without leaving Charlotte, here's the best of both worlds.

In a nutshell
: Formal but fun, The Ballantyne is one of the most luxurious
properties in Charlotte
, perfect for a celebration, staycation, or family getaway.
Rooms
Nearly all of the property’s 244 rooms sit inside the main hotel; 208 of them, to be exact. Configured with a king or two queens, all of which come dressed in Frette Linens, the rooms are generous in size and designed with a bit of Hollywood Regency glamour: think tufted headboards, brass accents, and a mostly neutral palette dotted with pops of color in the form of accent chairs, art, and throw pillows. All of the rooms have a separate shower and tub, along with Molten Brown products; choose a Grand Deluxe if you want to overlook the park (a lovely view, if you ask me!).
For those looking for more space than a traditional room allows, there are six Executive Suites (one of which I stayed in), which have two balconies and two bathrooms each; two Junior Suites, which are almost as large but with one bath and one balcony; and two Presidential Suites, which are primed for longer stays or hosting, thanks to the fully-stocked kitchens. Outside the main property, there is a 35-room Offsite Lodge (these rooms have more of a rustic feel) and a four-bedroom, 4.5-bathroom
cottage
for larger groups.
It’s also worth noting the hotel has four keys with
Green Key Global
, the second-highest possible rating within the sustainability certification.

Food and drink
There are plenty of dining options both on and just off property. Gallery Restaurant serves as the main on-site venue, open for breakfast, lunch, weekend brunch, and dinner, with a dinner service focused on (but not limited to) steak. I was delighted by the burrata and tomato salad, which comes dressed in olive oil and pearls of balsamic vinegar. Portions are generous, so consider sharing a few dishes. I'd also go back for the Pristine Pear Martini, made with Grey Goose pear vodka, ginger liqueur, pear purée, lemon, St-Germain, and honey.
The Dunbar Social by Topgolf Swing Suite is a must-visit, especially for families or sports fans. It opened in October 2025 in what used to be a cart storage area when the property had a golf course. The menu offers a more elevated take on sports bar food—think crab rangoon dip, fish tacos, and my personal favorite, a smoked beef brisket hot dog served in a brioche bun and topped with caramelized onions and mustard aioli—in a fun, laid-back area filled with golf and game simulators. (There’s also a full-service bar and TVs, making this a popular game-day spot.)
For a quick morning coffee, hit up the lobby coffee bar for a complimentary brew. If you’ve got a latte in mind, you can also purchase specialty drinks and quick eats like muffins here. In the evening, the space turns into the Ryal Bar, with a 4 p.m. “daily epicurean moment” to nibble or sip on; while I was there, one such moment featured lavender tea and macarons. This area also plays host to afternoon tea on the weekends, including monthly themed teas and holiday tea service in December.

Amenities and experiences
Service here is a well-oiled machine, executed with a dose of signature Southern charm. The Ballantyne is the only hotel with a
Clefs d’Or
–credentialed concierge in North Carolina, reflecting its commitment to highly personalized and knowledgeable guest services. This team can help guests set up a romantic weekend, birthday parties, excursions, flowers, local reservations, private transportation, and more.
Don’t skip a visit to the spa—services cover all the hits, from massages and facials to full-body treatments, mani-pedis, and hair treatments. Inside the locker rooms are steam rooms, and in the relaxation room is an array of snacks that I was pleasantly surprised to find included peach rings. I visited in summer, but in the cooler months, I’d love to take advantage of the robe warmer.
What to do nearby
While Ballantyne is a bit removed from central Charlotte (around a 20-minute drive to Uptown), it’s not lacking options for things to do nearby. Both
The Amp
and
The Bowl at Ballantyne
are right at its doorstep. The former is a 3,500-guest amphitheater that hosted the likes of Rick Springfield and Yacht Rock Review in 2026; the latter is a sprawling shopping center where you can pick up food from Popup Bagels, Honey Gelato, South Block Smoothies, Flower Child, and more—or take a SolidCore class and get your hair done at DryBar. Across the street from The Bowl is The Olde Mecklenburg Restaurant & Biergarten at Ballantyne, serving up barbecue and beer.
Around 20 minutes west of the hotel in South Carolina,
Anne Springs Close Greenway
is a sprawling nature preserve where you can fish, hike, bike, and go horseback riding. Carowinds theme park and the NASCAR Hall of Fame are both around 25 minutes away by car.

The backstory
In the early ’90s, the area now known as Ballantyne was nothing but open land, much of it owned by the Harris family. Johnny Harris had a vision to transform his family’s hunting grounds into a commercial space; this would eventually become a “live, work, play, and stay” community, replete with a country club, corporate park, homes, retail space, and in 2001, a luxury hotel called The Ballantyne. The property was opened by his sister Sara and her husband (the Bissells) and has since become the centerpiece of the area.
In 2017, the family sold most of the land to Northwood Investors, and in 2019, when Starwood (which the hotel had been a part of since 2005) and Marriott merged, it joined the Marriott family as a Luxury Collection hotel. Remnants of the original history still abound, though, from the Dalmatian statues at the front entrance (a nod to the Bissell family dogs) to photos of the property over the years in some of the common spaces.
In 2025, a mini renovation updated the hotel’s façade, added more seating and a second hot tub to the indoor pool area, and refreshed the Gallery Restaurant to give it a more modern feel. Today, it’s a popular pick for conferences and business travel during the week, with staycations and celebrations filling up rooms on weekends.
Insider tip
It’s hard to miss the hotel’s massive backyard, but I’d really encourage carving out at least a few minutes to sit in one of the Adirondack chairs or sway in a hammock out there, even if just en route to or from The Bowl. Bonus points if you do so at sunset—it’s such a peaceful area.
If you’re visiting around Christmastime, be sure to check out Gingerbread Lane, an annual community event centered around a gingerbread house competition (and a life-size gingerbread house!) that raises money for Levine Children's Hospital.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes; there’s not a kids club, but the hotel is well set up for families in room options, menus, and activities
Accessibility:
ADA-compliant rooms are available, along with accessible entrances to public spaces
Wellness:
There's a full-service spa (one of the best in Charlotte); a gym with Pelotons, Hydrow rowers, and weights; and locker rooms with steam rooms
Loyalty group:
Marriott Bonvoy
