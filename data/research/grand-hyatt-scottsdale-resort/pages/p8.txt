Grand Hyatt Scottsdale Resort
Whether looking for a grownup sun soaked getaway or a spring break spot for the whole family, this desert sanctuary delivers.

Reviewed by

Why book?
A spirited escape inspired by the Sonoran Desert, Grand Hyatt
Scottsdale
Resort has nearly a dozen sparkling pools, memorable cuisine, and sweeping mountain views.
Set the scene
Taking a cue from its handsome backyard, the resort brings the outside in whenever possible. The Saguaro-dotted city of Scottsdale revels in more than 330 days of sunshine each year. So, the big, movable glass walls in the lobby and on the backside of the resort spend far more time open than closed, and if it’s actually possible, make the
McDowell Mountains
look even more magnificent. The crowd taking in the view is an eclectic mix. You’ll see
couples
,
families
, conference-goers balancing business with pleasure, and come spring training, excited baseball fans roaming the halls, riding the elevators, and kicking back with a drink at Grand Vista Lounge, the buzzing center of the resort. Sophisticated, but not at all stuffy, the resort is good at giving every type of traveler something to smile about.
The backstory
The property, formerly known as Hyatt Regency Scottsdale Resort & Spa at Gainey Ranch, marks the first Grand Hyatt hotel in Arizona. Fresh off a $115 million property-wide renovation, while the resort’s good bones remain, the rooms, lobby, dining venues, pool complex, multiple meeting spaces, and spa have enjoyed a re-do. You’d be hard-pressed to find a stretch of the property that hasn’t been touched.
The rooms
The rooms feel new and modern, because they are. All of the resort’s 496 guest rooms, suites, and casitas, received a facelift, replacing heavy wood furnishings and a dark, brown-centric color palette, with muted desert hues and low-profile pieces. Every guest room, regardless of room category, features a balcony or patio, adding to the light and airy aesthetic. Casitas with up to four bedrooms are a great option for traveling families, those seeking more exclusive digs can check into the 2,450-square-foot Gainey House, accessible via a private driveway. Views of the McDowell Mountains took center stage in my Premium Mountain View King room. While the extended balcony felt nearly as big as the actual room, the bathroom was a bit small, but it had a walk-in shower along with posh Balmain toiletries.
Food and drink
The resort’s half-dozen dining concepts are spearheaded by celebrity chef
Richard Blais
. His interpretation of classics from a variety of cuisines is intriguing and tasty. At signature restaurant La Zozzona—which translates to “messy delicious” in
Italian
—the Crispy Mozzarella was a standout for me. Made with fresh mozzarella and taken to an unexpected level with drizzles of hot honey, this dish along with the housemade pastas, foccacia bread, and rotating seasonal menu seals the desire to return. Tiki Taka features an edgy dinner menu dominated by
Japanese
sushi and
Spanish
tapas. Top choices include the crispy rice topped with spicy tuna, bacon-wrapped dates, and the okonomiyaki, a Japanese cabbage pancake. Mesa Centrale serves breakfast, lunch, and dinner; from an Arizona Benny with green chile hollandaise to a Sonoran dog with chipotle aioli, and sweet potato enchiladas with black mole, everything has a Southwest flair. Poolside H2Oasis and Sandbar serve options ranging from nachos and chicken tenders to crispy ahi tuna tacos and French fries topped with beef bulgogi.
Wild and creative, the Grand Hyatt’s craft cocktail program is spiked with entertaining doses of theatrics compliments of smoke, fire, and liquid nitrogen. At La Zozzona, a cocktail torch is required to put the final touches on the Amalfi Margarita, while the restaurant’s take on an old fashioned, made with house-crafted wagyu-washed Maker’s Mark, is smoked tableside. Want more? The rum-based Shiso Fine at Tiki Taka is topped with a citrus smoke bubble and the Yuzu Delicious is delivered with a pint-size paper cone of pistachios attached to the rim of the glass.
The spa
Arrive early for your treatment so you have time to lounge by the outdoor pool flanked by fountains and fireplaces, or the lush garden patio with a hot tub and cold plunge. Spa Avania features 19 treatment rooms, but the plethora of indoor and outdoor cozy lounging spots gives it intimate vibes. The new menu of treatments includes massages, facials, and body wraps, but don’t forget about the Himalayan Salt Room, and full-service hair and nail salon. (I recommend the Hot Herbal Poultice Massage; it’s somewhat similar to a hot stone massage but utilizes a muslin poultice filled with steamed herbs to knead tight muscles and sore spots. After, don’t miss the walk-in showers decked out with rain showers, as well as a cascading system of multiple gushing heads along the sides.) The resort’s nod to famed architect
Frank Lloyd Wright
is arguably the strongest at the spa; originally designed by Scottsdale’s Swaback Architects + Planners, Vernon Swaback was Wright’s youngest apprentice. Steps from the spa there’s a 24-hour fitness center, eight pickleball courts, and a pair of tennis courts.
The neighborhood/area
Located near the base of the McDowell Mountains, the resort is part of the swanky Gainey Ranch neighborhood. What was once a 640-acre Arabian horse ranch, is now studded with myriad gated residential communities.
Old Town Scottsdale
, the city’s walkable hotspot for food, art, and entertainment, is just five miles away.
Taliesin West
, the summer home of Frank Lloyd Wright is about a 20-minute drive.
The service
Cheerful and outgoing, staff here seem to genuinely enjoy what they do and are quick to help you make the most of your time in their backyard. Get to know the concierge team that’s all but hidden in a corner of the lobby behind the front desk. They can give you the schedule for the free shuttle that runs to a range of hotspots including Old Town Scottsdale, McDowell Sonoran Preserve, Kierland Commons, and the
Desert Botanical Garden
. (During spring training, the shuttle also stops at Salt River Fields at Talking Stick and Scottsdale Stadium.) This is also the place to be for golf enthusiasts looking to snag exclusive tee times at adjacent
Gainey Ranch Golf Club
. The three nine-hole courses are only available to private club members and the guests of Grand Hyatt Scottsdale Resort.
For families
The number of kids roaming the property took me by surprise at first, but one lap around the 2.5 acre-water wonderland and it all made sense. There are a whopping 10 swimming pools, soft sand beach, and three-story waterslide. (Those seeking a more relaxing poolside experience can throw a towel down on one of the many loungers alongside the adults-only pool.) In addition to a beachside putting green, there’s a 28-foot climbing wall, and a rotating list of kid-friendly activities including face painting, balloon animal making, and a passport-style coloring book that’s paired with a goody bag. For parents craving some me time and questioning whether or not they should leave the little ones at home, it’s important to know there is no longer a kids club at the resort.
Eco effort
Guests are offered aluminum, reusable water bottles; four water stations are scattered throughout the resort. Accommodations feature low-flow shower heads and toilets, in addition to energy-efficient lighting. Excess perishable food is donated to local nonprofit Waste Not, to aid those facing food insecurity.
Accessibility
Grand Hyatt Scottsdale Resort has 14 ADA rooms spread across four room categories. All are designed for guests with mobility disabilities; assistive listening devices, closed-captioning decoders for guest room televisions, and text telephone devices (TTY) are available by request. In addition to accommodations, entrances, parking, restrooms, the spa, as well as paths to and from restaurants and meeting venues, are designed with accessibility in mind.
Anything left to mention?
Keep an eye out for “Eagle Joe,” an enthusiastic education ambassador from local nonprofit Liberty Wildlife. He roams the resort with rescued native birds of prey by his side and is eager to help you snap a selfie with feathered favorites including Callista, a great horned owl, and Salsa, a Harris’s hawk. Every Friday at 4 p.m., “Eagle Joe” invites guests to join him on the resort lawn to meet a half-dozen rescued birds, including a bald eagle.
