InterContinental Los Angeles Downtown by IHG
A 73-story vertical statement where the real luxury is the view—everything, from your room to the rooftop bar, leans into the fact that you're literally above the city.

Best for:
View obsessives, people who want to experience the city from above it, anyone who thinks the real
LA
is the skyline.
Location:
Downtown Los Angeles and blocks from Pershing Square and the GRAMMY Museum.
Can't-miss feature:
Spire 73, the tallest open-air rooftop restaurant and bar in the Western Hemisphere, where the view is so commanding it makes the food almost secondary.
In a nutshell:
This hotel is fundamentally about height. At 73 stories and 1,100 feet, it's the architectural statement downtown LA needed to make, and the InterContinental has smartly positioned everything—the restaurants, the lobby, the rooms—to lean into that vertical drama. You're not staying in downtown; you're staying above it.
Rooms
The 889 rooms span from the 31st to 66th floor, which means even a standard room comes with floor-to-ceiling windows and a view that justifies the price. The higher you go, the more LA unfolds below you—Hollywood Hills to the coast. It's the kind of view that makes you understand why people build buildings this tall in the first place. The rooms themselves are fine; they're not the point. The point is what you see when you open the curtains. Book high and facing west if you can. The Presidential Suite is 2,500 square feet of pure skyline theater if you're doing something genuinely celebratory.
Food and Drink
Three dining experiences, each leveraging height strategically. Spire 73 sits on the 73rd floor, the actual top, and it knows this. Fire pits, fountains, the Skyline Sunset cocktail, and a casual menu—burgers, salads, tacos, and wings—that understands its real job is to not block the view. It's cocktails-and-small-plates energy, which is exactly right for a rooftop.
La Boucherie, one floor below, is the serious steakhouse: premium, sustainably-sourced meat and seafood, a wine list of over 1,000 hand-selected labels, private dining rooms, and Starlight Booths that pair bespoke culinary creations with 360-degree skyline views. The 70th-floor Lobby Lounge rounds it out with inspired cocktails and small plates, anchored by a custom light fixture inspired by LA freeways.
Amenities and Experiences
The fitness center is 13,000 square feet of Technogym equipment with city views, the kind of place where you can justify the treadmill because the view won't let you zone out. Free weights, plyometric space, steam rooms, saunas. The outdoor pool on the 7th floor has No Dive Bar, a deliberately retro pool deck with a sideways pool complete with blue lane dividers and underwater lights. It's nostalgic summer camp energy in the middle of a luxury hotel. Club InterContinental lounge is on the 70th floor for those who want complimentary drinks and snacks bundled into their stay.
What to Do Nearby
You're literally positioned above the action: L.A. Live with Crypto.com Arena is close enough to see from your room. The financial district, arts district, and historic downtown are all right there, which means you can decide whether to go or just enjoy the view of people who did.
The Backstory
The building itself is the story. Wilshire Grand Center, designed to be the tallest structure west of Chicago, opened in 2017 as a statement about downtown LA's ambitions. The InterContinental claimed the upper 36 floors. Every major experience—dining, drinking, your morning coffee—happens with skyline views.
Insider Tip
Go to Spire 73 at sunset, not dinner—the light show is extraordinary, and the cocktails are easier on the wallet than food at that elevation. At La Boucherie, book a Starlight Booth if you're celebrating something; the 360-degree views plus a bespoke menu is the hotel's way of saying yes to occasions. And don't skip No Dive Bar just because it's on the 7th floor; the sideways pool with the lane dividers is fun in a place that's otherwise all spectacle.
Final Considerations
Family friendly:
The pool and No Dive Bar work for families; the restaurants are high-end and might work better for teens. Not a splash-and-play resort, but not unwelcoming either.
Accessibility:
Yes.

A 13,000 sq ft fitness center with Technogym equipment and city views, steam rooms, saunas, outdoor pool, complimentary fitness access.
