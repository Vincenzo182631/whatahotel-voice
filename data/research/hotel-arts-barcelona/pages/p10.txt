Hotel Arts Barcelona
With views overlooking the waterfront and city, you'll feel on the top of the world at this five-star stunner.

Best for:

Location:
Steps from the waterfront (and near Barceloneta Beach) in Barcelona
Can’t-miss feature:
A drink by the pool, which has incredibly comfortable lounge chairs, complimentary sunscreen and aloe, and multiple staff members on hand ready to race over and help you open an umbrella, deliver a drink, or pass along cool towels
In a nutshell
: There's much to love about Barcelona, and from this perch on the waterfront, you can see just about all of it, from the boats bobbing on the sea to the city buildings unfolding around you. A luxurious stay by the beach, Hotel Arts Barcelona features a decadent tasting menu, a pool scene worth sacrificing a whole day for, and tiny details—like complimentary eye masks and earplugs—that make all the difference.
Jamie Spain
Rooms
I stayed in the sprawling Club Suite with friends, and I was immediately drawn to the otherworldly views upon arrival. Once I got used to the incredible perch, I was able to appreciate the interior details too, like the light, simple color scheme that made the room feel even larger, and the marble bathroom sinks that were worthy of their own photo shoot. The bathroom itself was somewhat unique, with one-way glass that lets you see out of the shower and toilet areas (luckily, no one can see in), and a large bathtub perfect for a soak after a long day exploring. While there's a TV in the corner facing the bed, there's little chance you'll actually want to turn it on since there's so much to enjoy through the windows. My favorite part of the room? The turndown service, with tea, earplugs, and eye masks to promote a good night's rest.
With 432 guestrooms and suites, this is no small boutique hotel—but it never felt overwhelmingly packed. We visited at the end of spring, approaching peak season, and the common areas were grand and open; we had no trouble getting loungers by the pool or finding a staff member to help us navigate the building (we got lost once or twice trying to find the pool or dining venues).
Food and drink
There are four food and drink venues on-site, but the shining star is Enoteca Paco Pérez, a Michelin two-star restaurant that's seafood-forward and rooted in Catalonia. The rotating, seasonal tasting menu is equal parts food for your stomach and your eyes. I particularly enjoyed the sea cucumber, with yuzu miso, veal trotter, and monk’s cress, as well as the tuna tartare with caviar and nori. I also appreciated that the team was able to make an array of delicious alcohol-free drinks for me, including zero-proof wines and cocktails.
For daytime eats, the place to be is Marina Coastal Food, which features outdoor tables with sea views. This is Spain, so the only way to begin is with local tapas. From there, it's all too easy to head to the pool where you can indulge in drinks and snacks by the water.
Elsewhere, Bites & Sips is like an on-site coffee shop that offers light snacks and drinks, while Gehry serves a complimentary breakfast buffet. I also loved the Club Lounge, which offers snacks and treats at designated times throughout the day, as well as a buffet breakfast with made-to-order options.
Jamie Spain
Amenities and experiences
While the beach and marina are just steps away, to me, nothing beats a good pool day. There are two pools at Hotel Arts Barcelona: the family-friendly pool and the adults-only one. I spent the majority of my time at the family-friendly one, which was the perfect spot to unwind with a drink.
If you've had enough of exploring this incredible city and want to relax, the extensive wellness facilities include a spa with treatments like facials and massages, and a 24-hour fitness center. There's also outdoor gardens to enjoy if you're looking for a dedicated green space in the big city.
What to do nearby
This area of Barcelona is all about the beach and the nightlife. While you won't be strolling past centuries-old churches next door, this modern stretch is like a tiny slice of contemporary Barcelona: there are clubs within walking distance, plenty of pools, park access, and even a casino. It's also a great home base for wandering along the Port Olímpic marina.
The backstory
Built for the 1992 Summer Olympics, this property originally opened 33 years ago, but the guestrooms were updated in 2025.
Insider tip
In a building this tall (it's one of the tallest in the city), you'll have wide-open views of the waterfront, the city, and everything in between. Take advantage of the Club Lounge if you spring for a Club floor—the massive windows are just the beginning; the snacks, juices, and tasty treats were the perfect delights to indulge in after a hot, sunny walk along the beach or around the city. There were also a few games you could play in the Club Lounge, including a conversation card game similar to
We're Not Really Strangers
that we could not put down.
The strangest aspect of the hotel was that many of the staff didn't speak a word of Spanish, which could make communication a bit difficult if you're not an English speaker. The property may not feel as rooted in Barcelona's historic culture and background (although the dishes in the restaurant are thoroughly Catalonian) as some other options in the city, but it still delivers a wonderful stay. This property is best for those interested in contemporary, clean lines and bright whites and aren't too fussed about staying in a historic building.
