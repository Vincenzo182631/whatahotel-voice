Tortuga Bay Puntacana
A 30-room luxury resort on a pristine beach, imagined by the late fashion designer Oscar de la Renta during Punta Cana’s original come-up.

Reviewed by

Best for:
A secluded and frictionless tropical vacation
Location:
Punta Cana, Dominican Republic
Can’t-miss feature:
Out-of-this-world service, including a group chat with the hotel’s helpful embajadores
In a nutshell
: In 2005, Dominican fashion designer Oscar de la Renta designed this iconic property with 13 sunshine yellow villas rising from a lush lawn that flows right onto the sand. The pool and beach, both with bar service, are steps away.
Rooms
Each of the 30 rooms at Tortuga Bay Puntacana is nested within 13 bright yellow villas, either on or just steps from the beach. Each villa has up to three units, with the option to book a villa in its entirety. After check-in, I meandered down a path, through clusters of palm trees, and arrived at my room. A white bicycle with my name tag on it and a golf cart I had the keys to were parked outside. I climbed up an exterior staircase and unlocked the door.
I stayed in a junior suite, which featured two queen beds, a wraparound terrace with two outdoor seating areas, and design-forward, plush furnishings in a coastal palette. There was also an indoor chaise lounge, where I leafed through coffee table books; a bathroom with a soaking tub and slabs of locally sourced coraline stone; a stocked kitchenette; and a walk-in closet. The other booking options, which are perfect for families, include two-bedroom suites, three-bedroom villas, and four-bedroom villas, with ocean and beachfront views available. The larger accommodations have a gorgeous living room sandwiched between two bedrooms.
The AC was customizable, with the capacity to fill the room with frosty, frosty air and crush the humidity. Natural sights abounded. The room was surrounded by rows of tall palm trees and looked out on the ocean. Pelicans swooped overhead and night herons, with spiky yellow feathers flying from their heads, goofily pecked the grass for insects.
Food and drink
When vacationing in the Caribbean, you want to snack on all things juicy, colorful, spicy, and sweet, and Tortuga Bay Puntacana’s restaurants more than deliver on all fronts. Tortuga Bay Puntacana’s signature restaurant, Bamboo, sits catty-corner to the pool, and has a dining room with blush pink walls—a color handpicked by Oscar de la Renta. Open for breakfast, lunch, and dinner, the extensive produce-forward menu includes fresh fruit juices, grilled fish, and coconut utilized in unexpected ways. Every item on the menu was creative, elegantly plated, and delicious. There’s also the option to arrange a private chef experience at your villa—a table with string lights is set up on the lawn, and there are special options like a barbecue or a personalized menu. Guests have access to seven additional restaurants on the greater Tortuga Bay Puntacana premises; standouts include Mare and La Yola.
Amenities and experiences
Like any classic island resort, the beach and pool are everything. Tortuga Bay Puntacana sits on 1,300 feet of private beach with clean white sand and turquoise water. Crochet hammocks, lounges, and umbrellas abound, as well as a beach bar with waiter service. The pool and pool bar are sheltered by palm trees and offer a long list of cocktails, including my favorite, which was a coconut margarita. Guests have access to the Westin, La Cana golf club, and other spaces on the greater property, including additional pools and beaches if you'd like more variety.
Golf, tennis, and The Spa Puntacana Resort round out the stellar offerings. There are two golf courses, which host a PGA tournament annually; the Oscar de la Renta Tennis Center, which also has pickleball and padel courts; and The Spa Puntacana Resort. For adventurous types, there are water sports, including diving and kiteboarding, through the Aquatic Center and Kite Club Puntacana; as well as the opportunity to take a polo lesson or ride a horse at Puntacana Polo Club and Equestrian Center.
What to do nearby
My favorite outing was to Ojos Indigenas, a nearby ecological reserve. The subtropical forest had 12 crystal clear lagoons and 500 plant species. I wore my bathing suit, and after a hike, I went for a dip alongside the turtles and fish. Don’t skimp on the bug spray. The Village Puntacana, right by the airport, has shopping, parks, grocery stores, and more.
The backstory
Tortuga Bay Puntacana opened in 2006, designed by the late Dominican fashion designer Oscar de la Renta. Part of the original Puntacana Resort, which paved the way for the entire region’s tourism development, Tortuga Bay Puntacana was the destination’s first true luxury hotel. It’s owned by Frank Rainieri, the founder and developer of Punta Cana.
Insider tip
The room-service French toast is to die for. I really regret not ordering it twice. And don’t sleep on the mangoes in the fruit basket—especially if you’re on the East Coast like me. They don’t make ’em like that here.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes. According to the GM Blanca, Tortuga Bay Puntacana has more than a few families who are repeat visitors. The staff will stock the rooms in advance with toys or gear appropriate for their age. Children might get a teepee tent and toys, while older kids might have cards, board games, boogie boards, or a volleyball net.
Accessibility:
The resort provides wheelchairs, shower chairs, and wheelchair ramps that lead to the villas.
