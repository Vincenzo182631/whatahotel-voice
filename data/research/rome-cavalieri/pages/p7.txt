Rome Cavalieri, A Waldorf Astoria Hotel
A decadent, sun-drenched hotel hideaway with knockout views and a plethora of amenities.

Why book?
Rome Cavalieri, A Waldorf Astoria Hotel, is everything you want a five-star resort to be: grand, elegant, and secluded. This is an extravagant hotel hideaway for anyone on the hunt for downtime and relaxation, but also keen for a spot of culture, thanks to the hotel’s close proximity—a swift 15-minute shuttle bus ride—to Rome’s city center. No wonder loyal guests return year after year.
Set the scene
Located on 15 acres of private parklands, Rome Cavalieri is the kind of resort that harkens back to a time when going abroad heralded a grand adventure. A long, winding driveway leads you through manicured gardens, past two tennis courts, and towards the swimming pools, where cheerful waiters deliver club sandwiches and lemonade on silver platters, and cocktails feel like a good idea at any time of the day.
Give us the backstory
The Rome Cavalieri feels a bit like stepping into Rome’s bygone era: big, sweeping staircases, twinkling chandeliers—heck, even the elevators are lined with gold and marble. The Rome Cavalieri first opened its doors in 1963, backed by one of the most powerful families in Italy. In 2008, it joined the Waldorf Astoria collection, but the family ties remain, notably in the hotel’s extensive art collection, which includes Giambattista Tiepolo paintings, original signed Warhols, and a rare Beauvais Tapestry from 1725 (others in the series are in the Met in New York and the Louvre in Paris).
Who were your fellow guests
There are plenty of repeat vacationers here, some for leisure, others for business, alongside European families who have nipped over to make the most of Rome’s late, sun-drenched summers. Couples looking for an all-inclusive weekend getaway love the Rome Cavalieri—men in chinos and linen shirts, women in big hats and sunglasses lounging by the pool, breaking for lunch at Uliveto or a spot of tennis in the afternoon.
The good stuff: Tell us about your room.
The 345 rooms and 25 suites in this big Mediterranean palazzo are elegant and ornate, while remaining distinctly Italian with antique furniture, marble bathrooms, and big private balconies that open to sweeping poolside views, and beyond that, St. Peter’s Basilica and all of Rome. The bed is lose-yourself-in-it big, with silky soft bed linens, and the bathroom is all marble. If you’ve got the money, the Penthouse and Planetarium suites are truly special: Each has their own hot tub and wraparound terrace, plus private dining and lounge areas. Guests staying in the suites and Imperial rooms get automatic access to the Imperial Club, which offers VIP check-in, complimentary breakfast, and afternoon tea throughout the day.
Food and drink
All the restaurants at Rome Cavalieri are exquisite. In the summertime you can head straight from the pool to dinner at Patio 101, a laid-back garden eatery where the open kitchen has a huge wood-fired oven and a couple of Big Green Egg barbecues, producing perfectly-charred zucchini flower pizzas and freshly-grilled catch of the day. Cocktails are potent and the wine pairings are perfect, all served to a DJ spinning background beats.
Guests can have breakfast by the pool at Uliveto, which also does a decadent all-day dining menu, including pasta with oxtail ragu, seafood risotto topped with pistachios, roast lamb with olives and artichokes, and grilled lobster with garlic butter. The staff are kind and friendly, and eager to recommend dishes if you’re undecided. For a special occasion, head to Heinz Beck’s recently redesigned La Pergola, the only three-Michelin-star restaurant in the capital, for a genius, adventurous, and masterful 10-course Mediterranean gourmet menu. Room service, meanwhile, is as decadent as you’d expect—there’s the 15-course imperial breakfast, complete with quail eggs, black truffles, and pineapple carpaccio—or the silver service lobster, which comes with a side order of 500 white roses.
The neighborhood
Perched on Monte Mario, Rome’s highest hill, Rome Cavalieri is a 15-minute drive into the city. There’s not much directly outside the resort, but with 15 acres of landscaped grounds, three swimming pools, two tennis courts, and a woodland jogging track, it’s much more preferable to sit around and enjoy la vita lenta (the slow life). The hotel runs a free hourly shuttle bus, which drops off and picks up on Via Veneto, meaning the city is easily accessible if and when you need it.
The spa
The Ancient Rome-themed spa is a full-fledged luxurious health club. Treatments use Natura Bisse products and are excellent, but you could spend an afternoon using the spa amenities alone: hot and cold plunge pools, a sauna, and a big Turkish bath, all framed by glittering marble mosaics. The relaxation lounge is a recreation of a Roman rotunda, and there’s a hair salon and well-equipped gym too.
Anything stand out about other services and features?
Pets are welcome at the Rome Cavalieri; there’s even a pet-sitting service and a pet spa for pooches. Guests can choose from a number of luxury experiences, including helicopter tours, private yacht trips, truffle hunts, and the concierge can also arrange access to sites usually closed to the public, including the Vatican Gardens and Palazzo Colonna.
