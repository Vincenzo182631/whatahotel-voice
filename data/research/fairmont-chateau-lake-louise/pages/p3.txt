Why book?
For a tranquil and luxe stay in the heart of Banff National Park, the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise, enveloped in nature, can’t be beat. White glove-service, a cutting-edge wellness experience, and once-in-a-lifetime views await.

Set the scene
My partner and I arrived in Calgary late one October evening, eager to finally see the Canadian Rockies for ourselves.
Banff National Park
had been at the top of my list of dream travel destinations for ages. We had a two-hour drive ahead of us before we reached the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise in Alberta, Canada. Our very kind driver told us stories about Banff’s infamous grizzly bears, including Split Lip—who earned his nickname after a fight with his rival, known by locals as The Boss. I couldn’t tell where the mountain peaks ended and the dark night sky began. As we arrived at the hotel, a light snow began to fall.
When morning came, I hurried to my room’s window and drew back the floor-to-ceiling curtains. I was stunned. I stood in awe of the view that looked too good to be real: a turquoise Lake Louise shimmered in front of the snow-covered mountain tops. Instantly, I understood why travelers have flocked here for decades just to see this view.
The backstory
The historic Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise wasn’t always as grand as travelers know it to be today. Its story began back in 1890 when William Van Horne, the president of the Canadian Pacific Railway, opened a small log cabin for tourists arriving by train to stay in. Horne hoped that the cabin, known then as the Chalet at Lake Louise, would allow more travelers to experience the beauty of the Canadian Rockies. After a fire in 1924 destroyed much of the original cabin, it was rebuilt with a concrete structure and reopened as a large chateau-inspired hotel. More renovations over the years expanded and upgraded the Fairmont property to become the iconic stay it is today.
The rooms
With breathtaking views of Lake Louise and the
Canadian Rocky Mountains
surrounding the property, there is no such thing as a bad room. Each of the 539 rooms and suites at the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise feature modern finishes and elegant decor. I stayed in one of the Fairmont Lakeview King rooms and woke up each morning to the lake’s turquoise blue waters peering in my window. This standard-size room offered plenty of space for my partner and I. Along with a waterfront view, the room was well-equipped with key amenities like a coffee/tea bar, flat-screen TV, sitting area, ironing board, hair dryer, and Le Labo bath products. Luxe suites are also available at the Fairmont—especially ideal for families traveling together or couples celebrating special occasions.
The vibe is upscale and understated, while still feeling warm and homey. Our room was the perfect place to unwind after a long day of exploring the great outdoors.
Food and drink
We started off each morning with breakfast at Louiza, a casual and cool Mediterranean-inspired restaurant that is open daily. Guests can walk up to the breakfast bar to grab fresh-pressed juices, fruit, bagels, yogurt, pastries, and more (there are even to-go bags available, in case you want to take a cinnamon roll, or two, with you). Or, guests can take a seat and order from Louiza’s seasonal menu: think Brioche French toast, brown butter pancakes, and traditional eggs and toast. For a quick pick-me-up any time of day, stop by the Guide’s Pantry and grab coffee, baked goods, sandwiches, and more.
For lunch with a view, there is no better spot than the Fairview Bar and Restaurant. I spent most of my meal here staring out the floor-to-ceiling window at the glistening lake, but the food was equally worth raving about. I enjoyed the best salad I’ve ever had—it was served table-side with fresh Caesar dressing made right in front of me. In the evening, head to the Fairview’s intimate, upscale dining room for delicious meat and seafood. Make sure to try one of the butcher’s cuts, with locally sourced bison filet and elk striploin on offer. Signature cocktails are inspired by the wildlife in Banff National Park, including drinks called the Little Beehive and the Split Lip. And if you want something a little more laid-back after a busy day hiking or skiing, stop by the Alpine Social for good ole fashion comfort food and craft beers.
The spa
There are hotel spas and then there is Basin. The newest and most exciting addition to the Fairmont is the recent opening of
Basin Glacial Waters
, an all-sensory thermal bathing facility that’s fusing nature and science to offer its guests the ultimate indoor-outdoor wellness experience. The adults-only facility is focused on thermotherapy, a type of treatment that applies heat to relieve pain, relax muscles, and improve circulation throughout the body.
Kymberley Hill, director of public relations for the hotel, told me that for the first time, travelers are booking the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise with wellness top of mind. When I arrived at Basin, I was able to select from a series of curated Thermal Trails that are designed to guide you through its many offerings based on your intention for the day. Instead of feeling overwhelmed or confused about where to start, I followed my step-by-step trail through different treatments. From cold plunges and steam rooms to indoor-outdoor thermal pools with some of the best views that Lake Louise has to offer, there are restorative options for every individual’s needs.
The most meaningful part of my afternoon at Basin was taking part in a unique sauna ceremony called the
Aufguss ritual.
During the 15-minute group ceremony, rising heat and steam are swirled by the sweep of a towel, carrying the scent of pure essential oils through the air. The longer I was in the sauna, the hotter the space became. As sweat poured from every inch of my body, I couldn’t think about anything other than breathing and calming myself down. All other thoughts were pushed away as we were guided into total mindfulness. This ritual forced me to quiet my mind and push through the discomfort. The result was an instant mood boost, as well as more mental clarity, heightened senses, loosening of the muscles, and detoxification.
Separate from
Basin Glacial Waters
, guests can also visit the hotel’s on-site spa for more traditional services, like massages, facials, and manicures. I stopped by for an aromatherapy massage that helped alleviate stress and tension in the body. From Basin Glacial Waters and the spa to hiking trails and guided cold plunges, it’s clear that wellness is embedded into every offering at the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise.
The neighborhood/area
Nestled in Alberta’s Banff National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise is surrounded by the best of nature. Clear blue skies, mountain peaks, Victoria Glacier, and the emerald-colored Lake Louise create a storybook backdrop that’s worth flying for.
Guests can explore nearby hiking trails or take a slow stroll around the lake, and in the summertime, canoe peacefully through Lake Louise. When the temperature drops, the lake freezes over and becomes an ice-skating rink, transforming the hotel’s backyard into its very own winter wonderland. Three Rocky Mountain ski resorts in Banff attract skiers of all levels from around the world each year. And while there’s plenty of outdoor adventure to be had right on the property, the rest of Banff National Park is ripe for exploring. You’ll find mountain trails, wildlife, and glacial lakes in driving distance to the hotel.
The service
Exceptional service is at the heart of the Fairmont. Friendly staff greeted me as soon as I arrived and made sure I felt welcomed and comfortable throughout my stay. Guests can expect top-notch service and a close attention to detail at every turn. I learned something new from everyone I met, from restaurant servers who provided excellent recommendations to my cold plunge guide who offered expert advice on proper breathing techniques. Every part of my stay felt luxurious, allowing me to sit back, relax, and unwind.
Eco effort
I’m more than impressed with the hotel’s commitment to sustainability. Lake Louise is one of the most famous natural destinations in Canada, so preserving the landscape is vital to the historic property. For starters, the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise has some of the cleanest drinking water in the world. As water melts from the Victoria Glacier and travels down the mountainside, it brings salt and minerals with it into Lake Louise. This process enriches the water and gives the lake its spectacular emerald color. After it is purified, water from Lake Louise is pumped across the property. Staff at the hotel encouraged me to drink straight from the tap to enjoy the benefits of the clean water available on site.
I was also happy to learn that leftover soap and hygiene products from guest rooms are donated to
Clean the World,
a global organization that distributes recycled soaps and hygiene products to vulnerable communities in over 120 countries. More eco-efforts include the elimination of single-use plastic and sustainable restaurants menus full of locally and organically grown ingredients.
Accessibility
Accessible rooms are available with large door frames, a roll-in shower and tub with grab bars, raised toilet seats, and more features to accommodate each guest’s individual needs. Ramps are also available throughout the hotel, with the exception of the pool, as well as designated parking spots. Be sure to contact the hotel with any requests related to accessibility and to ensure an accessible room is available.
Anything left to mention?
I highly recommend taking advantage of the many guided trails and experiences available at the Fairmont Chateau Lake Louise. The highlight of my stay was the part I was most nervous about. I pushed myself to do a guided
cold plunge
in Lake Louise—yes, literally in the lake. Before braving the cold, my guide practiced proper breathing and mindfulness techniques with me so that I could keep myself calm during the plunge. She also went over the benefits of the cold plunge, including improved circulation.
As soon as my body hit the water, I felt panicked. That all stopped when I looked up and realized I was standing in the epicenter of the most unbelievable view I had ever seen. Completely mesmerized, I forgot all about the pain. Five-and-a-half minutes later, I walked out of the water—my body beet red and shivering. I felt practically invincible. But this feeling isn’t exclusive to a cold plunge in Lake Louise; you can chase it in more places than one at the Fairmont.
