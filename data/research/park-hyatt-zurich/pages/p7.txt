Reviewed by

Why book?
Smack in the center of town and under five minutes from the lake, this contemporary hotel ticks all the boxes when it comes to service and style.
Set the scene
Designer suits with briefcases and well-heeled tourists share the same elevators, just like the streets outside. Office buildings and wealthy residences line this neighborhood, but the hotel’s address between the lake and the old town means it is perfectly positioned for all types of visitors.
The backstory
Park Hyatt hotels are chic retreats at the top end of Hyatt’s scale of brands. They are known for notable art collections, impressive interior design, and high standards of service. This hotel is no different, although you won’t find flashy restaurants or even a head-turning facade once past the Maseratis and Rolls Royces that often park out front.
Inside, the look is rather sedate and calm, although the modern art is certainly eye-catching (there are nearly 100 original pieces around the building).
The rooms
A generous amount of natural light pours in through large windows, illuminating the hardwood floors and designer furnishings in subdued tones. While the look may not scream opulence or Swiss banking wealth, it is elegant, with subtle design pieces, art books on side tables, and contemporary paintings on the walls.
There’s nothing showstopping, but it all works well. You won’t find mesmerizing views (even on higher floors), but the electronic window shades, high-tech gadgetry, and oh-so-plush bedding are perfect for hard-working executives. Extra touches include a long row of international power outlets that pop up out of the desk, dental and shaving kits in bathrooms, as well as garment bags to take home in the closet.
The bathrooms have spacious vanities stocked with Le Labo products in pump bottles, rain-drenching showers, and in many, soaking tubs that open to the guest room for natural light via sliding panels. Others have exterior windows that let in natural light, but designers thought ahead and added privacy panels to prevent views into neighboring office buildings while still keeping the space bright.
Park Suites are particular favorites of repeat guests for their sizable living rooms, especially those on the corners. A real splurge is the Presidential Suite on the top floor because it has an outdoor terrace, a living room with a fireplace, a dining area, a small kitchen, and an office space.
Food and drink
Parkhuus is the all-day dining restaurant that starts off with a delightful buffet that crisscrosses Europe with its selection of cheeses and deli meats. It travels as far as the Middle East, with mezze options adding more variety. Later in the day, the menu further shifts its Mediterranean focus. The dining room attracts area business folk for an express prix-fixe lunch (salmon sashimi and pumpkin gnocchi are two popular options). The wine cellar can be reserved for private group events.
Swiss menu options are also available in the lobby lounge, and a weekly DJ enlivens the mood at Onyx Bar (so-named for the illuminated surface that makes up the bar itself). On the menu are small-batch whiskies as well as a long list of classic cocktails and mocktails.
The neighborhood/area
While the neighborhood is not that exciting for tourists, it is within a few minutes’ walk of the lake and of pedestrian-friendly shopping and dining. The city’s easy-to-use tram system has stops just two blocks away. If you want to walk to the train station for a day trip to Lucerne, it will take 15 minutes on foot (or much less via tram).
The service
Park Hyatt Zurich staffers are the perfect example of Swiss hospitality, knowledgeable and exhibiting just the right amount of interaction with guests.
For families
Family rooms are essentially two connecting rooms, and the staff can deliver special welcome amenities for young guests. The inventory includes everything from miniature robes and special toiletries to a tent where the kiddos can sleep with toys to keep them entertained.
Eco effort
Guests can borrow bikes from the reception to cycle around the lake. And the kitchen’s carbon footprint is minimal since most menu ingredients highlight locally sourced meat, seafood, and produce.
Accessibility
This modern hotel has numerous facilities for guests with mobility needs.
Anything left to mention?
The staff goes the extra mile here. And while there is no pool, locals often take a plunge in the lake to swim laps during their workday lunch break. It’s less than five minutes on foot, if you bring a towel.
