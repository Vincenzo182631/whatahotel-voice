Activities and Experiences in Sintra, Portugal
Experiences
Nestled among the Sintra Mountains in a 220-hectare natural park, Penha Longa Resort offers more than just a stunning setting. The hotel is a haven for outdoor enthusiasts with a 27-hole golf course in Portugal, a historic circuit that takes guests to a 14th-century monastery, and easy access to the nearby beaches of Cascais and Estoril, just 10 minutes away. A children's program includes a variety of experiences at the hotel, including an outdoor playground, afternoon tea, family picnics, and mini mixology workshops. Additionally, the recently renovated rooms provide a luxurious and contemporary environment for rest and relaxation, perfectly complementing the hotel's hospitality experience.

Sustainability
Established in 1994, the Sintra-Cascais Natural Park (Parque Natural de Sintra-Cascais) spans 14,583 hectares and is home to over 200 species of vertebrates, including 33 mammals, more than 160 bird species, 12 amphibians, 20 reptiles, and 9 freshwater fish. The park’s most prominent feature is the Sintra mountain range, which reaches a peak of 528 meters. In addition to its stunning mountains, the park boasts a scenic coastline of significant ecological value, along with a vast rural area filled with natural and semi-natural ecosystems that support a rich variety of flora, fauna, and habitats.
Approximately 47% of Quinta da Penha Longa is covered by forest. In 2020, a Forest Management Plan was created, dividing the forest into 10 distinct plots: one for conservation purposes (14.5 ha), five for both conservation and production functions (41.7 ha), and four dedicated to production (47.3 ha). The production areas primarily focus on cork oak, which is harvested every nine years, and pine trees, from which the pine nuts are collected.
In addition to routine forest management tasks such as cleaning and pruning, the plan includes measures for fire prevention, such as fuel management in protective strips and maintaining accessible paths. It also addresses pest control, particularly targeting the pine wood nematode and the oak pinhole borer, an insect that threatens cork oaks. Furthermore, the plan aims to control the spread of invasive species, especially acacia.
