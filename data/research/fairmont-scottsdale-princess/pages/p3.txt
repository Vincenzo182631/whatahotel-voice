Fairmont Scottsdale Princess
A massive, hacienda-style resort with a top-notch spa and proximity to TPC Scottsdale golf complex.

Why book?
You won’t need (or want) to leave this upscale resort, a gorgeous property with expansive, hacienda-style grounds, several excellent restaurants, multiple pools, plenty of space to roam, a top-notch spa, and access to several world-class golf courses.
Set the scene
The property is an homage to Southwestern elegance with its grand Mexican Colonial architecture stretching across 65 acres of the Sonoran Desert. Think stone and stucco, wood beams, wrought-iron chandeliers, tiled plazas, and courtyards filled with fountains, towering palms, and native desert flora. Such a massive resort—with such a multitude of offerings—lends itself to a cross section of guests, including a good chunk of business travelers thanks to a whopping 300,000 square feet of event space and a calendar packed with conferences. Its location adjacent to the TPC Scottsdale complex—home to the PGA Tour’s renowned WM Phoenix Open—brings serious golfers to the property. Families come to take advantage of the multiple pools, especially the kid-centric Sonoran Splash pool with twisting water slides, and plenty of guests are here simply for the luxe spa, one of the most impressive in the region.
The backstory
The original Scottsdale Princess opened in 1987, one of the first large-scale resorts to usher in a new generation of upscale properties drawing visitors to the destination. It became the Fairmont Scottsdale Princess in 2000, eventually undergoing a $60 million renovation and adding more than 100 guest rooms to the resort. And in 2022, the property unveiled 119 Privado Villas—residential-style studios and one-bedroom suites with separate pools, exclusive amenities, and dedicated concierges.
The rooms
Renovations over the last several years have given rooms and suites a more modern design feel, with geometric-patterned carpets, sleek wood paneling, and glass-enclosed walk-in showers. The color palette highlights desert hues with dark wood furnishings, sand-colored walls, and caramel leather headboards. All feature large sliding glass doors to patios or balconies, with views varying depending on the building you choose.
Food and drink
Prolific Mexican chef and restaurateur Richard Sandoval is behind two of the five restaurants on the property. At Toro, located in the clubhouse of the TPC course with a patio overlooking both the fairway and the McDowell Mountain Range, Sandoval focuses on Latin American fare blended with Japanese offerings. Think Peruvian-inspired ceviche, sushi rolls, and a selection of grilled steaks. His dinner-only Mexican eatery La Hacienda sits on the resort’s plaza with multiple patios outside, plus beehive fireplaces, wood-beamed ceilings, and a dedicated tequila bar (replete with an on-staff “tequila goddess” to help guide your search for the perfect agave spirit). In addition to a variety of tacos (don’t miss the lobster), enchiladas, and wood-grilled specialties, the place prides itself on dramatic presentations—think tableside guacamole, smoldering fajitas, and flaming, booze-spiked coffees. You’ll also find the all-day Ironwood American Kitchen, casual Plaza Bar, and Michael Mina’s Bourbon Steak on the property.
The spa
The spa was hands-down my favorite experience at the Princess. The luxe, multi-level facility is equipped with enough amenities to keep you luxuriating all day. You'll find steam rooms, dry saunas, hot tubs, and cold plunges in the locker rooms, as well as a tranquil rooftop pool, the most relaxing on the property. Spa guests also gain access to the property's private gym and fitness classes. For a skincare splurge, book a treatment at the Sisley-Paris Spa, the Fairmont’s spa within a spa, and one of the few Sisley outposts in the country. Specially-trained therapists perform the treatments—from gua sha facials to “phyto-energzing” body scrubs—all of which feature Sisley’s prestigious (and famously pricey) plant-based products.
The neighborhood/area
Located in the ever-growing neighborhood of North Scottsdale, an upscale suburban area with lots of large estates and gated communities, the Princess is a few miles from clusters of high-end shopping and dining complexes, as well as excellent hiking in the McDowell Sonoran Preserve.
The service
With a resort of this size servicing thousands of guests on any given day, you’re not going to get intimate, know-your-name service—but service is attentive and prompt. It also varies depending on where you are on the property, from my experience. Valet and bellhop staffers were especially eager to assist, and pool service was perfectly good but felt more low-touch. (The weekday I visited the main pool, there were no attendants available to assist with towel-and-chair set-ups, and when I asked for water when ordering lunch, I was directed to a serve-yourself water station across the pool, which feels unusual for a Fairmont). The spa service, however, was white-glove all the way.
For families
The aforementioned Sonoran Splash pool is geared toward kids, and the Sunset Beach pool—with sand imported from Florida—is a fun spot for little ones to play, too. There’s also a supervised kids club (for ages 5 through 12) available for an extra cost, and afternoon access to the rec center is included in the resort fee.
Accessibility
The resort features 16 ADA-compliant rooms.
