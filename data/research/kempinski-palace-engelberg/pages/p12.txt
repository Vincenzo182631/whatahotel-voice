# TITLIS Tower

### A New Landmark for Mount Titlis

At the beginning of June 2026, the TITLIS Tower opened - a spectacular structure designed by the world renowned architectural firm Herzog & de Meuron at over 3,000 metres above sea level. In the first three weeks alone, around 60 Swiss and international media professionals visited the tower, reporting on the new landmark with great enthusiasm.

The 360-degree panoramic view from the Horizon Deck is a truly unique experience, bringing visitors incredibly close to the surrounding peaks. Many guests also expressed immense respect for the construction workers, logistics teams, and project partners who realised this ambitious structure under highly challenging alpine conditions.

### A Legacy of Alpine Innovation

Mount Titlis shapes the image of Engelberg far beyond the region. With the project TITLIS Tower, TITLIS Cableways is entering an exciting new chapter. Visionary thinking has long been part of the mountain’s history: in 1913, the funicular railway to Gerschnialp opened; in 1927, Trübsee was connected by Switzerland’s first federally licensed cable car. This was followed in 1943 by the Trübsee to Jochpass chairlift, Switzerland’s first chairlift to operate in summer.

Another milestone that attracted international attention was the development of the glacier with the Stand to Titlis aerial tramway. This pioneering spirit culminated in 1992 with the Rotair, the world’s first rotating cable car.

### Iconic Architecture by Herzog & de Meuron

A pioneering spirit has been part of the Titlis DNA for decades, and the TITLIS Tower builds directly on this legacy. Herzog & de Meuron transformed the 76-metre-high radio tower, dating from the 1980s, into a new architectural landmark. Two glazed structures have been inserted horizontally into the existing framework, creating a striking cross-shaped form reminiscent of the Swiss cross.

_“Towers have always possessed a special radiance. They are landmarks, symbols, and signs in the landscape,”_ says Norbert Patt, CEO of TITLIS Cableways.

The tower is set to become the ultimate highlight of the Titlis experience. An elevator takes guests to the observation deck, the world’s highest Rolex boutique, and Joseph’s Restaurant. “ _Those who have already been on the tower during construction describe the panorama as something completely different, freer, wider, and far more impressive,_” says Norbert Patt.

### Engineering Marvel at 3,000 Metres

In addition to traditional Alpine tourism, the project is specifically aimed at an urban audience seeking world class architecture and design. Situated at an altitude of 3,000 metres and exposed to snow, wind, and extreme weather conditions, the TITLIS Tower is one of Europe’s most impressive structural achievements. Machinery, infrastructure, and all components had to be transported from the valley to the summit through a complex logistical operation, either by cable car involving multiple transfers or by helicopter.

_“The geology and alpine terrain present significant challenges, as does working in the thin mountain air,”_ explains Norbert Patt. Over 90 planners and contractors have been involved in the project, with up to 120 people on site each day, all while regular tourist operations on the mountain continued seamlessly. _“This requires close coordination, consideration, and precise organisation at every level.”_

For guests, this new landmark promises a deeply immersive Titlis experience, complemented by high end culinary offerings and breathtaking Alpine vistas. The visit is designed to move visitors and offer entirely new perspectives, from the moment of arrival to the final glimpse of the vast surroundings. The opening of the new Peak Station, another architectural masterpiece designed by Herzog & de Meuron, is scheduled for 2029.
