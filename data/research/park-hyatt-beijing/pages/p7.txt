Park Hyatt Beijing
A stay that's all about the views.

Reviewed by
Ramsey Qubein
Why book?
In a city with so many luxury hotels, it can be hard to stand out against the competition. But as one of the city’s tallest hotels, stand out it does. The views from almost anywhere around this property are unending (even on a smoggy Beijing day). And just wait until the sun sets and the city lights up like a Chinese Christmas tree. It gives off that infamous Park Hyatt Tokyo vibe, made famous from the film “Lost in Translation.”
Set the scene
This neighborhood is packed with high-end stores, glittering skyscrapers (including the famous CCTV headquarters), and a constant buzz of energy. You won’t get very far before a door person tries to help with your bags, directing you to the lobby on the 63rd floor. Once you’ve made it to the top, the elevators swing open to reveal large-scale floor art and sculptures leading to the small corner reception area.
The hushed buzz of clinking Champagne glasses or afternoon tea pots pouring steaming cups comes from the adjacent lobby lounge. You won’t find much else here, though. That’s because all of the other guest facilities are on different floors (the spa, pool, and gym down a few floors via a different set of elevators; the China Grill restaurant and bar upstairs).
The backstory
This is one of the city’s tallest hotels and was the first Park Hyatt to open in China. While it is no longer among the city’s newest openings, it has stood the test of time, welcoming visitors looking for fantastic city views and notable service.
It sits within the tallest skyscraper (Beijing Yintai Centre) on bustling Chang’an Avenue thoroughfare (the second tallest in Beijing). The hotel underwent an interior refresh in 2019, which subtly weaves elements of a traditional Beijing-style courtyard home (much like what you might find in the city’s famous hutongs). Look closely, and you’ll notice an artistic impression of koi fish in the carpeting or mahogany chairs, inspired by the mahogany doors of these homes. In guest rooms, the city’s native plum blossom features in the design.
The rooms
While the furnishings largely remained the same during the renovation work, the fabrics and artistic elements have changed in recent years. The most striking feature you’ll notice when you enter is that guests arrive first into the bathroom area of the room, with the vanity to one side and a massive glass shower stall and soaking tub on the other. I thought the adjustable layout of the room, thanks to sliding wooden doors, was a brilliant move because it allows for both privacy and views from the bathroom, depending on your needs. Just one gentle push on the doors reveals skyline views from the soaking tub or the shower.
My favorite feature of the room, though, was the floor-to-ceiling windows where I could stare out into the endless city from the sofa or while working on the spacious desk. I liked how there was a chair on either side to turn it into a dining table. While I found the mattress to be firm, the pillows were just right (and quite large). And when jet lag snarls your normal rest pattern, the window views and a cup of traditional Chinese tea awaiting each guest’s arrival become a pleasant distraction.
The Park Hyatt Beijing welcomes many business travelers, and the easy-access power and latest USB outlets show the renovation’s attention to detail. Even the drawers had stationery and office supplies.
Clean and minimalist, it’s clear the designers wanted the focal point to be the views. Upgrade to a room facing the CCTV headquarters or CITIC Towers for the most interesting views (you’ll thank me later when you’re awake at 3 a.m.). But even the Chang'an Avenue View rooms are entertaining. Given the building’s height, there really is no bad view.
Several suites add living areas, large dining tables, and more elaborate bathrooms. Otherwise, all of the accommodations have Park Hyatt signatures like Le Labo toiletries in pump bottles, fluffy robes and slippers, minibars, espresso machines, and plenty of bottled water.
Food and drink
The lobby lounge has a light meal and bar menu, but for more substantial dining, take the dedicated elevators to the 66th floor. China Grill is a stunner with its walls of glass offering up a 360-degree view and a triangular atrium ceiling. You are truly dining in the sky here.
Breakfast is a lavish spread of various live cooking stations with Chinese and Western options. I was most impressed with the Beijing specialties on offer, with small signs next to each one explaining the history of the dish. With only a handful of days in Beijing on this trip, I wouldn’t be able to make it to my favorite restaurants in town. So sampling things like rolled pancakes stuffed with eggs and veggies or tofu puff soup did the trick.
I enjoyed watching chefs roll and stuff traditional buns on the spot, and the breakfast salad spread was the most extensive I had seen in the city (there was even fresh burrata).
Later in the day, the menu shifts to a mix of Chinese and international dishes, but the view is the real star. China Bar, one level down, also prepares craft cocktails with the same views and occasional live music.
The spa
This is the second-highest spa and swimming pool in Beijing, and from the gym and indoor pool, you can take in the scenery from the massive 59th-floor windows. Like other pools in China, swimmers will need to don a swim cap to take a dip. A lifeguard is on duty, and there are two whirlpools that bubble over their infinity edges on either side.
While I didn’t use the spa, I looked around and found the massage and beauty treatment menu extensive (many of the treatments were based on Chinese medicine). Guests receive a modest coupon to use for a treatment during their stay.
The neighborhood/area
The area around the hotel is easily walkable for window shopping at the likes of Hermes and Chopard boutiques. China World Mall is directly connected to the via underground passageways, and the nearby Guomao subway station (a one-minute walk from the front door) makes it easy to get around. With just one connection, I made it from the new Daxing Airport to the hotel in under an hour. It’s often faster to take the subway than sit in traffic when visiting popular tourist sights like the Forbidden City and Tiananmen Square or Silk Street Market.
The service
The staff has a decent command of English here. Everyone I interacted with provided great service. For example, I covered my coffee cup to stay warm when I went to the breakfast buffet. As soon as I returned, a server offered to bring me a new one that was piping hot, just in case mine had cooled a bit. Another time, when looking for the restaurant that required a change of elevator, the reception staff walked with me to show the way and pushed the button once inside so I would not get lost.
For families
While this is not a family-focused hotel, there are kids, and you’ll find them happiest at the indoor pool. They also have big smiles when ordering from the ice cream sundae menu at China Grill.
Anything left to mention?
As with most hotels in the city, the Wi-Fi here is not as speedy as I would have liked, and many sites are blocked in China, including Google and Gmail, among others. Business travelers will find this especially frustrating, as even VPNs usually can’t break through these restrictions in the capital.
