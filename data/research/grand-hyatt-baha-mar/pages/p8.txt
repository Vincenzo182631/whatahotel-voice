Grand Hyatt Baha Mar
A Bahamas resort that has so much going on, there’s no need to leave.

Reviewed by
Lauren Dana Ellman
Why book?
This is the beating heart of Baha Mar—central, energetic, and the most convenient base for accessing everything on the complex.
Set the scene
Set on Cable Beach, Grand Hyatt is the most central of the hotels in Baha Mar (a resort complex with three hotels, a casino, a water park, and dozens of dining offerings), with direct access to the main pool areas, a large share of the 40-plus restaurants, and the shops and casino just off the lobby. You’ll notice the immense scale right away. By day, spot people moving between the beach, pools, and dining areas, as well as families heading toward the water park. By evening, it’s all drinks, live music, and the casino floor.
This property effectively acts as the central hub for the full resort complex, linking SLS’s trendier dining and nightlife with Rosewood’s quieter side, making it the most practical base for experiencing Baha Mar as a whole.
The backstory
Grand Hyatt anchors the larger Baha Mar development, which opened its first phase in 2017, a multi-billion-dollar resort complex designed to function as a destination in its own right. Alongside SLS and Rosewood, it forms a shared ecosystem of dining, entertainment, and experiences that feels more like a small city than a singular hotel. It was the first hotel to open in the complex and is still currently the largest.
The rooms
Rooms are bright and contemporary, with floor-to-ceiling windows and subtle pops of turquoise that nod to the surrounding ocean. All room categories include balconies—ranging from Juliet balconies to fully furnished ones—with views of the ocean, fountains, or lush grounds. The overall look is clean and modern with understated tropical touches, while Balmain bath amenities add an extra-luxe touch. Comfort is clearly a priority here, from plush beds to thoughtful in-room details like the surprisingly soft bathrobes—easily the coziest I’ve ever tried: thick, soft, and genuinely hard to take off.
For a more exclusive experience, there’s also The Reserve, which offers a quieter, hotel-within-a-hotel experience that occupies the top floors of the East Tower and features a private VIP entrance, an expansive lounge, and upgraded amenities. You can also expect private check-in, lounge access, and butler service in select residences—ideal if you want to take a step away from the resort’s scale without losing access to the breadth of activities.
Food and drink
Within Grand Hyatt Baha Mar, there are over two dozen dining and drinking options, making it easy to spend several days here without repeating a meal. At the higher end, Leola by Scott Conant and Marcus at Baha Mar Fish + Chop House anchor the scene with a more elevated nighttime atmosphere, while Shuang Ba offers some of the best Chinese food I’ve had in a resort setting, with classics served in an elegant space—the hand-carved Peking duck is a standout. (As a Chinese food connoisseur, I don’t say that lightly.) For something different, Cinkō Asian Latino Grill stands out as a kosher restaurant that brings together Asian and South American flavors in a way that feels both unexpected and well executed.
Mornings tend to revolve around Regatta Buffet, or, for something lighter and more grab-and-go, Café Madeleine or Bahama Bagel. I kept returning to Café Madeleine in particular—its Parisian feel is surprisingly convincing, and the pastries easily rival those I’ve had in Paris. Lunch is often an informal affair—poke bowls from Da Poke Bowl Shack, tacos from El Jefe, or something quick from Knosh or Takeaway 242 between pool and beach. There’s also Marcus Samuelsson’s Streetbird on the Beach, which channels Caribbean flavors with dishes like Calypso conch fritters, escovitch chicken, and plantain waffles.
The spa
The 30,000-square-foot Espa offers a striking contrast to the rest of the resort. Perched above the beach, it feels intentionally removed from the buzz. Treatments, such as the Seashell Serenity massage, focus on a full-body reset rather than quick fixes, using Espa’s aromatherapy-led approach to ease stress and restore balance.
The neighborhood/area
Located on Cable Beach, about seven miles from the airport and five miles from downtown Nassau, the resort is well positioned for excursions—but designed so you rarely feel the need to leave. Think of it as a destination in itself rather than a jumping-off point.
The service
Service is efficient, with the infrastructure to support a resort of this scale. The concierge can arrange everything from spa bookings to golf tee times and off-property excursions. While less intimate than smaller hotels, it runs smoothly and reliably.
For families
Grand Hyatt is the most family-friendly of the three Baha Mar hotels, largely because of how easy everything is to access. From here, you’re closest to the water park, main pool areas, and much of the resort’s casual dining, which makes a difference when moving between activities throughout the day with a little one.
Between the beach club, the wildlife sanctuary, and the Kids Explorers Club, it’s easy to fill multiple days without repeating an activity. The Game Zone arcade area is another easy win, especially for younger kids and teens, with a mix of high-tech games and nostalgic favorites that make a great break from the sun.
The wildlife component in particular adds something different—flamingos, sea turtles, and tropical birds are woven into the resort in a way that feels interactive rather than staged, and the daily flamingo parade is a highlight. There’s also enough variety to keep older kids and teens engaged, from slides and surf simulators at the water park to tennis and pickleball at the John McEnroe Tennis Center.
Eco effort
Sustainability isn’t a defining part of the Grand Hyatt experience, but it shows up in quieter, more practical ways (like refillable stainless steel water bottles in the rooms) rather than overt messaging.
The broader Baha Mar resort carries more of that focus, particularly through the wildlife sanctuary and marine programming, which reflect the property's emphasis on conservation and environmental education.
Accessibility
The large-scale layout includes elevators, ramps, and accessible pathways throughout most areas of the property, and the hotel also offers ADA-compliant rooms. However, it’s worth confirming specific needs in advance.
Anything left to mention?
Plan ahead—especially for dining and spa reservations—as the resort’s size and popularity mean prime slots fill quickly. And don’t skip the wildlife experiences or flamingo parade; they’re some of the few elements that feel distinctly Bahamian, adding a sense of place to an otherwise highly polished resort.
Beyond that, golf is a big part of the draw, with the championship Royal Blue course offering a scenic change of pace if you’re looking to step away from the beach for a few hours. But between the beach club atmosphere, the water park, and the sheer range of activities, the challenge isn’t finding something to do—it’s fitting it all in.
