The Cape, A Thompson Hotel
At The Cape, a polished design meets seen-and-be-seen energy and cinematic views.

Why book?
The Cape is that rare Los Cabos hotel where polished Thompson design, seen-and-be-seen energy, and cinematic views of El Arco collide. Here, perched on a surf-pounded beach, every room faces the ocean, and every sunset is a spectacle.
Set the scene
The Cape sits on a bluff above Monuments Beach, the local surf break that draws board-toting regulars from dawn to dusk. The energy is distinctly grown-up and glamorous: 30-somethings in designer coverups, friend groups popping bottles by the pool, and couples in resort wear heading up to The Rooftop for one of Cabo’s best party scenes. The infinity pool is practically on top of the shoreline, creating that Instagram-worthy effect where the water appears to spill directly into the bay. But with reservation-only cabanas claiming many of the prime loungers (and the in-pool chairs disappearing early), the savvy move is the quieter second pool at the opposite end of the pool deck. (Although it certainly loses a bit of the allure.)
The pool bar and open-air lobby pump out smooth beats into the night, and The Rooftop becomes shoulder-to-shoulder at sunset and stays that way until late. If you’re after calm, this isn’t the place. If you want style and spectacle, you’re in for a treat.
The backstory
Opened in 2015, The Cape is Hyatt’s Thompson Hotels brand at its most refined. It’s a collaboration between Mexico City architect Javier Sánchez and famed interior designer Marisabel Gómez Vázquez, whose work leans into midcentury Mexican modernism with a moody, masculine edge. Thompson is known for urban-cool hotels, and The Cape carries that DNA into a beach setting with buzzy public spaces and plenty of social energy.
The rooms
The Cape’s 159 rooms, suites, and villas share a sleek Baja aesthetic with concrete, wood textures, and somewhat masculine neutrals paired with sculptural lighting and elements. My Deluxe King Ocean View room felt immediately transportive, anchored by soft neutrals and a freestanding copper tub. But the terrace is where I spent most of my time on the two-person daybed facing the Cabo Arch, watching surfers carve through the break, and taking in a sunset so saturated it could have been painted. Splurge on a corner room or suite for wraparound views and more privacy from the pool-bar soundtrack.
Food and drink
Dining at The Cape is one of its biggest calling cards, thanks in part to Manta, the hotel’s signature restaurant from
Enrique Olvera
. Head over early or book a reservation—you’ll feel the pull of its moody lighting, Baja-meets-Pacific-Rim menu, and views. Linger over sashimi with chiles, grilled local fish, and mezcal cocktails. The hotel’s more relaxed all-day restaurant, The Ledge, turns out unfussy but polished plates, and breakfast here is especially good: The Cape Toast is a heavenly brioche bread-pudding-like French toast with toasted banana, almonds, and fresh whipped cream. The Rooftop becomes the hot ticket at sunset, with crowds flocking for cocktails and panoramic views—go early if you want a seat, or make a reservation!
The spa
Tucked quietly away from the social pulse of the hotel, the spa is on the smaller side. Inside, you can expect standard treatments but little space for extras. There is an outdoor area with a sauna, steam room, and cold plunge, and hot pools with views of the arch. It’s good for your everyday treatment, but definitely not the centerpiece of the property.
The neighborhood/area
Set on the tourist corridor between Cabo San Lucas and San José del Cabo, this hotel is close enough to nightlife and marinas to enjoy a night out while still removed enough to escape it all when you want to. Directly below, Monuments Beach is a true surfers’ haunt; it’s not really swimmable, but endlessly entertaining to watch. Leave the resort to dine at Sunset Mona Lisa, a neighboring cliffside terrace where Mediterranean-leaning plates play second fiddle to the show-stopping tangerine-pink sunsets.
The service
While the service was always served with a smile, I found it to be less polished than the views. While the property was exquisite, there were a few hiccups: I never received my welcome drinks, I got the wrong checks after meals, and housekeeping didn't arrive until right before dinner. I thoroughly enjoyed my stay, but it’s not for those who want white-glove service and to be waited on hand and foot.
Eco effort
The Cape follows expected practices like basic water conservation and some local food sourcing, and has put in place some energy-efficient systems.
Accessibility
Elevators connect all major areas,
accessible rooms
are available, and public spaces are navigable. However, some oceanfront paths and pool areas may be trickier for guests with mobility issues.
Anything left to mention?
If you’re a light sleeper, request a room farther from the pool bar or The Rooftop—the music is ambient and relaxing until you want to sleep. And if you’re hoping for some poolside relaxation, it’s best to plan your strategy. Unless you rent a cabana, your best chance at a prime lounger is a reservation, which comes with an F&B minimum at Point Break Bar (a bit over $200 for two or $700 for eight).
