Nimb Hotel
Housed in a 1909 Moorish-inspired palace, Nimb blends its storied heritage with impeccable design and exceptional guest care.

Best for:
Anyone drawn to Copenhagen’s history and heritage—or looking for a reason to fall in love with it.
Location:
Bernstorffsgade, in the very heart of
Copenhagen
, near the Central Station and the abutting
Tivoli Gardens
Can’t-miss feature:
The magical Tivoli Gardens to which, as a hotel guest, you get access to unlimited entries—and rides.
In a nutshell:
Nimb comes with one very rare backyard privilege: an amusement park. That’s not the only quixotic detail to the hotel: there’s its Moorish palace-meets-the-Taj-Mahal façade and the white peacock that materializes at your breakfast table each morning. Quirky at first sight? Sure. But walk through the front doors and you’ll be met with a refined sense of style that carries through your stay—and remarkable standards of service that always feel like a cut above.
Rooms
The 38 rooms are spread across two wings of the hotel: the original 1909 palace building and the more modern 2017 extension, known as Tivoli Corner, designed by architects Pei Cobb Freed & Partners (the firm behind the contemporization of the
Louvre
in
Paris
). While the details in each room are unique—old wood-burning fireplaces in the original wing, for instance—the overall look is remarkably consistent: Nordic minimalism meets Asian opulence, an unconventional pairing that, you’ll have to trust me, just works.
I've only stayed in the newer rooms, which I love for their neat proportions and cleaner lines. My favorite so far has been the Executive Suite, a spacious one-bedroom, two-bathroom affair with an enormous sun-drenched living room and terrace. Throwing open the blinds to watch Tivoli Gardens come alive each morning, then ending the day on the terrace, timing my cocktail sips to the screams from the Golden Tower—a ride that rockets you skyward before suddenly letting go—is hard to beat. Inside, clean-lined Nordic design pairs unexpectedly well with 18th-century East Asian antiques. Contemporary touches from Bang & Olufsen sound systems to Dyson hairdryers and a Sjöstrand coffee machine (once you use one of these, other machines seem decidedly ordinary) complete the picture.
Food and drink
The hotel has one signature restaurant, Nimb Brasserie, as well as poolside food and drink service (that includes sushi from the popular Sticks'n'Sushi next door) and one large and very charming bar. The highlight meal for me has always been the French-inspired weekend brunch served alongside the Brasserie’s vantage point into Tivoli’s flower gardens. That broad, sunlit terrace is also where you can take an aperitif or lunch.
But when considering your food options here, you need to take into account the myriad options available inside Tivoli, too. Copenhagen’s top tourist attraction is known for many things: its romantic gardens and the Rutschebanen, its century-old wooden roller coaster. What’s less known is that it is also a compelling dining destinations: Rubbing shoulders with its rides and food stalls are some of the most quietly respected restaurants in the city. There’s Fru Nimb with its vast smørrebrød menu; Cakenhagen, a café that doles out beautiful pastries; and the historic Grøften restaurant, which has run since 1874. But it’s the Japanese Pagoda, a beautifully illuminated tower nestled by the park's lake, that is the pinnacle of the dining experience here. Each spring—and extending beyond summer—some of the most talked-about restaurants from across the world take up temporary residence as part of an annual pop-up program. In years past, heavyweights like Koks from the Faroe Islands and Singapore’s Odette have danced through its doors, and each year, the lineup only gets stronger.
Amenities and experiences
There's plenty to lure you out into the city but you'll always be glad to return: to the very chic, heated emerald-green rooftop pool; the bar with its whimsical murals and roaring fireplace; and the park that centers your stay. There’s also the 300 m2 oasis that is the wellness area, with everything from a hammam to cryotherapy and light treatments. Or, if you’re so inclined: a hypoxic chamber with cardio vascular equipment so you can work out at high altitude. And if you find you’ve over-exerted in one way or another, you’re in the right place: the turndown service each evening delivers sweet treats as well as foot baths and face masks to your bedside.

What to do nearby
This is Copenhagen—where everything feels within reach and the legs can somehow go for miles and miles (everyone that loves Copenhagen knows a 20,000-step day is no big deal). Here, you’ll be drawn to everything from the harbor bathing spots to the bakeries, the midcentury design stores and natural wine bars. And don’t skip past the city museums: DesignMuseum Denmark, Thorvaldsen’s, and Glyptotek are personal favorites. Still, if a central location and access to public transportation matter to you, you can’t do better than this: Nimb is right by the central station, which means getting to a top draw like the Louisiana Museum or the towns dotting the Danish Riviera further north is easy-peasy.
The backstory
There’s so much history seeping out of the walls of this hotel: the original structure was built in 1843 as a marketplace addition to Tivoli selling everything from chocolate to fine gloves and flowers—and eventually including a restaurant that became the talk of the town for its forward-thinking menus and global influences (that commitment to excellent dining remains woven through the hotel and the Gardens). In 1909, the Bazar was replaced by a more striking Moorish-style palace, which for decades was the heartbeat of the city’s artistic and social scene. In 2007, the building underwent a massive update and reopened as a luxury boutique hotel. Nimb is owned by Tivoli Gardens and has been a member of the Small Luxury Hotels Collection since 2011.
Insider tip
Make friends with staff. Not just because they are incredibly warm and kind, but they are also among some of the best-connected people in the city and are quick to open doors to experiences you otherwise wouldn’t know about.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes, definitively so. (Tip: Festive/winter season is particularly delightful if you’re visiting with children—Tivoli sparkles with thousands of twinkling lights, traditional Danish holiday treats are plentiful, and there are even custom in-room Christmas trees.)
Accessibility:
All floors at Nimb are serviced by an elevator, making the entire hotel easily accessible for wheelchair users. However, not all rooms are wheelchair accessible, so enquire with the booking team when making your reservation
Wellness:
The range of Nimb’s wellness offerings will blow you away. A full-fledged, strikingly designed spa awaits, with exceptional therapists and a very well-equipped gym.
