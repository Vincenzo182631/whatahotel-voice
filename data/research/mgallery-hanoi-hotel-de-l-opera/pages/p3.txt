Hotel de l’Opera Hanoi
Across from Hanoi's Opera House, this theatrical boutique hotel boasts plush furnishings, opera-inspired interiors, and one of the city's most memorable dining rooms.

Best for:
Couples; Accor loyalists; travelers who enjoy a slower, more leisurely pace
Location:
Tràng Tiền Street, Hoàn Kiếm, directly opposite the Hanoi Opera House and surrounded by Hanoi's luxury shopping district
Can't-miss feature:
Lautrec, the soaring glass-roofed French brasserie whose dramatic atrium is the hotel's most beautiful and distinct space
In a nutshell:
While the nearby Sofitel Legend Metropole embraces history, Hotel de l'Opéra leans into theater. Rich colors, plush furnishings, and opera-inspired interiors give this boutique hotel a distinct personality, while its French Quarter address makes it one of the city's most convenient places to stay.
Dan Q. Dao
Dan Q. Dao
Rooms
The hotel has 107 rooms and suites, from compact Deluxe Rooms to the L'Opéra Grand Suite. I'd request one of the recently renovated rooms, which feel noticeably fresher while retaining the property's theatrical identity. Inspired by the neighboring Opera House, the rooms feature velvet upholstery, dark timber floors, jewel-toned fabrics, and artwork drawn from the performing arts. I particularly liked the bathrooms, with illuminated mirrors that evoke backstage dressing rooms, even if they felt a little compact relative to the suite itself. The king bed was wonderfully plush—among the most comfortable I've slept in during my time in Hanoi—though I found the lighting throughout the room to be a touch too uniform, lacking some of the mood the decor seems to promise.

Dan Q. Dao
Food and drink
Lautrec, the brasserie-style restaurant, is the reason I'd linger around the hotel. Set beneath an enormous skylight with lanterns cascading from the ceiling and a beautiful fountain anchoring the center of the dining room, it's one of the most impressive dining spaces in Hanoi. I could have done without the large LED screen, which slightly disrupts the otherwise elegant atmosphere, but it's still the architectural heart of the hotel.
I visited Lautrec specifically for the hotel's long-running afternoon tea. Served inside a lacquer music box with a choice of TWG tea or Maison Marou hot chocolate, it's beautifully presented. The savory selection was pleasant enough, but the pastries and miniature cakes were genuinely excellent. Elsewhere, Son Mai offers intimate Vietnamese dining beneath the atrium, while La Fée Verte—Hanoi's only dedicated absinthe bar—has become a city institution.

Dan Q. Dao
Amenities and experiences
The semi-outdoor heated pool and terrace offer familiar views toward the Opera House and Tràng Tiền Street, making them particularly enjoyable in the late afternoon or during the city's holiday celebrations, when fireworks illuminate the entire skyline. A 24-hour fitness center and Harmonie Spa complete the wellness offerings.
The atmosphere at Hotel de l’Opera Hanoi feels noticeably different from newer boutique hotels. The crowd skews slightly older—Western couples lingering over breakfast, afternoon tea, and cocktails rather than rushing through sightseeing. It gives the property a slower, more relaxed rhythm that suits the neighborhood.
What to do nearby
The hotel sits on Tràng Tiền Street, one of Hanoi's smartest boulevards and home to luxury shops including Hermès, Louis Vuitton, Cartier, and Rolex. The Hanoi Opera House is directly across the street, while Hoàn Kiếm Lake, the Vietnam National Museum of History, and the Old Quarter are all within a short walk. The hotel itself has become something of a backdrop for Hanoi's young people, who frequently gather outside to photograph its distinctly European façade.

Dan Q. Dao
The backstory
Hotel de l'Opéra opened in 2011 on the site of the former Dân Chủ Hotel—a colonial-era property that was reclaimed by the Vietnamese government after the revolution—before eventually being redeveloped under Accor's MGallery brand. Its theatrical identity takes cues from the neighboring Hanoi Opera House. Working with Vietnam's National Archives, the hotel reproduced the Opera House's original architectural blueprints as artwork displayed throughout the property, while rich fabrics, dramatic interiors, and composer-inspired spaces reinforce the performance theme. A major renovation completed in 2025 refreshed many of the guest rooms while preserving the hotel's distinctive character.
Insider tip
As an older heritage building, renovations here can be ongoing. Request one of the recently renovated rooms when booking, then make time for afternoon tea at Lautrec. The pastries are the highlight, while the lacquer music box presentation remains a memorable moment of whimsy.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes, though it's better suited to couples and adult travelers
Accessibility:
The property is wheelchair accessible with elevators throughout the hotel
Wellness:
There's a semi-outdoor heated pool, Harmonie Spa, a sauna, and a 24-hour fitness center
Loyalty group/hotel brand:
MGallery Collection
