The Carlyle, A Rosewood Hotel
The Carlyle is an Upper East Side institution that feels like a world of its own.

In a nutshell
: An UES institution beloved by A-listers, known for its old-world charm, live music, and understated opulence.
Madison Flager
Madison Flager
Rooms
Rooms start at 360 square feet for a Superior (king bed) or Deluxe Twin room. Book a Central Park Suite, situated on floors 27–33, for some of the
best views of the park
in the city. (The Presidential and Empire Suites guarantee the same lush greenery out your window.) Wherever you lay your head, you'll find Art Deco elements and elegant but playful decor, with geometric-patterned carpets, eclectic art (I loved the small animal figurines encased in glass in the bedroom of my Rosewood Suite), marble bathrooms, and Ludwig Bemelman–printed wallpaper.
For those really splashing out, the aforementioned Presidential Suite is an icon itself. Spread across the 26th floor, it's home to three bedrooms (one with a king, two with queens), four and a half bathrooms, a living room, full kitchen, and media area, not to mention 360-degree views of the city, a piano, and a private elevator landing.
Madison Flager
Madison Flager
Food and drink
Food and bev is a huge draw here, from afternoon tea in The Gallery (the truffle egg salad sandwich was a standout), with a design inspired by the Sultan's dining room in Turkey's Topkapi Palace, to meals at Dowling's, open for breakfast, lunch, and dinner. I could’ve sat in there for hours gazing at the more than 200 works of art covering the walls. The collection includes a mix of modern-day pieces from New York–based artists and historic artwork from the hotel’s archives, including original paintings and illustrations from Ludwig Bemelman. The all-day restaurant's namesake, Robert Whittle Dowling, is a former owner of the hotel who is credited with putting it on the map as the place to stay for celebrities, politicians, and royalty visiting the city.
Of course, a stay at The Carlyle wouldn’t be complete without a nightcap at Bemelmans Bar. It's named after Ludwig Bemelman, an illustrator best known for the
Madeline
children's books; in 1947, he was commissioned to paint large-scale murals in the bar depicting all four seasons in nearby Central Park. It's now one of the only places where his commissions remain on public display. In the evenings, the space is packed to the brim with New Yorkers and visitors listening to pianists and jazz trios croon hits spanning decades. A generous portion of bar snacks is brought over to every table, but if you’re looking for a full meal, you’ve got options—my mom and I enjoyed the Caesar salad, sliders, and shrimp cocktail.
Over at Café Carlyle, the song and dance continue, with performers like Alan Cumming, Rita Wilson, and Judy Collins making regular appearances. Of note, reservations (and jackets) are required. The supper club is open Tuesday through Saturday, with the first show starting at 8:45 p.m. (and an occasional second at 10:45).
Madison Flager
Amenities and experiences
On the third floor, you'll find a gym, hair salon, and
spa
with a
menu
of facials and massages, including foot reflexology and a prenatal massage. Attentive service and perks like a town car on hand to take you anywhere in a 20-block radius, an extensive room service menu, and old-fashioned elevator operators do well to make every guest feel like a VIP. The one thing that surprised me was the fairly minimal amount of common areas—there’s some seating in the lobby for those looking to take a breather outside their room, and of course, the aforementioned dining venues, but aside from that, you’ll likely spend time on property in your room (not a bad place to be!).
What to do nearby
You’re right around the corner from Central Park, so a morning jog or afternoon stroll is a must. The Met is also close by (so much so that this hotel is teeming with guests making their way over to the annual Gala on the first Monday in May). The Albertine, tucked into the French cultural center, is a gorgeous bookstore to pop into (be sure to go upstairs to see the hand-painted domed ceiling). Also nearby: The Frick Collection, the Guggenheim, and popular restaurants like JG Melon, Cafe Commerce, and Chez Fifi.
The backstory
Few hotels are as iconic as The Carlyle—it’s a New York City institution, one with its own
documentary
, a recurring role in the Met Gala, and a roster of past guests that includes the British Royals, Hollywood A-listers like George Clooney and Jack Nicholson, and sports giants like Roger Federer. First opened in 1930, changed hands a few times before being acquired by
Rosewood Hotels & Resorts
in 2001.
Insider tip
While reservations are still recommended, hotel guests take priority when seating walk-ins at Bemelmans, so if you’re popping in, be sure to show your room key to cut down your wait time. Also, music extends beyond the downstairs venues—more than 20 suites boast
Steinway
or
Baldwin
baby-grand pianos.
