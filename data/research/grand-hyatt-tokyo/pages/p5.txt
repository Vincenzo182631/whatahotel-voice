### An Exceptional Hotel in an Extraordinary City

My wife and I recently had an amazing stay at the Grand Hyatt Tokyo, and I can say without hesitation that it was one of the finest hotel experiences we've ever had.

From the moment we arrived until the moment we departed, everything about this property was exceptional. The hotel itself is beautiful, impeccably maintained, and luxurious without ever feeling stuffy or pretentious. Our suite was absolutely gorgeous, incredibly comfortable, and a wonderful place to return to after long days exploring Tokyo.

What really sets the Grand Hyatt apart though, is the service. Every single interaction we had with the staff was warm, gracious, professional, and genuinely welcoming. There's a level of attention to detail and hospitality here that's difficult to describe until you've experienced it. Nothing ever felt forced or rehearsed. Everyone simply seemed to take genuine pride in making our stay special.

The dining was fantastic as well. We particularly enjoyed The Oak Door, where we had an outstanding meal (wagyu steak was off the charts good), and Chinadoor was another highlight. We also loved spending time at Maduro, the hotel's beautiful and sophisticated cigar bar. A very special thanks to manager Vinnie Miliano and waiter Simon Eriksson, whose warmth, professionalism, and hospitality made our visits especially memorable. They are both tremendous ambassadors for the hotel and perfect examples of the exceptional service that defines this property.

The hotel's location in Roppongi Hills is also excellent, with easy access to shopping, restaurants, transportation, and everything Tokyo has to offer.

This was our first trip to Japan together, a country my wife had dreamed of visiting for years, and the Grand Hyatt Tokyo played a significant role in making the experience so memorable. We fell completely in love with Tokyo, its people, and its extraordinary culture of hospitality. The hotel embodied so much of what made Japan special to us.

We've stayed at some wonderful hotels over the years, but the Grand Hyatt Tokyo truly stands out. We were genuinely sad to leave, and we're already talking about when we can return.

An enthusiastic five stars. We wouldn't hesitate to stay here again.

### Guest of Honor felt almost meaningless — disappointing service and poor value

I expected considerably more from Grand Hyatt Tokyo, particularly because I am an Explorist and used a Guest of Honor award for this four-night stay.
Check-in immediately felt disappointing. There was barely any greeting and no proactive recognition of either my Explorist status or Guest of Honor. I had to ask about my status myself.
I had booked about a month in advance and applied Guest of Honor well before arrival. The hotel was clearly aware of our stay because they had already prepared an anniversary setup in our room. Despite this, we received exactly the base room we booked, on a relatively low floor, with no meaningful upgrade or room improvement at check-in.
I understand that suites are not guaranteed. The issue was the complete lack of meaningful recognition. Not even a better floor, view or modest room-category improvement was offered initially.
The hotel later suggested moving us between other rooms, but the proposed arrangement could have required three room changes during a four-night stay. With luggage already unpacked and an anniversary setup in the original room, constantly packing and moving was obviously not practical. We therefore remained in the original room for the entire stay.
In my view, that should not be treated as though we were offered a useful upgrade and simply declined it. An elite benefit should make a stay more comfortable, not require guests to move rooms repeatedly just to receive it.
To be fair, the anniversary gesture itself was excellent. The hotel prepared the room thoughtfully and provided chocolates and a full bottle of Ruinart Blanc de Blancs. I genuinely appreciated that and want to give the hotel credit for it.
Unfortunately, breakfast was very disappointing. The food quality was poor for a hotel at this level, and the service was even worse on one morning.
Our table was largely ignored. We had no proper cutlery or napkins and nobody took a drink order. Near the end of breakfast we specifically asked for cutlery, napkins and drinks. Two coffees arrived, but the cutlery and napkins never did. We literally finished breakfast using disposable chopsticks.
We were later told that the restaurant was “very busy” and that we had been seated at a table that had not yet been completely cleared. That explanation was difficult to accept because we had repeatedly watched staff spend considerable time warmly chatting with a German family nearby and playing with their child while our table remained unattended.
The difference in attention made us extremely uncomfortable. More importantly, once we had explicitly asked for basic cutlery and napkins and still did not receive them, being “busy” was no longer a convincing explanation.
I should also mention that the staff member involved approached me again on our final day and personally apologized. I appreciated that, which is why I am deliberately not naming the employee. My criticism is about the overall service standard rather than one individual.
The room itself also felt tired. The bedding was visibly yellowed, worn and aged, which made it feel much less clean and fresh than I would expect from a Grand Hyatt. The electronic toilet also repeatedly flushed itself when nobody was using it, apparently because of a sensor issue, which was both strange and surprisingly noisy.
Overall, the value was very poor. Grand Hyatt Tokyo charges premium rates, but neither the room condition nor the consistency of service matched the price or brand positioning.
Most disappointing of all, Guest of Honor added very little meaningful value to the stay beyond lounge access. After this experience, I would have a difficult time recommending that anyone pursue Guest of Honor specifically for better treatment at this property.
The anniversary setup was genuinely appreciated, and the later apology was a positive gesture. I want to be fair about both. But taken as a whole, this was a disappointing stay and well below what I expected from Grand Hyatt Tokyo.
