Reviewed by
David Moralejo
Why book?
The first time I visited Santa Caterina was in 2012. An impromptu road trip on the Amalfi coast led me to make a stop at this celebrated hotel with its fascinating history. It received its first guests in 1904 after Dr. Giuseppe Gambardella and his family decided to transform their house into a small inn with only six guestrooms. It was not the Gambardella family’s first foray into hospitality. Around 1870 they had converted their home at the time (not far from Santa Caterina) into a small hotel.
Now, 120 years later, with 72 rooms and suites including three villas near the original building, Giuseppe’s granddaughters, Giusi y Ninni, continue the legacy along with three of his great-grandchildren, Crescenzo Gargano, Beatrice, and Alessandro Camera—the fourth generation of hoteliers within one family. All of them pull off a miracle, day after day. While continually updating and refurbishing the hotel, they manage to also keep it always grounded in its past. It has the feeling of a place where time stands still and every detail is perfectly Mediterranean and entirely Italian.
Set the scene
Beyond the majestic main building, which sits on one of the endless curves of the Amalfi road, there are two new villas in addition to an older one, the beautiful Romeo e Giulietta. They are hidden among the landscaped terraces that dot the cliff, a classic image of Amalfi. The villas provide a different hotel experience for guests. While they serve to increase the total number of rooms at Santa Caterina, they offer greater privacy to those guests who want it as they can be rented out entirely by groups.
In terms of other changes to the hotel, the staff points out subtle improvements that I might never notice. They are always focused on even the smallest of details. Everything remains perfect and unchanging, but thanks to this constant attention. The elevator takes you down rather than up, descending along the cliff while you take in the panoramic view through its window like a portal to paradise; the pool at sea level remains just as salty, appealing, and relaxing; the curtains of the suites, dance their same waltz in the breeze; the Vietri tiles sparkle with the same joy with their patterns of flowers, geometric figures, and bright colors, and the housekeepers ring your bell with their usual smile to deliver delicious chocolates at sunset. The hotel’s gourmet restaurant, Glicine, now has a Michelin star, and the breakfasts, which were already a memorable feast, are even more so now.
Set the scene
The success of Santa Caterina, a member of The Leading Hotels of The World, is well known. It is celebrated not only as an overnight accommodation, but also as a romantic location for weddings and other celebrations. Couples, families with children, and solo travelers, as in our case, are welcome. Every guest feels the warmth of home here.
The usual hustle and bustle of Amalfi becomes peaceful at the hotel. An early bird trick of mine: Take an easy stroll into town while it’s still waking up, do some shopping, visit the Cathedral of St. Andrew the Apostle, with its photogenic staircase, and return to the hotel to enjoy its peaceful atmosphere. In the late afternoon Amalfi is always lively for an aperitif or dinner, but you will be able to wander the streets with fewer crowds than in the middle of the day.
The rooms
All of the rooms and suites have balconies, terraces, or large windows with views of the Amalfi coast. In room 22, to pick one example, the huge terrace will make you want to simply pass the day admiring the view. Furnished with family heirlooms from the early 20th century, the most iconic feature is undoubtedly its floors, again in multicolored Vietri tiles. Perhaps the most special suite is the Romeo e Giulietta, an intimate two-story villa perched high on the cliff above the sea with a terrace equipped with a heated infinity pool and solarium.
In some of the new villas, christened
Le Ville della Marchesa
, details include headboards upholstered with the famous Missoni zigzag and bathrooms that combine the original gray marble with the brightness of Vietri. Living areas have midcentury shelves and comfortable sofas. All the bath amenities are from Bvlgari’s white tea collection, with its delicate fragrance.
Food and drink
Let's start at the beginning of the day, with breakfast. Of course it is paired with a view of the sea, like everything else here. The classic buffet is an abundance of fresh seasonal fruit, vegetables, fresh mozzarella, Italian cheeses and cold meats, breads, and a splendid selection of pastries (be sure to try the
sfogliatelle,
delicious Neapolitan puff pastry filled with cream). It is complemented by a la carte dishes, eggs to order, and toast. The silverware and crisp cotton napkins are paired with cheerful local ceramic tableware.
Throughout the day, La Terrazza offers a selection of cocktails, bites, and snacks. We opted for a lunch of an excellent club sandwich paired with a lemonade, which seemed appropriate here in the kingdom of lemons. Restaurant al Mare is a stone’s throw from the sea, as well as the pool, making it the perfect choice during a long day of sunbathing and splashing in the water. A pasta with the fish of the day, accompanied by one of the many wines in the cellar, make for a perfectly unforgettable meal.
Glicine is open only for dinners and boasts a Michelin star thanks to the talent of its chef, Giuseppe Stanzione, who reimagines classic Mediterranean dishes with both a la carte and tasting menus.
A magnificent semi-open space with a huge open kitchen will open in 2025. It aims to be a hymn to Italian cuisine with wood-fired dishes and authentic flavors. There will also be a sushi bar featuring local, fresh fish.
Spa and wellness
The intimate spa offers a number of different treatments and beauty rituals, but the standout one is Oro di Amalfi, an exclusive massage with natural lemon oils that relaxes and stimulates the chakras. A gym with Technogym equipment and windows with views of the sea completes the experience. But there is nothing better than a dip in the Mediterranean or, if the surf prevents it—the lifeguards will let you know if it’s safe to enter the sea—then swim a few lengths in the pool, with its filtered water from the sea.
Anything left to mention?
When you approach Amalfi from Santa Caterina—the hotel has a free shuttle service throughout the day, but you can also walk there—you will see countless ceramic stores. You’ll want to buy everything, but it will be hard to resist the desire to take home a tile or two like those that form the floors of Santa Caterina. Beautifully preserved for more than a century, they are a gleaming reflection of the essence of the hotel. Pure Italy. Nothing more but also nothing less than that.
