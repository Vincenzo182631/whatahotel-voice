Why book?
For a French-colonial stunner in the heart of Ho Chi Minh City. Opulence abounds, with its crystal chandeliers, museum-worthy art, elegant afternoon tea, and white-glove service.
Set the scene
The hotel’s polished aesthetic invites a classy, cosmopolitan crowd. Out front, valets park a vintage 1970s Mercedes-Benz under the porte-cochère. While inside, well-heeled women in designer dresses chat over cocktails, A-list influencers snap photos at the iconic afternoon tea, and sophisticated couples sunbathe by the pool. The style is tasteful and refined; silk scarves, leather loafers, and chic blazers are typical attire.
The backstory
Park Hyatt Saigon stands on the former site of the Brinks Hotel Officers' Quarters, where US military officials lived during the Vietnam War. Its 2005 debut marked Hyatt’s first foray in Vietnam and ushered in a new era of luxury hotels in Ho Chi Minh City. The French colonial building is a homage to the city’s history, with an ivory-colored façade that matches the Saigon Opera House next door. After extensive renovations between 2015 and 2019, the property was transformed into a vision of old-world opulence. Public spaces are decorated with mahogany Belle Époque desks, George III-style Chinoiserie cabinets, Oriental rugs, and silver leaf ceilings. Black-and-white photographs and priceless lacquer paintings by Vietnamese artists adorn the walls, courtesy of Ben Thanh Art Gallery.
The rooms
Each of the 245 guest rooms feels like a proper pied-à-terre. They feature marble bathrooms, plush carpets, antique dressers, and custom Hanoia lacquerware. For a quiet respite, book a Park Deluxe room with a patio facing the poolside courtyard garden. Suites are fit for royalty, with a spacious separate living room and large picture windows bordered by French shutters.
Food and drink
The restaurants and bars maintain the same elevated glamour. Park Lounge knows how to make an entrance, with a sweeping staircase, crystal chandeliers, and a baby grand piano that is played in the evenings. Here, afternoon tea is a time-honored tradition. The all-day eatery Opera serves a sensational Sunday brunch, family-style Italian cuisine, and aperitivo on the terrace. On the second floor, Square One restaurant masters the art of fine dining. It has two open-concept kitchens, where chefs create innovative Vietnamese dishes in one and French brasserie classics in the other. Highlights include: Hokkaido scallop carpaccio, Long An-style duck breast, and Bến Tre king prawn phở with caviar. Have the sommelier pair your meal with a rare French bottle from the impressive wine cellar. Then, top it off with a nightcap at 2 Lam Son cocktail bar—the Lychee Martini is to die for.
The neighborhood/area
Park Hyatt is smack dab in the center of District 1, steps from the Saigon Opera House. Stroll around the neighborhood to see other famous landmarks like the Saigon Central Post Office, Notre-Dame Cathedral, and the bustling Ben Thanh Market. Not sure where to start? The concierge has maps of Ho Chi Minh City to point you in the right direction. They’ll recommend day trips (definitely do the Mekong Delta) and cultural experiences, such as the AO acrobatics show and the XQ silk embroidery workshop.
The service
Staff are quietly observant, instead of overly formal. They always address you by name and check in if you need anything or would like your favorite drink refilled—without you even asking. The concierge keeps a finger on the pulse of the city; they know every hidden gem and will happily tailor trips to your interests. They can even get you a table at some of the hottest restaurants.
Accessibility
Ramps and elevators make the building very accessible, and ADA-approved guest rooms have wider doorways, roll-in showers, and grab rails for stability.
Anything left to mention?
There are on-site tours for in-the-know guests (just tell the concierge to reserve a spot). On weekends at 5 p.m., an hour-long art tour showcases the hotel’s most prized pieces. Or, you can join the complimentary Chocolaterie tour on weekdays at 3 p.m. to learn how they make the artisanal treats. Build your own box of bonbons or macarons from the French patisserie as a sweet souvenir. Vietnamese cooking classes and chocolate-making are an added bonus.
