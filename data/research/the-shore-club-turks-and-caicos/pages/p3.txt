The Shore Club Turks and Caicos
A luxurious resort on Provo’s quieter side whose water is some of the calmest and clearest in the world.

Reviewed by
Emily Adler Sach
Why book?
For the type of deep, disorienting relaxation that makes you forget what day it is. Set on a wide, quiet stretch of Long Bay Beach, the resort feels removed but not remote. Pools are spaced out by lush tropical greenery, and beach loungers are spread far enough apart that you never feel crowded. It’s the kind of place where you can make friends over drinks or in the hot tub or spend an entire afternoon in silence without a single interruption.
Set the scene
The Shore Club sits on Long Bay Beach, the quieter counterpoint to the buzzy stretch of Grace Bay on the opposite side of Providenciales. While Grace Bay draws more of the headlines, Long Bay feels like an insider’s choice. The water here is strikingly clear and remarkably shallow—you can walk incredibly far toward the buoys without it rising much above your hips (or barely past your knees if you’re taller). Schools of small fish whoosh by, conch shells are scattered throughout the shore, and on calm mornings, you might even spot a stingray gliding by.
As for the crowd, there’s a noticeable group of well-seasoned snowbirds, many of them condo owners who return for weeks at a time each winter, children and grandchildren in tow. But it’s not exclusively retirees: Young couples on long-weekend escapes and honeymooners make up a decent share of the guest list too. No one is competing for attention—the aesthetic leans East Coast understated luxury, with linen sets, oversized sun hats, and Hermès slides rather than logo-forward designer pieces—but most guests dress just polished enough to match the surrounding setting.
The hotel’s focus on wellness is also a draw—beyond the Dune Spa, there’s a lap pool and gym tucked beside the yoga pavilion, as well as tennis courts that can be reserved for both tennis and pickleball. It’s easy to fill your days with structured activity if that’s your jam, but my personal favorite morning exercise was a long, uninterrupted walk along Long Bay’s straight, expansive shoreline, where no navigation was required.
The backstory
The hotel opened in 2017, a little over 10 years after its sister property The Palms started welcoming guests on the opposite side of the island along Grace Bay. CEO Stan Hartling is the mastermind behind both of these hotels, and is a name to know on the island—you’ll likely find him hanging around both properties talking to longtime guests and staff.
The rooms
Nearly 140 rooms and and suites and 6 private villas make up the property. Accommodations range from Garden, Pool, and Ocean View suites to sprawling one- to three-bedroom residences and a handful of penthouses.
I checked into an oceanfront one-bedroom suite that was completely engulfed in sunlight reflected off the turquoise waters and streaming in through the wraparound windows. The space felt like a chic beachfront apartment, with a generous kitchen, airy living room with a television, spa-like bathroom with a tiled shower and a big soaking tub, and plush king bed. Standout details included the cloud-like robes, abundance of towels, and—one of my personal favorites—a travel-sized bottle of Crest mouthwash. It’s such a small gesture, but one that’s often overlooked by most hotels, despite it being one of those doomed toiletries that often explodes in transit.
The large balcony was one of the most breathtaking I’ve ever seen, with several seating areas and incredible panoramic views that spanned from the endless blues of the ocean to lush greenery framing the rest of the property. And while the sound of crashing waves would’ve been welcomed, the suite was well soundproofed: no hallway chatter, and no upstairs footsteps (a particular luxury for anyone accustomed to apartment living in New York City).
Lastly, a peek inside the just-renovated two-bedroom penthouse was nothing short of spectacular—it features expansively high ceilings, panoramic views on two sides, multiple indoor and outdoor seating areas, and two bedrooms with more available to connect as needed.
Food and drink
Breakfast is served at Almond Tree, an indoor-outdoor space that also operates for dinner. In the mornings, do a lap of the buffet to choose between made-to-order omelets, fresh fruit, cereals, pancakes and waffles, breads and pastries, and rotating hot sides including breakfast meats and hash browns. A pamphlet of daily news sits on each table—a thoughtful touch, and a great way to avoid your phone first thing in the morning but still feel up to speed.
For more casual dining, the hotel offers beachside and poolside service throughout the day. The menu includes classics like salads, pizzas, fish tacos, sandwiches, and orders arrive promptly without long waits. There’s also a small bar directly on the beach, offering a quieter alternative for an afternoon drink. It’s simple and understated (you might even almost miss it!), providing a convenient place to gather or step out of the sun without the formality of a full restaurant setting.
Sui-Ren is the hotel’s signature high-end restaurant, focusing on Japanese cuisine with Peruvian influences. The menu is filled with refined staples like ceviches, spicy tuna crispy rice, sushi and sashimi, and several delightful desserts. It’s the most elevated of the on-site options and well-suited for a more formal evening—many diners were from outside the resort, as it’s become a destination of its own within Provo.
The spa
The facilities at The Dune Spa are small but mighty, providing great services without many bells or whistles. Walk through the tranquil hidden walkway—completely surrounded by lush greenery—to find the check-in desk, and you’ll already be whisked away into relaxation mode. Massages and other treatments take place in little huts, which your technician will guide you to. The main building holds locker rooms, which include a single outdoor shower with maximum privacy; I was practically floating on air after my Dune Signature massage. More specialized services like Thai massage, Shiatsu massage, and a slew of wraps, facials, and scrubs are available too.
The neighborhood/area
At a place like the Shore Club you’ll never really feel compelled to get up and leave, but if you do get restless, there's plenty to do nearby. The hotel is located in the Providenciales (known as Provo to locals and regulars) region of Turks and Caicos, also home to the international airport and majority of upscale hotels and dining on the island—most tourists spend time here as it’s more highly developed than the rest of the islands, and home to some of the most pristine beaches like Grace Bay and Long Bay Beach. It’s where Drake vacations—he’s often spotted at Noah's Ark Beach Club, a popular floating bar located just East of the aforementioned areas in the Leeward Settlement. You may find him and other celebs at Da Conch Shack—people gravitate there on Wednesday nights for a party-like atmosphere, when weekly special events are typically hosted.
Also worth checking out in the area: Vita restaurant at Rock House hotel for pastas, seafood, and an unforgettable sunset; Indigo or Blue Water Bistro (beachside) at Wymara for a nice vibe and really good food; and Coco Bistro, a consistent top choice for visitors. Infiniti restaurant at Grace Bay Club is great for a very upscale evening, but Parallel 23 at The Palms, known for its fish, pasta, and excellent desserts, remains one of the very best meals of my trip.
On property, Shore Club offers guests complimentary non-motorized water sports equipment, like snorkels, sea kayaks, paddle boards, and hobie cats. You’ll also find a variety of more adventurous water sports nearby the hotel available like jet skiing and kite surfing.
The service
Helpful, laid-back, and friendly. Staff helped with bags as needed, called for taxis promptly, and always welcomed us back with a friendly smile upon returning from dinner or other outings. Staff on the beach and by the pool were abundant and helpful, and the front desk was easily reachable by phone whenever we needed throughout our stay.
For families
The complimentary Jungle Jam kids' club draws a lot of families, as it provides half and full days of activities for younger guests ages 4–12. I personally witnessed several kids tugging their parents asking to go back to Jungle Jam, so I’d pretty confidently say it has the kid stamp of approval.
Accessibility
Guests will find accessible pathways across many of the resort’s shared spaces, including restaurants, pools, and beachfront areas, and elevator access in multi-level buildings. Additional transportation support, including airport transfers and on-island mobility coordination, can also be arranged.
Anything left to mention?
Note that there is a free shuttle to their sister property,
The Palms
, that runs every so often—it’s best to check with the front desk on exact timing but it definitely comes in handy. Otherwise, your best bet is to call a taxi to get around, though they don’t run cheap. I wouldn’t personally recommend renting a car in Providenciales, especially as an American traveler, as you’ll need to drive on the left side and not all roads are well paved, so it’s worthwhile to plan out dinners and keep taxi prices in mind as needed.
