Soho Grand Hotel

Best for:
A romantic getaway or partying friend group
Location:
Straddling SoHo and Tribeca along a quiet West Broadway block, Soho Grand is only a skip away from shopping and sightseeing galore.
Can’t-miss feature:
A martini and live music in ultrastylish cocktail bar Club Room
In a nutshell:
Tucked away from SoHo’s crowds but still within arms reach of its buzz, the neighborhood’s first luxury boutique hotel holds up as its best.
Skyli Alvarez
Skyli Alvarez
Rooms
Spacious with stellar views of
downtown Manhattan
, the Grand Corner King room harks back to SoHo’s industrial past with a sleek, boho chic twist. Room 1134’s distressed leather and cast iron accents, dimmable overhead lighting, and wide plank oakwood flooring counterbalance colorfully ombre shades, brocade throws, and a cushy seafoam blue loveseat at the foot of the (very plush) bed. As far as tech goes, there’s a 65-inch Samsung smart television with streaming capabilities and a Marshall speaker with quite a fabulous sound system at the marble work desk. Plus, the minibar’s well stocked with just about everything, from a portable charger and local chocolates to Andy Warhol playing cards.
The bathrooms feature a walk-in rain shower and adjustable handheld shower (also with dimmable lighting capability), marble vanity, lit mirror, and quite charming Saul Steinber–illustrated bird wallpaper—a common motif found throughout the hotel.

Skyli Alvarez
Food and drink
Open until 2 a.m. on Fridays and Saturdays, bustling and uber-retro
Soho Diner
is the property’s idea of a classic eatery, sans the prices (it’s SoHo, after all). A coffee counter by the restaurant’s hotel entrance is convenient for guests on the go, but make it a point to sit down for
brunch
with a milkshake and a hearty fried chicken sandwich or
burger
. If you’ve found yourself impatiently caught in the weekend crowd, head to the relaxed, teal-accented
Grand Bar and Salon
in the hotel lobby, with breakfast (get the buttermilk pancakes!) through late night offerings and DJs spinning vinyl most evenings.
Open May through late September, the airy and palm-treed beach bar
Gilligan’s
brings
Montauk
to
Manhattan
, with generous lobster rolls and oysters to share, plus flatbread pizzas and a must-try watermelon marg. Once dusk sets in, shimmy up to the moody
Club Room
for jazz and cocktails, candlelit.

Skyli Alvarez
Amenities and experiences
The hotel is especially pet friendly, as reflected by the cast-iron dog sculptures nestled throughout its lobby—which allegedly someone once tried to steal—and cozy dog park around the corner on Grand Street, available to guests and
annual members
. Pet bowls, beds, and crates are available upon request.
Soho Grand’s newest art endeavor is
The Gallery
, updated quarterly. Recent exhibits include illustrations from designer Bob Mackie and photographs by former Mudd Club bouncer Richard Boch.
What to do nearby
If you’re staying in Manhattan’s retail district, odds are you’ll want to shop. Why not skip the chains for a taste of arty old SoHo at
Women’s History Museum
, which, despite its name is not a museum but might as well be, considering the amount of true antiques and rare designer pieces that hang on its brazenly painted walls. For American vintage, opt for
Church Street Surplus
, whose storefront likely looks exactly how it did when it opened in 1972. Susan at the counter will strike up a conversation and make you do a twirl or two in ’60s denim and tweed skirts.
Meander along Lispenard and Broadway for contemporary galleries like
Galerie Sardine
,
Sargent’s Daughters
, or
Shrine
, whose exhibits are always changing. Walk on over to
The Roxy Hotel
, a sister property of Soho Grand, for lively jazz in its bohemian cellar bar
The Django
(but be prepared for the two-drink minimum). Or try well-loved
Bubby’s
for morning brunch or breakfast-as-dinner with some of Manhattan’s fluffiest pancakes.
The backstory
Opened in 1996 as SoHo’s first luxury boutique hotel, Soho Grand helped put its namesake neighborhood on the map among the aesthetes and style-inclined. The hotel was one of the first to install a lobby DJ booth, and Radiohead hosted their press launches for
OK Computer
and
Kid A
here. Its interiors, helmed by Bill Sofield, are imbued with tasteful whimsy—peacock murals, sinuous Great Dane sculptures, statement light fixtures, and watercolor florals—while its design shines light on SoHo’s industrial heritage, with a staircase lit by bottle glass, cast-iron molding, and masonry columns akin to nearby old warehouses. In 2019, the property underwent a full renovation, with terrace suite additions and updated bedroom interiors. Thirty years on, Soho Grand’s certainly still got its eclectic edge.
Insider tip
The fitness center on the third floor has an airy balcony that’s perfect for morning yoga. Don’t leave Soho Diner without snapping a picture or two at its photo booth and playing its Crosley Vinyl Jukebox, featuring downtown staples like Velvet Underground and LCD Soundsystem. Plus, if you’re going to Club Room for dinner and live music (7:30 and 9:30 p.m. shows) but want to return when it transforms into a voguish night club, just keep your stamp on and no further questions will be asked.
