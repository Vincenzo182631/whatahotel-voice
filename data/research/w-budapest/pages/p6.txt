W Budapest

Reviewed by

Why book W Budapest?
Five-star indulgence in a grand Budapest hotel (the Instagram captions write themselves) that's set to rejuvenate the W brand.
Set the scene
Taking over the UNESCO-listed Drechsler Palace, this is a design-lover's dream stay. Original neo-renaissance features such as the huge arched doorways and stained glass windows have been restored or faithfully recreated, but despite its history, there's a thoroughly modern buzz about the place. Fashionably dressed guests pose for photos beneath the glass ceiling of the courtyard while locals sip cocktails and gossip on the terrace. Every detail is carefully considered, right down to the moon-like globe lighting (which we're told is meant to emulate the bulbs surrounding a starlet's dressing room mirrors), and a calming palette of forest greens, powder pinks and deep blues gives the space a classic yet contemporary feel.
The backstory
This sleek space ushers in a new age of W. The chain has previously been known best as smart, sexy spots for drinks post-work and pre-club, but a welcome revamp aims to transform them into high-end hotels where you'll want to spend the night rather than merely start it. They couldn't have chosen a better spot for this metamorphosis than the imposing Drechsler Palace. Built in 1866 by legendary architects Ödön Lechner and Gyula Pártos and previously home to luxury apartments, a grand café, a bowling alley, the headquarters of the Hungarian Ballet Institute and (or so the rumour says) a bordello, it's already a dab hand at reinvention.
The rooms
There are 151 rooms in total – though all are slightly different, they're united in their cool, contemporary feel. Standard doubles are elegantly kitted out in rich shades of blue and chrome, with floor-to-ceiling windows (ask for a front-facing room for impressive views of the Opera House). The corner suites are larger and more striking, and those on the fifth floor take on chic, cosy cabin vibes courtesy of sloping wood ceilings and warm orange accents. The all-black Extreme WOW suite and all-white WOW suite draw inspiration from the building's dance heritage, representing
Swan Lake
's transfigured princess Odette and her evil double Odile; with classical murals, sculptural sofas and a huge circular bed taking centre stage, they're as showstopping as any prima ballerina.
The food and drink
The main restaurant is Nightingale by Beef Bar, which offers a suitably meaty, Asian-inspired menu: the beef nigiri with truffle butter is a particular highlight. But there are plenty of fish dishes and vegetarian options, too – the tangy wakame salad and light-as-air bao buns stuffed with shiitake and enoki mushrooms were both big hits around the table. The most exciting space, though, is the basement bar Society25, an intimate, dimly-lit speakeasy accessed through a bathroom mirror with a cleverly curated cocktail menu inspired by influential moments and figures from
Budapest
's history such as Zsa Zsa Gabor and Harry Houdini.
The spa
A spa has to be something special to stand out amongst Budapest's iconic thermal baths, and the Away Spa certainly is. Inside a mesmerising hall of mirrors inspired by Houdini's illusions, massages with seasonal Hungarian essential oils and rejuvenating mud wraps release any and all tension. Afterwards, soak up the atmosphere in the relaxation space, surrounded by endless reflections of the tiled pool and rippling water.
The neighborhood
Like most of Budapest, this area is a mix of glamour and grunge. The hotel faces onto the grandiose Andrássy Avenue, home to the opera house and an array of high-end boutiques such as Louis Vuitton, but leaving the main drag reveals edgier and arguably far more interesting streets. It's close to the old Jewish quarter, famed for its magnificent synagogue and impressive street art, and there are plenty of coffee shops and bars scattered around to refuel as you explore. Infamous ruin bar Szimpla Kertz may be more stag-do than secret hideaway nowadays, but a charming farmer's market takes over on selected weekend mornings if you'd like to see its chaotic, maximalist interiors in daylight. The area is also excellent for vintage shopping: Love Child Vintage is a pastel-pink dream with shelves overflowing with pretty jewellery and retro floral dresses, while Antifaktory offers a more rockstar vibe with original band t-shirts and weathered leather.
The service
Service couldn't be nicer or more accommodating. The bar staff, in particular, are eager to talk you through the menu, recommending
cocktails
and whipping up something unique based on your preferences – and if you enjoy their creations a little too much, the tray of complimentary fiery ginger shots at breakfast the next morning will help blast away the hangover.
Eco effort
Sustainability
is a major consideration here, with all electricity obtained from renewable sources, a recycling service that redirects 80 per cent of waste from landfill and an air-conditioning system specially chosen for its energy efficiency. In-room, all the bathroom amenities are zero carbon with refillable products rather than mini disposables in the shower, and drinking water is supplied in glass bottles rather than plastic.
Accessibility for people with mobility impairments
There are rooms available with accessible bathrooms, and the hotel also offers an ADA compliance kit to support guests where applicable. All the restaurants and bars are on level surfaces and can be accessed via lifts to support guests with mobility issues.
Anything left to mention?
Chess is one of Hungary's most popular pastimes and one that the hotel's design pays frequent homage to: once you notice the black and white grid pattern, you'll see it everywhere, from shower tiles to the lift floors. To celebrate 150 years of reunification between Buda and Pest, W Budapest commissioned artist Ronan Mckenzie to create a bespoke chessboard; the organic, abstract boards can be found in the hotel's top suites or rented for games in Society25 and the courtyard.
