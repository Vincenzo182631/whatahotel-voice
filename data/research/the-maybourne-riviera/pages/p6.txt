The Maybourne Riviera
The French debut for the Maybourne Hotel Group has redefined the sleepy village of Roquebrune-Cap-Martin.

Why book?
For spectacular uninterrupted views of the Côte d’Azur, seen from every room.
Set the scene
A 10-minute drive up the winding road from Monte-Carlo, The Maybourne Riviera has been built on a cliff face above the village of Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, in a convenient location just between Menton and Monaco, with Mount Agel behind. Presenting what must be some of the Riviera's most impressive views, every room of the hotel has access to 180-degree uninterrupted vistas of the Mediterranean sea and coastline stretching from Italy all the way along to the Principality and beyond.
The backstory
Housed in an ultra-modern building—once the Vista Palace, before Wilmotte & Associés were tasked with reimagining it—The Maybourne Riviera sits in the Qatari-owned Maybourne Group's portfolio; the same owners behind Claridge's, The Emory, and The Berkeley hotels.
The rooms
Sixty-five studios, including 24 suites, make up the Maybourne Riviera's diverse room offering that begins with 60- and 65-square-meter sea view studios, smaller but no less impressive than the one- and two-bedroom sea view suites, sized between 95 and 115 square meters. A step up are the beautiful duplex suites accompanied by private plunge pools and large furnished terraces, but the jewels in the crown of the glass-fronted seaside property are the spectacular, individually designed signature suites.
I stay in the one-bedroom, Rigby & Rigby designed, 165-square-meter Panoramic Suite that displays the ocean from every corner. On entry, the floor-to-ceiling windows frame a cloudless blue sky with a panoramic view of the coastline from the terrace, and even directly from the bed. The huge bathroom is fitted with his and hers rain showers and sinks, and a large travertine marble bathtub in the middle. The walk-in dressing room presents an uninterrupted view of the sea, so you don't miss the view for a minute, even while blow-drying your hair. Designed to feel like a private apartment, stacks of books on art and design line the shelves, and the living area comes with a large writing desk and huge Smart TV (not that you'll be watching it). It really is breathtaking.
Also worth noting: All rooms are kitted out with Smart TVs, Bang & Olufson Bluetooth speaker, and Dyson hairdryers. Every stay includes an arrival transfer, with the larger suites also inclusive of a return drop-off and personalized meet-and-greet service at Nice airport. Complimentary sun loungers at La Môme Riviera, daily breakfast, welcome bottle of Champagne on ice, access to Surrenne spa and daily shuttle to Monaco are also part of all room categories.
Food and drink
Every restaurant at the hotel is blessed with breathtaking views. Wake up early and enjoy a petit déjeuner of freshly baked croissants, slices of smoked salmon with sourdough, and a frothy cappuccino at Riviera Restaurant by Chef Sébastien Tantot, surrounded by sea and sky. Pause from a day of relaxation by the infinity pool for a light lunch at La Piscine (open for lunch and dinner), where the extensive menu encompasses a refreshing spin on a classic burrata salad by pairing it with delicate slices of tomato and strawberry carpaccio, taramasalata with rye crisps, Wagyu katsu sando, grilled fillet of sea bass, and a large selection of freshly baked pizza if you're seeking something a bit heartier, all served against the backdrop of the dazzling Med.
For pre-dinner drinks and nibbles, a table on the terrace at Le 300 is in high demand. Led by mixologist, Julien Lecharpentier, who muddles creative concoctions inspired by the ocean, served with a casual but elevated menu that consists of a smoked salmon club sandwich, steak frites, fish and chips, or bitesize blinis and deviled eggs.
In 2025, abc Kitchens (also at The Emory) by New York–based chef Jean-Georges Vongerichten, has replaced Mauro Colagreco's Ceto. Located in the same elegant space on the 14th floor, surrounded by floor-to-ceiling glass windows, the restaurant is more casual in mood with a menu designed to encourage local clientele to return for dinner more often to sample an array of mezze-style small plates of squash blossom quesadilla, green chickpea hummus, and crispy calamari to share, alongside a long list of freshly caught seafood grilled over charcoal, crab, or caviar linguine and a number of sustainable vegetarian salads, ricotta ravioli, and beetroot carpaccio. Again, enjoyed surrounded by mountain, sea, and sky.
Also new for 2025, Cannes institution, La Môme, has taken over the Maybourne's beautiful beach club with La Môme Riviera, a day-to-night venue nestled on the edge of the water at Cap-Martin, populated with orange-and-white parasols and loungers, transforming into a romantic candlelit restaurant at night.
Spa and wellness
New for summer 2025 is Surrenne Riviera, the second Surrenne wellness concept after a successful debut at London's The Emory in 2024. Described as a longevity sanctuary, the facilities are spread across three stories, with an exquisite gym and fitness studio where you can pound the treadmill overlooking the Med, or take Lagree and spin classes surrounded by nothing but blue sky. The spa has been finished with a sauna, steam, and cold plunge out on the terrace, so you can push your boundaries while perusing the super yachts waiting patiently in the bay below.
The service
Flawless, as expected. I left my bikini behind and the concierge kindly shipped it back to me in Dubai, without question.
Eco effort
The Maybourne Riviera's restaurants use seasonal ingredients in the menus and support local producers as a priority. For more information on sustainability practices, visit the Maybourne website.
Accessibility
The Maybourne Riviera offers suites and studios adapted to people with disabilities and reduced mobility, contact the property for more in-depth information.
Anything else to mention
The hotel has four independent floors of four to five bedrooms that are available for floor privatization, allowing families and groups traveling together to enjoy a uniquely personalized experience. As part of a floor privatization, the hotel includes round-trip transfers, a two-hour sunset cruise, a complimentary 60-minute massage per guest, exclusive access to the beach with complimentary loungers, free shuttle to Monaco, minibar with complimentary treats and soft drinks, and full spa access.
The property has an ongoing schedule of complimentary experiences that include Pilates and sunrise yoga classes, cocktail and pizza-making masterclasses, guided hikes on the Le Corbusier Trail and cultural outings to Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, among many others. A full list is available from the concierge.
