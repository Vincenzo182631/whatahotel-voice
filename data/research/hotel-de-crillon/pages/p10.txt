Hôtel de Crillon, A Rosewood Hotel
A living, breathing landmark transported into the 21st century with utmost precision and care.

Why book?
Elegant, historic, and sophisticated, the Hôtel de Crillon is an ode to all things Paris. If you’re a Francophile, this is the hotel to visit.
Set the scene
Hôtel de Crillon straddles two universes: a historic destination that has seen the likes of Louis XV and Marie Antoinette and utter luxury in the heart of Paris. Interiors are palatial and perfectly matched to the grandeur of the exterior. The lobby sees a long hallway branch off into various bars, restaurants, patisseries, and the spa. The hotel continuously reveals surprises and architectural delights, with guests checking in via an inviting room laden with elaborate wood detailing and plush chairs. If you peek across the hall, you can glimpse the bar, Les Ambassadeurs, a true feat of conservation and transformation.
The backstory
Rosewood Hotels & Resorts are no strangers to luxury, focusing on meticulously reflecting the destination’s history, culture and individuality in each hotel. This is personified in Hôtel de Crillon, A Rosewood Hotel. A true piece of history, the hotel was built at Louis IV’s request and later served as a residence for Count de Crillon until becoming a hotel in 1909. The hotel reopened in 2017 after a much-awaited four-year renovation led by a team of architects. The team successfully preserved the hotel’s architectural riches while providing guests with unmatched luxury.
The rooms
The ornate detail extends into the 124 rooms. Soft grays, blues, and gold pops create unity, while antique drinks carts, Taschen books on Matisse, and objets d'art create an eclectic yet tasteful space. The bathroom is a celebration of marble, equipped with a deep tub and in-mirror TVs—because why not? And I could only compare sleeping in the bed to being enveloped in the most voluminous and inviting cloud. There was much to enjoy, but we started by opening our portes-fenêtres, grabbing a book, and nestling into the chaise lounge as we listened to the patter of rain.
Each offering, whether a room, suite or Les Grand Appartements designed by Karl Lagerfeld, perfectly epitomizes Paris. The 10 signature suites are the hotel’s crowning jewels, with Suite Louis XV overlooking the Place de la Concorde, Eiffel Tower, and Grand Palais. The Suite Duc de Crillon boasts the original woodwork from the former private chapel of the Duc de Crillon (the rest now resides in New York’s Metropolitan Museum).
Food and drink
This summer has been busy for the hotel as they expanded their already robust food offering to three restaurants, a pâtisserie, and a bar. Breakfast is served in Jardin D’Hiver, where guests have endless options, from a Parisian breakfast to a Chic breakfast, which includes a Mimosa. Be sure to take in the walls, which shimmer with purple Mica stone. Nonos & Comestibles is the restaurant and deli space led by Michelin-starred chef Paul Pairet. Nonos is where supper takes place. The low lighting and warm tones create an inviting atmosphere. The environment matches the ethos of the menu—contemporary yet timeless. We started with focaccia, Pairet’s take on Tzatziki and fresh bread with creamy soy-infused butter. The menu focuses on the grill, so we indulged in the teriyaki marinated roast of the day. Comestibles was the perfect lunch destination, set in a beautiful courtyard with delicious yet informal options for diners on the go. And be sure to visit the Butterfly Pâtisserie after lunch for a little treat or two.
Finally, Bar Les Ambassadeur. Ornate and regal are the two words that come to mind when considering the hotel’s shining star. The ceiling is a registered landmark that the architects worked to restore and transform to drive the bar’s sultry, stately atmosphere. The cocktail menu is designed as a board game where guests can throw dice to choose a drink. And if you’re looking to take it a notch up, visit L’Ecrin, where guests can enjoy a fine dining experience.
The spa
The spa is the pinnacle of tranquillity. There is a distinct feeling of entering an underwater oasis as you navigate the curved corridors, soft blues, and sandy hues to the swimming pool. Comprising 17,000 pieces of gold scales, the pool glimmers in the sun via the linear glass roof. Heated stone beds and eucalyptus steam rooms help you unwind and prepare for your spa treatment. I chose the Rêverie Face and Body Massage—a CBD experience with Huages. The treatment starts with a couple of drops of Réveil (CBD oil) to help your body relax, followed by a Gua Sha face massage and a whole-body massage using Huages products. The CBD helps you to unwind without adverse side effects. The spa also has a grooming salon, barber, and fitness area.
The service
Reserved yet warm, undeniably professional, and in a class of their own.
The neighborhood/area
There isn’t a better location for first-time visitors to Paris. The hotel is positioned right on Place de la Concorde, with the Musèe de l’Orangerie, Jardin des Tuileries and the Louvre all within walking distance. Undoubtedly, this is the neighborhood to fall in love with Paris, whether for the first or 100th time.
Eco effort
There’s no doubt that Hôtel de Crillon has made a considered sustainability effort. The room and suite amenities are recyclable, including aluminum tubes for beauty products and water bottles. The keys are wooden to reduce the use of plastic within the hotel, and guests can even book hybrid transportation via the hotel.
There are anti-waste actions and measures to reduce energy consumption on the property, and suppliers are vetted to ensure they hold the same sustainability philosophies as the hotel. And the locally sourced vegetable and fish menu at Jardin d’Hiver is a fun touch to their vast sustainability efforts.
Accessibility
Four premier rooms are adapted for those with disabilities, offering easy circulation for wheelchairs and modified furniture heights for people with reduced mobility. The rooms also include emergency connections and alarms, including telephones located in every area. A lighting mechanism is provided to alert guests to the presence of an employee at the door (in addition to knocking).
The reception provides devices for hearing and visually impaired guests, and staff undergo specific training for disability requirements. The restaurant offerings are all equipped to welcome people with disabilities, including a ramp that allows access into Jardin d’Hiver, connected to the bar, Les Ambassadeurs.
Anything else to mention?
Can I rave about the beds again?
