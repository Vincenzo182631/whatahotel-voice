Reviewed by
Kristen Schott
What's this place all about?
Visiting Washington, Virginia, is like journeying to a storybook land—it’s an enchanting enclave, a country village that seems to have resisted the fast-paced speed of modernity and instead remained in a simpler time. The Inn at Little Washington exemplifies that feeling. What began as a restaurant in 1978 is now a three Michelin-starred dining establishment and much-lauded 23-room boutique hotel (a Relais & Châteaux member). It spans 24 acres and is accented by mountain views, trails, a farm—even a chicken coop with a crystal chandelier. The main inn’s cheery blue two-story façade, with its flags flapping in the wind, invites you inside, where you’re immediately transported into a world of whimsical, curious luxury envisioned by famed chef and proprietor Patrick O’Connell. The whole experience is brought to life with interiors by London stage and set designer Joyce Evans. Perhaps you’ll sink into a chair fit for royalty in the living room or head to the Monkey Room, where paintings of the primates tease from the walls while you sip a cocktail—whetting your palate for the best dinner experience of your life. (It's no wonder the inn was the subject of a recent PBS documentary.)
What’s the crowd like?
Because dining at The Inn at Little Washington is an investment, usually reserved for a special occasion, guests are fairly discerning about their food.
What should we be drinking?
Like everything here, the cocktails are grand. They are also cheekily named, like the Inn Cider Scoop. But the wine is not to be missed. Optional pairings are $195 per person and will always succeed. But there are plenty of worthy by-the-glass options including the Clementine Carter Grenache from Cali's Santa Barbara region, which has a loyal following. In total, there are 2,400 selections on the wine list, which has a strong focus on labels from Europe and California, in addition to Virginia bottles (in an homage to the restaurant's home). You might uncork the 1899 Château d’Issan, 1893 Château Pichon Longueville-Comtesse de Lalande, or 1966 Vega-Sicilia Único, three esteemed wineries. You could easily splurge on a bottle (there are more than a few four figure price tags) but there are plenty of affordable selections should you want to exercise restraint.
Main event: the food. Give us the lowdown—especially what not to miss.
The meals here are poetic. They showcase the bounty of Virginia, the self-taught, James Beard Award-winning chef’s skills in the kitchen, and his belief in the theatrical side of a dining experience. The experience is well-orchestrated, and the food is on point: There are two tasting menus and staples include carpaccio of herb-crusted Elysian Fields baby lamb loin with (yep) Caesar salad ice cream and the dessert served with a wink—the Apparently a Pear (a cheesecake pretending to be fruit).
And how did the front-of-house folks treat you?
Doormen greet you by name at the entry. The service itself is smooth and well-executed, with each plate arriving at just the right pace. Menu advice and descriptions are offered and welcomed; the staff is only too happy to share with you the inspiration behind each plate or where the ingredients are sourced (like from the extensive garden, which yields thousands of pounds of produce a year).
Anything else we should know?
If you can book a reservation at the private (and wildly popular) Kitchen Tables, do it. The two tables, dubbed the inner sanctum, sit amid the beautifully designed kitchen. The decor was inspired by the dairy room at Windsor Castle, with handpainted blue and white Portuguese tiles, a bay window looking out on the garden, and a Vulcan range with a copper and brass hood (built to order in France). Experiencing it all from the front row is a main course in itself.
Who should we bring with us?
This is where you want to go to make a statement and impress. Take note: The menu is inventive, so proceed with caution if your partner is a picky eater (like mine). Additionally, because the restaurant is part of the larger inn (accommodations start at $395 and both feature rooms and two-story suites, many with balconies and garden views), you'll want to plan well in advance, with whoever you're bringing, to stay a night or two.
