The Ritz-Carlton Beijing, Financial Street
In true Ritz-Carlton fashion, this is a leader in the city with top-tier service, a pair of excellent restaurants, and timeless decor that tells the story of Chinese culture

Reviewed by
Ramsey Qubein
Why book?
Surrounded by office buildings and a luxury shopping mall, this modern glass tower is a popular property in the center of the action. While it appeals to business travelers, leisure guests will find proximity to tourist attractions like the Forbidden City a big draw. And then there is the legendary service for which the brand is known, which did not disappoint during my short visit.
Set the scene
Walls of glass in the lobby fill it with natural light, and the decorative wooden lattice patterns that cover them showcase symbols of long life and good fortune. Four tall banyan trees have grown to towering size in the main lounge since the hotel opened two decades ago. And prosperity flower blossoms are notable in ceiling light fixtures and floor carpeting. These are just a few of the many examples of how Chinese philosophy and beliefs are interwoven into the design here.
Traditional music plays overhead while guests sip cups of Chinese tea in the lounge, complete with polished jade floors. Staffers glide through the lobby, some in bespoke Chinese attire and others in buttoned-up suits. It’s not all business here, though, as evidenced by the small staircase at one of the reception desks for kids to climb up and check in themselves.
The backstory
The Ritz-Carlton Beijing, Financial Street was the second Ritz-Carlton to open in China, but now it is one of three in Beijing alone (the third is set to open later in 2026). Despite being nearly two decades old, the glass façade and modern interiors defy its age. Regular upkeep assures that the design stays relevant with the times, but also includes many of the original furnishings, woodwork, and design pieces of its opening day.
The rooms
The cloud pattern along the hallway carpeting is another symbol of a good future. Outside each guest room door is a liuli crystal animal sculpture, yet another good luck charm meant to bless your stay.
Inside, the rooms here feature plush carpeting and a light color palette of gold and beige with pops of green and red in the fabrics. Large work desks are stocked with stationery and office supplies, plus plenty of international power ports, an indication of the type of guests here during the week. Deluxe rooms are the standard category, but my Executive Suite at the corner of the building was almost three times as big. It featured a large sitting room with a dining table and a curved sofa, and the staff had taken the time to draw cute images on the windows of local landmarks (don’t worry, they were temporary drawings).
The cloud-like duvets on the large beds did wonders for my 12-hour jet lag fit that was finally beginning to subside. With just the click of a button, the blackout shades can rise or lower, and from my room, I could see the red rooftops of the Forbidden City. I had a walk-in closet and bathroom with an enormous tub by the windows. The Ritz-Carlton brand has switched over to Diptyque toiletries at all of its properties, and there were plenty of extras from shaving sets to mouthwash on the vanity, too. The soaking tub had an attached pillow, but it was the huge shower stall that really impressed. It had a full-length, heated mirror inside.
Club-level rooms on the 18th floor are in for an extra treat with doting service and the offer of five food and beverage presentations throughout the day in the club lounge. I stopped in for lunch and light refreshments one day, and there were several hot dishes, a beautiful salad, and a soup spread (I loved the egg and tomato soup), plus nearly a dozen exotic fruit platters. Later in the day, the afternoon tea service was elaborate, and the staff took great care in preparing a vegetarian spread based on my preferences.
The dinner spread had a similarly robust selection plus an open bar. Perhaps my favorite part was the candy station with several Chinese candies (the White Rabbit–branded milk chews are addictive).
Food and drink
There are two restaurants at the property. Greenfish is an all-day dining venue on the lobby level. This is where a beautifully presented breakfast buffet appears each morning with fresh-baked pastries, all kinds of breads, salads, and yogurts, a Chinese selection of specialties like braised beef noodle soup, and an omelet station. Later in the day, it switches to a Chinese cuisine blend featuring Hunan- and Shandong-inspired dishes.
Qi is the Cantonese restaurant one level above the lobby under the helm of a talented, young chef serving delicacies using ingredients from all around China. With some advance notice, I had a seven-course vegetarian menu with dishes like chilled broad bean with morel mushroom, rice soup with black truffle, and fried rice with pumpkin, which tasted Michelin-star worthy and was presented with flair. This venue is popular with hotel guests and those who want to entertain clients for both lunch and dinner.
The Crystal Lounge and bar takes its name from the crystal slab that makes up its long countertop. On Friday evenings, a jazz band sets up in the lobby lounge. There’s also a more traditional Chinese-inspired venue one level above the lobby, XuanLangBar, which focuses on more exotic cocktails.
The spa
Stretching more than 16,000 square feet, the spa and health club, one level below the lobby, is an oasis away from the hubbub outside. Unique in town is the indoor swimming pool, which has a big movie screen at one end showing black-and-white vintage films while you swim. I have visited thousands of hotels in my career, and that was a first for me.
The large fitness center has windows facing the pool, just in case you wanted to catch the rest of your favorite flick. The spa menu covers all the bases for relaxation, and in the male and female changing areas, there are saunas, steam rooms, hydrotherapy tubs, and day loungers to rest before or after a treatment.
The neighborhood/area
The lobby has direct access to the high-end Seasons Place shopping mall with every big fashion brand you can think of, as well as plenty of dining options. I enjoyed walking around the neighborhood amid shining skyscrapers and stumbling upon the neighborhood’s famous hutongs. These are old alleyways between old brick courtyard residences that have now been turned into trendy stores, restaurants, and bars. It’s a lively scene if you want to find something off the beaten tourist path.
The service
Ladies and gentlemen, that is the term The Ritz-Carlton brand uses for its hotel staff. And it’s a moniker they take very seriously. Almost the entire team here is Chinese, except for the incredible guest relations manager from Spain, who gave me plenty of insider tips she has discovered during her time in the city. You can’t pass by someone without a greeting. Housekeeping seems to slip in and out of rooms unnoticed, while the restaurant staff seems to remember your personal preferences from day to day.
For families
On weekends, the hotel sees more families, especially during holiday periods when business travelers aren’t out in full force. As such, the hotel has some creative programming for kids. Beyond the children’s check-in area, they can roam the hotel on a scavenger hunt, collecting clues for a prize, and even order up an in-room teepee and special gifts. Arts and crafts activities, like decorating traditional Chinese lanterns or cooking classes, are on the agenda throughout the year (both kids and adults can participate).
Anything left to mention?
The hotel developed special rate packages that include the room and a private, VIP-guided tour of the Great Wall or other popular Beijing sights. It’s ideal for business travelers who are short on time, as well as travelers who like special access without waiting in lines.
