One Aldwych
For a boutique stay in the throbbing heart of London’s West End

Reviewed by
Arati Menon
Why book?
For the thrill of being in the heart of
London
’s West End—and the ability to retreat to its calm Art Nouveau-style interiors.
Set the scene
Set in a handsome Edwardian building off The Strand, One Aldywch is an arresting presence, not least because of the jutting angle of the front end of its facade (
New York
's Flatiron will spring to mind). Its central location and boutique size make it appealing to a cross-section of travelers: theater buffs who will appreciate its proximity to the best of West End; culture lovers who will enjoy the short walk to the institutions of South Bank; and business travelers for whom the discretion of a more intimate hotel in central London makes for a worthy refuge—to fit in that quiet power lunch or for the reprieve of a good night’s sleep. Even with the ebb and flow of its guests, the lobby, with its towering floral arrangements, red leather seating, and large arched windows, exudes a sense of calm–a point of relief in the beating heart of the city.
The backstory
The latest iteration of One Aldwych may be contemporary, but the legacy it carries is storied. The building that houses the hotel was built by famed Anglo-French architects Mewès & Davis (of Ritz,
Paris
and London fame) at the turn of the 20th century as the home of the Conservative daily,
The Morning Post
. Fun fact: the newspaper’s printing presses were installed in the basement, where the hotel’s swimming pool currently sits.
The Morning Post
, where Winston Churchill was once a war correspondent, was eventually bought by
The Daily Telegraph
in 1937, and its staff moved out of the building. Decades later, the Grade II listed building was bought by the hotel’s (very private) owners and opened as a hotel in 1998; it later underwent an extensive refurb in 2019 at the hands of architect Robert Angell. Today, the hotel is one of the few truly independent boutique hotels in its class in the city.
The rooms
The hotel’s 101 suites and bedrooms, irrespective of size, share the same chic, peaches-and-cream aesthetic. I stayed at the Waterloo Bridge suite, named—you guessed it—for its incredible framing of the bridge and South Bank in the distance. The suite comprised a graceful foyer, a large, curved living and dining area, walk-in wardrobe and changing room, and a separate bedroom and bathroom. As a counterpoint to the heritage of the building, the aesthetic on the inside is clean and elegant; think: pastel shades and curated objects d’art—no chintz or Georgian chandeliers in sight. In fact, the rooms are designed to feel like a home, and I got quite comfortable padding about my suite pretending it was my imaginary Tribeca townhouse. This sense of comfort is abetted by the fact that before your stay, the hotel checks in on details like your favorite teas, and your preferred morning papers, and ensures you have them handy. There were other treats upon entry: more chocolates than I needed, chilled champagne, and the tastiest bites of fruit encased in glass domes. Art is an integral part of the hotel’s interiors, with original pieces in every room and public space (ask for a guided tour of its 400-strong collection). There are other thoughtful details: heated floors and towel racks, umbrellas tucked into the closet, the softest throws made by weavers on the Isle of Skye, and a Bang & Olufsen sound system. And I loved the toiletries from Mitchell and Peach, a fifth-generation English floricultural estate in Kent, finding every opportunity to get heavy-handed with my use of them.
Food and drink
Seasonality and local sourcing are the cornerstones of Indigo, the main restaurant that sits on a mezzanine floor overlooking the lobby bar. The chef’s love for British ingredients and imaginative flavor profiles, coupled with the fact that its menu is entirely dairy- and gluten-free makes its offering quite unique. Expect dishes like roasted butternut squash soup with chestnuts and sage followed by Yorkshire partridge with parsnip or Highland venison with heritage beetroot and fondant potato (and very good gluten- and dairy-free bread and butter). There are separate lunch and dinner menus, the latter offering both à la carte and tasting options. For a speedier pre-theater bite (spicy crab cakes, anyone? Truffle choux buns, perhaps?) and drink, the lobby bar with its views into the streets is a good option—I enjoyed an excellent hot chocolate there on a rainy afternoon but the cocktail menu appeared well-crafted.
My favorite dining experience however remained our in-room breakfasts, set up by the window overlooking Waterloo. With tendrils of morning light hitting our plates of fruit, granola, pastries, and eggs and unhurried jazz streaming through the speakers, we let ourselves pretend there wasn’t a real life we had to return to.
The neighborhood/area
Set in the heart of the West End, there isn’t much you can’t access from here. Covent Garden with its shops and markets is at its doorstep; the best West End theaters are within a ten-minute walk; the National Gallery and British Museum are a stroll up the Strand; and over the bridge South Bank, one of the focal points of the city's culture scene, unfolds. Once there, follow the riverside route and take in everything from the National Theater to Shakespeare’s Globe and Tate Modern. To really get under the skin of its surroundings though, sign up for one of the hotel’s guided programs with local experts from the worlds of art, music, and theater who will unravel sides of London you’ve never experienced.
The service
From the very moment we were greeted by the doorman, to our convivial servers at dinner, and the folks at reception, we felt incredibly well taken care of. The staff are friendly, professional, and sincere.
For families
At first glance, the hotel, with its powder-fresh interiors and luxury amenities doesn’t seem like it’s made for families, but the hotel goes out of its way to welcome them. From the way many suites are designed to easily be adapted for family stays to the family-oriented recs from the concierge, the mini-size bathrobes and slippers that appear on request, and the entertainment that is on hand for young guests (my nephew and I went on a “treasure hunt” walk with a map they had handy for kids), families will feel well taken care of. But the kid-friendly experience the hotel is most loved for is its
Charlie and the Chocolate Factory
high tea. Featuring cakes and sherberts and fizzy drinks it’s an indulgent reminder of how the hotel is anchored by its proximity to London’s theaterland.
The spa
I blame the many splendors of Covent Garden for keeping me away from the hotel’s pool—a subterranean cocoon of tranquility with chlorine-free water and underwater music. Which means, I also didn’t have the time for a spa treatment, a disappointment given the range of face and body treatments on offer, with products from Spanish skincare brand Natura Bissé and British skincare brand, OSKIA. I did however pop in for a quick session at the hotel’s well-equipped gym that hotel guests have 24-hour access to.
Eco effort
In line with the brand’s commitment to sustainability, One Aldwych partners with local suppliers and producers as much as possible. On the roof, beehives provide the hotel's honey and in the basement, guests can enjoy laps in a chlorine-free swimming pool.
Accessibility
One Aldwych has accessible rooms available with wide doors, walk-in showers, grab rails, and emergency pull cords. Spacious lifts are available for use throughout the hotel.
Anything left to mention?
If you can spring for it, book one of its signature suites, each one of which comes with a special something–a pretty terrace, winning views, or an eye-catching domed ceiling.
