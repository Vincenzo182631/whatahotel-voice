Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpur
Park Hyatt's first hotel in Malaysia is also its tallest in the world.

Why book?
From the moment you stepped into the lobby on the 75th floor, you are transported to another realm. Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpurs’s true luxury is the majesty of its views. Atop most buildings, you usually see other buildings. But from Park Hyatt’s rooms and suites, you see the vista and lay of the land. It’s spectacular seeing the sprawling metropolis, and the mists drifting in from the hills flanking
Kuala Lumpur
and the changing clouds and natural light.
Set the scene
That theatre of Kuala Lumpur, cocooned within Park Hyatt’s elegant luxury, aligns with the significance of its setting. Merdeka 118 is not just a skyscraper, but an expression of Malaysia’s history and ambition. It towers over the deeply historic Stadium Merdeka where independence from the British was first proclaimed in 1957. Its façade features diamond-shaped facets, evocative of traditional Malay handicrafts, and its spire is said to resemble Tunku Abdul Rahman’s raised hand as he declared “Merdeka!”
Backstory
Soaring to 2,227 feet, Merdeka 118 currently ranks as the second-tallest building in the world, just behind the Burj Khalifa. Its scale positions Malaysia on the global stage, much like the Petronas Twin Towers did in the 1990s. Developed by Permodalan Nasional Berhad (PNB), Malaysia’s largest fund management company, the project also signals Malaysia’s strength in finance, real estate, and global investment. A partnership with a world-class brand like Park Hyatt is fitting. This is the group’s 50th hotel worldwide, and its first in Malaysia.
The rooms
Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpur offers 252 rooms, including 27 suites, spread between the 100th and 114th floors. Each room features floor-to-ceiling windows, turning your space into a private viewing deck over the city. When you’ve had your fill of the view, a touch of a button lowers the blinds. Suites feel more like elegant apartments, with open layouts, folding doors and warm wood accents throughout. Local heritage is expressed in subtle ways: the weave motif in the tiles and carpet, décor touches such as a chocolate-filled tiffin carrier, a labu sayung pottery piece, and a graceful orchid plant. In the suite, you can soak in the bathtub as the city lights fade into dusk, or use the shower smoker to infuse your room with Park Hyatt’s distinctive bergamot scent. Together, these details create an atmosphere conducive to deep, restorative sleep.
Food and drink
High above the city, Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpur is redefining the capital’s dining scene with a trio of experiences that are as refined as they are rooted in place. At Merdeka Grill, prime cuts and sustainable seafood are prepared with theatrical flair—think cedar-wood roasted salmon or Chateaubriand carved tableside (sublime with freshly grated wasabi). Park Lounge offers a more leisurely rhythm, where heritage recipes such as Botok Ikan and Gulai Taucu Minang are reimagined, and afternoon tea is a ritual of delicious savoury bites followed by a decadent roaming dessert trolley. With Scandinavian-trained Executive Chef Stig Drageide at the helm and Pastry Chef Holger Deh weaving chocolate alchemy, Park Hyatt KL’s culinary programme delivers not just a meal but a sense of occasion—luxury dining grounded in Malaysian soul. Menus lean on
Malaysia’s own terroir, with produce and spices sourced from small local growers, giving international dishes a distinctly Malaysian resonance. For instance, the vanilla beans in the desserts and chocolates are from Penang in the North.
Then there is Cacao Lab & Bar, Kuala Lumpur’s first chocolate-themed cocktail destination,
where cacao takes centre stage in inventive cocktails and curated tasting flights paired with
whisky, cognac, or rum, set to a 75th-floor skyline. Plant-based options and zero-proof
drinks are also thoughtfully included for mindful drinkers—when I declined alcohol at
dinner, the sommelier promptly returned with sparkling tea and poured it as ceremoniously as
champagne.
The spa
My most memorable experience here was at the spa, a serene sanctuary floating on the 99th floor. The circadian therapy I tried was designed to harmonize with the body’s natural energy cycles, leaving me deeply relaxed and restored. The heated massage bed and warm
towels were especially comforting, easing me into a state of calm. Afterwards, guests can create their own bath salts infused with locally sourced botanicals, a thoughtful touch that ties each ritual to a sense of place. The spa facilities include a 100-foot infinity pool, steam rooms, and a Himalayan salt alcove—all exclusive to hotel guests, with sweeping views that extend the sense of elevation and escape.
The neighborhood
Merdeka 118 rises between the historic Stadium Merdeka, where independence was declared in 1957, and Stadium Negara, making the hotel’s setting deeply symbolic. While the tower’s mall and office spaces are still gradually opening, the arrival experience at Park Hyatt feels seamless—attentive ushers guide you to the lifts that whisk guests straight to the reception on the 75th floor.
The location is part of the Warisan Merdeka (“Heritage of Independence”) initiative, an
urban regeneration project aimed at revitalising Kuala Lumpur’s historic districts while
preserving their cultural resonance. From here, the city’s heritage is at your doorstep:
Chinatown lies less than a mile away, and rail stations beneath the tower connect you
easily to the wider capital. For deeper exploration, Park Hyatt curates guided tours to
nearby cultural and historical landmarks, weaving the hotel stay into the broader narrative
of Malaysia’s past and present.
The service
Service at Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpur is as impeccable as you’d expect from the brand,
but what sets it apart is the warmth and easy friendliness of the staff. There’s a genuine
graciousness here that feels less rehearsed and more instinctive—a reflection of Malaysia’s
renowned hospitality.
In conclusion
Perched more than a hundred floors above the city, Park Hyatt Kuala Lumpur
offers guests a perspective few have experienced before. Yet for all its rarefied elevation,
the hotel remains anchored in its setting, celebrating Malaysian provenance, heritage and
artistry. It’s a thoughtful recognition of Merdeka 118’s cultural significance in modern
Malaysia. To stay here is to experience luxury woven with national pride. Although high up, it isn't knee-wobbling or dizzying to stand at the windows. The city stretches out in calm panorama; it’s lofty but not terrifying, more soaring than stomach-churning. The views inspire awe, not vertigo—so even those with a fear of heights could find it captivating rather than nerve-wrecking.
