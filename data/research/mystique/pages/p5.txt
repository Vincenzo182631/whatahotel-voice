Mystique, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Santorini
This 42-room adults-only hotel is among the most luxurious on Santorini.

Why book?
Perched at the entrance of the village of Oia with stunning caldera views, this nearly 20-year-old clifftop hotel (it opened in 2007) has recently undergone a series of updates and expansions, consistently making it one of the most sought-after bookings in Santorini.
Set the scene
Like many of the island’s other top hotels, Mystique has an enviable address on the caldera in Oia, the picture-perfect cliffside village that you’ve seen on every postcard of Santorini. But the hotel doesn’t just rely on location alone. Since opening in 2007, Mystique has introduced numerous upgrades to maintain its favorable reputation among affluent visitors. This includes a 2024 makeover of its 42 cave-like suites and villas plus a refreshed culinary program at its fine-dining kitchen Lure. And like many of the hotels on the caldera, Mystique is adults-only with a minimum age requirement of 14 years old so expect to see mostly couples.
The backstory
Owned and operated by Empiria Group, one of Greece’s premier hospitality firms with five-star projects all over the Cyclades and the Peloponnese; Mystique is part of the brand’s trifecta of luxury hotels on Santorini.
Istoria
is located on Perivolos Beach and
Vedema
in Megalochori is more of a resort concept. Mystique is Empiria’s only property on the caldera, and when it first opened in 2007, it only had 18 rooms. It has over doubled in size since, creating a labyrinthine footprint along the cliffside with its two restaurants bookending the property.
The rooms
In 2024, Mystique unveiled fully renovated suites and villas. While the white-washed Cycladic vibe remains (you’re still getting cave-like lodging), interiors were updated with more contemporary style. Athens’ Interior Design Laboratorium freshened up the interiors with a softer palette of neutral hues (seemingly every shade of brown is represented through furniture and accessories) but added quirky details, like the upcycled olive pits repurposed into table tops. And, of course, it wouldn’t be a caldera hotel in Santorini if not every single one of the 42 rooms come with breathtaking views of the Aegean Sea.
Because you are in the caldera and staying in a hotel with cave-like accommodations, no two rooms are exactly alike. Some might come with a bathtub and a bigger bathroom; others might have an outdoor jacuzzi. If you want to splurge, the hotel has a handful of villas that are a bit secluded. The Secrecy Villa, for example, is perfect for honeymooners with its sprawling terrace and plunge pool, but its more hidden-away location on the property offers more privacy.
Food and drink
Mystique has a pretty comprehensive culinary program with two restaurants and two bars. For breakfast and lunch, there’s Charisma where you can tuck into Greek and international favorites. For breakfast, you can fuel up with cheesy pies or with a plate of Tyravogulo, a rustic egg-based dish typical to Santorini. During the day, tarama dip and tomato fritters share menu space with caesar salads and smash burgers. Celebrated Greek chef Yiannis Kioroglou recently took over the kitchen at Lure, the hotel’s fine-dining space which serves a menu of Mediterranean classics. Lure Bar has a fantastic selection of Santorini wines; while poolside Circe Bar might be better for a round or two of frozen margaritas.
If you’d prefer a more intimate culinary experience, there’s the Secret Wine Cave, a 150-year-old wine cellar that guests book for tastings of rare vintages or private dinners.
The spa
Given the cliffside location, Elios Spa is a cozy operation of just two treatment rooms (one of which is for couples). You get your usual treatments, like deep-tissue massages and hydrating facials. There are also more unusual services, such as the After Sun Cure, which will slather you in an aloe serum before it finishes with a relaxing scalp massage.
The neighborhood/area
The hotel is located right at the entrance of Oia. You do need to walk down a series of steps to reach the entrance of the hotel. (Of course, upon arrival for check-in hotel staff is available to help bring your luggage to your suite or villa.) If you’re in Santorini for the caldera views, you’re in the right place. From Mystique; shops, bars, and viewpoints are all within easy walking distance. For example, the Three Domes (a trio of blue-topped churches) are 15 minutes away. Further-flung sights (including the red beach and the fish taverns of Ammoudi Bay) will require driving. Empiria has a transfer service to whisk you wherever you want to go.
In addition, because Oia is the busiest part of the island, businesses here tend to have a longer season. Mystique, for instance, is open from mid-April to early-November. And bonus:
Mia’s
, one of Santorini’s top restaurants (for both views and food), is located just above the hotel.
The service
The staff is friendly and helpful, but like at many coveted hotel in Oia, high-occupancy can lead to slower service. This might be especially obvious during congested pockets of the day, such as breakfast when you may need to politely remind servers about your coffee order. But everyone is otherwise hospitable and can help with recommending activities around the island, making dining reservations, or booking transfers to various attractions. In fact, Empiria has a fleet of cars that services its guests around the island (at cost).
For families
Most of the guests who stay at Mystique are couples (many on honeymoons). Occasionally there are larger groups of adults. In fact, the hotel only accepts guests who are 14 years old and over.
Eco effort
Mystique partners with Nordaq to provide guests with filtered water and minimize single-use bottles around the hotel. There’s also a focus on local sourcing, whether it’s the furniture or the food and beverage.
Accessibility
Travelers with mobility concerns may struggle with staying at a hotel in the caldera, where you are constantly walking up and down stairs.
Anything left to mention?
One thing to note about staying on the caldera is that hotels and villas are staggered up and down the slope of a cliff. And walkways snake around some of these properties. This means that other people can often look into spaces that you might otherwise think is private. At Mystique, if you’re staying in a room that comes with a patio or an outdoor jacuzzi, remember that someone might be able to see you from another part of the hotel or from outside the property altogether. If that’s a concern, make sure you request a suite or villa that is as tucked-away as possible.
