### Do not celebrate your special day here, or stay at all for that matter.

My sister and her now husband held their wedding reception at the Andaz Ottawa ByWard Market just a couple of weeks ago, in a private event space within the hotel. What should have been one of the happiest and most memorable occasions of their lives was unfortunately overshadowed by a series of service failures, culminating in an alarming security incident involving complete strangers, stolen property, verbal abuse, and an apparent lack of meaningful intervention from hotel staff and management.
While there were some positive aspects to our stay, the way this situation was handled was unacceptable, and the experience left me questioning the hotel's standards of security, hospitality, and accountability. Even before the wedding celebration began, there were unnecessary frustrations. The makeup artist, who was responsible for four makeup applications, requested a barstool to facilitate her work and was denied one by hotel staff or management on the grounds that it was not permitted. Both the makeup artist and the separate hairstylist have worked with brides at this very hotel on numerous occasions and had never encountered such a restriction. This immediately raised questions about the hotel's willingness to accommodate the practical needs of a wedding party. The wedding reception itself was held in one of the second-floor studio spaces. The room was intimate, appropriately sized for the number of guests, and beautifully decorated. Unfortunately, the service did not consistently match the setting. Bar staff were unable to accommodate non-alcoholic beverage requests outside of soda and juice- this lack of flexibility was disappointing. At approximately 11:30 p.m., as the evening was winding down, the bride, groom, myself, and at least one other guest were tidying personal decorations and gathering leftover wine bottles that had been purchased as part of the wedding event. During this time, two individuals who were complete strangers to everyone attending the wedding entered the private studio space uninvited, announcing that they were there to “tour the venue.” Their presence was unexpected and inappropriate, particularly given that the room was being used for a private wedding event and guests were still present. I turned away briefly to alert the groom to what was happening. In those few moments, the two individuals took it upon themselves to sweep up several wine bottles and head toward the elevators with them. We had to intercept them near the elevators to recover the property. What followed was even more disturbing: the individuals directed foul language at us, including toward the bride and groom, on what should have been one of the happiest nights of their lives.
At a “luxurious” hotel in the nation’s capital, a couple celebrating their wedding had to confront strangers who had entered their private event space and taken property that did not belong to them, only to be met with verbal abuse when they attempted to recover it.
Hotel staff and bartenders appeared stunned but somewhat responsive. However, once the individuals continued toward the elevators and the immediate confrontation subsided, the response seemed to lose momentum. There was no clear sense that the situation was being taken seriously or that meaningful action would follow. I understand that the newlyweds subsequently attempted to contact hotel security and requested that these individuals be removed from the property. They were reportedly advised that nothing could be done without further proof. This response is difficult to comprehend. The bride and groom, myself, other guests, and hotel staff were available as witnesses to the incident. What more was expected? Were we supposed to accept that strangers could enter a private event space, take property, verbally abuse the people who challenged them, and simply carry on with their evening? As our group made its way to the rooftop bar to continue celebrating, we discovered that the two individuals were part of a larger group gathered there, wholly under the influence. Their demeanor and conduct when they saw our group arrive only heightened our discomfort. After what had just occurred, finding these same individuals still on the premises, in another space where our group was attempting to celebrate was deeply unsettling. I personally felt unsafe, and the bride and groom’s final wedding evening moments were unsalvageable. The hotel had an opportunity to take control of the situation, reassure the affected guests, and demonstrate that this behavior would not be tolerated. Instead, the individuals remained on the property, and the couple's final moments of wedding-night celebration were effectively ruined. The issue was no longer simply the theft of property. It was the complete disregard for the privacy of a private event, the verbal abuse directed at the couple, and the apparent absence of decisive action to protect the guests affected. We were left to navigate an uncomfortable and potentially volatile situation ourselves, rather than being able to trust hotel staff to manage it appropriately. A wedding is an occasion that cannot be recreated. The couple had every right to expect a safe, private, and celebratory environment for the event they had paid to host. Instead, their wedding night ended with an incident that should never have occurred and a response that did little to restore their sense of security or peace of mind. I am also grateful that my six-year-old daughter was not present at the wedding and there to witness this incident. Seeing adults behave this way, particularly toward a bride and groom on their wedding night, would have been deeply distressing. As a parent, I cannot imagine having to explain why strangers were allowed to behave this way or why the hotel did not appear able to intervene effectively. Outside the wedding events, my own room offered an excellent view of downtown Ottawa, which I appreciated. However, much of the furniture showed noticeable scuffing and wear that did not align with my expectations of a luxury hotel. There was also no hand soap in the room, a relatively minor oversight but another indication that attention to detail was lacking. The hotel's reliance on text messaging rather than a conventional phone line to reach the front desk or concierge also felt impersonal and inconvenient. For a hotel positioned as a luxury property, I expected a more accessible and responsive guest-service experience. Parking was another frustration. The lack of available parking and the construction surrounding the hotel block made arrival and departure challenging. Although valet parking was available, it came at an additional cost, with no apparent accommodation for guests navigating these circumstances. Individually, some of these issues might be manageable. Together, they painted a picture of a hotel experience that fell well short of the standard expected for the price and positioning. I understand that the hotel has since been in communication with the newlyweds following feedback and poorly rated reviews from wedding guests. However, I am not aware that the core concerns have been satisfactorily resolved.
I would like to see a clear explanation of how two uninvited individuals were able to enter a private wedding event space, take property, verbally abuse guests, and remain on the premises afterward despite the incident being reported. I would also like to understand why the presence of multiple witnesses was apparently insufficient to prompt meaningful intervention. The central concern is the hotel's apparent failure to respond appropriately to a security incident that directly affected the safety, comfort, and dignity of its guests.
My sister and her husband entrusted this hotel with an occasion they will remember for the rest of their lives. They deserved better than to have their wedding night overshadowed by the reckless actions of strangers and a hotel response that, from our perspective, fell terribly short. Beautiful event spaces and downtown views cannot compensate for a lack of security, accountability, and genuine guest care. Until these concerns are meaningfully addressed, I would have serious reservations about recommending this hotel for a stay in any capacity, never mind for a wedding or any other important private event.

### THE place to stay in downtown Ottawa

Unbeatable location half a block from Byward Market. Great decor and impeccable front desk service. Room vacuuming was just so-so when we moved in, but problems were swiftly addressed. Got free valet parking with AAA/CAA rate and well worth it given how crazy downtown is in August. Be aware you will need to notify front desk 30 minutes in advance to have your car brought up. If I go to Ottawa again here is the place I would stay.
