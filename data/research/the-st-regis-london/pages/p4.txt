The St. Regis Budapest

Why book?
If you are already aware of, or have stayed with, the St. Regis brand, then the only thing you’d need to be convinced on is
Budapest
, which is an easy sell in itself. If you’re not aware, The St. Regis is defined by its bespoke service, so whether you choose to dive head-first into this amazing city—or elect for an indulgent hotel day—they’ve got you covered in a multitude of ways.
Matt Charlton
Set the scene
Greeted by a grand staircase, a cavernous atrium, and a staff for which nothing is too much trouble, you will be made to feel as if you very much belong in this former palace… in fact, if the Austro-Hungarian Empire had endured, then this very well may be what a modern-day palace would look like. Subtle tones, warm lighting, plenty of daylight, curated wall art and sculpture, and soft textures make for high-end yet informal communal spaces that never feel crowded, even when the hotel is full.
Reception and concierge desks are tucked to the side for a low-key check-in, and throughout the hotel, there are floral details and organic patterns—including a very impressive bronze leaf sculpture which lines the atrium like ivy—which pay homage to Budapest’s rich artistic heritage and The St. Regis’s gilded age beginnings. Blue ombré corridors have been imagined as pathways through Budapest's architectural layers, with doors positioned to resemble landmarks along the Danube.
The backstory
The St. Regis Budapest opened at the start of May 2026 in the historic Klotild Palace. One of the city's most iconic architectural landmarks was commissioned by Archduchess Maria Clotild of Saxe-Coburg & Gotha at the turn of the 20th century, the twin Klotild and Matild Palaces envisioned as a magnificent gateway to Budapest—framing the newly built Elisabeth Bridge and welcoming travelers into the heart of Pest.
Over more than a century, the building has witnessed war, political change, and cultural revival, standing as a testament to the city's resilience and artistic spirit. It feels like it’s filling (and possibly bettering?) the gap left by the Ritz Carlton Budapest, which closed at the end of 2025. Indeed, many of the fantastic staff–or hosts, as they’re known here—have transferred from there.
Such attention to detail in both the design and the service results in a very well-oiled machine, especially considering how new this all is.
The rooms
Ranging from a superior room, via the Klotild Tower Suite–where you can have a personalized version of the hotel’s nightly sabrage ceremony with a private Budapest view as a backdrop—and up to a 1,800-square-foot Presidential Suite, each accommodation is sleekly finished with tasteful nods to the building and surrounding area.
European oak sets off one of three subtle color themes—red, blue, or purple—that you will see in the soft furnishings and carefully selected chinaware, all set against a backdrop of paneled walls in a warm white.
Tech is seamless and integrated, with Chromecast, Bluetooth speakers, simple lighting controls, and sensor lights to and in the bathroom. The simplicity of the butler/room service would be truly capped off if The St. Regis offered a dedicated app instead of relying on a mobile site or the hotel phone.

Matt Charlton
Food and drink
The 99 Sushi Bar and restaurant is the hotel’s main dining offering, and like its other iterations around the world, it doesn’t disappoint. I would particularly recommend the wagyu gyozas and the egg rice.
An à la carte breakfast menu is served in the same tranquil setting, with hosts offering trays of fresh pasties, juices, and yogurts to complement your main dish. This may well have been opening jitters, but service in both cases felt quite slow.
Elsewhere there is Klotild patisserie for both guests and general public, serving excellent pasties and coffees, an impressive bar, the atrium for afternoon tea, and a very well-stacked room service menu from which the wagyu beef salad was incredible.
The spa
Found in a cave-like basement, The St. Regis’s spa comes complete with an indoor pool, Finnish sauna, hammam, experience showers, and three private treatment rooms with a lounge and relaxation area. I would definitely recommend the Performance Massage if you have aching muscles—just one more of them and it would have fixed me.
So often an afterthought in hotels, the Technogym-filled fitness room is anything but, and occupies an impressive two-tiered top-floor space with a sloping glass roof. There were a few teething problems tech-wise, but nothing that shouldn’t be fixed by now.
The neighborhood/area
The building was once the gateway to Pest, so the hotel is impressively situated on the edge of the Danube, on the main thoroughfare of the city—with all the shops, attractions, bars, and restaurants you could ask for within easy reach. That said, traffic noise is never an issue. Talking of restaurants, local favorite Felix is just over the Elisabeth Bridge and well worth a visit, with its Italian American influences, tasty chicken piccata, and generous portions.
For families
This does feel like a very elegant, serene adult space, but the three units of two-bedroom duplex suites are set across two levels, creating a strong sense of separation between living and private spaces, so well-suited to families.
Accessibility
There are elevators to every floor (including a separate one to the gym), ample gangways everywhere, and two accessible rooms.
Anything left to mention?
The butler and concierge service is everything. Trousers were pressed within 20 minutes; I expressed an interest in Budapest’s Cold War history, and they arranged a city tour for me with one of the best—Andrea Magyar Goczo, whose company
JoAn VIP Travel
has looked after the likes of Rod Stewart and Jeff Bezos; I told them I wasn’t drinking, and my fridge was filled with 0.0 beer; they were out of pistachio pastries when I asked, but I was greeted with a takeout box of freshly baked pistachio cookies at checkout. It all leaves you with a very sweet taste in your mouth—sometimes literally.
