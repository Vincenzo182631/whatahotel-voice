Reviewed by

Why book?
This luxury lifestyle-led hotel has a prime location that works for singles, couples, foodies, shoppers, and sightseers alike.
Set the scene
The 15th-floor lobby feels like a mini United Nations filled with people and accents from just about everywhere, including a good helping of cool
Hongkongers
who work in fashion and manufacturing. Paths cross at Birdsong lobby lounge and bar, an airy double-height space where mid-century-style terrazzo floors and starburst chandeliers blend with curvaceous pink velvet chairs and dusky blue drapes, with the city shimmering in the background. It’s the place to be at 5 p.m., when guests can partake in Kimpton’s group-wide “social hour.” In Hong Kong, that includes a choice of complimentary Chardonnay, Shiraz, fruit punch, and sweet and savory canapés.
The backstory
Part of the Intercontinental Hotels Group, this is one of a dozen Kimpton hotels which are slated to open over the next 18 months from Frankfurt to San Antonio to Riyadh. The Hong Kong property is currently the largest in the hip pet-friendly portfolio, with nearly 500 rooms slotted between floors 18 to 38, topped with a rooftop swimming pool. The hotel feels more boutique than the numbers suggest thanks to the cleverly designed sky lobby, attentive staff, and the flying V-shape building, which ensures sparkling city and harbor views from every corner.
The rooms
Hong Kong hotels tend to charge a hefty premium for their harbor view rooms, so it’s a major draw that every room at the Kimpton overlooks the city’s famous waterway—at a reasonable price point too. You can watch the sun rise above the bottle-green South China Sea from the entry-level rooms, which look east in the direction of the old Kai Tak airport (now a cruise terminal and sports mega-complex). Higher categories are located on the upper floors, while the top-end rooms and suites stare out across Victoria Harbour towards the instantly recognizable Hong Kong Island skyline. Interiors are an on-trend blend of warm wood panels, sage, and chartreuse soft furnishings, rattan cabinetry, and slabs of bold black and white marble. Other trendy touches include Tivoli radio-style Bluetooth speakers, Japanese Lemnos clocks, playful contemporary Hong Kong artworks, and mini-bars stuffed with local brands such as Loveramics ceramics, Elephant Grounds coffee pods, and Conspiracy chocolate bars.
Bathrooms are semi-open-plan with electronic toilets and smart rain-showers stocked with Korean NonFiction toiletries hidden behind fluted glass doors and chocolate-brown slipper tubs visible in the room—great for a dirty weekend, less appealing if you’re staying with a friend. It’s a similar story in the swish Kimpton Harbour Suites, where I stayed, which has a sunken jacuzzi positioned next to the bedroom window. Personally, I loved lolling in the tub watching the city flickering below.
Food and drink
The star of the culinary show is undoubtedly Jija, helmed by two-Michelin star chef Vicky Lau, local hero and founder of Tate Dining Room. Rather than opting for the fine dining Lau is famed for, here the menu is more casual, focusing on the comforting flavors of Southwest China and Yunnan. There are a number of dishes I would go back to this buzzing restaurant for in a heartbeat, including the chicken liver parfait with scallion focaccia, the lime shredded chicken, and the Yunnan Paris-Brest made with silky praline cream and seven-color peanuts.
There’s a vintage Palm Springs vibe at the 50th-floor Swim Club, a sunny happening spot with a 30-meter infinity pool lined with sun loungers, blue frilly parasols, and vertiginous views. When you’re not frolicking in the pool, you can peacock at the indoor bar, which serves chilled and grilled tacos and zingy cocktails such as the Pike made with gin, Mancino blanco, and bread and butter pickles. The anytime option is the international restaurant Hillside, which is open every day for breakfast, lunch, and dinner—don’t miss the Hong Kong-style egg waffles in the morning.
The spa
The 11
th
floor is dedicated to wellness with an up-to-the-minute gym, a Hyrox partnership, and male and female changing rooms with steam rooms, saunas, and whirlpools. An Angsana Spa, staffed with Thai therapists, is due to open in April 2026.
The neighborhood/area
This Kowloon-side neighborhood offers the perfect Hong Kong mix of gritty and glam. You’re just one block from the Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront, where you’ll find the Avenue of Stars promenade, the Star Ferry pier, and a number of museums, including the woefully underrated Hong Kong Museum of Art. The surrounding streets are equally cinematic. Genre-defining Hong Kong films such as
Police Story
,
Chunking Express,
and
Infernal Affairs
were all shot in these moody streets packed with cha chaan teng cafés, foot massage parlors, old-school jewelry shops, and open-fronted bars.
The service
Young, perky, and proactive.
For families
The hotel is better suited to grown-ups, but families—and pets—are welcome. There are a number of connecting rooms, and little ones are given special amenities, including slippers, shampoo, shower gel, and toothbrushes. Cats and dogs get chew toys and treats, as well as bowls, pee pads, and litter trays.
Eco effort
Guests are supplied with stainless steel water bottles, which can be refilled at filtered water stations on each floor. There’s a no-single-use plastic policy, toiletries come in refillable bottles, and all of the lighting is LED.
Accessibility
All of the public spaces are accessible, including the pool deck and Swim Club. Ten entry-level King Essential East Sea view rooms have been adapted for wheelchair users.
Anything left to mention?
This Kimpton encourages guests to explore Hong Kong’s green side (more than 70% of the territory is protected green land) with free-to-rent gear and hiking guides packed with trails suitable for every level.
