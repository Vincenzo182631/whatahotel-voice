The Beekman, A Thompson Hotel
Gorgeous design, two excellent restaurants, and a location in a newly revitalized downtown NYC neighborhood.

Best for:
Couples of all ages looking for a chic city break
Location
: Lower Manhattan’s Financial District
Can’t miss feature
: The bar—its crowning glory, and the heart of this delicately wrought Queen Anne structure.
In a nutshell:
Textured and romantic, this is the place to pretend you’re “somewhere else”— while one of New York City's buzziest downtown hubs awaits just outside its door.
Arati Menon
Rooms
The vintage-styled rooms are cozy yet comfortable (the beds deserve their own callout for lulling me into sleep); twice-daily housekeeping includes
turndown
; and the design, while charmingly shabby-chic (the mini bar, filled with local brands of snacks, is housed on a cart dressed in vintage fabric and fringe), gives way to contemporary comfort where needed. The bathrooms are modern classics with Carrara marble tiles, oversized rain showers, and Brooklyn-native
D.S. & Durga
bath amenities. Things aren’t perfectly soundproofed from the comings and goings of the guests on the floor, but the higher up you are, the further from the bustle—and closer to that gorgeous skylight.
Arati Menon
Food and drink
The two on-site restaurants are helmed by heavy hitters: Daniel Boulud at Le Gratin and Tom Colicchio at Temple Court. The former isn’t owned by the hotel but is a valuable addition to it. We had dinner at Temple Court, where you can expect classics like Steak Tartare and Baked Alaska paired with a solid selection of wines, set against a moody backdrop.
But that bar—that seductive mix of velvet and fringe that hums with good energy—is where you’ll want to spend the most time, with its magnificent nine-story atrium and skylight, and bar snacks that are actually rather more-ish (hello, onion dip with caramelized onions and confit garlic oil).
This is not a hotel where food and drink feel like an afterthought. The best moment may simply be ordering a drink in the Bar Room beneath the incandescent atrium, then heading to Le Gratin for dinner and upstairs to bed, as I did. That frictionless, indulgent progression on a snowy New York City evening was a major part of the charm.
Activities and experiences
While there isn’t a spa on site, the gym was a real surprise, given that I was expecting a small hotel gym. Instead, the space is a dual-level fitness center featuring Peloton studio spin bikes and plenty of top-tier machines. True to the style and age of the building, the bar retains old-world charm with wood-paneled walls and vintage fitness posters that made me chuckle.
What to do nearby
The Beekman is located in the heart of Downtown Manhattan, surrounded by some of the city’s most touristed sites, including the Brooklyn Bridge, City Hall, Wall Street, Federal Hall, and the 9/11 memorial. Other notable neighbors include Paris’ shopping import Printemps; Danny Meyers’ buzzy restaurant Manhatta for drinks with a sky-high view; and the revitalized Seaport. SoHo and the Lower East Side, with their thrumming energy, are both a short subway ride away. In the warmer months, the renovated walkway along the West Side Highway is a stellar spot to people-watch and stroll on the Hudson.
Arati Menon
The backstory
The hotel opened in 2016 following a major restoration. The building, once known as Temple Court, was first constructed in 1881, and the atrium was designated an interior landmark by the New York City Landmarks Preservation Commission in June 2024. The hotel became part of Hyatt’s Thompson Hotels portfolio in 2019. That combination helps explain the appeal: it has the bones of a true historic New York building, but it’s operated with the polish and service standards you’d expect from a modern luxury brand.
This is a truly special building with a remarkable history. Its address has hosted everything from New York’s first staging of Shakespeare’s Hamlet to the original home of New York University. When completed in 1882 and renamed Temple Court, the building was groundbreaking for its height, terra-cotta façade, and soaring atrium. Be sure to take in the details: delicate Victorian wrought-iron railings and balustrades adorned with flowers, wings, and sunbursts. On the afternoon of our arrival, I spent time wandering the property, admiring its architectural flourishes—this is definitely something I’d recommend to every guest.
Insider tip
If you’re visiting on a weekend, don’t miss jazz afternoons when local crooners lend extra oomph to the already special The Bar Room.
The thing not to miss here is obvious: that atrium. It’s not just pretty; it’s what gives the place such gravity, and now it’s officially protected as an interior landmark. Also useful to note: There is a daily destination fee, so guests should factor that into the rate.
Final considerations:
Family-friendly:
Better suited to adults, but welcomes kids
Accessibility
: Yes; a wheelchair-accessible property offering ADA-compliant rooms with features like 32-inch wide doorways and roll-in showers. Accessible rooms are available in various categories.
Loyalty program:
