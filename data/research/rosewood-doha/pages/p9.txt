Why book Rosewood Doha?
Doha
’s newest hotel offers a curated take on luxury, complete with sweeping Arabian Gulf views and opportunities to connect with Qatari culture.
Set the scene
It is fitting that we are transported from the
airport
in a midnight blue Rolls-Royce (complete with crystal water glasses in the back and fibre optic lighting that makes it look like the ceiling is covered in a smattering of stars) – the car exudes the same kind of carefully crafted, dependable luxury that Rosewood Doha is aiming for.
A 30-minute drive from Hamad International Airport delivers us to Lusail, dubbed Qatar’s Future City. On the south-easterly tip of its Marina District, the two towers of Rosewood Doha emerge, seemingly from the sea, encased in a striking white latticed facade inspired by the forms of a coral reef. The hotel’s entrance is understated: two simple white doors swept open by staff in sandy-hued suits with dramatic cape-like draping at the shoulder; a reminder that this hotel may like to keep things low-key, but isn’t averse to the odd flourish.
Public spaces are intimate and inviting – homely in scale and softened by a palette of sage green, buttercup yellow and an assortment of blues. The reception area features immediate nods to Qatari culture, many sourced from the winding alleys of Doha’s famed Souq Waqif, including brass-studded wooden boxes traditionally used to store a woman’s dowry; conch shells and coral fragments that celebrate Qatar’s marine environment; wooden miniatures of the hulking dhows that used to sail these waters; and coffee table books highlighting Qatari history, architecture and culture. Elsewhere, delicate capiz shells have been hand-cut and hand-painted to create wall coverings in shimmering shades of emerald green and metallic grey, and artworks by local creatives such as Yasser Al Mulla and Azzam Al Mannai adorn walls. Individualised experiences also allow guests to engage with Qatari culture, from pottery classes with local architect and ceramicist Reema Abu Hassan at the charming, sun-filled, sea-facing Clay Encounters, to visits to Souq Waqif for interactions with local artisans, including traditional falcon-glove makers.
The backstory
Rosewood was launched in 1979, when a sprawling historic home in Dallas, Texas, was transformed into The Mansion on Turtle Creek, creating the blueprint for residential-style hotels that maintain a strong sense of place. The Hong Kong-headquartered hotel brand now manages 33 hotels, resorts and residences in 21 countries, including The Carlyle, A Rosewood Hotel in New York, and Hotel de Crillon, a Rosewood Hotel in
Paris
. Rosewood Doha is its third property in the Middle East, joining existing hotels in Jeddah and Abu Dhabi.
The rooms
The property’s homely feel extends across its 155 rooms and suites, 162 serviced apartments and 276 residences. In my one-bedroom Reef Suite, which includes a dining table for eight, a pantry kitchen and a walk-in wardrobe, a marine-inspired palette reflects the ever-changing colours of the Arabian Gulf, framed by floor-to-ceiling windows and the organic shapes of the building’s latticed outer shell. Top-tier suites are referred to as houses, but even in Al Wajba, which has its own majlis, family room, dining area, private spa room and gym with custom-made Pent equipment, there are textured headboards, russet-coloured statement chairs and bowls of freshly cut flowers to keep things feeling warm and inviting.
Food and drink
I have an exceptional Chinese meal at Koo Madame, named for an adventure-seeking former First Lady of China, famed for piloting planes, penning novels and living until the grand old age of 103. With wood-roasted duck mastered by chef Jingjing, hand-pulled noodles prepared with flair in the open kitchen, clay pots, delicate dim sum and dumplings, the menu encompasses hearty flavours from across China, served in a space that transports guests to 1920s Shanghai. Olive trees and stonework arches frame the entrance to Mila, a
breakfast
spot that does away with the traditional buffet and serves up Levantine favourites for lunch and dinner, and the decadent-looking Stoke and Stoker harks back to the golden age of travel, with a lovingly curated selection of mocktails and cocktails. But I find myself returning time and again to the Butterfly Room, for the rows of pretty pastries – pink-topped peach pavlovas, red-fruit short cakes with a cherry-red glaze, coconut concoctions topped with swirls of yellow – and a decadent afternoon tea bookended with a dedicated chocolate trolley.
The spa
Once fully operational in September, the members-only Asaya Social Wellness Club will serve as one of Doha’s most holistic wellbeing facilities, with treatments and experiences extending across various pillars, including skin health, nutrition, physical movement and community. There will be ear seeding (a form of auriculotherapy rooted in Traditional Chinese Medicine); treatments inspired by local healing practices and using products by Qatari wellness brand Allum; hamam rituals; a padel court; and personal trainers specialising in specific forms of fitness. At the heart of the club is Asaya Kitchen, where nourishing dishes are prepared using sustainably sourced seafood and produce sourced from a local farm, Blessed Land.
The neighbourhood
Envisioned as Qatar’s second city, the rapidly developing Lusail will eventually cover 38 square kilometres, four separate islands and 19 multi-purpose residential, mixed-use and commercial districts. Lusail will eventually be home to 200,000 residents and a whopping 22 hotels – but there are already plenty of architecturally impressive buildings in situ, including the Lego-inspired Marina Twin Towers and the crescent-shaped Katara Towers, designed to emulate traditional Qatari scimitars and home to the Raffles Doha and Fairmont Hotel Doha. Even on a hot evening in July, the marina promenade running in front of the Rosewood is filled with people strolling by the sea and children playing in the interactive fountains.
The service
Warm and intuitive across the board.
For families
Little ones are well catered to via the Rosewood Explorers programme. There’s a dedicated kids pool, outdoor play area and an indoor kids club with a library, cosy reading nooks, wooden toys, games and plenty of other ways to stay out of trouble.
