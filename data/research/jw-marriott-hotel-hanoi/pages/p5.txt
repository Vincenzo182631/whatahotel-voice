JW Marriott Hotel Hanoi
Generous rooms, warm service, and an enormous indoor pool make this lakeside hotel a solid business stay—or a rewarding retreat from central Hanoi.

Reviewed by
Dan Q. Dao
Best for:
Business travelers, families, Marriott Bonvoy loyalists, travelers who appreciate some distance from the city's bustle
Location:
Mễ Trì in western Hanoi, beside the Vietnam National Convention Center
Can't-miss feature:
The enormous eighth-floor indoor lap pool (one of the largest you'll find at any hotel in Hanoi) and the breakfast buffet
In a nutshell:
An established, business-oriented hotel whose spacious suites, strong dining, and especially warm service make the journey from the historic center worthwhile.
Rooms
JW Marriott Hotel Hanoi has 450 rooms and suites. With entry-level rooms measuring roughly 515 square feet, there's plenty of room to spread out before you even consider an upgrade. I'd look at the lake-facing categories to make the most of the setting, or an Executive Suite if you want a separate living space and Executive Lounge access. This is a particularly comfortable choice for a longer stay for business travelers or those who need to WFH while on their trip.
My suite had something I do wish more luxury hotels took seriously: a proper desk setup. The dedicated study cove was genuinely useful for work, and I liked the classic wood paneling. The decor could feel a little utilitarian, but that didn't bother me; the generous proportions and practical layout were more consequential.
Alex Yem
Alex Yem
Food and drink
The Michelin-selected French Grill is a strong argument for spending an evening in at the hotel. My tasting menu included caviar service and a perfect cut of steak served in a setting ideal for a special occasion or business deal. Elsewhere, Akira Back brings the eponymous celebrity chef's Korean-Japanese cooking to Hanoi, while Kumihimo offers polished Japanese fare and JW Café anchors the hotel's all-day dining.
Breakfast at JW Café is expansive, with Northern classics phở and bún chả, a bánh mì station, sushi, salads, and Western classics. But my favorite detail was considerably less grand: a Shin Ramyun instant-noodle bar with a generous selection of toppings, including bok choy, tofu, mushrooms, and eggs. Other guests, among them many South Korean families, seemed just as enthusiastic as I was. The range made sense for the crowd, which also included Vietnamese, Korean, and American travelers. Despite the hotel's business orientation, it also seemed popular with locals for staycations and weekend brunches.
Alex Yem
Alex Yem
Amenities and experiences
I loved the enormous indoor lap pool. Suspended on the eighth floor beneath soaring floor-to-ceiling windows, it has the scale to make swimming a proper part of your stay rather than an afterthought. The surrounding Wellbeing on 8 complex includes a well-equipped fitness center, sauna and steam rooms, and Spa by JW.
Downstairs, JW Lakeside Gardens adds welcome breathing room to an already generously proportioned hotel, with walking paths, an herb garden, cooking activities, and children's programming. Between those grounds and the pool, there's enough here to justify leaving part of the day unscheduled.
The service deserves particular credit. I found the staff consistently warm and genuinely attentive—noticeably so, even by luxury hotel standards. It's the sort of place where I'd feel completely comfortable bringing children or settling in for an extended stay.
Alex Yem
Alex Yem
What to do nearby
Plan around the location. The historic center is roughly a 30- to 40-minute drive away, so this works best if you're happy to do your sightseeing in planned outings rather than popping in and out of the Old Quarter. The hotel's complimentary shuttle into Hoàn Kiếm Lake makes getting downtown pretty convenient, but if you’re staying local, there are still a few worthwhile diversions nearby, from the striking Hanoi Museum to Keangnam Landmark 72's restaurants, observation deck, and shopping, as well as The Garden Shopping Center for a casual meal or coffee.
As a repeat visitor to Hanoi, the distance from the city center became part of the appeal for me. After long days exploring the city or taking trips to Ninh Bình and Hạ Long Bay, I appreciated returning somewhere quieter and more spacious. If you want to spend every evening wandering the Old Quarter, the commute may wear thin. If you value space and calm, it's a worthwhile trade-off.
Alex Yem
The backstory
Opened in 2013 and developed by Bitexco, the hotel was designed by architect Carlos Zapata, whose striking cantilevered building takes inspiration from Vietnam's dramatic coastline and the image of a dragon rising beside the water. The exterior remains one of the most distinctive pieces of contemporary architecture in Hanoi. Inside, the mood is considerably more restrained, prioritizing comfort, generous proportions, and an operation that has had more than a decade to perfect its service for business travelers, families, and Vietnamese staycationers alike.
Insider tip
Head outside for a walk or run. The lakeside gardens wrap around the hotel with its quiet walking paths, seating, and far fewer people than you'll find indoors. It's an easy place to calm the mind before or after a day in bustling Hanoi, without ever technically leaving the property.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes. The spacious accommodations, indoor pool, gardens, and warm service make this an easy hotel to recommend for families.
Accessibility:
Yes. There are accessible rooms and accessible routes to the restaurants, pool, spa, and fitness center.
Wellness:
Wellbeing on 8 is where you'll find the indoor lap pool, Spa by JW, sauna, steam rooms, and fitness center.
Loyalty group/hotel brand:
Marriott Bonvoy
