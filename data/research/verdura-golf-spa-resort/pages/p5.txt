Verdura Resort, Sicily
Striking suites and creative restaurants complement lush green Sicilian countryside and soothing Mediterranean views.

In a nutshell:
Verdura Resort embodies a quiet, considered luxury, formed by expansive space, unhurried serenity, and sweeping Sicilian landscapes. Opened in 2009, the resort has now completed the first chapter of an ambitious three-year design transformation with stylish new restaurants, reimagined public spaces, and beautiful suites.
Rooms
At a resort as exceptional as Verdura, it takes remarkable thoughtfulness and creative vision for the rooms themselves to become one of the things you remember the most. Yet that’s precisely what Paolo Moschino (Philip & Paolo) and Lady Olga Polizzi, director of design at Rocco Forte Hotels and Sir Rocco's sister, have achieved with the redesign. I stayed in one of the new spaces (number seven, to be exact), and was captivated from the moment I opened the door. Rather than stepping straight into a room, you’re welcomed by a private terrace, where an orange tree, a long, inviting, olive green sofa, and a collection of colorful Sicilian ceramics set the tone for what's to come.
Inspired by the surrounding landscape and Sicily’s distinctive aesthetic, the generously sized 72 meter squared Grand suite comes alive in a palette of ocean blue, deep green, juicy orange, washed terra-cotta, and sophisticated eggshell red. Despite the richness of color, everything feels beautifully cohesive, a design language that is fresh, considered, and full of unexpected details. One of the most memorable reveals is the view from the shower, which looks onto a light-filled courtyard where a giant cactus stands against a dramatic backdrop of floor-to-ceiling, hand-painted green-and-white tiles.
There are a further six categories across 203 rooms, ranging from Acacia villas to deluxe rooms, every single one has a sea view and private terrace.
Food and drink
Bridget Mills-Powell
Guests can choose from eight restaurants dotted across the resort, each with its own distinct character. There’s freshly caught fish at the beachside restaurant Amare, cliff top views and warm trattoria atmosphere at Liolà, and Japanese-inspired dishes at Nagori, to name just a few.
Overseeing it all is creative director of food Fulvio Pierangelini, who has shaped each menu around fresh, seasonal ingredients, many plucked straight from the resort’s organic garden, from oranges, lemons, and aromatic herbs to pomegranates, prickly pears, vegetables, and almonds. That garden-to-glass philosophy extends to Scirocco Bar, where I tried one of the signature cocktails: the Zagatini, a martini made with citrus-infused gin and tangerine marmalade.
My culinary highlight came at the aforementioned Liolà, where I ordered the crudo di gamberetti di Sciacca, a distinctly local take on ceviche, combining delicate raw shrimp with a pistachio vinaigrette and tomatoes so fresh they were actually sweet. Dessert was chosen from a beautifully laden table of traditional Italian treats, where the only challenge was deciding between classics such as tiramisu, a bright, glazed cherry topped cassata Siciliana sponge, or a torta alla panna e fragole, a wonderfully light strawberry-and-cream cake.

Amenities and experiences
One of the fortes of this Rocco Forte hotel is the luxurious space, whether you experience it in the never-ending view of the coast, a generously sized suite, or the vast green landscape, which includes two 18-hole championship golf courses and a 9-hole, par 3 course.
But that's not quite your thing, there are plenty of other ways to make the most of Verdura. Cycle along the coastline, set sail across the Mediterranean, dive with certified instructors, explore on horseback, or pick up a racket for a game of tennis or padel.
What to do nearby
Although this is certainly the type of resort you could relax in for days on end, a venture out to the nearby town of Sciacca is highly recommended. Just a 15-minute drive from the resort, the historic fishing port makes for an idyllic evening excursion. Wander through narrow alleyways dotted with colorful tiles, intricate mosaics, and sun-faded façades. Sciacca is also known for its ceramics, one of Sicily’s oldest artistic traditions, so if the hotel’s interior details have inspired you, head to one of the boutiques here to pick up your own unique, hand painted piece.

Insider tip
If you're arranging an airport transfer, ask for Palo. He turns the 90-minute drive into part of the experience, with an impromptu stop for local gelato, stories about the landmarks along the route (including the stretch of coastline where Dolce & Gabbana once staged a fashion show), and a running list of the celebrities he's chauffeured to the resort over the years.
Wellness
Surrounded by olive and orange groves, the 4,000-square-meter spa has been brought to life by Irene Forte, Rocco Forte’s wellness director and founder of her eponymous skincare brand. Sustainability is woven throughout. Solar panels help produce the spa’s hot water, while the B Corp-certified skincare line is formulated with natural ingredients and plant extracts, many grown on the brand’s own organic farm.
I opted for a facial and could immediately sense there was something distinctive about the products. With each stage of the treatment came a new fragrance: first pistachio, then rose, hibiscus, and lavender, turning the skincare experience into a sensory journey.
For families
Verdura Resort is said to be the most family-friendly of all the Rocco Forte hotels, and it’s easy to see how it earned that reputation. The kids club is a thoughtfully designed space for children of all ages. In fact, it’s so well done that I didn’t mind spending time there myself. There’s a wooden climbing wall and gymnastics area, games galore, a heated swimming pool with an impressive collection of inflatables, an art station, a tasteful soft-play area for toddlers and even a kitchen and science lab, where children were busy making rockets when I visited.
And the entertainment extends far beyond the kids club. There are sporting academies, horse riding, and visits to the resort’s on-site farm, as well as quad biking and movie nights under the stars.
