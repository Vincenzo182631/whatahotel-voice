In a nutshell:
The Waldorf Astoria Chengdu rises 52 stories above the city's Financial City district, with a Michelin-selected Chinese restaurant, a full-service spa, and sweeping views of Chengdu's skyline.
Iona Brannon
Rooms
The 289 rooms and 31 suites span the upper floors of the 52-story tower, all with sweeping views overlooking
Chengdu’s
skyline—city, park, or the iconic Twin Towers of Financial City. I stayed in the Twin Tower View Suite, 105 square meters of luxurious living and sleeping spaces, a marble soaking tub, a double vanity, and views that were, without question, one of my favorite parts of the stay. At night, the towers have a light show that made me stop and stare out the window again and again before bed. Every room comes with the signature Waldorf concierge cabinet, a discreet built-in nook for contactless service, and marble bathrooms stocked with
Aesop
toiletries.
Iona Brannon
Iona Brannon
Food and drink
The culinary highlight of the hotel is Infinite Luck, on the 50th floor in an intimate dining room where the city spreads out within view. The restaurant was selected for the Michelin Guide in 2026 thanks to a revamp of its culinary vision by executive chef Tony Yang, a Chengdu native with over three decades in Sichuan gastronomy. My waitress presented a carved wooden map of Sichuan, pointing out corners of the region where menu ingredients originated, and I especially loved trying the local red wines.
Limited Edition Grill serves Western dishes. On the 49th floor, Peacock Alley is the place for afternoon tea; meanwhile, the hotel serves breakfast at Brasserie 99, a buffet of local flavors and made-to-order stations alongside à la carte Western classics like eggs Benedict and pancakes. The hotel bar, Limited Edition-Sip, is worth a visit for its craft cocktail menu, rich leather interiors, and 52nd-floor city views.
Amenities and experiences
I had a 90-minute combination massage and facial treatment at the Waldorf Astoria spa. Sessions start with a warm towel and apple tea. I loved touches like the small arrangement of fresh flowers beneath the massage table, placed at eye level. The Chinese-style massage works through acupressure that was painful yet releasing, targeting all the knots that had built up over my trip. The 60-minute facial that followed, using Valmont products, was so deeply relaxing that I fell asleep. We ended the session with a bowl of Chinese beauty soup made of snow fungus, goji berries, and red dates. The final result was so impressive that I sent before-and-after selfies to all my friends.
The 48th floor also houses a 25-meter heated indoor pool overlooking the city skyline as well as a 1,200-square-meter fitness center open 24 hours for in-house guests with sauna, steam, ice, and salt rooms.
What to do nearby
The hotel is steps from the luxury mall
in99
and a one-minute walk from Jiaozi Park, an ecological greenway with lotus leaf-shaped bridges, a lakeside boardwalk, and an outdoor circular theater. My best recommendation came from my concierge, who pointed me toward Eastern District Memory (Dongqu Jiyi). The former electronics factory complex was renovated into a creative arts and culture hub. I spent an afternoon wandering its converted warehouse lanes, eating spicy glass noodles, and sipping fun-flavored coffees. The hotel is also close to the metro, only a 10-minute walk from the Financial City North metro station on Line 1, the city’s main north-south spine. It’s easy to connect directly to attractions like Tianfu Square and Chunxi Road, the city's main shopping and dining district
Insider tip
Ask your personal concierge for a neighborhood recommendation. Mine sent me to Dongqu Jiyi, and it became the highlight of my time in the city.
