Explora Patagonia National Park
The only luxury lodge in the newly formed Patagonia National Park is a front-row seat to sprawling grasslands, snow-capped Andean peaks, and (if you’re lucky) a puma or two.

Why book?
Explora’s lodge in
Patagonia National Park
, set in remote southern Chile’s Chacabuco Valley, is the only luxury stay in the newly formed park and the best way to see this still-pristine and wildly untouristed swath of wilderness. The park is part of an enormous tract of land that American conservationists and rewilding philanthropists Kris and Doug Tompkins donated to the Chilean government to turn into protected parkland sustained by ecotourism. There is access to miles of amazing hiking trails, gin bottle-blue glacial rivers and lakes, and ample opportunity to spot the again-thriving native wildlife. That the lodge is supremely chic, with excellent food and the kindest, most enthusiastic and knowledgeable guides makes it a true treasure.
Set the scene
This place is very remote, which you’ll sort of grasp when you try and locate it on the map, but won’t fully understand until you’ve walked the proverbial walk. Or, in this case, experienced the multiple flights and long van ride it takes to get here: It’s about a two hour flight from the Santiago airport to the Balmaceda airport and from there, about a six-hour van ride to the lodge; or the quicker (and way more baller move) one hour-long charter flight from Balmaceda to the tiny Cochrane airstrip, followed by about an hour van ride to Explora. Unlike other better-known and more-touristed parks in Patagonia, like Torres del Paine and Los Glaciares, there’s a strong chance you’ll have this park to yourself. You pull in and the main lodge and 13 guest spaces (eight standard rooms and five suites) sit in this small valley surrounded by rolling hills and steeper ice-capped mountains in the distance. The grassy area around the lodge is a favorite place for herds of guanacos (a close relative of the llama) to graze and sun themselves. All this privacy and access to nature doesn’t come cheap and the guests are a well-off but sporty, active crowd. Think this year’s Arc'teryx gear by day and quiet luxury by night (Loro Piano, maybe a camel-hued Brunello sweater). But the point of coming here is to experience the park and the guests are here to do just that. They’ve chosen the Explora brand for its commitment to responsibly exploring nature and this specific lodge for its dedication to rewilding and protecting the environment.
The backstory
Explora has three decades of experience in South America and has set the benchmark for adventure lodges and guiding in the region. As mentioned, Explora is the only luxury lodge in Patagonia National Park. The hospitality brand holds a 30-year concession to run the lodge, which is part of the Tompkins Conservation project and managed by the National Forestry Agency. It’s a tripartite relationship that is continuing to shepherd this roughly 740,000 acres of Andean wilderness filled with forests, open steppes, ice fields and glacier-melt lakes and rewild its endemic species that were nearly wiped away after decades of over farming, including guanacas, Andean condor, and the elusive puma.
The rooms
The room decor is exactly right for the location, simple but so beautiful with wide-planked floors, beamed ceilings, wood-framed windows and built-in armoires. The attention to detail and craftsmanship is very high. Colors are neutral and echo the surroundings rather than competing with them—stone-gray throw blankets and pillows, beige arm chairs, white walls–and artwork is at a minimum, maybe a tasteful landscape photograph in a simple black frame. It’s all very understated and elegant without feeling at all over thought or precious. Think the room equivalent of that sumptuous but simple Brunello sweater…
Food and drink
The main lodge is the heart of the property and where the casual reception desk, sweet bar, dining room and lounging spaces are. It’s where you meet your guide in the morning, gather for a beer or a pisco sour after a trek, read before dinner, and where you take all of your meals. Like the guest rooms, the lodge is beautifully constructed from reclaimed wood, repurposed stone and recycled copper. It is understated but lovely, with high wood-beam ceilings, comfortable but modern couches and arm chairs and lots of windows that look out at the surrounding park. On our first night, we spotted a puma through the bar windows and all quietly snuck out to see it slinking behind the berm of the creek. Meals and drinks are all inclusive here, which makes everything easy and stress free. For such a small lodge, the food is stellar. The breakfast options are plentiful–yogurt and fresh fruit, pastries, made-to-order eggs while lunches are nourishing and usually eaten mid-excursion: think thermoses of soup, sandwiches, grain salads, and some delicious sweet treat. Dinners, while not fancy, are exceptional–grilled meats on the plancha, vegetarian pastas, and always some excellent local vegetable and perfectly dressed salad–and an impressive wine selection. Like with the decor, it’s about quality and attention to detail–nothing flashy but everything extremely well executed.
The spa
There is a small spa with three outdoor hot tubs and two treatment rooms. I didn’t visit during my stay but there’s no way a soak in one of those tubs after an all-day trek doesn’t feel divine.
The neighborhood/area
There’s no neighborhood to speak of. The closest town is Cochrane, which had under 3000 people based on the last official count I could find, and was only connected to the rest of Chile via a main road about 35 years ago. Cochrane is about a 45-minute drive from Explora . The town has a small park and a few shops and tourist-focused spots–like a place to rent kayaks for trips along the Cochrane River.
The service
From the general manager to the guides and the chefs–everyone is warm, professional and extremely capable. At such a remote place, everyone has to know what they’re doing and do it dependably. The guides I had were wonderful. Young, enthusiastic, very passionate and knowledgeable, and a lot of fun, which is huge when you’re on an 8-hour hike with someone.
For families
As stated previously, it takes a lot of travel time and effort to get here. It might be too much for young kids, and while kids of any age are welcome, this lodge does not really seem like a solid choice for the very little ones given the emphasis on outdoor activities–trekking, kayaking, biking, wildlife spotting. I see it as a great fit for outdoorsy, nature-curious kids, ideally, in my opinion, those 10 years and up.
Eco effort
Patagonia National Park, and this lodge which is dedicated to exploring the park, is one big eco effort. This park is the result of the Tompkins’ passion for this part of the world and their desire to rewild this part of Chile that had been overfarmed and overgrazed to the point of almost eradicating its native flora and fauna. The Tomkins’ conservation efforts have had a tremendous impact in terms of helping restore the puma, guanaco, Andean condor, and rhea (native ostrich) population. There is a wonderful museum in the park that interactively showcases the landscape and cultural history of the Chacabuco Valley as well as this part of the region's ecological loss and path to recovery. Additionally, Explora is a B Corporation and is certified as a CarbonNeutralCompany. It is committed to recycling and repurposing throughout this lodge as well as its other lodges, and, as a company, it is involved with the cultural side of sustainability in terms of preserving local culture and making sure money stays in the surrounding communities through hiring and sourcing locally whenever possible.
Accessibility
One of the guest rooms is suitable for accessibility needs; the paths that connect the lodge to the guest rooms are paved. Excursions via van are possible.
Anything left to mention?
The guided excursions make the stay and there are many different ways to fill your days. There are dozens of hiking options depending on your desired level and how long or how far you want to go, including, in the extremes, technical, high mountain ascents and slow saunters around the lodge to spot wildlife. One of my favorite and the most visually remarkable hikes I took was around Lago Chico. It wasn’t the hardest or longest hike I took during my stay, but the views of Lago Chco and Lake Cochrane and the jagged, icy Andes in the distance were amazing. There are also kayak excursions, bike trips, bird watching treks, and overland excursions via van.
