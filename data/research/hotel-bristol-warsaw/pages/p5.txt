## Discover the Real Essence of the City

Founded over 700 years ago, Warsaw has grown to be Poland’s most vibrant and dynamic city. It captivates visitors with two exceptional royal residences, the enchanting Old Town, and the 18th-century tenement houses and palaces along the Royal Route, as well as parks offering peaceful retreats.

Heart of Warsaw

Ideally situated along Warsaw’s prestigious Royal Route, Hotel Bristol is at the heart of the city and surrounded by historic palaces, churches, and statues dating from the 16th century to the present. Nestled next to the Presidential Palace, it is also within walking distance of Warsaw's most renowned sights, such as the Old Town, the Royal Castle, the National Museum, the National Theater, and the Opera House. The latter two are over 200 years old and continue to feature classical world-renowned works by legendary composers like Beethoven, Bellini, Tchaikovsky, Mozart, Puccini, Wagner, and more.

Taking a horse-drawn carriage ride or renting bikes are memorable ways to see the city and discover its art galleries, epicurean restaurants, cafés, parks, gardens, and museums. These include the Copernicus Science Center: the largest scientific institution of its kind in Europe. For luxury shopping, the high-end VITKAC department store on Bracka Street is just 15 minutes from the hotel on foot. It houses international boutiques, including Dior, Bottega Veneta, Gucci and YSL.

Guests wanting to explore the city’s culture and history can join the city’s myriad tours and visit Wilanów Palace, the baroque residence of King John III Sobieski, located 12 kilometers from the hotel. Tours to Zelazowa Wola, the birthplace and home of composer Fryderyk Chopin, are also available. Located in a baroque-style castle, this multimedia museum invites visitors to fully experience the famous musician's complicated, inspired life.

Throughout the year, Warsaw comes alive with a vibrant calendar of festivals, including the Warsaw International Film Festival, the Jazz at the Old Town Square Festival, and a variety of events celebrating jazz, Beethoven, and baroque compositions—many of which take place just minutes from the hotel. From May through September, guests can also enjoy open-air Chopin concerts in the picturesque Royal Lazienki Park.

The official language of Warsaw is Polish, and the currency is the Polski Zloty (PLN). The electrical system is standard European 220 - 230 Volt. American electrical devices need a converter and an adaptor; both of which are available from the hotel.

General shopping hours are Monday to Saturday 11 am to 9 pm. Shops are closed in Poland on most Sundays, with some exceptions during the year. During the Christmas holidays, nearly all shops and restaurants are closed on December 25th and 26th.

The Future & the Past

Welcoming the 21st century with striking modern architecture and two international airports, Warsaw connects travelers to over 135 global destinations across more than 50 countries. As Poland’s largest city and the cultural and business heart of Central Europe, Warsaw blends Western and Eastern influences to create a unique tapestry of tradition, culture, art and innovation. Alluring and inspiring, timeless and yet romantic, the city today is closer to Western Europe than ever before, preserving its extraordinary atmosphere and with a touch of historic grandeur.

The city is transforming rapidly, with new investments reshaping Warsaw’s skyline day by day. Highlights include the state-of-the art National Stadium, the Copernicus Science Center, and new museums such as the Museum of the History of Polish Jews POLIN and the Warsaw Uprising Museum, which offer an unforgettable and stimulating feel to anyone who visits them.
