The Ritz-Carlton, Santiago
This 15-story hotel has polished service, a great health club on the top floor, and a tasty, new Chilean menu representing various regions of the country.

Reviewed by
Ramsey Qubein
Why book?
Guests stay here to be in the heart of the city’s bustling business and shopping district. You can walk everywhere, whether it is to corporate offices, top restaurants, or shopping malls, and when you come back, the well-known Ritz-Carlton hospitality is waiting for you.
Set the scene
Towering skyscrapers fill the window-framed panoramas here with the Andes Mountains in the backdrop. This 15-story hotel is considered the city’s top luxury hotel and is perfectly positioned for both business and touristic pursuits.
Doormen won’t let you lift a finger when you arrive, swinging open the doors or offering a bottle of water as you enter the wood-paneled lobby. Discreetly in a back corner is the reception desk, and a large chandelier illuminates the fresh flower arrangements below. While the lobby does not have much space for lingering aside from a few tufted armchairs, it is a quiet place to grab a bite to eat in the lobby lounge. Live music happens during the evening hours to pick up the pace with a pair of grand piano ivories waiting to be tickled.
American accents are in full force here as this property welcomes plenty of U.S. tourists and business people. But what I really noticed from the moment I arrived was the hushed elegance of the public spaces and the courtesy of the staff. It’s hard to describe it, but it’s just oh-so Ritz-Carlton.
The backstory
It first opened its doors in 2003 as the first Ritz-Carlton in South America. Today, it is still the only one on the continent, and when it debuted, it immediately elevated the hospitality standards for the Chilean capital. Just a few blocks from here is the W Santiago, another luxury property in the Marriott portfolio. And like The Ritz-Carlton, when W opened in 2009, it was also the first hotel for the W brand in South America, cementing this city’s high-end profile for the luxury-focused traveler for years to come. Other big names have since followed.
The rooms
If I had to use one word to describe the rooms here, it would be peaceful. The color tones are beige, navy, and blue, the enormous Ritz-Carlton mattress with chunky pillows and plush duvet, and excellent soundproofing instantly calm you. After a redeye flight, it’s just what you need.
I appreciated having an extra-wide desk with international power ports by the windows, and if you score one of the junior suites, you’ll have a large sitting area, too. Bottled water was never in short supply during my stay. Other nice touches are Nespresso machines on the countertop and interesting art pieces depicting cultural artifacts of the native Andean culture. Framed in my room was a pre-Hispanic calculating tool that was made from colorful strings and beads. Others filled hallways and public spaces, giving a museum-esque touch as you meander.
Bathrooms are coated with marble and feature a tub and shower, both stocked with Diptyque toiletries in pump bottles and bath salts with loofahs. Tucked away in a leather box are all types of toiletries, from toothbrushes with eco-friendly sachets of toothpaste and razors.
Most impressive was the housekeeping service, which goes above and beyond by folding clothes left out, tying charging cables with a Ritz-Carlton branded Velcro strap, and organizing your personal effects with great care. It’s an extra step that shows attention to detail.
The property is designed with all rooms on one side of the building, assuring a better view of the surrounding skyline rather than a neighboring building. Beyond the standard Deluxe rooms, there are also Junior suites and larger Executive suites, the latter with a separate living room. The largest Presidential Suite has hosted quite a few bigwigs over the years.
High floors are the better option to take in the views, and rooms with Club Lounge access are a treat. The Ritz-Carlton brand has perfected what a great club lounge should be, and Santiago is no different. There are five food and beverage presentations throughout the day, including hot breakfast, light lunch and dinner spreads, and an evening nightcap service with desserts. Alcohol is available throughout the day, including several popular Chilean wine options, and the Chilean candies and pastries in glass jars are addictive.
Food and drink
In addition to the lobby lounge, which serves drinks and light bites throughout the day, there is also Estró Restaurant offering a Chilean-inspired menu. The executive chef works with a local farm that grows half of its harvest for the hotel. They then let the property select the best of the best, right down to the leaves of lettuce, where chefs can select the best ones to use for guests.
Interestingly, before I learned this, I was thinking to myself just how vibrant some of the kale and vegetables were on the buffet. Even the fish is line-caught by local fishermen who are able to provide the freshest catches to the hotel regularly for ceviches or grilling.
As such, the menu changes seasonally with a big overhaul in the coming months to highlight even more Chilean produce and dishes. During my stay, the menu read like a geography lesson of Chile. The salmon came from Puerto Montt in the south, the lamb from Patagonia, and the octopus caught in Coquimbo, north of Santiago.
A coffee shop with outdoor tables sits next to the restaurant, but its entrance is on the street. There is another bar by the pool for cocktails on the outdoor terrace.
The spa
The top floor wellness space has a day spa with traditional massage and facials, but also several regionally-inspired treatments like the “Ancestral Sensory Journey” experience I had. It incorporates herbs and plants from different regions of Chile to create an exfoliating hand scrub followed by an oil-based massage. Fully rejuvenated after my treatment, the men’s and women’s relaxation areas have steam and sauna facilities plus fantastic skyline views from the windows.
On the other side of the 15th floor is the large indoor pool that sits beneath a curving glass ceiling that brings in ample sunlight. The pool is heated for year-round use, and there is also a whirlpool, outdoor terrace with lounge chairs, and a full fitness center one floor above the pool.
When I arrived with my laptop to catch up on some emails by the pool, an attendant quickly brought over a large ice bucket with bottles of water. And during a quick work break visit to the gym, I was surprised to find a fridge stocked with bottles of water and Gatorade, plus protein bars and fresh fruit. There’s also a jogging station by the front entrance of the hotel with towels, running maps, and bottled water.
The neighborhood/area
Las Condes has it all, from great restaurants to eclectic shopping. It’s a 15-minute walk to the tallest building in South America, Gran Torre Santiago, where you can take a high-speed elevator to the top and enjoy epic views of the city and the Andes. Cerro San Cristóbal is also a short stroll from The Ritz-Carlton, where gondolas transport you to the top to see the Virgin Mary statue that towers over the city and the beautiful gardens surrounding it.
Also in the neighborhood is the W Santiago, which has an outstanding weekend brunch that I often go to when in town. It is also part of the Marriott luxury portfolio, and the concierge here can help set up a reservation for the poolside brunch, which I highly recommend.
Pro tip: Provide your Marriott Bonvoy number when dining there, as you can earn points, even when not staying at the W as a guest.
The service
Known as Ladies and Gentlemen, The Ritz-Carlton places a high benchmark on its employee standards, and in Santiago, it is clear that they have chosen the best of the best. For example, when I ordered coffee at breakfast in the club lounge, I asked for filtered coffee instead of the strong espresso available. They didn’t have any there, but offered to call the restaurant downstairs to have some sent up. Or one day, when I returned to my room while the housekeeper was cleaning, she offered to come back later and personally introduced herself, asking if I needed anything extra.
For families
This is not really a kiddie kind of place, but there are special welcome gifts for young guests who check in here. And the staff goes out of their way to make them feel welcome, especially at the restaurant where there is a Ritz Kids menu for the most discerning of palates.
Anything left to mention?
As someone who has stayed in nearly a dozen hotels in the city, this is a clear frontrunner for best hotel in Santiago. It has polished service, a great health club on the top floor, and a tasty, new Chilean menu representing various regions of the country.
