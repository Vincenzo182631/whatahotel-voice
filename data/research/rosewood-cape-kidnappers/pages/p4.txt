Why book?
Rosewood Cape Kidnappers is the pinnacle of luxury on Hawke's Bay and the ideal place to unwind—especially if you're a golfer or a birder.
Set the scene
Fly into the little regional airport at Napier, drive through the city's Art Deco downtown, and continue along the coast and up into the hills until you reach Rosewood Cape Kidnappers, a 6,000-acre coastal country retreat and working farm perfect for unwinding in luxury. Many guests come here to golf—the Tom Doak–designed course is legendary—but there are also families on vacation, couples, and even solo travelers. They hail from all over, from the US to Japan to Australia.
The backstory
The story begins with the acclaimed golf course Cape Kidnappers, built by the American Robertson family in 2004; the luxury hotel followed in 2007, joining the Robertsons' other lodges Kauri Cliffs in Matauri Bay and Matakauri in Queenstown. Luxury hotel group Rosewood entered the picture in 2023. As for the name of the property, it dates to Captain James Cook, who named the location after members of the local Māori community attempted to ‘rescue’ a Tahitian cabin boy from his ship, believing he was being held captive.
The rooms
The rooms are minimalist country-house chic: lofted ceilings with timber trusses, off-white shiplap, and barn doors. Deep soaking tubs sit in front of picture windows overlooking the sea, while a tucked-away wet bar has a mini fridge. They're split between two locations, one just above the timber-and-stone main lodge, and one descending down a hill—all offer ocean views. There's also a massive four-bedroom villa with a full kitchen for groups and families, and it can be booked as a two-bedroom accommodation as well.
Food and drink
The main dining room serves three meals a day, including a farmhouse-style buffet breakfast with à la carte hot dishes, and lighter fare like soups and salads for lunch. Cocktail hour is hosted throughout the main lodge, and it's followed by a thoughtful dinner menu that skews global with a hint of Asian touches (think: sashimi as a starter and yellowfin tuna with gai lan (Chinese broccoli) as a main). You can add on a three-glass New Zealand wine pairing for an additional charge. For a second option, guests can dine at the clubhouse on the golf course with a more casual menu.
The spa
Quiet treatment rooms perfect for pampering line a hallway in a building just above the main lodge. Following your massage, sit on the terrace with a cup of tea.
The neighborhood/area
The overall areas of Hawke's Bay is perfectly suited to leisure travelers—it's a wine region, it has fly fishing, and there's a cute downtown in Napier. We never left the property during our brief stay, but if we had been there longer, we'd have certainly made day trips out into the region.
The service
The service is warm, but nonintrusive. Many of the staff working at Cape Kidnappers are young adults from abroad on short-term work visas—some are here to combine their hospitality work with their conservation work at Cape Sanctuary (more on that below), as well as with some personal travel in New Zealand.
For families
There are two-bedroom family suites available, but don't expect a kids club. Instead, families can watch sheepdogs at work, go hiking, and meet encounter native birds throughout the property.
Eco effort
Rosewood Cape Kidnappers is home to Cape Sanctuary, which brands itself as “New Zealand's largest privately owned and funded 'mainland island' conservation project.” Thanks to a predator fence, pest control, habitat restoration, and species reintroduction, this is one of the best places on mainland New Zealand for native wildlife, notably birds, including kiwis. Both daytime and nighttime tours are offered exclusively to Cape Kidnappers guests. While we weren't able to take a tour during our stay, we did make it out to the gannet colony—tens of thousands of the birds come to nest here, putting on an utterly entertaining display against the backdrop of the Pacific Ocean. There's also a kaka aviary and feeding station in the woods near the main lodge. If you time your visit just right, you might catch volunteers refilling the stations, with kaka flying in for the feast.
Anything left to mention?
Complimentary activities change day to day and include options like flower-arranging classes, foraging workshops, and farm tours. There are quite a few hiking trails on the property, too. My favorite amenity was the infinity-edge pool, which we found perfect for stargazing during an after-dinner dip.
