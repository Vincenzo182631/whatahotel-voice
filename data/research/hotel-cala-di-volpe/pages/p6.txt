Cala Di Volpe, Sardinia
This low-key luxury architectural treat is a Sardinian institution.

Reviewed by

Why book?
For a stay at a singular place—at once in a time warp yet luxuriously modern. There is no other hotel like this, unless you can think of another where architectural prowess meets a starring role in a James Bond film; it even has its own
coffee-table book
. The indulgence, the sweeping views, the extensive grounds, the design detail wherever you turn—you could say nobody does it better.
Set the scene
Cala Di Volpe is to be found in its namesake bay, meaning Fox Cove—hence the hotel’s Lego-block fox logo—within a small community located in Arzachena in the province of Sassari, in the ritzy northeast of what is already a very high-end island destination. Subtle elegance is key here: an understated driveway to the entrance, nestled amongst lush planting, and no imposing marble lobbies or shiny surfaces. In fact, you’d be hard-pressed to locate a straight line here (apart from a level floor, thank goodness).
You are amongst the jet set; return customers going back decades, mixing with moneyed newlyweds, celebrities, and the higher echelons of society. It’s refreshingly more casual than flashy—apart from the
Dolce and Gabbana
collaboration in the pool and bar areas (a 2025 season partner).
The backstory
Now a member of Marriott’s Luxury Collection, the hotel—the first built in the region and opened in 1962—was designed by the legendary self-taught architect Jacques Couëlle. He existed on the edge of the modernist movement, but used the whitewashed Mediterranean style and bent it to his will, with curves, arches, spirals, picture windows, and stained glass. Like I said, not a straight line in sight. Inspired by local fishing villages, it was slinky and sexy enough to grab the attention of the James Bond location scouts, and played a starring role in
The Spy Who Loved Me
... the song will not leave your head during your stay here after learning this fact.
It has recently been renovated by the Parisian studio Moinard Bétaille, purposely keeping the older rooms pretty much as was (with necessary contemporary fixtures and fittings) for the aforementioned return guests, but adding an incredibly sympathetic but uber-modern wing which retains the organic, rustic, cave-like qualities of the original design. On a walk through the hotel, you can time travel from the 1960s to the present day.
The rooms
At once luxurious and womb-like, and packed with personality, the accommodation maintains the feel of the rest of the hotel. In the bathroom, rain showers, baths, and even toilets are nestled within what feel like naturally occurring nooks, with
Acqua Di Parma
toiletries liberally scattered throughout.
The furniture—bedside tables, the shelves, even the sofa—is built into the rough whitewashed walls, with asymmetrical bamboo ceilings forming a calming canopy. My suite had a balcony with a sweeping view over the bay, and of course, all modern comforts such as a top of the range Bang & Olufsen Bluetooth speaker and Chromecast TV, for those nights where you can’t bring yourself to leave your magic grotto.
Special mention must go to the Harrods suite, which, being named after the world-famous London department store, is everything the brand implies. (
Beyoncé
has stayed here, for goodness sake.) For the price of a small house deposit, you will get 2,690 square feet of space, which includes three king bedrooms, a bar, a wine cellar, and a private infinity pool on a panoramic roof terrace.
Food and drink
Breakfast is a treat: those views, silver service, and freshly made sweet pastries, a buffet, and an à la carte menu (I highly recommend the omelet). The Beef Bar, as the name may indicate, is a carnivore’s paradise of generous portions and excellent cuts with an incredibly naughty truffle mash.
The culinary headliner, however, is Japanese restaurant Matsuhisa, opened by world-renowned chef Nobu in 2018, where the famous black miso cod is served, alongside tuna sashimi and salmon tartare caviar, again, all with those views. Tables throughout were readily available at the time of visiting, but this was at the end of the season, and this is apparently a favorite dining spot for the yachts to moor in the evening at the height of summer.
The friendly bar and its knowledgeable staff sit sunken in the lobby area. Be sure to order the Bellini made with white peaches; it wouldn’t be a proper visit without it. A word of warning, too—beware the very audacious seagulls.
The spa
The Shiseido Spa, to be found down a spiral staircase near the concierge, is even more grotto-like than the rooms. Along with the usual high-end offerings of massages and facials, you can book a private 45-minute Thermal Baths session (complimentary for hotel guests). This intimate ritual includes a steam room, sauna, emotional shower, and genuinely lovely natural Sardinian scrubs, along with a curated selection of herbal teas. It will leave you feeling cleansed.
The neighborhood/area
During my short stay, the Cala Di Volpe area seemed to be the hotel and the hotel only. To explore the island further, you would need a rental (there are taxis too, but they are extortionate), but a short drive away, you will find the resort’s 18-hole golf course and academy. This is very much an experiential hotel—to call it a resort feels too low-rent, but you could easily spend a week here and occupy yourself with activities such as
tennis lessons
, a well-equipped gym, one of the largest saltwater pools in Europe, or a private beach accessible by speed boat every half hour. The concierge is always able and willing to arrange excursions, tours, a boat to the Maddalena Islands, fishing trips, diving, snorkeling, sailing, horseback riding, and airport transfers. Or you could just eat, drink, and relax.
For families
For such a high-end, low-key place, this is very family-friendly. There is the aforementioned massive saltwater pool, as well as a garden accessible to guests and a petting zoo. Children’s menus, a playground, and activities are available too.
Accessibility
There are accessible rooms and communal areas, with elevators to all floors.
Anything left to mention?
The hotel is the experience, so make sure you have a good snoop for those architectural treats, and amongst all the world flavors that are being offered to you, make time for the excellent Italian cuisine at Le Grand. Maybe a more casual day eatery might be useful, but you can be sure that you’ll never be hungry.
