Why book?
Tucked away in the hills above Los Angeles, staying at Hotel Bel-Air is like being a guest at a grand private estate with lush gardens, extreme privacy, and old Hollywood glamour.
Set the scene
The hotel is located in an exclusive gated community deliberately removed from the hustle of LA. There’s no surrounding neighborhood scene, and that’s exactly the appeal. Spread across 12 acres and designed in a Spanish Colonial style, this residential hideaway attracts discreet Angelenos, international travelers seeking old Hollywood charm, and industry heavyweights who prioritize privacy above all else. Many guests and patrons are regulars, lingering over martinis as a fireplace crackles in the background. Their looks skew timeless and understated: linen blazers, loafers without socks, oversized sunglasses.
The backstory
The hotel traces its roots to 1922, when oil tycoon Alphonzo Bell purchased more than 600 acres of land to create a pastoral residential community he named Bel-Air. Two decades later, Hotel Bel-Air opened, quickly becoming a refuge for Hollywood royalty and global tastemakers, hosting everyone from Grace Kelly to Elizabeth Taylor. The property even served as the setting for Bert Stern’s famed 1962 photographs of Marilyn Monroe, taken shortly before her death. Today, Hotel Bel-Air is part of the Dorchester Collection, a small portfolio of landmark hotels that also includes the nearby Beverly Hills Hotel. The iconic pink-walled property—home to its resident swans Hercules, Athena, Odette, and Chloe—last underwent a major renovation in 2011. Nearly eight decades on, its identity remains remarkably consistent, rooted in a classic, residential sensibility. Thoughtful modern touches—like in-room iPads, automatic fireplaces, and high-tech Japanese toilets—bring the experience more into the present.
The rooms
While each of Hotel Bel-Air’s 58 rooms and 45 suites varies in size and character, all blend vintage Hollywood elegance with contemporary comfort, with interiors by Alexandra Champalimaud. The look is classic and calm: muted neutrals, linen drapery, warm woods, and soft leather accents that nod to the property’s Spanish Colonial roots. It all creates a sense of lived-in ease and quiet sophistication. Most accommodations, ranging from roughly 450 square feet to more than 2,200 square feet, are set along meticulously landscaped pathways, but the standout options are the 12 canyon rooms and suites. Built into the hillside, they feature retractable glass walls, expansive furnished patios, and private spa pools that make the most of the hotel’s hidden setting. For guests who plan to spend time at the iconic oval pool, the junior poolside suites are ideally located for easy dips.
Food and drink
The hotel’s main restaurant—aptly named The Restaurant—sits in a garden-like, alfresco setting serving seasonal Californian cuisine. While nothing on the menu is especially daring, the quality of locally sourced ingredients and meticulous preparation shines through in every dish I sampled. Don’t skip the house-made bread basket—a generous spread of ficelle, garlic croissant, black truffle brioche, and multi-seed lavash served with Bordier butter that’s dangerously easy to finish before the first course arrives. A sleeper hit is the organic Jidori chicken, a deceptively simple main that’s elevated by black Burgundy truffle and a flavorful jus.
Desserts come from the onsite patisserie (my crème brûlée was perfectly decadent). In between meals or for a nightcap, head to The Living Room, a lobby-lounge space anchored by a central fireplace, where guests and locals gather for weekend afternoon tea or what regulars insist is the coldest martini in town.
The spa
The hotel’s spa features seven treatment rooms and uses products from Swiss luxury skincare brand Valmont. The standout service is an OxyLight facial, which uses multiple advanced techniques, including LED, oxygen, microcurrent, resurfacing, and ultrasound in a single session. (Insider tip: Book with Bette, who delivers one of the most thorough and deeply relaxing facials on property.)
The neighborhood/area
Hotel Bel-Air may feel secluded, but it’s still well positioned for classic LA outings. Beverly Hills and Rodeo Drive are a short drive away for shopping or people-watching, and the Getty Center offers one of the city’s best cultural hotspots. Santa Monica isn’t too far, either (traffic permitting). And after a day navigating crowds, returning to the hotel feels kind of like slipping back behind a velvet rope.
The service
The service strikes an ideal balance between polished and personal, all without being overly stuffy. Staff greet you by name, and requests are handled quickly and efficiently.
Accessibility
The hotel is fully ADA compliant, with specific guest rooms that feature lowered signage and door viewers, closed-caption televisions, braille room numbers, and emergency assistance systems. Bathrooms are designed for wheelchair access and include grab bars and roll-in showers. Public areas throughout the property are wheelchair accessible, with wide corridors, ramps, designated parking spaces, and elevators conveniently located near accessible rooms.
Anything left to mention?
The food and beverage prices are certainly eye-watering (bread service at dinner is $25, for instance), but the quality and atmosphere of the venue make it feel justified. Also, don’t skip out on going for a swim in the pool that’s framed by towering palms; it’s one of the most iconic in Los Angeles.
