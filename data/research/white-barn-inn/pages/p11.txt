White Barn Inn, Auberge Collection
It's all about enchantment, discretion, and relaxation at this historic Kennebunk establishment known for its exclusive clientele.

Reviewed by

Best for:
Travelers looking for something upscale but unfussy; anyone who appreciates a hotel where every tiny detail has been thought through
Location:
Tucked away from the touristy bustle of downtown Kennebunkport, but close enough to stroll into town; an easy jumping-off point for exploring Maine’s southern coast
Can’t-miss feature:
Year-round, the White Barn Inn Restaurant is a nonnegotiable reservation; in the summer, make sure to carve out time for a pool day
In a nutshell
: New England charm and hospitality can be found in every corner of this intentionally tousled haven. The overall vibe, from the thoughtful hospitality to the relaxed interiors, is New England's version of barefoot luxury—Barbour-clad luxury, if you will.
Rooms
There are 31 accommodations that make up the White Barn Inn: rooms in the main inn, suites, cottages, and four seasonal houseboats (yes, houseboats!). The decor is undeniably New England coastal without ever veering into a nautical cliché: varied plaids, soft seafoam greens, and just-the-right-amount of tousled textiles—from the extra quilts to the Roman shades—that make everything feel perfectly lived in.
The houseboats (Cora, Nettie, Grace, and Ivory, named for the White Barn Inn’s original founders and their children) are the obvious choice if you want something memorable—floating on the Kennebunk Marina, they imperceptibly rise and fall with the changing tide, making one feel intimately part of the coastal landscape. From the rooftop of my houseboat, Nettie, I could wave at boats passing by as I sipped my morning coffee.
Of note, houseboats are not recommended for those traveling with children between the ages of 1-12, for safety reasons, and are seasonal, so plan accordingly.
Food and drink
White Barn Inn’s restaurant is legendary in these parts, and a tough-to-get reservation—for good reason. The setting is a destination unto itself: a rustic barn, dimly lit, filled with a cozy, constant din from both visitors and locals alike accompanied by a live piano. My summer visit certainly held its own magic, but I found myself dreaming about how special a meal must be in this refurbished barn during the fall and winter.
There are two prix fixe menus with optional wine pairings (one made up of four courses, the other six, at a slightly higher price point). The menu leans heavily into Maine’s seafood and seasonal produce. During my summer visit, the day-boat scallops and Jonah crab with sweet corn were standouts. For other meals at the hotel, Little Barn is a more relaxed option, serving breakfast, lunch, and dinner and available for in-room dining.
Amenities and experiences
The pool is a can’t-miss if you’re visiting in the summer. Nestled within the back garden of the inn, surrounded by trees and mossy stones, it looks as if it grew there long ago, and I often find my mind wandering back to the tranquil morning I spent poolside with my book and a delicious, fresh fruit chia pudding.
The Saltwood spa is smaller and treatment-focused rather than a place you’d linger for an entire spa day, but my prenatal massage was lovely—and the thoughtful post-treatment debrief from my therapist, Jalene, was one of those unexpectedly memorable touches.
Guests can borrow bikes (the tiny Maine license plates are a reminder that Auberge truly thinks through every detail) or reserve a car from White Barn Inn’s small fleet of five Mercedes-Benz vehicles for four-hour blocks on a first-come, first-served basis. There’s even a “Barbour Borrowing Closet” equipped with jackets, wellies, and umbrellas available to borrow during your stay, ideal for chilly Maine evenings and any coastal excursions you find yourself participating in.
What to do nearby
You could happily spend your entire stay cocooned within the cozy refuge of the White Barn Inn (I had a hard time dragging myself from the pool), but this is Maine after all, so you’d be remiss not to take advantage of the abundance the area has to offer.
The hotel can arrange a plethora of New England–centric outings, from lobster boat excursions and
oyster shucking classes
to private beach picnics, surf lessons, and cycling tours along the coast, while the towns of Kennebunkport and Kennebunk are right at your fingertips for independent wandering, shopping, and dining. Portland is also within easy reach for a day trip.
Personally, I’d recommend taking advantage of the Mercedes-Benz partnership, and take a joyride around the rugged coastline in a convertible, stopping at as many out-of-town lobster pounds as possible. My personal tip? Skip the Clam Shack and instead, get the lobster roll and clam chowder from the very lowkey Langsford Road Lobster & Fish House.
The backstory
White Barn Inn has been a Maine landmark for more than 150 years, evolving from a saltwater farm into a Civil War–era boarding house, ice creamery and, eventually, one of New England’s best-known country inns. The restaurant opened in the historic barn in 1973 and quickly established the property as a culinary destination. The property joined Auberge Resorts Collection in 2018, and a subsequent redesign refreshed the rooms and public spaces while deliberately holding on to the inn’s old New England charms.
Insider tip
Don’t just book your room and dinner and call it a day. White Barn Inn (and Auberge overall) is unusually good at the little, highly specific extras: the Barbour borrowing closet, the house bikes with their miniature Maine license plates, the Mercedes-Benz cars and the rotating roster of coastal experiences. Ask the concierge what’s happening during your stay and choose one or two activities to build into your itinerary. The hotel’s experiential programming changes seasonally, so there’s often something you wouldn’t know to look for in advance.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes—but I’d consider the property more naturally suited to couples and adults. The houseboats are specifically not recommended for children ages 1–12.
