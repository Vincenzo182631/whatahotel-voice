Splendido, A Belmond Hotel, Portofino
A princely estate whose understated glamour shares the spotlight with Portofino’s natural beauty.

Reviewed by
Matt Ortile
Why book?
This is the crown jewel of Portofino, iconic and eternal. If you want to do your trip right, you check into this stately albergo presiding over the hillside and the bay below. (Or the Splendido Mare, its sister property situated in the town’s piazzetta.)
Set the scene
On a warm afternoon in late June, I gasped upon glimpsing the bright pink azaleas blooming on every balcony and terrace embedded into the marigold facade of the Hotel Splendido (
SPLEN
-di-doh, per favore), that iconic and princely estate set high on a hill overlooking the viridian waters of Portofino. It was a sight so ravishingly beautiful it relieved any anxiety I had about witnessing my driver squabble in rapid Italian with other chauffeurs on the winding road leading to the resort. The capable shuttles, modern in every way, were too big to pass each other on these streets that have barely changed since the 1940s. They were skinny, tree-lined, and meant exclusively for, I assumed, nimble Vespas and chic coupes bearing Humphrey Bogart or Elizabeth Taylor to paradise.
After many pivots and turns, we reached Splendido at last, where the staff efficiently worked through check-in processes for me and the group of travelers I accompanied. We had just arrived at the train station in nearby Santa Margherita Ligure, where we had disembarked from our chariot, the Venice Simplon-Orient-Express. My group resembled the guests who were already checked-in: Gucci-clad couples from every generation who have saved up (if not cash, then time-off) to celebrate honeymoons, anniversaries, and milestone birthdays. Interspersed among them were the visitors staying at the piazzetta’s alberghi in town, here for a spritz or Negroni; or the Instagrammers with Airbnbs in nearby Parragi, taking selfies at the outdoor restaurant, La Terraza—their ultimate goal after hiking to the summit of this veritable Olympus.
The backstory
The building in which Splendido makes its fabulous home was first built in the 16th century as a Benedictine monastery. When it found new life as a hotel in 1901, it started to draw Hollywood types like Elizabeth Taylor and Ava Gardner. It eventually came under the stewardship of Belmond in 1985, best known for their luxe collection of historic hotels, trains, and boats. Common among the properties are a spirit of individuality (no two are the same) and a distinct sense of place. Indeed, Splendido puts the spotlight on its environment, as a majority of its balconied guest rooms face the green-blue waters of Portofino Bay.
This slice of the Italian Riviera is essentially the interior’s decor—large glass French doors beautifully let the outdoors into rooms—but the hotel itself was made even more gorgeous, thanks to recent renovations in the pool area, one of the restaurants, and the top-of-the-top Baronessa Suite, completed for the 2023 summer season. (Splendido’s season lasts from late May to early October.)
For my visit, in June 2024, as part of Belmond’s
When Legends Meet: From Paris to Portofino journey
on the
Venice Simplon-Orient-Express
, 12 rooms had completed a renovation and redesign by the designer Martin Brudnizki. The rest of the work was completed in 2025, for the hotel's 40th anniversary. The key count has decreased, but all guest rooms have been expanded into junior suites, standard suites, or lavish signature named suites, i.e. La Baronnessa, La Dolce Vita, et al.
The rooms
In Splendido’s newly renovated guest suites, Brudnizki’s signature texture-on-pattern-on-print maximalism takes on a softer, subtler play—more perfume than syrup. The color palette seems to pull from Monet’s
Water Lilies
: lavenders, celadons, and Alice blues. Brudnizki worked with the Italian furnishing house
Medea
to create beds, chintz chairs, chandeliers, and more to weave a milieu that’s luxurious in its coziness and lived-in vibe. And whatever’s not three-dimensional is painted on: Instead of crown molding, painted floral patterns line the walls and sprout from light sconces. This sensibility is wonderfully demure (to use the
mot du jour
) and understated, letting the true star—the sweeping views of Portofino Bay, glimpsed through the doors to the balconies—really shine.
The room I stayed in, Room 479, had yet to receive its Brudnizkification, but much like the revamped rooms, its soft white walls and soft white sheets and soft white furniture with splashes of Carrara marble defer to the view of the Bay. (I cannot emphasize enough how much of glorious nature seeps into the rooms at Splendido; songbirds and sunrise were my alarm clocks for the duration of my stay.) Still, the amenities and tech were present, unobtrusively so: The well-stocked minibar had a Nespresso machine, and bedside outlets came with ports for USB-C cords. In the bathroom were Acqua di Parma toiletries and a shower tiled with black stone; the closet had flip-flops and a tote for pool days; and the balcony had a small table and two chairs. Speaking of, nearly all of the rooms have a balcony and some view of the sea; the rest may have a view of the flourishing gardens or Splendido’s main building.
Food and drink
Most meals at Splendido revolve around La Terrazza, the open-air terrace with grand views of the harbor. Breakfast, included in the room rates, offers a bountiful help-yourself buffet, with pasticcini, juices, and fruit to spare; warm dishes like eggs and crêpes are made-to-order. Lunchtime is dominated by Ligurian classics (i.e. seafood and plant-based plates) and the signature dishes of Lombardian chef Corrardo Corti, who has been at Splendido since 1999. For dinner, “proposal food” like lamb racks and grilled swordfish are the stars.
La Terrazza is an all-day spot, and though the kitchen closes at a reasonable hour, guests like to linger after last call. I once returned from Portofino town after dark to see—hear, at first—folks on the terrace belting out Elton John standards with an affable pianist. This spot is the heart and soul of Splendido, but worry not if the weather does not cooperate; the restaurant is covered and there’s an indoor space for dining inside the main building, next to the morning-to-night lobby bar for easy access.
There’s also the more casual poolside venue, Splendido Grill, which serves lunch and dinner. This too has also recently been redesigned, with a palette of greens and corals, as well as a sunroof that can be retracted on beautiful days. The main attraction though is a signature dish of the hotel: the trofie al pesto, Liguria’s most famous import—though here it’s served in the
avvantaggiate
style, a heartier spin with potatoes and green beans. The Grill also serves snacks and drinks to poolgoers; the zero-kilometer cocktail list is particularly fun, highlighting ingredients local to the region. My favorite was La Vela, made with Portofino dry gin, pine nut vermouth, Amaro Camatti, and Campari.
The spa
During my stay, Splendido had a partnership with Dior to create a pop-up spa experience dubbed Jardin des Rêves Dior, featuring two spa cabanas situated in the gardens. Treatments included facials and massages, including a face-and-body package christened “Splendidior,” naturally.
As far as fitness goes: Guests have complimentary access to the property’s iconic tennis court (reservations required), and can train with a tennis pro for an extra fee. The 24-hour gym is small, well-equipped, and—yet again—offers a view of the bay. Though you likely won’t need to spend much time there since the hike to Portofino’s piazzetta and docks offers a good (and steep) walking workout. (There is a shuttle bus that goes back and forth between Splendido and Splendido Mare.)
But the star of the show is the heated saltwater pool that, depending on where you sit, stand, or float, seems to spill over into the bay. The whole pool area faces the sea, with plenty of huge umbrellas and daybeds (some more secluded in the gardens for a peaceful nap). As part of the hotel redesign, the pool area is now a zesty terracotta and the pool tiles are a gorgeous viridian that mimics the sea below. The pool’s maximum depth is 7.2 feet, and it was long enough where I could practice laps once a set of parents retrieved their frolicking kids.
The neighborhood/area
Portofino began as a fishing village fronting the bay, this little crook of Ligurian coastline. Now, it’s now a resort town with an official full-time population of 379 people who are drastically outnumbered by tourists in the high summer seasons. Portofino’s central piazzetta and docks are a 15-minute walk or 5-minute shuttle ride away from Splendido; the shuttle drops off passengers at Splendido Mare, the sister property in the heart of the action. If you need a spot of shopping, the likes of Bottega Veneta, Louis Vuitton, and Longchamp are just off the piazzetta; there are restaurants and gelaterias and bars aplenty.
Even if you’re escaping to Splendido for a quiet retreat, to be divorced from the bustle of that picturesque piazzetta, it can still be fun to drop into town for a spot of people watching over a spritz or a game of backgammon with a Portofinese nonno. The hotel does have a nice menu of excursions that includes kayaking in the bay, tours to nearby Cinque Terre, and a visit to local eco-farm
La Portofinese
where lunches and wine tastings are available.
Fun fact: The hotel is smack in the middle of Parco Regionale di Portofino, a protected region stretching from Camogli to Rapallo, established in the 1930s. This means that the natural landscape is largely unchanged from the 20th century, and so the views from bedrooms at Splendido are the same views Elizabeth Taylor saw when she made her repeat visits to this exquisite Ligurian hideaway.
The service
Consistent and friendly seven-star style. At dinner, after getting up to take a photo of the moonlit bay, I returned to my seat to find my napkin folded on the table. When I was checking out, I was proactively offered a to-go box so I could bring with me the breakfast I couldn’t finish before the chauffeur brought me to nearby Santa Margherita Ligure for my train back to Milan. Splendido is well staffed by charismatic good-time ragazzi, all very professional and conversant in multiple languages.
Notably, I was struck by their beaming pride—in the work they do, the property, being part of the Belmond universe. One barman, when asked what he’d like to do when the resort closes for the season, said he’d like to try working at one of the other Belmond hotels, maybe the one in Santa Barbara because he’d heard the place—and California as a whole—in winter is beautiful. If you’re ever at El Encanto, A Belmond Hotel, Santa Barbara, keep an eye out for him and ask him to make you the Ava Gardner cocktail from Splendido.
For families
There’s a kid’s club and babysitting service upon request. For larger groups, ask the hotel team for connecting rooms, and for anything else you might need, from board games to bottle warmers. Extra beds and cots can be brought into all rooms.
Accessibility
In the main building, there are elevators to grant guests access to all areas of the hotel, though some sections of the garden require taking stairs. The shuttle to the piazzetta helps with sight seeing; book one in advance. Given the steep terrain—the hotel is high on a hill—it’s best to consult with the Splendido team to arrange a stay that’s accessible as possible.
Anything left to mention?
If you are still reading, please accept my thanks for bearing with me and my total enamoration with Splendido. Reading this all back, I am afraid I speak of it like a handsome lover I fatefully met one weekend in Portofino. Well, stay a few nights here and I’d venture you’d feel much the same way. But let me be first to admit: Not one of us can have Splendido all to ourselves. It belongs to you and me and anyone else lucky enough step out onto one of those azalea’ed balconies, past and present—and universe-willing, a very long future.
