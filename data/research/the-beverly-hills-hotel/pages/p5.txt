General information

Dress code

At the Polo Lounge, we encourage you to dress for the occasion, so ask that you refrain from wearing baseball caps, beanies, ripped or shredded denim, crop tops, nightwear, swimwear, men’s sleeveless shirts, or any clothing showing offensive gestures or messages. Sweatshirts and hoodies are permitted, but not with the hood pulled up. After 4pm, shorts are not permitted for men; ladies may wear tailored shorts (excluding denim). We do not permit flip-flops (including Birkenstocks) or sportswear (including tracksuits). This dress code does not apply to children under 12.

THE CONCEPT

More than just celebrity glamour

American cuisine with an Italian edge and a specials board worthy of intense daily investigation. You might come for the stories but you’ll come again for the food.

THE VIEW

Polo patio

With its spectacular array of palm trees and flowers, the patio is one of Los Angeles’ premier and prettiest outdoor dining settings. Equally wonderful for a sunny breakfast or a twinkling night of soft music and familiar faces.

How the celebrities do it

And where to sit?

You’ll have to ask Polo Lounge director Pepe De Anda to show you how the celebrities do it. There are power booths, there are seats that let you watch the doorway, there are alcoves for intimate dates and not being seen. Pepe knows them all.

The Music

A sophisticated soundtrack

The Polo Lounge speaks to a bygone era of Hollywood glamour, a place to entertain and be entertained. Live music flows almost all day long, and a piano player serenades diners at lunch and dinner. On Sundays, brunch on the patio is accompanied by light jazz.

> The Polo Lounge has a magical atmosphere, a special energy. This place is unique.

Pepe de Anda

Director of Polo Lounge
