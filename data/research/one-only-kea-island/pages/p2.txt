One&Only Kéa Island, Greece: First In
One&Only brings its signature flair for adventure to the little-known Cycladic island of Kea's chic lagoon setting.

Why book One&Only Kéa Island?
For an unrivaled location in terms of seclusion and tranquility. One&Only’s 14th resort sits on its own naturally-formed peninsula with panoramic views across the Aegean and to mainland
Athens
beyond (you can just about see Cape Sounion, the iconic mount with the Temple to Poseidon). There isn’t another dwelling for miles around, just the island's beautiful rolling, untouched hillside with undiscovered ancient ruins lying in wait. The sheltered cove, which the peninsula protects, complete with a tree-shaded beach club, expansive spa, multiple restaurants, yacht trips, scuba diving, and kids club, all make for a thoroughly polished island getaway, but it's crowd-free sunset views from private terraces (with private pools) is the real jewel in the crown for any holiday-maker.
The backstory
The history of Kea is not that of a typical Greek island. The first thing you might notice is that you had to Google where it was. The second is how startlingly close to Athens it is. Even the local Athenians have trouble locating it on a map, not helped by the fact that it's also known as Tzia. This is largely due to the third point of difference; unusually for a Greek island, it doesn’t have a harbor town, so it has never been a part of the ferry-route tourism track. In ancient times, approximately 400 BC, Kea was a thriving point of trade, being one of the largest of the Cyclades and the closest inhabited island to Athens. There were two temples (one to Apollo and a second to Athena) as well as an amphitheater above the Paralia Mikres Poles Bay (coincidentally, one of the most beautiful untouched beaches, only accessible by boat or ancient hiking trail), and Kea had its own currency, denoting a strong economy. However, in the 7th century, this all vanished; the temples were abandoned and later looted, and Kea never again revived its trade or harbor settlements. Instead, the island became self-sufficient, with the majority of residents managing their own farmland, with the island still bearing its patchwork quilt of stone walls and boundary lines.
The island is so undeveloped that One&Only had to fully excavate their land in collaboration with the Ministry of Culture before work could commence; archaeologist Petroula Tatsopoulou oversaw the first-ever excavation of the Vroskopos beach valley, discovering ancient settlements in the area, as well as other villages with Copper Age settlements and clay figures that predate Greece’s Olympian mythology. As part of the hotel’s commitment to preserving and celebrating the island's heritage, it will offer excursions to local excavation sites, as well as the two temples and amphitheater. Being mindful of its construction—the resort is five years in the making—each and every stone guests see is the stone found on the peninsula, excavated and repurposed to keep the area's natural materials intact.
Set the scene
Imagined by veteran resort architect John Heah (the mind behind Aman Resorts, Four Seasons, and the Maybourne hotel group), this is high-end minimalism at its best, with raw natural materials and techniques traditionally used on the island. The majority of the large structures use a type of concrete that mottles as it dries, which mimics the wind-torn texture of the Doric columns in the Temple to Athena ruins on the other side of the island. There is also a focus on air and light, with the main lobby and restaurant having vast open walls, open-air ceilings, indoor gardens, and neat pools of water, all of which allow the Mediterranean breeze to circulate through. In fact, there is an emphasis on the “Four Elements” throughout, a reference to Greek Philosopher Empedocles’ four divinites: earth (Hera), air (Zeus), fire (Hades), and water (Persephone).
The hotel
For such a seemingly contained resort, there's a lot of ground to cover. At the foot of the valley is the tree-shaded beach club, as well as the expansive spa and the meeting point for scuba diving and other sailing excursions (this summer, it has a gorgeous wooden 1967 yacht, ready to be commandeered for day trips or cocktails at sundown). Tucked within the valley is an area for both the kids club (Kids Only) and the sports center (Club One), the combination of which makes for one seriously impressive all-serving family zone; aside from the children's play rooms (which are vast, and have soft play areas as well as pretty wooden toys by Kid’s Concept) there is a tennis court, half basketball court, games consoles, and bikes for hire. The resort’s layout around the steep hill of the peninsula and slope into the valley means that each area is tucked away and separate from the rest. The landscape was inspired by the inland town of Ioulida, a hilltop village with cascading residential dwellings and winding paths (no car-worthy roads), and a church perched at the precipice. One&Only followed this with its villas winding up from the jetty to the central building at the peak, which houses the reception, boutique, and five distinctive dining options.
The rooms
Each room is its own private villa suite, self-contained and set into the hillside inside its own high-ceilinged box-like free-standing structure, with private courtyard entrances and individual 26-foot infinity pools. Each is decorated with original art from emerging Greek artists, something which is seen throughout the resort. There are 63 villas (most with sea views, some with beach views, with either one or two bedrooms) and currently 14 private homes. Rooms come with vaulted ceilings and floor-to-ceiling windows, plus all the considered touches you would expect from the group—super-soft slippers and robes, hair dryers, nail kits, plush eye masks, sun hats, a cocktail station with fresh fruit for garnishes, even a yoga mat and lavender sleep spray for pillows.
Food and drink
Food across the resort’s various restaurants is Greek and local but with a considered twist. Dimitris Katsanos is the executive chef, cooking up classics such as grilled octopus or sfakiani pita at the main restaurant Atria, but expect a handful of fusion additions: spanakopita gyoza or grilled broccolini served with seared manouri cheese with hazelnuts. The star of the menu is the seafood, freshly caught and grilled with Aegean lemon and a choice of glaze and sides. There are also daily specials, usually Greek family favorites, eggplant stuffed with minced beef, or orzo with tomato and chorizo, as well as sharing platters (including the best Greek salad imaginable)—particularly great for families. The wine list is seriously notable, led by sommelier and Kea resident Georgia Marouli (a note on hiring: the hotel had aimed to employ 10% Kea islanders in its 300-strong staff, the island in total only has a population of around 3,000), but easily surpassed this.
The hotel has also invited locals to its opening, and anyone is welcome to visit the beach club as per Greek laws. Marouli has sourced over 200 labels, and around 85% are Greek, including many small-batch wines from little-known vineyards. Marouli also leads the wine tastings in the dining room Epicora. There are several other dining options, including a poolside grill and cocktail terrace, but the jewel in its offering is Bond Beach Club, a gorgeously untouched restaurant with its feet in the sand, shaded by ancient trees, making for a remote and off-road setting. One final thing to note is the honey; the hotel works with third-generation beekeeper Georges Thodos and his wife, Geraldine, who produce Kea’s signature wild thyme honey—try it with the feta pita or with the seared beef carpaccio.
The spa
One&Only is no stranger to championing pioneering spa brands, having previously partnered with Clinique La Prairie for One Za'abeel, Dubai, and Henri Chenot for Portonovi. For Kea, the group worked with Subtle Energies, a wellness brand founded by Ayurveda Practitioner and Clinical Aromatherapist Farida Irani, offering a more holistic approach to health and wellness than simple spa treatments. The partnership has been largely overseen by Irani’s son and Director of Operations, Nick Irani, who has created a bespoke menu of treatments, as well as more thorough wellness programs which include the resort's sports facilities and classes. The treatments tap into Greece’s wellness heritage, from an overriding influence of element-based health and balance to the utilisation of local ingredients, such as a specific Greek rose for the exclusive Aphrodite’s Rose facial. The spa itself is worthy of a visit, with spaces over three floors with an indoor-outdoor pool, spacious treatment rooms, outdoor relaxation areas, and steam and sauna rooms.
How to get there
Guests take a private speedboat directly to the resort (30 minutes), a helicopter from the airport to its helipad (15 minutes)—not currently offered by the hotel—or a private boat (80 minutes) from
One&Only Aesthesis
, the sister resort that opened last November in Athens.
Anything else to know?
This is a scuba diver’s paradise; Kea is surrounded by several shipwrecks, including that of the Britannic, the sister ship to the Titanic, and the paddlewheel steamboat Patris. Many of these wrecks have been undiscovered for decades, with commercial access only being granted recently. Local diver Yannis Tzavelakos has led the way in Kea scuba exploration and now runs the resort’s dive center.
