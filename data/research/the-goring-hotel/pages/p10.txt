Why book?
For a delightfully fun taste of London at its most posh.
Set the scene
This stately hotel sits around the corner from Buckingham Palace, with doormen dressed to the nines waiting to collect your bags. It would be easy to feel intimidated, if not for the warmth of the staff at every step. While this was once the landing pad of members of parliament and those on royal business, the demographics have evolved over the years, with more tourists and business travelers from America, Australia, and Asia passing through. Still, fellow guests tend to be well-dressed—the surroundings invite it—save for the spandex-outfitted ones stepping out for a run.
Enjoy a touch of whimsy here influenced by the raucousness of the Edwardian era, when the hotel first came to be; the hotel pet, a shetland pony named Teddy, brings a bit of the countryside to London, and if you look closely at the main level’s wallpaper, you'll spot a tux-wearing pig, monkeys sipping on Champagne, and more cheeky designs.

The backstory
Opened in 1910 by OR Goring, who came from humble beginnings in Dresden, The Goring was a feat from the start as the world’s first hotel to have central heating and en suite bathrooms in every room. Now it has over a century-long legacy to look back on—and impressively, is still run by the same family today. Given its location as one of the closest hotels to Buckingham Palace, it’s no wonder the hotel is steeped in history: It has hosted every serving prime minister since it opened; Winston Churchill held meetings here during the Second World War; and Kate Middleton spent the night before her 2011 wedding to Prince William. It’s also the only hotel to have been granted a
royal warrant
by the late Queen Elizabeth II.
The rooms
Classic English at its best—silken patterned walls, antique dressing tables, dramatic drapes, antiques over the bed. I stayed in a junior suite with an elegantly decorated living room (think velvet furniture, pastel pink armchairs I wish I could teleport home, and a decorative fireplace), cozy bedroom, and marble bathroom complete with both a tub and a separate shower. Small delights can be found in the lighting panels (settings include bright, calm, cosy, and oooh!), evidence that this isn’t a place that takes itself too seriously, and the flat-screen TV at one end of the bath. I also appreciated the classical music humming on the living room radio upon arrival, and master off switches by the door and right next to the bed, so you don’t have to guess and check a half dozen switches before getting to sleep. There are several room and suite options available (all either garden view or street view, some with balconies), but for royalists really seeking an opportunity to splash out, there’s no better option than The Royal Suite—decor spans from a life-size portrait of Queen Victoria to letters handwritten by royalty, as well as the same silk that can be found in Buckingham Palace’s Throne Room.

Food and drink
The one-Michelin-starred Dining Room is open to guests and visitors for breakfast, lunch, and dinner, with the goal of using top-quality, seasonal British ingredients to create dishes that stick in diners' heads. That means taking a swing at new dishes and serving up twists on timeless classics. Come for dinner and you’ll get an elevated experience from the amuse-bouche to the goodnight treat. Among the most popular dishes: the signature eggs Drumkilbo, a favorite of the Queen Mother that has been iterated on over the years, Chef Squire says (the current seafood salad recipe mixes Cornish crab, aged caviar, and native lobster). Freshly renovated in 2024 (its first major update in more than 30 years), the kitchen now has its own herb garden installed so the team can produce their own herbs and microgreens, as well as a dry ager for meats—head downstairs for a tour, or just to take a look at the chefs at work.
The neighborhood/area
This is a supremely central location, situated on a charming street in Belgravia, a stone’s throw from Buckingham Palace, and within walking distance of St. James Park, Chelsea, Westminster, and Mayfair. The Royal Albert Hall is a 10-minute cab ride away, and Victoria Station, a five-minute walk away, connects you to the rest of London.
The service
The aim here is to make guests feel as much like a royal as the hotel's neighbors, and they succeed at every turn. With just 69 rooms, the staff can really get a sense of who is here on any given day, and it helps that several members of the team have been at the property for decades. Footmen handle your luggage at check-in and checkout; the concierge team is ready to make miracles happen, whether it’s scoring a hard-to-get reservation or last-minute tickets; and guests staying in suites are treated to a complimentary in-room happy hour. Those staying in a Belgravia Suite, Royal Suite, or Garden Suite have their own dedicated footman to fulfill their each and every wish.
A sweet plush sheep (Barbara, a Goring legend!) greeted me on arrival (to match the larger one in the living room), and I came back to bedside amenities like a lip balm and pillow mist each night. The kitchen staff is equally inviting; head downstairs to see them at work and hear about the latest ingredients being used by executive chef Graham Squire (previously of Michelin-starred Trinity and The Lickford Inn in West Sussex) and his team.
For families
While at first glance this posh residence might not seem welcoming to children, The Goring in fact pulls out all the stops for little ones—they’ll receive The Goring Passport to keep a record of their trip, a kid-friendly welcome drink upon arrival, and access to a bedtime story library; kiddos are also invited to come down to the kitchen for a bit of dessert decorating. A dress-up box can be brought to the room when creativity calls. For parents traveling with babies, cots, bottle warmers, changing mats, and more helpful tools are available, as are babysitting services. Interconnecting rooms that sleep two adults and up to three kids are available. Visit Big John, the head concierge, for tips on family-friendly activities around London.
Accessibility
The hotel has two handicap accessible rooms, and elevator or ramp access to all levels.
Anything left to mention?
The massive outdoor garden is a real boon—I visited in February, but can imagine it coming to life in the warmer months as a spot to take a break from touristing and relax with a good book, glass of Champagne, or cup of coffee. In the summer, guests can play a round of croquet here.
