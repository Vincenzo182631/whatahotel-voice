Enchantment Resort
Located on the ancestral land of the Yavapai Tribe and in the Boynton Canyon vortex, Enchantment Resort draws in numerous visitors looking for energy and healing.

Why book?
One of the original high-end Sedona hotels, Enchantment Resort continues to be one of the area’s best year after year. For decades, Enchantment has honored, preserved, and shared one of the most beautiful areas of Arizona while working to ensure that the resort is worthy of its location. You could book for true seclusion, the shared award-winning spa at Mii Amo, or the numerous outdoor activities—but really, you’re coming for the views. And if that’s the only reason, it’s enough.
Set the scene
Driving into Sedona is already impressive; one minute you’re barreling down the highway, and then suddenly, soaring red rock formations surround you. But driving to Enchantment Resort, following the road down into Boynton Canyon, getting out of the car, and being surrounded by red walls? You can’t help but feel this is a sacred place—one that has been sacred for generations. It’s the ancestral land of the Yavapai Tribe, and the Boynton Canyon vortex has drawn in numerous visitors looking for energy and healing. The impact of that realization, combined with the serenity and natural beauty of the canyon, make an unforgettable first impression.
The backstory
Enchantment’s unique location in Boynton Canyon meant that the creation of the property involved several layers of authorization. Owner John Gardiner’s original goal was to open a tennis ranch; he, the architects, and designers (including a young Mark Candelaria from the award-winning Candelaria Design) had to receive permission from the Yavapai Tribe, National Forest, and Sierra Club in order to build. The resort originally opened in 1987; in 2018, the Trail House was added to the property.
The rooms
The resort houses 218 casita guest rooms, which are set in adobe, pueblo-style buildings. They run from fairly standard designs to several different suite levels. Rooms are furnished comfortably in the expected Southwestern decor, but avoid overdoing it thanks to restrained, contemporary hands. Most come with a corner fireplace and all have decks or patios.
Food and drink
There are two restaurants on property: Che Ah Chi and Tii Gavo; both serve dinner, while Che Ah Chi also offers breakfast and lunch. Tii Gavo, which means “gathering place,” is more casual; if the weather is good, dining outside or around the firepit is the way to go. The food is Southwestern-inspired, but you can still enjoy steak frites or grilled branzino. At Che Ah Chi, which translates to “red rocks,” Arizona ingredients are incorporated into an elevated menu. Both restaurants are open to the public, and Enchantment guests are also allowed to dine at the guest-exclusive Hummingbird at Mii Amo. Che Ah Chi’s mushroom curry is particularly flavorful, but you can’t go wrong with the Arizona Black Angus beef tenderloin either.
The spa
Enchantment doesn’t have its own spa, but fear not, because merely steps away at the
Mii Amo resort
(also owned by the Enchantment Group) is an award-winning spa that Enchantment guests can take advantage of. What better place to have a spa in Sedona than here, within the Boynton Canyon vortex? The Mii Amo spa menu has something for everyone, whether you’re interested in a simple Swedish massage or one of their signature treatments that speak to Sedona’s uniqueness. Beyond that, the spa offers meditations, ceremonies, energy clearing, and more.
The neighborhood/area
Since the resort sits at the bottom of Boynton Canyon, there isn’t a walkable neighborhood nearby—and that’s more than OK. Far from the crowds, this is the place you come to for true peace and respite, to soak up the quiet while sitting outside at night or sipping coffee with the sunrise.
The service
The staff does an excellent job of balancing the refinement of a luxurious resort with a sense of casualness. Enchantment is a thoroughly delightful getaway, but it’s particularly great for outdoor activities. Trail House is the epicenter for all things outdoors, and it's staffed with expert guides who can set you up on hikes, provide rental equipment, or lead you on an excursion. The Artist Cottage is not to be skipped either, where creative activities like watercolors and pottery come paired with a glass of wine.
For families
Unlike Mii Amo, where guests need to be at least 16 years old, Enchantment welcomes families with children of all ages—and has programs designed to keep them entertained. Kids can attend Camp Coyote, where trained counselors will lead attendees in activities like nature walks and painting, or take private mountain biking sessions. With a pool in the summer and endless outdoor activities all year round, it’s a good place for families to connect.
Eco effort
Enchantment takes its role in Boynton Canyon seriously, and the Boynton Canyon Preservation Fund was set up decades ago by the resort to help care for the land it sits on (part of Trail House tour fees go toward the fund). In addition to water conservation efforts, there’s a reusable water bottle provided at check-in that is part of the Join the Pipe organization. The bottle is made out of sugarcane and there are refill stations around the resort.
Accessibility
Enchantment offers rooms with several different accessible configurations, and the resort is wheelchair accessible.
Anything left to mention?
The property’s layout can make it a bit difficult to find your way around, especially at night. Staff are more than happy to provide battery-powered golf cart rides around the resort and even over to Mii Amo for reservations. Staying at Enchantment also means access to the private Seven Canyons golf course, so don’t forget your clubs.
