The Ritz-Carlton, Bali
A dreamy beachfront property built into a cliff overlooking the Indian Ocean.

Why book?
For a sublimely beautiful, peaceful stay in a quiet corner of Nusa Dua overlooking the Indian Ocean.
Set the scene
While other stretches of Bali's coast may be known for their party scene and packed beaches, Nusa Dua offers a more tranquil environment that feels a world away from the crowds. Upon entering the lobby—a massive, pitched-roof, open-air clubhouse of sorts—my eyes were immediately drawn to the view: The airy entryway looks out over the entire resort, with lush green trees obscuring the walkways and bungalows below, and the Indian Ocean off in the distance. It looks far away, but once you pop down the towering glass elevator, it’s only about a 10-minute walk to the water (golf carts flutter by with impressive frequency to help you get around, too). Also near the beach is the stunning oceanfront wedding chapel.
Around the resort, I saw a mix of families with young kids; older couples reconnecting; and funkily dressed couples posing for photos. The majority of visitors come from the US, Japan, China, Indonesia, and Australia. Throughout the property's winding walkways, I came across many triangle-shaped figurines, said to represent Bali's temples; each one invites a moment of pause and reflection, as do the ocean views and prancing monkeys scampering above your head from time to time.
The backstory
Opened in early 2015, the Ritz-Carlton Bali marked the brand's return to the island (another Ritz-Carlton Bali existed in Jimbaran from the late ’90s into the early aughts). Set on a massive swath of cliffside and beachfront land, it's a luxurious, family-friendly property that brings in Balinese culture by way of cuisine, nightly dance performances, and cultural activities on offer. For Marriott loyalists looking to explore more of the island, head over to the equally stunning
Mandapa Reserve
in Ubud, which opened in the same year.

The rooms
There are tons of different room types here—including 34 clifftop and ocean-view villas with 24-hour butler service—catering to various types of travelers. Among the most opulent is the Sky Villa, a two-bedroom abode with an infinity pool, outdoor tub, and killer beach views from its top-floor perch. I stayed in a signature suite—a bungalow-style lodging with a dreamy backyard—and I spent as much time as I could dipping into the private pool, lounging on the rattan wicker furniture, and making use of the outdoor shower and tub. (While the backyard is pretty secluded thanks to greenery and dividers, more reserved visitors will be happy to know there are slatted sliding doors to add a bit more privacy to your outdoor baths, as well as an indoor shower.)
Rooms are designed with lots of woven textures that bring warmth to a neutral color palette, and take every opportunity to invite the outdoor environment in. A soft fabric couch was ideal for a post-dinner lounge; light wood dressers contrasted with deep wood accents; and the pagoda ceiling over the bed made the space feel open and airy.
Food and drink
The first night of my visit lined up well with the Nusantara night buffet at Bejana (hosted every Saturday), a great way to sample a whole bunch of Indonesian dishes at once, including the famous spit-roasted suckling pig, presented whole in the dining venue, and all the sambal your heart could desire. Down by the beach, lunch at the Missoni Resort Club (part of a year-long collaboration with the fashion brand) meant an Italian-influenced menu and lots of seafood, including some massive and juicy grilled prawns. Come hungry to breakfast at Senses—stations are seemingly endless, with Western, Chinese, Japanese, Indian, and Indonesian cuisines represented, plus a rainbow-colored display of fresh fruit and juices.
The spa
Arrive early to your spa treatment, and don’t skip the hydrotherapy pool (open to all guests)—the secluded area has five jet experiences that cleared my mind and had me ready to slip into a robe and over to my treatment room. There, I enjoyed one of the best massages I've ever had—it was a mix of Balinese relaxation and deep tissue; I loved the incorporation of stretching and how gently the movements were to reposition me throughout, using sheets to guide my limbs. Treatments start with a traditional foot blessing and end with tea and cookies. With 10 single and 4 couples rooms, it’s never too busy. Before or after treatments, you can also visit the steam room, sauna, and indoor pool, or kick back in the relaxation room (privacy curtains included).
The neighborhood/area
The hotel is an easy 30 minute drive from the airport, and while you could easily stay on property the whole time and be perfectly content, it's a short drive from Jimbaran Bay (I recommend a stroll through the fish market and a walk on the nearby beach, studded with fishing boats coming in and out with fresh catch) and Uluwatu (known for its surfing). There are also several golf courses close by.
The service
As with any Ritz-Carlton property, service is impeccable; here, the brand's high standards mix in the warmth and spirituality of Balinese hospitality. Upon arrival, I was greeted with a welcome blessing and tea in the lobby. Golf carts are speedy to arrive at your room when you want to get around more quickly than you could on your feet, and staff are plentiful yet discreet. The sunscreen bar by the pool was a fun addition, and helpful if you forgot to pack SPF.
For an over-the-top, photo-ready spread, order a
floating breakfast
(available to guests staying in one of the villas or suites with pool access).
For families
This is a somewhat surprisingly family-filled property—there’s the Ritz Kids club, of course, housed in a dedicated building just for little ones (kids ages 4–12 can attend without parents), as well as family-friendly activities like exploring coral reefs, learning about sea turtle preservation (and if you time your visit just right, watching the sea turtles hatch), partaking in night safaris, and more. A Ritz Kids menu is available in all on-site dining venues, and nanny services can be booked through the concierge.
Eco effort
Following Marriott practices, you can opt for housekeeping every day or not; there are water stations to fill up reusable bottles and recycling bins in the rooms.
Accessibility
The property has accessible on-site parking, and valet parking for vehicles outfitted for drivers in wheelchairs, as well as rooms outfitted for various accessibility needs (more information available
here
). Elevators are available throughout, and there are accessible routes to public areas including the dining venues, pool, spa, business center, and gym.

Anything left to mention?
Through November 2025, guests will spot turquoise and emerald green Missoni stripes around the resort; the Missoni Resort Club brought with it an on-site boutique selling beachwear and accessories, including a few exclusive styles; a takeover of the beachfront restaurant, serving Italian-inspired dishes and spritzes in adorably on-theme goblets; and Missoni-splashed pool umbrellas, lounge chairs, and even golf carts.
If you get a chance, sign up for the Balinese soul purification ritual—during the beachside ceremony, a priest runs through prayers to the Balinese and ocean gods, among others, supplicating for your health and wellness and dousing you in holy water to purify and cleanse the body, mind, and soul, all while making offerings to the gods. More properly, these kinds of ceremonies are done at a temple in eastern Bali with spring water, but I was told you can also get holy water from the ocean; I found it to be quite a moving practice, regardless of your religion.
