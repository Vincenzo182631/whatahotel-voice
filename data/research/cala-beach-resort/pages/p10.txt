Sofitel Barú Calablanca Beach Resort
Colombia’s first luxury beachfront resort is a striking, modernist foil to the colonial-era charms that saturate Cartagena less than one hour away.

Reviewed by
Paul Jebara
Why book?
I’m not the only one who once held a misguided assumption that postcard-caliber beaches lie just outside the 18th-century walls of Cartagena’s historic center. For proper beach time, you really need to head to Isla Barú. It’s a popular spot for day trips, situated less than an hour outside of town. Until the Sofitel Barú Calablanca Beach Resort opened on the island’s north coast in late 2021, Colombia had a dearth of luxury beachfront resorts. But now, it has a superb one: the modern, scene-stealing sister of Cartagena’s grand dame, the Sofitel Legend Santa Clara Cartagena. Combining several days at each offers a resplendent one-two punch of urban thrills and beachfront bliss on
Colombia’s Caribbean coast
.
Set the scene
There are two ways to reach Isla Barú: by boat and by car. The property can arrange either, but the former is faster—and arguably more glamorous (the road is allegedly ridden with potholes). The boat departs from the Cartagena Nautical Club and cruises past the glitzy Bocagrande district, which could easily be mistaken for downtown
Miami
in a photo. My yacht transfer was shared with other guests: a middle-aged Colombian couple who kept to themselves on the top deck, and a rather rowdy multigenerational group from New York who hijacked the sound system with clubby beats. (I seldom ran into either party during my stay—a testament to the hotel’s expansive layout. But we’ll get into that later.)
Sofitel Barú Calablanca Beach Resort occupies its own cove, so it genuinely exudes private island vibes; I felt them as soon as I stepped onto the wooden pier and slipped into the electric golf cart that whisked me off to the swish lobby. The clientele was surprisingly varied, but skewed slightly toward couples rather than family groups; most came from the U.S. or South America. On my second day, I learned that a large film crew with a few big-name actors were posting up at the hotel, as they were shooting a new television series on the remote parts of the island. Favorable tax incentives are bringing a ton of film production to
Colombia
(Isla Barú in particular, which can stand in for any tropical locale), so I suspect the Sofitel will become a hot spot for high-profile guests. Not condoning stan culture here, but bronzing next to an award-winning celebrity at the pool was still pretty cool. The main building—it houses the lobby, two restaurants, multiple bars including a rooftop lounge, and the wellness center—is a striking piece of architecture with stacked volumes and massive walls of glass that recall a contemporary art museum.
The rooms
There isn’t a smidgen of Caribbean-inflected kitsch to be found here. The Sofitel’s 188 rooms and suites sit inside modernist freestanding blocks of concrete, stone, and native wood; each named after birds found on Barú (my ultra-spacious Opera Suite was on the fourth floor of La Garza—The Heron). But all rooms are pretty big and breezy, reflecting a neutral color palette and pared-back mood board that wouldn’t look out of place in Montauk, the Whitsundays, or a rural valley in Crete, with spare decorative notes like textured natural fibers and hand-stitched tapestries. And
all
rooms, regardless of category, boast private balconies or terraces (some with hot tubs) that peer out to the Caribbean blue without obstruction. There are six two-bedroom Family Suites, which are ideal for families or couples traveling together. Being lulled to sleep by the waves crashing outside my window was divine. The one minor snag? I’d hoped that there would be fresh-ground Colombian coffee atop the minibar to fuel my mornings, perhaps accompanied by a French press to hit that classic Sofitel touch à la française. But a Nespresso machine and pods filled with Colombian grains was a solid compromise and, admittedly, a lot more convenient.
Food and drink
Having just been spoiled by Cartagena’s high-octane culinary scene, the bar had been set pretty high for what to expect food-wise at the resort—especially since there aren’t any other restaurants outside the property’s proverbial gates. But boy, was I impressed. There are three restaurants and seven (!) bars at the Sofitel, each with its own distinct epicurean theme. Humo, a robata grill that fuses pan-Asian and pan-Latin American flavors with a lick of French finesse (it’s Sofitel, after all), was my favorite. The dishes bookending dinner were most memorable: the buttered mini bao bun starter (“fun buns,” as my table nicknamed them after our second order), and the chocolate bonsai tree dessert, which sprouted from a plot of candied “dirt” and came adorned with sponge cake “leaves.”
One floor down from the art-filled lobby is Calablanca, where a vast breakfast spread is served (I opted for the fried-to-order empanada de huevo and some addictive granadilla fruit, which is like a larger, less tart passion fruit). At lunch and dinner, fab seafood specialties are available. My Pavlovian response to the smell of barbecue also brought me to the open-air Bahía beach grill for a selection of parrilladas (charred meats) and stellar margaritas. There, shoes are optional. In both restaurants, polychromatic hand-woven light fixtures from artisan and designer Angela Ramos Garcés sway from the ceiling—a nod to Colombia’s fique basketry traditions. If there’s one place for sunset, it’s the rooftop Bar La Pérgola, one of the few spots on the resort where you’ll find guests “turning up” into the wee hours.
The spa
The sleek 3,000-square-foot wellness center is surprisingly compact for a beach resort of this size, but a SINO (Spa In Name Only) it is not. An efficient floor plan packs in the essentials for rejuvenation: small hammams and saunas in the locker rooms, four treatment cabins, and a sea-facing outdoor terrace with ergonomic chaises for post-treatment reflection. During my one-hour massage I received a “hands-on” introduction to 100 percent natural Éccora products, a women-owned Colombian cosmetics brand that harnesses the scientifically proven power of organic tropical botanicals. The line is used exclusively in the spa’s offered rituals, along with Ayurvedic oils and therapeutic aromas. Squeezing in a workout in the state-of-the-art gym—a poolside fishbowl nearly twice the size of the spa—was also a treat.
The neighborhood/area
There’s little reason to leave the property unless outdoor pursuits in the rainforest or at sea are on the agenda. But in the coming years, the island will change—a lot. Isla Barú is steaming toward becoming Colombia’s premier upmarket beach destination, comprising dozens of new vacation homes (the entire first phase of villas was sold within a few weeks), a luxury hotel down the coast from the Sofitel, and a glistening marina with restaurants, bars, and shopping. You’ll get a sense of the scope if you arrive at the hotel’s boat slip, where there’s a rather unsightly billboard with renderings of the mega-project. Now’s the time to enjoy the island while it’s still largely undeveloped, and the truly brilliant vistas gifted by the Sofitel remain unspoiled.
The service
Friendly and attentive, but never oppressively so. The knowledgeable concierge team can organize everything from coffee tastings led by reps from renowned finca San Alberto to “moonlight” beach dinners and tours of the National Aviary of Colombia nearby (this one I highly recommend, what with a fifth of the world’s bird species calling Colombia home). The team is eager to impress with surprise touches: One morning I woke up to a full breakfast platter floating in my hot tub. Most of the staff live in Cartagena, and can give great tips on what to see and do in the city if you’re spending time there after Barú.
For families
Lots of resorts generously tout themselves as catering to everyone, but Sofitel Barú Calablanca excels at appealing to all types of travelers—families included. The tiered layout of the sprawling resort encourages guests to find their own pockets to retreat to, which is key to ensuring comfort and privacy if that’s desired. Though occupancy was high, the public areas never felt crowded. With four out of the five outdoor pools designated as adults-only, families with kiddos were generally out of sight except for at the restaurants during meal times. Down by the beach you’ll find the Kid’s Villa (I repeat:
villa.
Not club.) for ages 5 to 12. If I were 20—er, fine, maybe 30—years younger, I’d be thrilled to expend my overabundance of toddler energy playing with the glut of toys and puzzles while Mom and Dad were off at their spa treatments. Complimentary nanny service is also offered.
Eco effort
As the first resort on the island, the Sofitel sets a solid precedent for how to operate responsibly in an incredibly biodiverse (and inevitably fragile) ecosystem. It’s the only property in the Colombian Caribbean that is LEED-certified, which gave me pre-arrival confidence in its eco-minded efforts. The buildings don’t necessarily blend into the environment, but raw and recycled local materials feature prominently in their minimalist construction. Water and energy consumption has been optimized to minimize waste. A few areas of the grounds were still under construction during my stay, and much of the landscaping was in its nascent stages, but nearly all the plants used are endemic to the island. There will also be a nursery that cultivates native species and plants from the region.
Accessibility
The hotel’s main quad is friendly to those with mobility issues, but the rest of the property will present some challenges. While there are multiple elevators in the main building and each guestroom block, I did a lot of walking and stair-climbing in between. From my beachfront room at the base of the resort, I timed just under 15 minutes to reach the lobby on my two feet by cutting through the grounds up a steep set of steps. Taking the road that loops around the property tacked on an extra minute or two (and a few extra sweat beads on my forehead). I’d probably take better advantage of the fleet of golf carts next time to get around.
Anything left to mention?
Ironically, the beach itself was just okay. The hotel keeps the sand immaculate, no doubt, but I encountered an unsettling amount of plastic debris while wading into the sea (I thought I was stepping on a swath of seagrass, but it was actually a cluster of plastic bags). I could have just been unlucky that day: it’s understandable that strong winter winds occasionally carry over detritus from Cartagena’s port. But it was off-putting enough to put the kibosh on future dips, unless they were in the pool or a few hundred yards off the shore jumping off a boat. Installing some sort of filtered barrier would probably help.
