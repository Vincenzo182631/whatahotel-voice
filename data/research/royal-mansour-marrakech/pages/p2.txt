Royal Mansour Marrakech
Founded by King Mohammed VI, this private, palatial resort took 1,200 master craftsmen over three years to construct.

Reviewed by
Todd Plummer
Why did this hotel catch your attention? What's the vibe?
It took over 1,500 local artisans to create this
Marrakech
icon, and no detail was overlooked, from the sumptuous stucco work to the manicured walled gardens. When we think of traveling to a place like Morocco, this is the kind of hotel we dream about.
What's the backstory?
Once you've encountered the opulent interiors, it should come as no surprise that the hotel is owned by none other than the King of Morocco himself.
Tell us all about the accommodations. Any tips on what to book?
The 53 keys here are anything but traditional hotel rooms—each one is designed as its own riad. After passing through your own private entry courtyard complete with fountain, you enter your three-story accommodation. Even the entry-level riads start at 1,500 square feet and come with plunge pools and sun decks overlooking the city, so there isn't a bad riad in the house.
Is there a charge for Wi-Fi?
No charge. It's fast.
Drinking and dining—what are we looking at?
Chef Yannick Alléno, who runs several Michelin-starred restaurants, is the mastermind behind the property's seven food/beverage outposts, which range from Italian and Mediterranean to pan-Asian. You won't go wrong at any of them, but make sure you secure a reservation at the fine dining destination La Grande Table Marocaine, which discerning travelers regularly hail as one of the best restaurants in the world. The lamb shoulder tagine for two is legendary.
And the service?
For a property of this caliber, the service is expectedly wonderful. All riads come with 24/7 access to a personal butler, who can coordinate dinner reservations and arrange tours of the nearby Medina. But the most interesting thing is the property's network of hidden tunnels and elevators, which allow the staff to respond to each and every request in a magically discreet fashion. One minute the staff is delivering bags to your room, and the next, they're gone.
What type of travelers will you find here?
True to Morocco's history, guests at Royal Mansour are a cosmopolitan bunch—and everyone is treated like royalty.
What about the neighborhood? Does the hotel fit in, make itself part of the scene?
Given how serene and private the hotel feels, it's almost strange to think how close it is to Marrakech's bustling Medina. The location couldn't be more central, but the layout of property makes it feel like you're on a private estate miles away from the hustle and bustle.
Is there anything you'd change?
When you consider the decor, the individual riads, the top-notch food and beverage, and the proximity to Marrakech's top destinations, you start to realize that everything about this hotel is perfectly executed. Is there such thing as being too opulent?
Any other hotel features worth noting?
The hotel offers an exclusive excursion to the nearby home of the late master perfumer Serge Lutens, which is not open to the public and does not allow photos. It's once-in-a-lifetime details like this that really set Royal Mansour apart from other hotels.
Bottom line: Worth it? Why?
It's a splurge, but if you're doing to go big, this is the place to do it.
