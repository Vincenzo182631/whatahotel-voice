Reviewed by

Why book?
Anse Chastanet is a retreat for active travelers who enjoy its unique open-air luxury, whether enjoying a romantic honeymoon, scuba diving, or just exploring eco-paradise.
Set the scene
This is a honeymooner’s dream, with two soft sand beaches, coral reefs, and enough activities—from jungle biking on the property’s trails, to snorkeling with a marine biologist, to an ultra-luxe private sunset catamaran sailing—to keep guests as active or relaxed as they’d prefer. Honeymooners are a significant portion of the resort’s clientele, along with scuba divers and others seeking active adventures in paradise.
Located an hour’s drive or a six-minute helicopter ride from St. Lucia’s Hewanorra International Airport (UVF), Anse Chastanet is located just below its sister resort
Jade Mountain
, an ultra-luxe mountaintop getaway.
Anse Chastanet is located on 600 acres and comprises 51 accommodations, with four restaurants and three bars for plenty of variety. The Soufriere Marine Management Area is just off the beach, with opportunities for scuba diving with PADI 5-Star Scuba St. Lucia, as well as snorkeling, either independently or with a marine biologist. Additionally, people can go kayaking, stand-up paddleboarding, windsurfing, and enjoy other watersports. On-site activities range from jungle biking to bird watching, with opportunities to visit the property’s organic farm and chocolate laboratory, play tennis or pickleball, take yoga classes, get a massage or spa treatment, join a mixology class, and more. Private activities, such as sunset catamaran sails and beach dinners, can also be arranged for the ultimate in romantic luxury. Off-property, try activities like zip lining, horseback riding, or a visit to the rainforest.
The backstory
In the early 1970s, Canadian architect Nick Troubetzkoy came to St. Lucia, where he joined with partners to create Anse Chastanet. Originally, it started with a dozen rooms but has since transitioned into a 600-acre property offering 51 accommodations on-site, each designed individually to avoid “cookie-cutter” rooms. In 2006, Troubetzkoy’s team launched sister resort Jade Mountain, which includes 29 ultra-luxe sanctuaries and is located higher on the mountain.
The rooms
Anse Chastanet is known for its unique open-air rooms for an immersive Caribbean experience. Some have louvered walls, while others are fully open-air. Each room is carefully angled and designed with privacy in mind (this is honeymooners’ paradise, after all).
My husband and I stayed in 7E “Passion Flower” and enjoyed spectacular views of both the iconic Pitons (the two stunning volcanic spires that are a UNESCO World Heritage Site) and the Caribbean Sea, with two large balcony areas furnished with comfy seating to relax and soak up the incredible views. The room has louvered walls, and I loved the indoor-outdoor concept. A few small birds came into our room now and then, but the breeze, combined with a yellow lighting scheme and bed net, kept bugs mostly away.
“Most rooms feature airy louvered walls and large windows, designed to welcome natural light and the island’s gentle sea breezes while maintaining comfort and privacy,” says Karolin Troubetzkoy, executive director of marketing and operations at Jade Mountain and Anse Chastanet resorts. “Only a small number of rooms feature open-wall design, and these are carefully matched to guests who specifically enjoy this immersive style.”
There are a variety of room categories, with each individual room uniquely styled and decorated with vibrant paintings created by visiting artists. One of the most popular rooms was constructed around a tree—which is located in the shower—and it also includes a swing. Another standout is the Piton Pool Suite, which, as you might guess, offers a private pool along with stunning views. The massive Beach House is 1,600 square feet, including a 450-square-foot covered patio. If air conditioning is a must, be sure to opt for a room that includes it, such as the garden-view Beachside Deluxe Rooms, which are lower on the hill and provide great beach access.
“At Anse Chastanet, the vision has always been to create living spaces that feel authentic to the Caribbean environment: relaxed, artistic, and naturally connected to the surrounding landscape,” Troubetzkoy says.
Food and drink
With four restaurants and three bars, we found plenty of variety, and the food was always delicious. We enjoyed breakfasts with gorgeous views in The Treehouse Restaurant, with lunchtime beachside dining at Trou au Diable Restaurant, serving burgers, sandwiches, salads, and Caribbean and Creole specialties. Enjoy the East Indian-Caribbean fusion menu at Apsara, and dine at Emerald’s Vegan Cuisine for even more options.
Be sure to check the schedule for special events like executive chef dinners, and even a conservation-focused
Lionfish Dinner
to raise awareness about the invasive species, while sampling delicious dishes featuring locally harvested lionfish.
One of my favorite dining experiences at Anse Chastanet was taking the water taxi to the resort’s second beach, Anse Mamin, to enjoy the beachside Jungle Grill. The grill serves up fare like burgers and sandwiches, and the dorado fish burger with Caribbean coleslaw I ate was one of the best fish sandwiches I’ve ever had. After eating, we enjoyed a lazy afternoon relaxing in the
palapas
, reading and napping in comfortable loungers between swims, covered by a thatched canopy low enough to provide shade and perfect ocean views.
The spa
Enjoy a wide variety of massages, Ayurvedic rituals, and treatments from around the world at Kai Belté Spa. Be sure to check out the menu of chocolate-focused options like a chocolate facial and mocha massage, or opt for an organic seaweed leaf wrap or warm spiced mud wrap.
The neighborhood/area
Located on the southwest coast of St. Lucia, Anse Chastanet is in a prime location for gorgeous views of the Pitons, as well as quick access to the ocean, including the vibrant coral reef just a quick snorkel from shore. The resort’s 600 acres are the perfect setting for numerous on-resort activities, such as jungle biking, guided walks and hikes, and birdwatching. The hotel team can book off-resort excursions in the area, too: Sulphur Springs is a popular location for mud baths, or you can sign up for deep-sea fishing, zip line adventures, or a trip to explore the local market. Another fun option is touring the organic
Emerald Farm
, which grows much of the resort’s produce and has a microbrewery on site.
The service
The team at Anse Chastanet goes out of their way to arrange incredible experiences for guests. The resort publishes a schedule of activities, including both on-resort and on-island options, though they can also arrange private versions of many offerings, including boat charters, sunset sails, specialty dining, and a variety of special occasion experiences.
For families
Children ages eight and over are welcome year-round
Eco effort
Anse Chastanet focuses on conservation of both its 600 acres of land and the surrounding reef areas with coral restoration and marine conservation programs, in addition to a weekly menu focused on invasive lionfish. Additionally, the property sources much of its organic produce and flowers from Emerald Farm, which guests can tour during their stay.
Accessibility
Since the property is located on a steep hill, access may be a challenge for people with limited mobility. A shuttle is available to some locations, but there are many stairs, uneven pathways, and no elevators. Beachfront and lower hillside rooms can provide easier access, but guests with accessibility concerns should contact the resort to discuss their specific needs.
Anything left to mention?
Anse Chastanet excels in creating exceptional romantic experiences. My husband and I enjoyed a private sunset sail on the luxury sailing catamaran
Jade Serenity
. We sailed along St. Lucia’s coastline, sipping drinks and eating light bites freshly prepared by the onboard chef, while we spotted a large group of dolphins swimming and leaping out of the water all around the boat. Afterwards, we transferred to a water taxi which brought us to the resort’s second beach, Anse Mamin, where we experienced a Private Castaway Beach Dinner set up just for us, dining with our toes in the sand listening to the lapping ocean waves, as we enjoyed a delicious multi-course meal under the stars.
