Bellagio
A grand dame in the middle of a serious glow-up, with iconic fountains out front and some of the Strip’s most meaningful new dining and nightlife momentum inside

Reviewed by
Andrea Bennett
Best for:
First-timers who want the classic
Vegas
postcard, return visitors who care about dining and design upgrades, and anyone who still believes a hotel should make an entrance.
Location:
Center Strip, directly across from Paris and Caesars, with the fountains acting as both front yard and landmark.
Can’t-miss feature:
The Fountains of Bellagio, obviously, but right now the bigger surprise may be how much new energy Carbone Riviera and Pinky Ring have injected into the property after dark.
In a nutshell:
Bellagio remains one of Vegas’s defining resorts, but ongoing room, casino, and spa renovations plus a pair of headline-making openings have it feeling far more current than nostalgic.
Rooms
Bellagio’s accommodations are in active transition, which is good news if you book carefully. The big, completed project is the Spa Tower: 819 guest rooms and 104 suites redesigned by Champalimaud with a Lake Como palette, custom furnishings, and a much softer, more tailored feel than older Bellagio rooms. Spa Tower penthouses got the same treatment, bringing the higher end of the inventory in line with the resort’s newer luxury feel. The next phase is underway in the Bellagio Tower, where roughly 400 suites are being renovated, so expect some temporary disruption. Standard Bellagio strengths still apply—good room sizes, many with fountain views, and bathrooms that lean heavy on marble and soaking tubs—but right now it pays to request the newest Spa Tower rooms whenever possible.
Food and drink
Carbone Riviera is the headline here, and for good reason. Open since November 2025 in the old Picasso space, Major Food Group’s seafood-driven sibling to Carbone translates the brand’s maximalist Italian-American glamour into a Bellagio-appropriate Riviera fantasy, with whole fish, crudos, and some of the most coveted tables facing the fountains. (Look for the $3 million Riva yacht,
The Fortuna
, docked outside. It takes select guests on a little cruise around the fountains.) Pinky Ring has become the other major current of energy: Bruno Mars’s lounge is part cocktail den, part live-music room, part de facto celebrity clubhouse, and it has done a lot to make Bellagio feel like somewhere people go after dinner rather than just sleep after seeing the fountains. The resort still has plenty of legacy weight—Lago, Mayfair Supper Club, Spago—but Carbone Riviera and Pinky Ring are the clearest signs that Bellagio is actively editing its next chapter, not just preserving the old one.
Amenities and experiences
The Bellagio pool complex remains one of the prettiest on the Strip, all cypress trees, courtyards, and restrained Mediterranean fantasy. The Conservatory & Botanical Gardens still function as a kind of public theater for the hotel, changing seasonally and drawing as many non-guests as actual hotel residents. Retail is strong, especially Via Bellagio’s luxury corridor, but the bigger operational story is the ongoing casino floor renovation, which is gradually modernizing one of the most familiar gaming spaces in Las Vegas. The one caveat: the 55,000-square-foot spa is closed for a major remodel through November 2026, so this is not the moment to book Bellagio primarily for a spa weekend unless you’re willing to wait for the relaunch.
What to do nearby
Bellagio’s center-Strip position remains one of its biggest advantages. You can walk to Caesars, Paris, Aria, Cosmopolitan, and the Shops at Crystals without much logistical drama, and the fountains make it one of the easiest hotels for rideshares and meet-ups. For people who like to roam, it is hard to beat as a base: old-guard luxury in one direction, newer food and nightlife in the other. If you stay put, though, that works too—the fountains, Conservatory, restaurants, Pinky Ring, and casino give you plenty to do before you ever consider crossing Las Vegas Boulevard.
The backstory
Bellagio opened in 1998 as Steve Wynn’s answer to European grand-hotel glamour, complete with the dancing fountains, Dale Chihuly glass ceiling, and a level of floral theater the Strip had not really seen before. For years it embodied classic luxury in Las Vegas, which also meant that parts of it risked feeling frozen while newer resorts chased flashier versions of the same idea. The current renovation cycle is Bellagio’s answer to that problem: fully redone Spa Tower rooms and suites, Bellagio Tower suite upgrades, an active casino refresh, a spa overhaul, and new culinary and nightlife anchors that bring in the kind of guest who might once have defaulted to Wynn or the Cosmopolitan.
Insider tip
If you want the freshest room product, book Spa Tower over Bellagio Tower for now; the completed redesign there gives you the best odds of landing in a room that feels newly luxe rather than merely well maintained. For dinner, try for an early Carbone Riviera reservation and turn it into a full Bellagio night—fountain views at dinner, then Pinky Ring for cocktails and live sets, where Bruno Mars has become such a regular presence that the room can feel more like his personal after-hours salon than a standard hotel lounge. And unless you are specifically tracking the spa reopening, hold off on a wellness-forward stay until after November, when the remodel is scheduled to debut.
Final considerations
Family friendly:
Yes, more than many luxury Strip hotels, thanks to the fountains, Conservatory, and broad appeal across age groups, though the dining and room rates skew upscale.
Accessibility:
Strong overall, with accessible rooms, elevators, and a relatively navigable center-Strip footprint for a resort of this scale.
Wellness:
Temporarily limited by the spa closure; pools and fitness remain, but the full spa experience is off the table until the November 2026 reopening.
Loyalty group:
Part of MGM Rewards, making it easy to combine stays with other MGM properties across the Strip.
