Why book?
With a location on Vancouver’s working harbor, a culinary program that serves 100 percent sustainably sourced and ethically caught fish, and an ongoing activation series that invites guests to be a part of the rhythm of the Pacific Northwest, Fairmont Waterfront offers pure coastal luxury through and through.
Set the scene
Walking into the lounge-like lobby with its gorgeous floor-to-ceiling windows, you can expect to see versions of every type of traveler— business travelers, celebratory couples, cruisers (thanks to the hotel’s proximity to the cruise terminal), families. With a prime location, excellent service, and abundance of meeting spaces, Fairmont Waterfront truly has something for everyone. Be sure to pack comfortable walking shoes for days spent exploring Vancouver, sport coats and flowy dresses for the hotel’s happy hour featuring $1.50 freshly shucked oysters, and a swimsuit for a nighttime dip in the 50-foot rooftop swimming pool, heated year-round and offering sweeping panoramic views.
The rooms
Currently undergoing a massive renovation project across all 489 guest rooms and signature suites, guests can expect fully refreshed and modernized accommodations inspired by a coastal palette with textures and furniture that bring the outdoors in. Led by the award-winning Canadian design studio CAMDI, the project will pause for summer 2026 and resume in the last quarter of the year, officially scheduled to wrap in 2029. All rooms will have a 65-inch flat-screen television, in-room safe, a Nespresso machine, Le Labo bath amenities, flexible seating options, and floor-to-ceiling windows. While the rooms are a significant focus of the renovation, updates will also be made to the lobby, ARC Restaurant & Bar, and pool area.

Food and drink
ARC Restaurant and Bar is the property’s singular on-site dining experience, featuring locally sourced ingredients and produce plucked straight from the rooftop garden to create expert odes to West Coast cuisine. Led by Executive Chef Harris Sakalis, fan favorites include the B.C. Chinook salmon served with Dungeness crab mashed potatoes and the handmade ricotta ravioli made with sunchoke cream, forest mushrooms, and kabocha squash. For a full experience of all the Pacific Northwest has to offer, guests can also order Sakalis's curated Province to Plate three-course experience. Starting at $79, choose from multiple options for each course (including gluten-free and vegan options).
For the lucky travelers staying over a weekend (I sadly was not), don’t skip the hotel’s Bottomless Weekend Brunch. Named one of Canada’s Top 100 Brunches by OpenTable, it includes bottomless tasting plates—pulled pork Benedict, lobster mac and cheese, buttermilk chicken and waffles—and complimentary coffee or tea. If you’re looking to add a cocktail experience to brunch, I recommend the Aviary made with Waterfront gin, Chartreuse, honey syrup, Champagne acid, and rosemary that gets smoked tableside.
Lara Kramer
The neighborhood/area
You’re in the center of it all when you stay at Fairmont Waterfront. An easy walk to historic Gastown, the Vancouver Convention Center, and endless shops and eateries, it’s the perfect launch pad for exploring a truly vibrant city. If you’re looking to visit Vancouver’s iconic seawall, the world's longest uninterrupted waterfront path, you can travel by wheels thanks to the property’s complimentary bike program available to all guests.
The service
The service is warm, friendly, and attentive at every turn. For an extra indulgent experience, you can book a stay through the property’s Fairmont Gold program which provides a hotel-within-a-hotel experience. Guests are given a private check-in separate from the main front desk, a dedicated concierge team, and exclusive access to a private lounge which serves complimentary snacks and light bites throughout the day.
For families
Given its central location and year-round pool access, Fairmont Waterfront is a solid hotel choice for traveling families. Also worth noting: If you want to take advantage of the property’s complimentary bike program but have little ones, you can rent kid bikes, child trailers, child seats, trail-a-bikes, and tandems directly across the street from the hotel. During my visit, I saw several families with younger children enjoying the rooftop pool and utilizing the hotel's central location to explore the city.
Eco effort
The property has earned a 5 Green Key rating and 4 Green Key Meetings rating from Green Key Global, a hotel and lodging sustainability certification program that evaluates energy and water use, waste management, and greenhouse gas emissions.
Launched in 1996, the 2,100-square-foot rooftop garden grows herbs, fruits, vegetables, and edible blossoms without the use of herbicides or pesticides. Not far from the garden is the hotel’s outdoor Bee Program, led by chief beekeeper Julia Common who has over four decades of experience in apiculture and a background in agricultural science. During the warmer months, the team offers daily Bee Tours that introduce guests to the world of beekeeping and educate them on how to support these local pollinators.
The hotel has numerous partnerships to support their overall mission of sustainability and community, including those with Vancouver Food Runners, La Tablée des Chefs, and Soap for Hope.
Accessibility
The hotel complies with all applicable provincial accessibility legislation and building codes in British Columbia, including barrier-free access points, elevator access to all floors, and thoughtfully designed rooms and suites that support guests with diverse mobility and accessibility needs.
Anything left to mention?
As a hotel located on the traditional territories of the Musqueam, Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh Nations, Fairmont Waterfront is committed to initiatives that help create meaningful economic opportunities and platforms for Indigenous voices. They’ve teamed up with Talaysay Tours, an immersive Indigenous tourism and education company, to offer guests a
special booking package
which includes a 90-minute walking tour led by a local First Nations guide to learn about coastal trees, land, and Indigenous people. During my stay, I had the incredible pleasure of joining this tour and it is now in my top recommendations for anybody visiting Vancouver.
The hotel’s efforts will continue to evolve through the newly announced formal agreement with Indigenous Tourism B.C. which focuses on expanding their work with Indigenous-led tour operators, artists, and cultural leaders across the province. Guests can expect new hotel programming, such as an Artist-in-Residence series which launched this March, and additional activations including traditional Indigenous foods featured on the in-room dining menu rolling out in May.
