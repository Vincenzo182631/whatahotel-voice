The Ritz-Carlton Signature Suites
Suites
Experience the finest in luxury at The Ritz-Carlton, Vienna, nestled in four preserved 19th-century palaces. The prestigious suites seamlessly blend modern elegance with the timeless beauty of Vienna's rich heritage. From original historical frescoes to exclusive partnerships with the Wiener Symphoniker and the Albertina Museum, each signature suite offers a journey that celebrates Vienna’s music, art, and architecture.
Honoring Vienna's Musical Tradition
The Ritz-Carlton Symphony Suite
As a tribute to Vienna's rich musical heritage, The Ritz-Carlton Symphony Suite has created an exclusive partnership with the Wiener Symphoniker, the esteemed and globally renowned orchestra, to welcome guests to a residence adorned with exquisite and luxurious furnishings.

The Ritz-Carlton Symphony Suite
Designed as a Music Piece
In a metaphorical symphony, the architectural design mirrors the structure of a sonata, bringing to life two opposing themes – past and future. Drawing its unique character from a playlist of specially composed pieces by the Vienna Symphony Orchestra, the suite immerses you in music from the moment you enter. Facing Beethoven Park, it echoes the premiere of his Symphony No. 9 in Vienna in 1824.

The Ritz-Carlton Albertina Suite
Craft Your Masterpiece
The Ritz-Carlton Albertina Suite honors Anton Ötzelt, the owner, builder, and architect of Palais Ötzelt, who once housed his art collection in this building. Immerse yourself in artistry with a suitcase easel and a Ringstrasse sketch for you to trace. Take home your personalized creation as a cherished souvenir.

The Albertina VIP Experience
As a guest of The Ritz-Carlton Albertina Suite, you're not just booking a suite; you're securing a VIP experience at the Albertina Museum. Immerse yourself in Central Europe's largest collection of modern art, featuring exhibitions from Monet to Picasso. Scenic Ring Boulevard Balcony

Presidential Suite
Heritage-Protected Frescos
Situated within the former Palais Gutmann, named after the rags-to-riches family who once owned it, the suite still preserves an original masterpiece by August Eisenmenger. Adorning the area around the living room chandelier, the ceiling from the former Belletage depicts twelve months represented by angels. Eisenmenger also painted the famous Golden Hall ceiling fresco of the Vienna Musikverein.
