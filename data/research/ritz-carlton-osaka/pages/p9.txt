The Ritz-Carlton, Osaka
An Osaka hotel inspired by an English country mansion.

Reviewed by
Tamara Hinson
Why book?
For a five-star hotel with a fantastic location and beautiful decor, which (on the inside, at least) recalls an 18th-century British aristocratic mansion. Nothing’s too much effort for the wonderful employees, known for going above and beyond for guests, many of whom are repeat visitors.
Set the scene
There was something about the entrance to Ritz-Carlton, Osaka, which reminded me of my favorite London pub—I suspect it’s the oak-paneled doors and the wrought-iron light fixtures. Sadly, my much-loved local doesn’t have the top hat-wearing doormen who open this hotel’s doors with a flourish. This is one of Osaka’s grandest hotels, tucked inside a minimalist, modern skyscraper; a popular base for travelers who appreciate its old-school charm and dedication to customer service. It’s located in the Umeda district, to the north of the downtown area, but less than half a mile from Osaka Station (if you’re arriving by shinkansen, you’ll arrive at downtown Osaka’s Shin-Osaka Station, three miles to the north, but still easily accessible). Its location is the reason its guests are a varied mix—everyone from parents with kids in tow to business travelers who crave a bit of extra space.
The backstory
The Ritz-Carlton Osaka opened in 1997, 91 years after the opening of the
Ritz London
, which might well have inspired the design of this Japanese hotel with its antique-stuffed public spaces and Georgian-themed decor. That said, numerous renovations, including a huge overhaul in 2016 and another room-focused reno in 2024, have ensured the feel of this hotel stays sophisticated, not stuffy.
The rooms
You’ll find the 291 rooms spread across floors 24 through 37, so great views are guaranteed—as is a sense of space: even the smallest rooms are 433 square feet. Generally, the decor leans British—Laura Ashley–like lamps top bedside tables carved from dark oak, and there are winged chaise lounges which wouldn’t look out of place in
Downton Abbey
. But this isn’t a hotel that's afraid of modernity. For example, behind the embroidered, swag-like curtains hanging from the box cornices are swatches of extra fabric which double as blackout blinds, and bedside panels allowed me to control everything from the lighting to the curtains without leaving the comfort of my enormous bed. It’s worth pointing out that guests who prefer Japanese decor over Georgian can book one of the two Japanese Suites, a decadent tribute to the land of the rising sun. Highlights here include the artfully weathered Teppei flagstones near the suites’ entrances, beautiful tatami flooring, and enormous bathtubs carved from Mikage—a type of milky-white granite sourced from nearby Kobe.
Food and drink
The jewel in this hotel’s culinary crown is La Baie, a Michelin-starred French restaurant which serves time-honored classics alongside innovative twists on French staples. (I recommend the spicy roast duck fillet.) Celebrating a special occasion? Book the spectacular Riedel Room, a private dining room with gilt mirrors and hand-carved furnishings. The hotel’s Xiang Tao restaurant serves up Chinese delicacies, and Splendido does a mean line of Italian classics, albeit with Japanese twists—like burrata served with shishito peppers. I also dined at Hanagatami, which specializes in kaiseki (multi-course) cuisine and teppanyaki. I opted for the latter, and loved how the seating counter, carved from fragrant hinoki wood, overlooked a beautiful Japanese garden. Specialities at The Bar are martinis (there are 100 varieties on offer) and malt whiskies, while the Lobby Lounge is the setting for the hotel’s afternoon teas, served to a soundtrack of jazz.
The spa
The spa has five treatment rooms and, unlike in the rest of the hotel, Japanese influences dominate when it comes to the finer details. One of the most popular offerings is a matcha-infused facial, and certain massages use sake. Products are top-notch—the spa works with both Espa and La Prairie. Swims in the 66-foot pool come with a view of a small garden, and there’s a well-equipped fitness center, open 24 hours a day.
The neighborhood/area
Umeda, a transport hub and business district, hasn’t traditionally been the preferred base for travelers, simply because people have gravitated to the area around Shin-Osaka Station three miles to the north, or to neon-drenched Dotonbori to the south. But change is afoot. In 2024, Umeda’s Grand Green Osaka, a sprawling park designed by Seattle-based firm GGN, was unveiled. Designed as a tranquil space in Osaka, it’s crisscrossed with walking paths. There are endless opportunities for culture fixes here too, including at the nearby Osaka Science Museum, which reopened in August 2024 after major upgrades to its exhibition spaces.
The service
Service is impeccable and yes, formal, but there’s not a hint of stuffiness. Concierges are knowledgeable and passionate, and staff will go out of their way to please. I’m a keen runner, and when I mentioned this to a member of staff, I was asked if I’d like to go for a run around Osaka Castle with a member of management, and informed that guests regularly join staff members on jogs or cycle rides around the neighborhood. Staff will happily conduct impromptu art tours—the hotel’s collection includes numerous priceless masterpieces by 18th-century European artists—and guests can also join daily origami lessons, held in the lobby.
For families
The Ritz-Carlton Osaka offers what might just be the city’s coolest experience for children—The Ritz Kids Night Safari designed for two adults and two children. Rooms are kitted out with a tent, mattress, and lantern, and kids are given a stash of amenities, including a water bottle, treasure map, pajamas, bathrobe, and slippers.
Eco effort
There are plastic water bottles in the bedroom, but on the plus side, the shampoo, conditioner, and body wash (Diptyque, no less) are in fixed bottles. The hotel has been certified by Green Key, an international environmental certification program for the hospitality industry, largely thanks to initiatives such as the use of low-energy lighting and restricted use of potentially harmful substances like synthetic detergents.
Accessibility
Almost all of the hotel—including conference facilities, restaurants, and the fitness center—is wheelchair accessible, and there are 30 accessible rooms. There are disabled parking spaces on-site, too.
Anything left to mention?
Don’t miss the opportunity to go for a run or a bike ride with a member of staff—it’s a great way to get the inside scoop on the surrounding area.
