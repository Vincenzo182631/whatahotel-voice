The St. Regis Florence

Why book?
A symphony for the senses, if you want a hotel with two centuries of history, thoughtful design, attentive service, and some of the best views over the Arno River, St. Regis Florence is the spot for discerning travelers who care as much about heritage as they do about comfort.
Set the scene
Simply put, The St. Regis Florence is a masterpiece of a hotel. Which, in a city as storied in beauty and history as Florence, is no small claim. From the moment you step into the grand lobby, you’ll be greeted with hand-painted frescoes, Murano-blown glass, and crystal chandeliers framing the space with theatrical ease. A stay here feels unapologetically regal, which is fitting given the hotel’s long history of hosting royalty, dignitaries, and cultural and political power players. The scene reflects that pedigree. Honeymooners and impeccably dressed Europeans in the latest Gucci and Versace can always be counted on in the rotation alongside multi-generational families on milestone trips and annual five-star holidays.
This is a hotel with romance and celebration in mind, whether that means marking a once-in-a-lifetime occasion or indulging a lifelong love of history, art, and design. The crowd skews international and spans generations, but there’s a common thread: many guests are repeat visitors, returning not out of habit but loyalty. In a city defined by legacy, The St. Regis Florence doesn’t just place you in the historic center of one of the world’s most historic cities; it is a destination in its own right.
The backstory
Occupying a former 15th-century palazzo designed by the same Italian architect behind the Duomo, The St. Regis Florence has been woven into the fabric of the city for centuries. Long before it became the five-star hotel that it is today, the building was constructed as a private palazzo for one of Florence’s most noble families. In 1866, it was converted into the Grand Hotel Royal de la Paix, which was not only one of the city’s first luxury hotels but also a favored stop for royalty and dignitaries, including Queen Victoria, who was one of the hotel’s earliest guests. In 2011, after a nearly year-long renovation, the property reopened as The St. Regis Florence. The hotel went to great lengths to ensure that its antiques, decorative objets d’art, and Renaissance-era details were preserved, and they remain visible in the public spaces and guest rooms to this day.
The rooms
A strong sense of place and historical charm are baked into the stay here. With 99 guest rooms and suites in total, every room comes appointed with a Florentine, Medici, or Renaissance–inspired design. Think: hand-painted details, gold and gilded accents, custom furnishings, and crystal chandeliers that make it feel as though you’ve stepped into a grand private salon from a bygone era. High ceilings and tall windows, the best of which frame the Arno River, reinforce that palazzo scale. Each room offers heavy drapery, richly patterned textiles, and curated artworks that harken back to the property’s aristocratic private palazzo roots. Despite the historical detailing, the rooms are well-balanced, comfortable, and above all, elegant.
The marble-clad bathrooms are equally indulgent. Finished in Carrara marble, with double vanities, deep-soaking tubs, and separate walk-in showers, the heated towel racks, generous counter space, and signature St. Regis amenities are the perfect touch. Suites elevate the stay and include the hotel’s signature St. Regis Butler service, which is on hand to accommodate any and every request imaginable.
Food and drink
There are few places that feel as indulgent to sit and enjoy a cocktail as beneath the stained-glass ceiling of the St. Regis’ Winter Garden. Every night at 5 p.m., during the St. Regis’ nightly Champagne ritual, theater and ritual come to life. The bar’s signature Brunello Bloody Mary nods to the hotel’s long-standing history as the drink's inventors and remains a house classic, richer and more savory than most, thanks to the wine-forward twist. Cocktails lean classic rather than experimental, keeping with the room’s old-world glamour.
A multi-purpose salon, the Winter Bar is the place for dinner, drinks, and all things aperitivo. Dinner is offered both à la carte and as a set tasting menu that spotlights refined Italian cooking and seasonal ingredients. It’s a destination dining experience in its own right, but it never forgets its starring role as the hotel’s social center of all things beauty, ritual, and occasion. Just off the lobby, Café Ginori is the hotel’s all-day café and also offers light lunch and an afternoon tea led by a certified tea sommelier.
The spa
Compared to the grandeur of the rest of the property, the on-site spa is comparatively scaled-back. What it lacks in size, it makes up for in personalized treatments. There’s a small hammam and a few spa suites, but the hotel is more than happy to arrange an in-room massage.
The neighborhood/area
Set along the Arno River, it’s less than a 15-minute walk to the Duomo. The hotel is tucked just enough away from the frenetic city center to feel centrally located while remaining just outside the fray.
The service
In true St. Regis fashion, the service and attention to detail are next level. Anticipatory without feeling overwhelming, don’t be surprised if a favorite cocktail or an interesting Florentine fact that you casually mentioned to your butler or someone on the concierge team happens to appear in some way, shape, or form on your bedside table by the time you go to bed that same day. While the service is top-notch, it doesn’t feel suffocating. If you have a request, rest assured, your butler will do everything in their power to make it happen, from turning a last-minute request for a private sunset cruise down the Arno River to securing a day-of reservation at one of the best restaurants in town. The service here is all about helping you create a lasting memory that stays with you long after you leave.
For families
This is a good option for families, thanks to its convenient location near some of Florence’s most popular attractions. The hotel also offers complimentary roll-away beds for kids.
Accessibility
Because The St. Regis Florence is a historic building, room accessibility is available, but limited and varies by room category. The hotel suggests contacting them directly to discuss specific mobility or accessibility needs to confirm which accessible rooms and features are available for your stay.
Anything left to mention?
While every room is an experience, opt for a river-facing view with a balcony for something truly memorable.
