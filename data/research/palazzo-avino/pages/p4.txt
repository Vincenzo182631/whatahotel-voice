Reviewed by

Why book?
From its prime position on the peak of Ravello’s hilltop, the multi-tied, pink parasol-speckled gardens of Palazzo Avino exude the Dolce Vita allure that Amalfi is famed for. The unparalleled view, tip-top food, manicured lawns and sunny service make for a seriously pampered experience. Serene, discrete, and expertly curated, this is Italianate splendor at its finest.
Set the scene
Though
Amalfi
has no shortage of decadent, bygone-era boltholes, few palazzos have balanced age-old charm with modern chic adeptly. Known as the “pink palace of Ravello”, Palazzo Avino has done just that. A once 12th-century private home, the villa is steeped in traditional Italian charm with breathtakingly timeless terrace views across the Monte Avvocata valley and the yacht-speckled Bay of Salerno beyond. Guests can take a shuttle down to its private beach club, a rocky suntrap with a separate pool, secluded sun loungers, and staircases that drip down the rock face into the sea.
The backstory
First opened in 1997, sisters Mariella and Attilia Avino took over the hotel from their father, Giuseppe Avino, adding a great dollop of color and personality. The family had been looking at abandoned properties in the region and immediately fell in love with the architecture and its potential. The renovation took two years, and the sisters are still updating and tweaking the villa with new color schemes and clever offerings. Their style credentials led to the opening of adjacent store, The Pink Closet, selling full-throttle Italian glamor via the likes of sparkly Rosantica evening bags, embellished mules by Giannico, glittering Attico dresses, and plush hairbands by Bluetiful - a style staple of the sisters themselves. The other fashion accolade of serious note is the beach club's Valentino takeover this Summer, a first for the fashion giant, that saw the sun loungers and parasols painted Valentino red with kitschy touches such as a retro changing booth and water skies.
The rooms
The decor is Poseidon’s palace by way of Moda Operandi, with pink shell tiles, glossy seafoam-blue bathrooms and chic scalloped headboards. There are no straight lines here: doorways are arched, ceiling domed, mirrors wavy, mimicking the mountain-scape coastline and swirl of the ocean in one. The rooms are spacious, the best ones coming with views across the bay or balconies big enough to dine al fresco. I travelled with my toddler, and there was ample space for her cot (provided) and a seating nook, which conveniently doubled as a changing station.
Food and drink
Breakfast is held on the achingly romantic, vine-covered veranda, where mist is still rising up the valley before 8am. Lunch can be taken poolside (the stone-baked pizza does not disappoint) or on a separate terrace overlooking the sea. Fresh fish and seafood are the menu's stars, with waiters proudly announcing the catch of the day, but there is also fresh pasta, served al dente with fresh garlic pesto or vine-ripe tomatoes. The highlight is Michelin-starred restaurant Rossellini’s, where plates of lobster linguine or saffron and shrimp risotto are served by candlelight. The wine selection is also of note; ask for the Mar-a-viglia white wine, made at the family’s vineyard La Cascinetta. At the end of the evening, head to the dedicated Martini Bar for one of its over 100 iterations.
The service
Friendly and welcoming, the restaurant staff wear bright pink braces over their crisp white shirts and seem to remember all of the regular guests. For such a professional operation, there’s a very relaxed atmosphere, perfect for families, as waiters whisk over plush high chairs and pinch baby’s cheeks as they whisper ‘bambino’.
The area
Ravello certainly has a hush-hush feel, less bustling than Positano; there are no coaches of tourists or ferried-in day trippers. At 1,150 feet above sea level, and with its discreet hotel scene, it's more suited for elegant adventures away from the maddening crowds.
Who comes here?
Ravello has long been a destination for legendary figures, particularly for creative types looking for inspiration. Composer Richard Wagner wrote the second act of the opera Parsifal in Ravello, while D.H. Lawrence wrote Lady Chatterley's Lover here. Today, the hotel is stomping ground for fashionable honeymooners (we clocked newlywed brides wearing ostrich feather-trimmed Sleeper pyjamas to breakfast) and young families looking for a glossy escape.
Accessibility
There is wheelchair access inside the hotel but the outside—with its Escher-staircases—would be a challenge.
Anything else to mention?
There’s a sumptuous spa, which includes a hydro pool, Turkish steam bath and treatment rooms, and a high-tech outdoor gym on one of the lower lawns.
