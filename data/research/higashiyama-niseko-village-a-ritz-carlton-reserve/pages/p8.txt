Higashiyama Niseko Village, a Ritz-Carlton Reserve
An intimate hotel with scene-stealing mountain views—and the first Ritz-Carlton Reserve in Japan—lands in powder snow nirvana Niseko

Why book?
The latest addition to Niseko’s already thriving hotel scene, Higashiyama Niseko Village stands out as an intimate retreat in a
prime ski spot
. The close-up views of nearby Mount Yotei looming through glass walls are the scene-stealer here.
Set the scene
Niseko, a small ski resort on Japan’s northernmost island Hokkaido, has evolved into a hangout for thrill-seeking snow lovers with a healthy wallet over the past decade. A flurry of architect-designed hotels and Michelin-starred restaurants have earned it the nickname the Aspen of Asia. While it’s soaringly popular among overseas visitors in winter, when the snow melts it transforms into one of Japan’s best-kept summer secrets, with green mountain forests, outdoor activities, seasonal cuisine, hiking, local dairy farms, and wildflower fields.
The backstory
Higashiyama Niseko Village—owned by YTL Hotels and operated by Marriott—is one of a small, elite collection of Ritz-Carlton Reserves (the fifth in the world when it opened in December 2020). The concept is simple: a focus on catch-your-breath beautiful natural settings, combined with the lavish touch Ritz-Carlton is known for. “Niseko is beautiful year-round,” says Gregory Brossard, director of operations here. “It gets about 10 meters of powder snow every winter—and in the summer, we have great seasonal food.”
The rooms
The old Japanese proverb ‘*kachou fuugetsu’—*which translates as ‘flower, bird, wind, moon’ evoking a sense of the transient beauty of nature—is a key concept at the hotel. Hints of the natural world are threaded through the design, from swathes of dark latticed wood and plush forest-like carpets to the delicate cherry blossom motifs on fabric panels above the bed. Floor to ceiling windows frame either the snow-capped Mount Yotei (in the Yotei rooms) or forested ski slopes (in the Niseko rooms). Bathrooms are equally smart: deep grey slate bathtubs offer a Japanese-style soak-with-a-view.
Food and drink
The quintessentially Japanese concept of seasonal ingredients, known as
shun
, is a driving force behind its two restaurants. Yukibana is the buzzy heartbeat of the hotel, with its curved seating, walls of windows, and chandeliers made of deer antlers. Here, local and seasonal Hokkaido ingredients take centre stage—in the Niseko Eggs Benedict at breakfast, for example, Hokkaido king crab is served with onsen egg, shredded red chilli pepper Ito Togarashi, cranberry toast, and shiso hollandaise. Later in the day, the creative local theme continues—from a deliciously creamy mozzarella sandwich using cheese made at nearby Takahashi Farm to a hearty Buta don, with tamari soy-glazed Makkari pork, egg yolk, winter truffle, and steamed Yumepirika rice. At the far end of the open-plan ground floor is Sushi Nagi—an intimate counter restaurant with wall panels depicting pines, where chef Taira Tsuneyoshi skillfully presents an array of local treats, from uni sea urchin to Botan-ebi shrimps.
The spa
A slower pace unfolds in Spa Chasi La Sothys—
chasi
means sanctuary in Hokkaido’s indigenous Ainu language—which is home to four treatment rooms and a French-beauty-meets-traditional-Japan menu. Foot massages use a heated volcanic black stone from Hokkaido, while signature treatments include Enrich Five, which combines massage with hot compression on acupuncture points, a Sakura Ritual to revitalize both face and body and a velvety rose-scented Secret de Sothys Excellence Luxury Body Ritual. Guests can also reserve sessions in a small private onsen which has knockout forest views.
The neighborhood/area
The property is set within YTL Hotel’s Niseko Village, which is also home to three other properties. This is one of four key resorts that make up the Niseko United ski area’s 2,100 acres of slopes. At the base of Mount Annupuri, the hotel is perfectly positioned for guests to jump straight on a gondola and onto the slopes, with countless ski and snowboard trails, high-quality off-piste powder skiing, and scenic family-friendly routes through the forests. Niseko Village is also home to an international ski school, a handful of brilliant restaurants, an outdoor activity park that’s perfect for summer, and two golf courses.
The service
Smooth, smart, and friendly; staff hail from around the world. Plus, every guest at Higashiyama Niseko Village has a 24-hour butler called a *do-san—*a sleek crew who are on call for every possible need.
For families
It’s family-friendly, with its convenient ski slopes and smiley staff. Seven of the suites can be interconnected to adjacent rooms for larger family groups.
Anything left to mention?
There’s no need to carry your ski clothing across the globe: as is often the case in Japan, the hotel offers ski wear rental on-site, from jackets to trousers—and at Higashiyama Niseko Village, it is all stylish and like new.
