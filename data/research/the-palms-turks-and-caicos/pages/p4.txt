First In: The Palms, Turks and Caicos
For a stay right in the sweet spot between the beach and the pool

Why book?
Sometimes, you just want a vacation that requires nothing beyond trotting between
the beach
and
pool
and back again. The Palms, which just unveiled a top-to-toe renovation of its pool deck (along with a new restaurant and other spruced up nuts and bolts), is the venue for that. Bring a stack of books and few plans other than dinner reservations.
Set the scene
Life at The Palms orbits around the pool—and for good reason considering the swim-up bar, swaying palm trees, and ample options of loungers and day beds. The crowd is dominated by
East Coasters
who have jetted down for some
winter sun
: a mix of young families watching their kids splash around on floaties, retirees baking themselves like lizards while flipping through the newspapers, and honeymooners sipping on rum punches. Personal style here leans well-heeled and preppy.
The backstory
The 72-room property has sat on this white sand strip of Grace Bay Beach in Providenciales for two decades now, having long ago established itself as a stalwart of the
Turks and Caicos
hotel scene, along with its ritzy sister property
The Shore Club
. Its arrival back in 2005 was part of a wider evolution of Turks into the destination it is today—one that’s now plum with hotel and resort options. And while yes, it’s a resort, the atmosphere maintains a level of intimacy and familiarity between guests and staff that’s usually easier to come by at a smaller hotel; so much so that you’ll likely see owner and CEO Stan Hartling—a
Canadian
transplant who has made Turks his home—wafting around in shorts and sunglasses keeping an eye on things. With the resort’s 20th anniversary approaching later this year, Hartling saw it as high time for an $18 million redo, primarily at the pool deck, which has shed its slightly dated 2000s era look in favor of a more millennial-friendly design sensibility that would look quite at home among the laidback, earth-tone hotels of present-day Tulum.
The rooms
Room options span one, two, and three-bedroom suites, with high ceilings and plenty of natural light. Additionally, there are also eight penthouse suites for even more space. The renovations haven’t extended throughout the bedrooms (which possess incredibly comfortable beds made up with 300-thread-count Egyptian cotton sheets) yet—that’s been promised in phase two, according to The Palms team. This means they’ve so far retained their longstanding washed out ocean and coral tones and mahogany furniture, which, while befitting of the destination and the certain kind of traveler who frequents it, can feel a little out of step with the times design wise. The bathrooms, however, have already received a thorough facelift, with gleaming marble floors and showers, often with two separate heads, and deep tubs to soak in. Penthouse suites also have outdoor showers if you’re the kind of traveler who fancies a rinse below the palm trees, and all suites come with spacious balconies to wind down on after a day of sun, many of which have excellent views of the ocean.
Food and drink
Breakfast
is outdoors on a shaded patio where eggs are made to order and local fruit is piled high at the buffet; lunch, meanwhile, means ordering from the swim-up bar and restaurant Plunge, freshly done up after the recent reno and complete with bar stools in the water. (Dishes are the requisite
Caribbean
poolside fare: think coconut shrimp, Caesar salad, and deep fried conch.) For dinner, there’s fine dining spot Parallel23, which serves up a pretty excellent surf and turf dish featuring Caribbean spiny lobster delivered on white table cloths, and the brand new
Mexican
Sí Sí, which pulls off a menu of spicy tuna tostadas, tender carnitas tacos, esquites, and shrimp coconut ceviche. Do not sleep on ordering a cast iron skillet of rich, buttery corn cake on the side, and the local dish of cracked conch that’s been snuck into the appetizers (this is still the Caribbean after all).
The spa
The Palms is known for its spa, which takes up an entire acre of the 12-acre property and which has earned a few awards over the years. You could easily spend the whole day at the spa: Mornings begin with yoga and pilates classes; there are tinkling water features and cabanas to retreat to before or after a treatment; as well as a sauna and steam room to dip in and out of as you please. There’s a range of massages and facials available, using organic products from Sothys and Zents, plus regionally-inspired rituals like the 90-minute ‘Mother of Pearl Conch Polish’ or the ‘Zareeba,’ which incorporates a herbal steam using therapeutic herbs sourced locally. I opted for the Four Palms Restorative massage and walked away feeling considerably less achey in the shoulders—and regretful that I hadn’t decided to give up my entire day to decompressing in one of the peaceful cabanas.
The service
The service here is low-key yet omnipresent, with a 24-hour concierge to set up experiences on the water (say snorkeling, or a sunset cruise) and a butler service that will always make sure there’s a cooler of iced water beside your sun lounger at the start of the day. The bartender at L’Attitude Bar, meanwhile, makes a mean martini.
For families
Families abound at this resort, which has a kids club on site that will accommodate ages 4-12 for both full and half days. Most activities on offer take place outside in the sunshine and in the water. But, on rare occasion the weather doesn’t deliver, there are plenty of indoor activities to keep the kids busy. There’s a babysitting service, and all the suites have kitchens with stovetops and fridges should guests want to stock up on snacks and groceries or need to feed their kids in a more homey setup than one of the restaurants. This is one of those self-contained beach resorts primed for family vacations, minus the kitsch of water slides and the like.
Accessibility
The property features elevators, ramps, and accessible pathways, can accommodate guide pets, and provides beach wheelchairs.
