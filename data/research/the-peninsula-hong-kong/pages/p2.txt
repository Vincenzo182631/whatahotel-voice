Why book?
For the intoxicating mix of myth and modernity, indulgent facilities, and polished service.
Set the scene
Arrive at The Peninsula
Hong Kong
in style—perhaps in one of its signature Rolls-Royce Phantoms Extended or Bentley Bentaygas gliding over Tsing Yi Bridge, or by helicopter, swooping over Victoria Harbour before landing on the rooftop helipad. Either way, you’ll step into a grand lobby flanked by stone lions and pages in crisp white uniforms, where history and glamour meet beneath soaring ceilings.
The backstory
Opened in 1928 by the Kadoorie brothers as the finest hotel east of Suez,” The Pen has witnessed nearly a century of Hong Kong’s transformations—from ocean liners and tea dances to world wars, financial crises, protests, and pandemics—yet its poise has never faltered. It remains a symbol of the city’s cosmopolitan spirit and unshakable resilience.
The rooms
Most of the 297 rooms and suites occupy a 30-storey tower added in the ’90s, where champagne tones, glossy dark woods, and silk accents meet impeccable detail. Door and drawers silently glide open to reveal leather-bound shoe-shine kits, electronic nail-polish dryers, and butler boxes. A silent air-conditioning system has been designed so that you’ll never feel a draft. Meanwhile, bedside control panels—available in nearly a dozen languages—operate lighting, curtains, TVs, and room service orders. Harbour-view rooms are unforgettable, especially as neon reflections shimmer across Victoria Harbour at night.
Food and drink
Eight exceptional venues await. The Lobby serves Hong Kong’s most famous afternoon tea between Corinthian columns. At the same time, a live string quartet serenades you with Bach and Vivaldi (happily, in-house guests can make a reservation). Visit in autumn or spring, and you can also book afternoon tea onboard The Peninsula Star Ferry, quaffing scones and Champagne while touring the harbor. Michelin-starred Spring Moon is one of the loveliest Cantonese restaurants around, serving exquisite dim sum, roast meats, and seasonal dishes inside a stained-glass and rosewood tea house. Michelin-starred Gaddi’s offers a classic French fine-dining experience that’s charmed guests since 1953, and Felix pairs bold, modern European flavors with dazzling skyline views.
The spa
The Roman-style pool opens onto a terrace with panoramic views, while the tranquil spa blends marble, bamboo, and water features into a serene retreat. Treatments range from Han Therapy massages to Margy Monte-Carlo facials and VOYA treatments crafted from organic ingredients. Aside from 14 crepuscular treatment rooms—six with harbour views, including the two private suites— you’ll find steam rooms, saunas, relaxation spaces and a well-equipped out gym. The hotel’s “Live Lived Best” program extends wellness into every stay, with in-room fitness options and mindful dining.
The neighborhood/area
The Peninsula commands one of Hong Kong’s most prized waterfront spots, facing the Star Ferry, the Avenue of Stars waterfront promenade, and the Hong Kong Museum of Art, right across the street. There’s no shortage of shopping options in the vicinity, including Chanel, Harry Winston, Graff, and unique brands such as Globe-Trotter, Chrome Hearts, and Jacob & Co. in the Peninsula Arcade, as well as luxury names and giant Japanese department stores along Nathan Road. You’re also within a hop-skip of the new West Kowloon Cultural District, home to the marvellous M+ Museum, as well as the equally impressive Hong Kong Palace Museum and Xiqu Centre for Cantonese opera.
The service
Round-the-clock red-carpet treatment delivered by warm, thoughtful, professional staff is what makes The Peninsula tick.
For families
Children are welcomed with signature teddy bears and mini-bathrobes, and can join Peninsula Academy like a chocolate bear-painting workshop, dim sum making, or farm and garden visits. Connecting rooms, roll-out beds, and babysitting services are also available.
Eco effort
An EarthCheck-certified pioneer, The Peninsula Hong Kong banned shark’s fin early on, introduced plastic-free amenities scented by a local perfumier, and halved its carbon footprint through technology and behavioural change.
Anything left to mention?
When Hollywood heartthrob Clark Gable stayed at the hotel while filming
Soldier of Fortune,
he confused the barman by requesting a screwdriver. The staff member promptly returned with the actual tool. Amused, the actor offered a gentle lesson in mixing the perfect vodka and orange juice—just one of countless tales this legendary hotel has collected over nearly a century.
