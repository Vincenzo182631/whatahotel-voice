Why book?
Because it’s one of the few places in Vail where you get big, apartment-style residences
and
full hotel amenities—ski valet, spa, rooftop pool—in the same building.
​
Set the scene
Vail has always been an American interpretation of the Alps, and The Arrabelle is one of the few properties that commits fully to the illusion. The façade layers creamy stucco with rough-cut stone, arched windows, and timber Juliet balconies, and roughly two dozen delicate, hand-painted alpine murals that add a subtle, storybook softness to its grand proportions.
Planted directly on Lionshead’s main square, the hotel sits exactly where the neighborhood converges, and its clock tower rises above the surrounding buildings, functioning as a practical landmark—it’s the easiest way to orient yourself in this part of town. Nearby ski shops and rental counters sit shoulder to shoulder with sunlit patios pouring Aperol spritzes, easy burger-and-beer joints, and a handful of polished dining rooms that fill with a well-heeled crowd once the ski boots come off. Small galleries and boutiques are in the mix, too—but so are the practical stops for hand warmers, forgotten goggles, and anything else you realize you need five minutes before the first chair.
The lobby functions as the hotel’s default meeting spot. The room is spacious, with high ceilings punctuated by exposed wood beams and iron chandeliers, and a range of comfy leather chairs. It’s where people pause by the large stone fireplace after coming in from the cold, wait for friends who’ve realized they forgot their lift ticket in the room, or sort out dinner plans before heading back out. That crowd runs the full age range but tends to be made up of people who treat visits to Vail as a tradition rather than a one-off. In the lobby, you’ll spot multigenerational families in coordinating ski suits, couples in town for milestone birthdays, and friend groups on their yearly ski trip. The style leans classic and mountain-literate—expect Ralph Lauren knits, Bogner one-pieces, and outerwear that's likely to far outprice the season lift ticket.
The backstory
Opened in 2010 as part of the Vail Square development, The Arrabelle was designed to echo the grand alpine hotels of Europe. It’s managed by Vail Resorts under the RockResorts banner, a collection known for slope-side locations and consistent, polished service.
The rooms
The Arrabelle has 81 accommodations, split between traditional hotel rooms and a collection of private residences that range from one to five bedrooms. The hotel rooms follow a consistent design ethos, with carved-wood headboards, gas fireplaces with hand-painted murals, thick drapery, and balconies that face either the Gore Range or Lionshead Square. The palette runs warm—creams, russets, forest greens—with layers of texture that nod to traditional mountain décor without ever feeling fussy or dated.
The residences are where things get more interesting. While they share the same architectural bones, each one has its own décor. Artwork, textiles, and even furniture selections vary, which keeps them from feeling like replicated luxury condos. Some lean darker and more classic; others feel lighter and more contemporary within the hotel’s overall European framework. These spaces also feature full kitchens, dining areas, living rooms with fireplaces, and laundry, making them especially appealing to families or groups who want a base that feels like home during a week’s stay.
Across the property are thoughtful touches, such as plush robes, high-quality linens, and minibars stocked with complimentary Nature Valley bars and Celsius electrolyte packets. Bathrooms are spacious, often with separate soaking tubs and showers, and there’s enough storage to actually unpack rather than live out of a suitcase.
Book a mountain-view room if you can; plaza-facing rooms are great for people-watching but can catch noise during peak season.
Food and drink
Tavern on the Square is the hotel’s anchor restaurant, opening directly onto Lionshead’s main plaza and functioning as both dining room and après headquarters. The menu sticks to elevated comfort options, such as bison chili, steak frites, and a substantial bison burger. Truffle fries seem to be almost obligatory by late afternoon. By 4 p.m., the bar is the place to be. Arrive early to avoid hovering and choose one of the signature cocktails—the Maple Old Fashion is particularly good.
Breakfast is offered as a prix fixe with options like made-to-order omelets, yogurt with granola and berries, and more substantial plates for those heading out early. It’s quick and reliable—the only friction point is that espresso-based drinks incur an additional charge, which feels slightly at odds with the bundled format where coffee is complimentary.
The spa
​Treatments focus on recovery and include things like deep-tissue massages for tired quads and oxygen facials for high-altitude dehydration. I opted for a ski-specific recovery treatment that targeted quads, calves, and lower back, and incorporated cupping to release fascia and ease muscle tightness.
Locker rooms include a steam room and a sauna, and guests tend to move between them before settling into the dimly lit lounge, where oversized chairs are spaced far enough apart to feel private.
The real standout, though, is the rooftop pool deck—heated year-round—with a hot tub facing the mountains.
The neighborhood/area
Staying at The Arrabelle means you’re in Lionshead, which many regulars choose over Vail Village for the access. The Eagle Bahn Gondola is right there, which means fewer logistics in the morning and a much easier mid-day reset if you want to pop back to your room. If proximity to the mountain is your top priority, this address makes a noticeable difference.
If you’re not clipping in, the Arrabelle sits within a short walk of Vail’s outdoor ice rink, where kids wobble under string lights in the late afternoon. For something quieter, the Gore Creek path runs just behind the plaza and offers a flat, scenic stretch for a morning walk.
If you forgot anything (or have a shopping itch to scratch), you’ll find outposts of outdoors brands like Patagonia and Helly Hansen, plus Vail Sports and Christy Sports for rentals and tuning, as well as last-minute goggles, hand warmers, or upgraded gloves when the temperature drops. Smaller boutiques dot the plaza with jewelry, art, and mountain-inspired gifts.
For classic ski-town energy, Bart & Yeti’s is a much-loved local institution serving Tex-Mex plates and strong margaritas, with a deck that fills quickly on sunny afternoons. Garfinkel’s handles the louder après crowd with pitchers and mountain views, while Blue Moose Pizza is the easy, no-reservations answer for families.
The service
Service here is white-glove without being too stiff. Ski valets make sure your gear is ready for first chair, and the front desk is efficient during the morning rush. The concierge can wrangle dinner reservations and arrange private ski instructors, though peak-season requests require advance notice. There’s also a courtesy shuttle that makes it easy to reach restaurants and bars that would otherwise be a trek from the hotel.
Accessibility
The hotel has elevators, accessible guest rooms, and ADA-compliant public spaces. It’s worth confirming specific room layouts in advance if you need roll-in showers or additional mobility features.
Anything left to mention?
Be sure to check out the sky bridge connecting The Arrabelle’s buildings—not only is the view of the ice skating rink and the mountain incredible, but they often do surprise pop-ups with a hot cocoa cart or cookies.
