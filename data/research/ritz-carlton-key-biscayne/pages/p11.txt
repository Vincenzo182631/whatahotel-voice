The Ritz-Carlton Key Biscayne, Miami: First In
A sun-washed tropical escape that’s quietly social, design-forward, and easygoing

Why book?
Because after a $100 million renovation, The Ritz-Carlton Key Biscayne, Miami feels back in sync with island life and the instincts of modern travelers: a sun-washed tropical escape that’s quietly social, design-forward, and easygoing–with the pulse of Miami just a bridge away.
Set the scene
Crossing the Rickenbacker Causeway from the Miami mainland, sapphire water flanks ribbons of highway on both sides. The skyline fades. The air shifts. It soon becomes clear you’ve landed on an island that’s worlds apart from Miami proper. At both ends of Key Biscayne, nature rules over protected areas draped in sea grapes and palm forests. Yet at the island’s core lies a hamlet of pricey homes and residential communities, one of which features a grand dame hotel on a prized stretch of shoreline: The Ritz-Carlton Key Biscayne, Miami.
From the moment the glass doors swing open, it’s evident you’re entering a new and improved version of the storied property—one that cost $100 million and brings this wildly popular Ritz outpost into a new chapter. A double-height foyer stretches upward, sculptural white chandeliers suspended overhead, intricately patterned wood panels climbing the walls before the open space rises to a third level. Your eyes are instantly drawn toward the cerulean waters of the Atlantic Ocean through triple-story glass panes, an immediate reminder that you are firmly in the sun-soaked tropics.
The lobby feels alive without tipping into chaos. Couples, families, and corporate groups move easily through the space, many of them repeat guests returning to see the transformation firsthand. Contemporary lounge tracks pipe softly through overhead speakers as conversations unfold in teal-toned seating clusters designed for lingering. Cream marble corridors branch off in either direction, leading to boutiques and guest rooms on one side, the spa and additional amenities on the other. At the center, the Rum Bar has been reintroduced as an open-plan anchor, now flowing naturally between the lobby and the rest of the property.
The crowd feeds into a tropical resort setting that invites relaxation. Daytime attire skews practical, heavy on athleisure, beach cover-ups, and tennis whites. Evenings remain easygoing outdoors, while indoor venues invite a more refined Miami sensibility, never at the expense of comfort. The overall feel is social without pretense or stuffiness, unmistakably shaped by island life.
The backstory
When it opened in 2001, The Ritz-Carlton Key Biscayne, Miami quickly established itself as one of the brand’s most coveted resorts during Ritz-Carlton’s peak era. Over the years, it received light updates, but nearly a quarter century later, the property was ready for something more substantial. Enter a $100 million transformation completed throughout 2025, led by global luxury developer Gencom in collaboration with design studios Hart Howerton, DesignAgency, and Chapi Chapo Design.
The result is both a reinvention and a recalibration. The hotel has been brought firmly into the present as a seaside retreat that appeals equally to modern families and couples, while maintaining the warmth that made it a favorite among longtime guests. It feels like a confident next chapter for the island escape.
The rooms
Guest rooms and suites have been nearly completely redone, corner to corner, and brought into a modern design vernacular. They’re intentionally restrained, allowing the views to take center stage. Strawberry-blonde woods, warm golden fixtures, and textured beige carpeting create a calm backdrop for floor-to-ceiling windows or sliding glass doors that open to the water. Tall, upholstered headboards and wood-framed televisions reinforce the continuity of the palette.
Suites introduce a touch more contrast. An outward-facing L-shaped sofa in a muted burgundy tone offsets the light woods and echoes the blues and greens beyond the glass. Bathrooms are generous, finished in gold-veined marble, with deep soaking tubs, separate showers, and Diptyque bath products. The overall effect is coastal and composed, with layered natural materials and colors drawn directly from sea and sand.
In all fairness, one part of this big-deal renovation does feel overlooked: guest room lighting. The rooms and suites still don’t have overhead illumination, and during times without natural daylight, the rooms can feel dark. It may seem minor to some but was one sticking point for me.
Food and drink
Luma, an Italian grill where house-made pastas share the spotlight with well-composed proteins from the Josper oven, anchors the dining program as the resort’s signature restaurant. Early favorites include spicy lobster tagliatelle with charred tomatoes and Calabrian chili, seared scallops “carbonara,” and the bone-in ribeye Romano. Aperitivo hour runs from 5 to 6 p.m. at the bar, ideal for a pre-dinner sip, and the restaurant spans ample indoor seating along with a spacious terrace for watching moonlight illuminate the bay. Back indoors, a chef’s table faces the open kitchen and offers a tasting menu, while a live-action pasta-making station allows guests to watch mafalde, bucatini, and other shapes come together using imported Italian 00 flour.
Just beyond Luma, the reimagined Rum Bar connects to the lobby. Despite its name, the bar operates as a globally minded cocktail destination with equal attention paid to other spirits, along with small bites and an impressive cigar selection. (Truth be told, however, the Rum Bar Daiquiri—made with Diplomático Plata rum, passion fruit, lime, palm sugar, and salt—was the clear favorite during my visit.)
Out by the beach, Paralía, open for both lunch and dinner, focuses on Aegean-inspired dishes meant for sharing, from traditional hot and cold meze and Greek village salad to more inventive touches like savory baklava. Nearly every table flaunts front-row ocean views, but if you want the firepit table, book in advance; it’s the most coveted seat on the property. Live music adds to the evening energy, particularly on weekends, with rotating performers across Paralía, Luma, the lobby, and the Rum Bar. Music typically runs Thursday through Saturday evenings.
For mornings into midday, Miami-based, healthy fast-casual favorite Pura Vida sits just off the lobby and draws a steady stream of locals and guests for green juices, smoothies, and wraps. Breakfast is also served daily at Luma, either à la carte or buffet-style. At lunchtime, Dune Beach Bar defines the resort’s vacation mode. It’s a no-reservations, toes-in-the-sand lounge serving burgers and ceviches, with DJs spinning on weekends from early afternoon.
The spa
The Ritz-Carlton Spa, Key Biscayne is a 30,000-square-foot retreat that feels deliberately removed from the energy of the rest of the resort. Sixteen treatment rooms, including couples suites, a full-service salon, and several restorative lounges—including a spa garden—form the core of the experience. A new, high-tech oceanfront fitness center makes it surprisingly easy to keep a routine, even on vacation, while Miami-based Tremble operates a dedicated studio with reformer Pilates-focused programming. Complimentary fitness sessions take place on the grand lawn, ranging from yoga to body sculpting. Tennis, pickleball, and padel courts at the Cliff Drysdale Tennis Center continue the property’s long-standing connection to the sport, which stems from Key Biscayne hosting the Miami Open for three decades—until 2018—and becoming a destination for tennis pros.
The neighborhood / area
Key Biscayne is a barrier island across from downtown Miami. Since the construction of the Rickenbacker Causeway in 1947, the island has evolved alongside the city while maintaining its own identity. Surrounding waters remain protected, preserving coral reefs and mangrove ecosystems that lend the island a sense of quiet separation.
In theory, Key Biscayne presents a rare combination of proximity and escape. Downtown Miami is about 20 minutes away, yet the island feels self-contained and intentionally slow-paced. For many visitors, arriving means never leaving the resort. If you’re hoping to immerse yourself in Miami’s nightlife or South Beach scene, I’d say this isn’t the place to stay. But if resort life is the goal (with no more than one or two Miami outings), this is exactly where you want to be. Still, leaving property for an excursion to Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park—home to a mile-long beach, South Florida’s oldest lighthouse, and extensive walking and biking trails—is a must, especially for first-timers.
The service
Following its December 2025 reopening, many longtime staff members returned, joined by new faces energized by the property’s next chapter. For a hotel fresh from a major overhaul, service feels confident and well-coordinated. Attention is consistent without feeling overwhelming.
For families
The resort has long been a favorite with families, and the updates only strengthen that appeal. Ritz Kids offers half- and full-day programming centered on culture, exploration, nature, and responsibility, with activities like beach walks and environmental learning rather than routine crafts. The expansive family pool area includes a splash pad, shallow zones, and plenty of float options. Nearby is Scoop, a gelateria created in partnership with Dolce Vita, including a mobile component housed in a repurposed Fiat. Families also gravitate toward Stefano’s, a permanent wood-fired pizza truck steps from the pool.
A large oceanfront lawn creates a natural buffer between family spaces and the adults-only pool and hot tub, ensuring clear separation—and enough distance that family pool noise stays contained. The full-service beach area is popular with both families and couples, and across pools and the beach stretch, expect attractive new loungers and furnishings.
Accessibility
All ADA standards are met.
Eco effort
Single-use plastics have been eliminated across the resort. Aluminum bottles and cups replace plastic, even poolside, supported by a robust back-of-house sorting and recycling system. Water refill stations are positioned throughout the property, encouraging guests to reuse bottles and tumblers.
Anything left to mention?
The Ritz-Carlton Club costs extra, but it’s a smart splurge. Access comes with a large Club Lounge serving food throughout the day, a dedicated concierge, and Club-level rooms on the ninth floor. You’ll still want to spend time in the resort’s restaurants and bars, but the lounge works well for a quiet breakfast, a nightcap, or a pause between plans when you want a calmer setting (and the best-of-the-best Ritz service).
