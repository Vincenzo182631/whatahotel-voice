Can’t-miss feature:
Getting a drink in the “hidden” Gaudí Secret Garden by St-Germain, the rooftop cocktail bar that feels like a magical, secluded oasis with excellent views of the Barcelona skyline, including the Sagrada Familia. Go during sunset for a particularly photo-worthy experience.
In a nutshell
: If you want to moonlight as royalty, there's no better place to bed down in Barcelona than at El Palace. Classic luxury abounds at this hotel, from the Beaux Arts architecture to the white-glove service with a smile. It's no wonder that this property is known for having hosted monarchs, aristocrats, public figures, artists, and more.

Rooms
From the second you set foot in El Palace—entering through the massive front doors, opened for you by the white-gloved doormen, who then guide you to the opulent check-in desk where you'll receive a pair of old-timey room keys that jingle around in your bag—you'll know you've entered the upper echelon of Barcelona hospitality. The 120 rooms and suites are all lavishly decorated with rich fabrics, swanky chandeliers, heavy drapes, and mostly muted colors. Room types include Classic Rooms, Superior Rooms, and Two-Bedroom Family Rooms, as well as a slew of suite options, from Junior Suites to Three-Bedroom Family Suites that sleep up to seven. Spring for one of the Art Suites if you're looking for a particularly special stay, as I did. Each is inspired by a reputable prior guest and comes with private airport transfers, butler service, and a Champagne welcome. There are also Roman-style mosaic bathtubs for an Instagram picture worth a thousand words. I haven't quite yet reached the stage in my life where I own my own palace, but a stay at this glittering property is as close as I've ever felt.
Jamie Spain
Jamie Spain
Food and drink
I'd skip a meal at The Rooftop Garden (the experience was subpar when I went), grab drinks at the attached Gaudí Secret Garden by St-Germain (since the rooftop delivers far more on views than bites), and head downstairs to the restaurant in the Grand Hall (aptly named El Hall) when your stomach starts rumbling. It was shockingly empty when we dined there, but the food was excellent—parts of the menu looked similar to what was offered on the rooftop, but when the food arrived, it was leaps and bounds better. The beef tenderloin and salmon were exceptional, but the pesto gnocchi and tempura artichokes were what really surprised us, since they were such unexpected hits. While you don't have a rooftop view, the mix of Belle Époque and Art Deco architecture in the Grand Hall more than makes up for it.
The crown jewel of the property is undoubtedly Amar Barcelona, led by Michelin-starred Sevillian chef Rafael Zafra. Featuring Mediterranean foundations, plenty of seafood, and every type of caviar imaginable, this is more than just dinner—it's art. We enjoyed a decadent tasting menu, and hits included the sliced tuna belly, sautéed clams and portobello mushrooms, classic Catalonian Santa Pau white beans with tuna noten and Iberian pancetta, and whole fish prepared tableside alongside a decadent sauce.
A daily breakfast can be found at El Jardín, which serves local favorites, a slew of juices, and rotating hot dishes.
Amenities and experiences
My biggest regret from my stay was not making it down for afternoon tea in the Grand Hall. I'm a big fan of traditional British tea services, and the one at El Palace looked incredible, complete with scones, sweet treats, and finger sandwiches in a vast, mirrored space.
Mayan Luxury Spa, the on-site wellness space, offers a variety of treatments, massages, facials, and rituals inspired by Mayan culture. There's also a sauna and a small rooftop pool.
What to do nearby
The hotel's location, in the heart of Barcelona in the Eixample neighborhood, is excellent for first-time visitors like myself, and just as wonderful for old hands like my travel companions, who had little interest in the tourist traps and were far more invested in immersing themselves in the day-to-day in each neighborhood. It's just two blocks from the luxury shopping and sightseeing on Passeig de Gràcia, and within walking distance of the Plaça de Catalunya, Catedral de Barcelona, Arc de Triomf, and the Museu Picasso de Barcelona. You're also a few minutes away from a couple of train and bus stops, and surrounded by great places to eat, drink, and shop.
Jamie Spain
The backstory
Opened in 1919 as the Ritz of Barcelona, this property has over a decade of excellent service under its belt. It has since been rebranded as El Palace Barcelona, but guests can still expect the same stately service and decor. Over the years, the property has seen a number of famous faces, from Duke Ellington and Whitney Houston to Salvador Dalí, Carlos Ruiz Zafón, and Sophia Loren.
Insider tip
If you can swing it, stay in one of the El Palace suites, which are all unique and inspired by different well-known figures. The Ronnie Wood suite, for example, transports you to a unique world that intertwines Art Deco with the Rolling Stones' rock and roll.
Jamie Spain
