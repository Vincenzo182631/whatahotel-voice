Miraflores Park, A Belmond Hotel, Lima
A see-and-be-seen spot for luxury travelers who want all the amenities.

Reviewed by
Megan Spurrell
Why book?
This is the go-to hotel for every touring musician or celebrity in Lima, thanks to its reliably five-star service and excellent amenities.
Set the scene
Located in my favorite corner of Miraflores—quiet, leafy, and residential—you're well situated right on the malecón (the lush, clifftop park along the Pacific), across from a small park where locals do morning workouts and walk their dogs, and just near the new footbridge to Barranco, which makes it easy to pop over to the neighborhood's great restaurants. The hotel is a natural jumping off point for international travelers hopping on Belmond's circuit of the country—the brand also has hotels in Cusco, within the Sacred Valley, and just beside Machu Picchu, plus a luxe train running through the Southern countryside—and you'll be able to tell whether these polished guests are at the start of their trip, starry eyed and voraciously asking questions about the destination, or at the tail end of a robust journey, slowly savoring drinks in the lobby bar before packing it up and heading home. There's also a polish to the space that feels primed for business travelers, though I usually see more of them coming in and out of the JW Marriott down the road. When somebody big is in town, and particularly when they are staying here, you'll know, as paparazzi (and plenty of Peruvian teenagers, phones outstretched) will be waiting across the street for a glimpse of Dua Lipa or Shakira.
The backstory
When Miraflores Park originally opened in 1996 under a different brand (Orient-Express), it was one of the first high-rise hotels to position itself along the city's scenic coastline. It was brought under the Belmond umbrella in 2014 and has continued to be a grand mainstay in the city, particularly for luxury travelers who want all the amenities (24/7 gym, spa, pool, top-tier restaurant, and bar) right at their fingertips. I've traveled to Peru regularly over the past 10 years, and I'll admit—for a while, I discounted it, given the shiny blue-tinted glass exterior that looks more dated than what lies within. Inside, though, the team goes above and beyond to meet international travelers' expectations of a luxurious stay, and the space itself keeps up, with a postcard-worthy lobby and a restaurant that draws even locals for special brunches and dinners. A current series of upgrades, including those to the famous ocean-view rooftop pool, is underway, ahead of Peru's summer.
The rooms
All of the rooms are treated as suites, with most offering either a balcony, an in-room sauna, or a terrace with a plunge pool, depending on the category you spring for. Inside the presidential suite (naturally, the choice for headlining musicians passing through), it's clear why high-profile travelers choose the Belmond as their base. Sure, you could go to the gym upstairs, but there is also a set of weights and other workout gear right in the room; a private plunge pool means you don't have to rub elbows with anyone else on the rooftop. And in most rooms, a varying-sized seating area allows guests to pop in for a cocktail and enjoy the view. The vibe across categories is fairly classic: Picture crisp white and taupe as the main color palette with friendly pops of color (deep blue tiles in the plunge pool, a chartreuse velvet sofa, a red, white, and blue pinstriped daybed on the terrace). It's all very classic, even reminiscent of a Southern California aesthetic to me (slightly Newport Beach), without leaning too heavily into coastal or Peruvian clichés, which are easy pitfalls in a city like Lima.
One word to the wise: This is one of those hotels with impossible-to-understand light switches. Which, to be fair, is most hotels, but I nearly lost my mind trying to get the bathroom dark at bedtime. Next time, I would ask for a clear overview when being shown the room so I wouldn't futz around at 10 p.m. after a long, wine-filled dinner.
Food and drink
The main restaurant, Tragaluz, is a destination in Lima, and approximately one bite into my dinner, it was clear why. Yes, this is fine dining, and yes, this is a city with some of the world's very best chefs and tasting menus, so it seems counterintuitive to stay in your hotel, but Tragaluz has made its name for a reason. This isn't the type of luxury where tweezers are used to place your napkin on the table, nor a conceptual exploration of the country's flora and fauna (which is to say, it's a different experience than at the bucket-list restaurants like Central, Maido, and Merito, where you're probably headed as well). It's a little more country club, without the stuffiness—you could just as easily bring your grandmother, a colleague, or a group of girlfriends. The music plays just loud enough to set a vibe without interrupting your conversation, and the lights are bright enough to read your menu while still feeling cozy. As for the food, there was a take on conchas a la parmesana, a popular Peruvian seafood dish, that was here reimagined with a Parmesan foam atop fresh, perfectly cooked scallops from just down the coast. Local producers also make flavorful cheeses and send over carefully procured mushrooms that explode with flavor. The somm, perhaps one of the most passionate I've ever met, was giddy opening up beautiful bottles, telling us about each as if it were his firstborn. It was a fabulous meal, and perhaps I shouldn't have been surprised to come down for breakfast and find it was just as effort-filled and tasty. (The 11th-floor restaurant, where breakfast is typically served, is currently under renovation.)
The spa
I didn't get a chance to pop into the spa during my stay, though trust that they have one. Zest Spa uses organic products from the Amazon, and treatments range from Reiki sessions to deep-tissue massages to anti-aging facials. There is a gym up by the pool, though it's a little bare-bones.
The neighborhood/area
Miraflores is the home base for most travelers for a reason. It's a straight shot along the Costa Verde coastline to the airport, and the wider neighborhood is full of restaurants, bars, and shopping. But in this exact section of Miraflores, the traffic is thinner, the noises are dimmer, and you can appreciate the sweeping ocean views this city is known for.
The service
The service is reason alone to bed down at the Belmond. Because, like the best service, it shows up in unexpected ways. When I stepped out for a money run, I returned to find cold towels and water waiting out front with the security guard before I'd even set foot back in the lobby. While viewing my room, I mentioned that I liked to have a lot of water on hand, and the team immediately sent up six (yes, six!) bottles of purified water. Removing the need to ask again was so appreciated. And everyone was, unsurprisingly, seriously kind.
For families
As a big hotel with thick walls, I wouldn't be surprised to see families here. It's not so intimate that you need to worry about bothering other guests, and children will love the park out front and the pool on the roof. At Christmas time, the entire lobby is decorated with gingerbread houses and other sweet treats that kids will enjoy.
Accessibility
There's a ramp at the main entrance, and elevators up to the rooms.
Anything left to mention?
If you're planning a trip in 2026, reach out ahead of time to find out what may be under construction as part of the renovation. It didn't impact my December 2025 stay (in the sense that there was no noise or visible construction), though I did miss out on taking a dip in that famous rooftop pool, which was closed. Luckily, I was staying in a suite with my own plunge.
