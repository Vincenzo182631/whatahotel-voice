Grand Hotel Timeo, A Belmond Hotel, Taormina
Old-world grandeur with postcard-perfect views in Taormina's first—and arguably most luxurious—grand hotel.

Reviewed by
Hannah Chubb
Why book?
For a historic stay opposite the famous Greek Theatre with the best views of Mount Etna in town, plus antique artwork and gobsmacking interiors that will have you stopping every step to take another picture.
Set the scene
While deciding where to embark on a minimoon after our October 2025 wedding in Tuscany, my husband and I may or may not have combed through this “
Where to Travel for Hot Weather in October
” story before landing on Sicily. We had visited the Italian island a few times over the years, because my husband has family near Palermo, but never ventured east before—naturally, Taormina called our name. We’d watched season two of
The White Lotus
, after all.
After spending a couple of days in the beach town of Cefalù, we arrived at Taormina-Giardini Station by train and began a winding taxi journey up the mountain to Grand Hotel Timeo, one of two Belmond properties in Taormina. (While Timeo is set up in the hills, overlooking the Ionian,
Belmond Villa Sant’Andrea
, a 13-minute drive away, sits at the bottom of the mountain, on the Bay of Mazzarò.) The pedestrian street leading up to the property was lined with shops selling Trinacria trinkets and ceramic pine cones, both symbols of good fortune in Sicily, and stands serving granitas, gelato, and greasy arancini. At the end of the street, we came to a fork in the road: to the left, the gates to the iconic Greek Theatre; to the right, the front door to our home-away-from-home for the next two days. We hung a Roger, allowed the doormen to whisk away all our worldly possessions, peeped the Dior-branded Vespa perched by the front door, and quickly began to understand what all the Belmond fuss is about.
A step into the lobby is like a step back in time to Sicily’s golden age: Roman, Arabic, Greek, Spanish, and Norman influences shine through in the design choices, all old-world grandeur in pretty, 21st-century packaging. Glide right, towards the sun, and you’ll come face to face with Ristorante Timeo and Bar Timeo, which bleed out onto the terrace overlooking Mount Etna and the Naxos Bay. I don’t know if it was the post-wedding sleep debt or just the pure, unadulterated beauty of the glittering Ionian, but the view brought tears to my eyes when I got my first glimpse. When we peered downwards, the property’s panoramic pool and six acres of lush gardens also came into the picture.
The backstory
Timeo started as a simple guesthouse back in 1850 when a man named Don Francesco La Floresta began renting out rooms in his family home to guests. It was put on the map, however, by Otto Geleng, a Prussian baron and watercolor painter who first booked a room in the early 1860s, inspired by the landscapes that continue to draw creatives to this day. As the story goes, Otto brought his paintings back home to Germany and was met with criticism—people didn’t believe that the sparkling shoreline, snow-dusted volcano, and colorful cliffside villas they saw in his brushstrokes could possibly exist in the real world. To save face, he invited a handful of haters to visit Taormina to see the beauty with their own eyes. This is credited as being the catalyst for the tourism boom in town—with Taormina becoming a stop on the
grand tour
—and Timeo was officially transformed into a hotel in 1863. In the years since, it’s remained an artistic haven for those coming to paint, draw, photograph, and write about the landscapes, as well as famous faces like Elizabeth Taylor, Audrey Hepburn, Bob Dylan, and Elton John, many of whom visited while attending the film festival at the Greek Theatre.
The rooms
I was out of tears by the time we reached our room—a sea-view suite with a terrace straight from my Sicilian dreams—but I was certainly rendered speechless. Much like the rest of the hotel, the 40 rooms and suites on the property (almost all of which have terraces) are sophisticated yet comfortable, with ornate gold-framed artwork, candelabra sconces, clean white linens, and floor-length curtains tickled by the breeze. Our massive bathroom had a soaking tub, dual sinks, and a rainshower (with full-sized Acqua di Parma products), all enveloped by so much marble, I felt like I was rinsing off at the Parthenon.
The soft drinks in the minibar were complimentary, which is always a nice touch, and the staff left several treats for us during turndown every night, including a lavender pillow mist and two sets of Tom Àdam pajamas we still wear while lounging around the apartment. We slurped tea out of the Ginori 1735 Oriente Italiano teacups in bed every morning, but I’ll never forget the nights we spent plopped on the wrought-iron balcony set, sipping Champagne out of crystal glasses, watching the sparkling sun turn to twinkling lights around the crescent-shaped bay, and absorbing the reality that Taormina is indeed, somehow, not a figment of the imagination.
Food and drink
My husband and I are huge foodies, and Timeo did not let us down. While Timeo Grill, the poolside eatery, was closed for the season, we sampled our way through dishes and tipples at the other three outposts: Bar Timeo, a moody all-day lounge once frequented by Truman Capote and Tennessee Williams; Ristorante Timeo, where an unbelievable complimentary breakfast is served every morning, and alfresco lunches and dinners feature fresh fish, Mediterranean mains, and Etna wines; and Otto Geleng, the Michelin-starred fine dining spot named after the artist that brought the hotel into the spotlight back in the 19th century.
As a picky eater, tasting menus always give me a tinge of anxiety, but our three-hour evening at 16-seat Otto Geleng did nothing but please my persnickety palate. I knew we were in for a treat when we sat down and were handed a water menu with H
2
O options from Sicily, Tuscany, the Dolomites, Iceland, France, Poland, and beyond, each with notes on their fixed residue and sodium levels. The following dishes—and wine pairings—from executive chef Roberto Toro were similarly thoughtful, with creative twists on Mediterranean fare. The bread service and the Jerusalem artichoke changed my life, and I’ll leave it at that.
The spa
If wellness is your endgame, Timeo can play with the best of them. In addition to the stunning pool and state-of-the-art fitness facilities (personal trainers are available upon request), Timeo has a standout standalone spa with three single treatment rooms and one couples treatment room, plus a hair salon.
What made my stay truly exceptional was my visit to Le Jardin des Rêves Dior, a pop-up wellness experience that was introduced in 2023 in honor of Timeo’s 150th birthday. Housed in leaf-green treehouses perched amongst the cypresses in the garden, the two private treatment spaces were designed by Maria Grazia Chiuri, creative director of Dior, and feature the fashion house’s iconic branding, making them prime Insta bait. All of the treatments use
Dior skincare products
, including the massages—I took one for the team and tested the signature Constellation massage, where the therapist went nice and slow while working out every knot I didn’t know I had.
The neighborhood/area
It’s shocking how quiet and removed Timeo feels, considering the Greek Theatre, one of the most popular tourist attractions in all of Sicily, is its next-door neighbor. I recommend visiting as early in the morning as you can bear (it opens at 9 a.m., and you can find tickets
here
), because there’s not much shade as you walk around, and Taormina can get
hot
, even during shoulder season. Worth it, considering the ancient amphitheater was built in the third century BC.
You’re also in a great location to get to other Taormina hot spots like Corso Umberto (a 10-minute walk), the Duomo di Taormina (12-minute walk), and Isola Bella (13-minute drive). Bam Bar, known for having the best granita in town, is just down the street, and we found an incredible charcuterie spot, La Bottega del Formaggio, with the best meatballs I’ve ever had after a five-minute stroll.
The service
The staff members here were so kind, caring, and accommodating, I almost felt like I was back with my husband’s “blood is thicker than water” Sicilian family members. From the front desk to the spa and Otto Geleng, everyone seemed to love their jobs—American tipping culture be damned. They really went above and beyond one afternoon when my husband and I came into the lobby with grocery bags full of snacks and wine, and asked if the front desk could hold them for a few hours because we were late to our next excursion. Instead, they offered to bring everything to our room—and when we returned that night, everything was organized and waiting for us.
For families
While definitely designed for luxury-seeking adults, we did spot a few little guests during our stay, including a baby so fresh there’s no way it had celebrated its two-month birthday yet. Staff members were sweet to them all, slinging kids' meals with a smile at the restaurant and playing peekaboo when the newborn started getting cranky. If you’re traveling with your kiddos in tow, ask about the Children’s Welcome Kit, babysitting services, children and teen treatments at the spa, and the supervised Smile Club at Villa Sant’Andrea—a quick shuttle ride away, where little ones can play and participate in activities like cannoli making and seashell hunting—which Timeo guests are free to take advantage of throughout their stay.
Eco effort
Belmond works with EarthCheck, a sustainability advisory group, to measure and manage its environmental impact within its properties around the world. Timeo’s departure gift, given to guests at turndown on their last night, is a nod to the property’s commitment to sustainability: a handmade bookmark made by local female artisans using plantable paper with seeds that will sprout Sicilian flowers. (I, for one, can’t bear to rip mine up, so it’s still being used to hold pages!)
Accessibility
The property is wheelchair accessible—though access is limited in the gardens—with elevators between floors. Staff are eager to assist and accommodate wherever possible.
Anything left to mention?
Take advantage of the shuttle service down to Belmond Villa Sant’Andrea when you’re ready to soak up some rays. The sister property sits right on a private beach along the Bay of Mazzarò, and Timeo guests get full access to all the amenities—including umbrella-shaded loungers with beachside service. I highly recommend heading over midmorning for peak sun and indulging in a waterfront lunch at one of the three alfresco restaurants. Take it from me: the spaghetti with clams at Ristorante Sant’Andrea is worth the trek.
