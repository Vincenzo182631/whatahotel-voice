Las Alcobas, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Mexico City
Las Alcobas earns its reputation as one of Mexico City’s top hotels, delivering on design, amenities, service, and especially food.

In a nutshell
: Quiet luxury is the throughline at this fan-favorite hotel in the upscale neighborhood of Polanco. Think fresh lilies in the lobby, in-room orchids, and imported Italian bed linens, as well as a small but perfectly formed on-site spa. The central spiral staircase in the common area, with its ultra-plush carpeting, steals the show.
Lauren Cocking
Lauren Cocking
Rooms
Las Alcobas offers a moody-luxe atmosphere with a touch of Mexican influence throughout its 35 surprisingly spacious suites and three penthouses, where dark rosewood and fresh, fragile orchids take center stage. Despite having Canadian design duo Yabu Pushelberg at the helm of the interior decor, the details remain remarkably rooted in Mexico, from the hefty Cathedral-inspired relief door that hides a well-stocked minibar of Mexican snacks and spirits (alcohol comes at an extra fee, though, unless you’re in a penthouse suite) to the hand-embroidered artisanal runners at the foot of the beds.
However, Mexico steps aside to make way for luxury Italian linens and Bvlgari bathroom products (though you
can
opt for a bar of Mexican-made soap from a literal platter left in the bathrooms), as well as Japanese-style electronic bidets. Speaking of the bathroom: Be sure to book a complimentary aromatherapy bath service in your in-room jet tub. And definitely snag a Corner Panoramic room to appreciate the curved floor-to-ceiling windows—the double-thickness glass means you’ll barely hear a peep from the busy avenue below.
Lauren Cocking
Lauren Cocking
Food and drink
You’ll start your stay with a refreshing welcome drink on arrival. But breakfast is where the dining comes into its own. There’s just one on-site restaurant: Anatol, led by chef Rodrigo del Valle. Here, the dark wood and plush furnishings carry through from the rooms into the dining area, where tall windows leave tables pooled in light for the guests-only breakfast and brunch service, where you can choose from à la carte or continental (the latter comes included in the price of your stay). Don’t miss the baked-on-site breads—the buttery scone is a standout.
For lunch or dinner, the restaurant is open to all, and the dishes mosey into Italian-influenced territory, with a few rogue additions such as Korean-style chicken sandwiches, though the menu changes seasonally.
Amenities and experiences
Aurora Spa, a petite two-cabin wellness center on the second floor, is the principal on-site amenity at Las Alcobas. It's the perfect spot to retreat to for a facial or massage (prebooking required). Sure, this spa might not have the expansive hydrotherapy circuits of bigger all-inclusives, but you’re in the heart of the Mexican capital, after all, not Cancún. Even so, the adjustable massage tables (with built-in warmers) provide an incredibly cozy experience and echo the quietly luxurious amenities found in the rooms.
Otherwise, the biggest amenity here is the location—Las Alcobas puts you at the ideal jumping-off point for exploring Mexico City from its central spot in Polanco.
What to do nearby
Las Alcobas has a privileged position on one of the swankiest avenues in one of the swankiest neighborhoods in the Mexican capital, meaning you’re within a stone’s throw of some of the city’s top luxury shopping destinations—featuring both international and Mexican brands—as well as the notable Soumaya Museum with its dragon-scale exterior. To the south of the neighborhood sits the sprawl of Chapultepec Park, replete with running trails, modern art museums, and the massive National Museum of Anthropology. If you’re overwhelmed with options, the hotel concierge is always on hand to help you plan a curated day out.
If the on-site restaurant doesn’t tempt you, you’re just half a block from cult-favorite Mexico City diner Klein’s, as well as within walking distance of the IYKYK tacos de cochinita pibil from Taquería Turix.
The backstory
Once an apartment block that owner Samuel Leizorek purchased in 2001, it wasn’t until nine years later that Leizorek opened Las Alcobas, a labor-of-love hotel that’s managed to maintain its own individuality despite being incorporated into the coveted Marriott Luxury Collection. In fact, it’s one of the few luxury hotels in the group that remains an owner-managed property, and you can feel it in the details, right down to those aforementioned Italian linens.
The name, Las Alcobas (meaning “the alcoves” in English), is a callback to the location where the Spanish once stored their treasures; here, the philosophy is that the invitados (a word that best translates to “guests” in English) are the treasures you'll find inside.

Insider tip
It may sound like a small detail, but the super-plush carpets on the spiral staircase boost the luxe factor significantly, making it preferable to pad down to breakfast rather than take the elevator, soundtracked by the trickling of the ground-floor water feature.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
No, not really; this property feels adults-only to its core, and children under 12 aren’t allowed.
Accessibility:
No, the property isn’t fully ADA-accessible, though some rooms have accessibility
features
.
Wellness:
There’s a small on-site spa with two treatment rooms on the second floor.
Loyalty group/hotel brand:
Marriott Luxury Collection
