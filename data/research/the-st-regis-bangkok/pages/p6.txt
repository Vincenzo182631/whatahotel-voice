Why book?
Sophisticated style meets white-glove service in the center of bustling Bangkok, complete with 24-hour private butlers, a world-class medical spa, and spacious suites overlooking the Royal Bangkok Sports Club.
Set the scene
The St. Regis is a refined respite from Ratchaprasong, Bangkok’s upscale shopping district just outside the front door. The hotel draws an older, distinguished clientele: CEOs in sharply tailored suits conduct business meetings in the lobby lounge while Thai socialites in designer dresses and high heels carry Chanel shopping bags from the glam Central Embassy mall nearby. Many extravagant weddings are held in the Astor Ballroom, so you may also see hi-so families sporting silk chut Thai sets and artisanal clutches.
The backstory
The St. Regis has a long-standing legacy. New York’s affluent Astor family—considered Gilded Age royalty—founded the St. Regis on Fifth Avenue in 1904. Today, there are more than 60 St. Regis hotels within Marriott International's luxury portfolio, including the Bangkok debut in 2011. All St. Regis properties uphold the brand’s timeless traditions and gold standards, from the bespoke butler service and elegant afternoon tea to the sunset Champagne sabrage ceremony and the signature fragrance, Caroline's Four Hundred, that wafts around the common areas.
The rooms
Space may be a luxury in Bangkok, but it’s a given at the St. Regis. Within its sleek 47-story tower, the hotel houses 228 rooms, including 51 sprawling suites and an extravagant three-bedroom penthouse. Everything here feels high-quality. Suites have plush carpets, full-size living rooms, and marble bathrooms. A favorite is the Caroline Astor corner suite, with floor-to-ceiling windows facing the 18-hole golf course and city skyline (yes, even from the bathtub). Plus, the beds are an absolute dream. Their 300-thread-count Egyptian cotton linens, feather-down comforters, and personalized pillow menus guarantee a good night’s rest.
Food and drink
There’s a surprising number of dining concepts spread throughout the hotel. On the ground floor, you’ll find The Lounge café, Zuma sushi bar and Japanese izakaya, and the Michelin-starred IGNIV Bangkok by Swiss chef Andreas Caminada. Come hungry—this fine-dining restaurant has a hearty 19-course tasting menu, focused on shared plates that change seasonally. If you don’t have room for dessert, you can select a dozen sweets from the candy wall to box up and savor later.
The 12th floor is the main hub—reception is here as well as VIU restaurant, the St. Regis Bar, and the Drawing Room, where afternoon tea is served. You’re spoiled for choice at VIU’s impressive breakfast buffet, especially during the seafood-focused Sunday brunch. Stations are organized by country (Thailand, Japan, China, India) and theme, like noodles and satay. Standout bites include pandan sticky rice and imported French cheeses from Les Frères Marchand. The rest of the day, VIU serves Italian dishes, inspired by chef Matteo Fontana’s family recipes. The set lunch is a deal, with entrées like braised duck ravioli in a pumpkin puree and morel mushroom sauce. At sunset, head to the St. Regis Bar to sip a Siam Mary (a twist on the St. Regis staple Bloody Mary) made with Thai chili, basil, and lemongrass.
The spa
The state-of-the-art medical spa, recently renamed
Bangkok LIFE Longevity & Biohacking Hub
, covers 16,000 square feet on the 17th floor. Here, a crew of skilled clinicians builds a program based on your body. But these aren’t your typical treatments: cryotherapy, Emsculpt body contouring, Cooltech fat freezing, IV drips, hyperbaric oxygen chambers, laser therapy, and the bestselling Thermage facelift. Even without an appointment, guests can unwind in the two-story relaxation room, hydrotherapy circuit, and hammam. The spa will soon add an infrared sauna and Himalayan salt room, too. Tip: It’s open until 10 p.m. and much quieter at night.
Across the hall, the 24/7 fitness center features TechnoGym equipment, cardiovascular monitoring systems, and a Thai boxing ring for 1:1 training. After breaking a sweat, kick back by the zen outdoor infinity pool. The attentive staff carries complimentary sunscreen, cold towels, and mango juice shots straight to your lounge chair.
The neighborhood/area
The St. Regis sits along Ratchadamri Road, a major thoroughfare in the shopping precinct. But you won’t have to walk far to get around. Take the elevator to the second floor, where a bridge leads to a BTS Skytrain station. This efficient metro connects the whole city, without having to brave the chaotic, crowded streets. Just down the road (or a single stop on the tram) are swanky malls like Central Embassy, Siam Paragon, and Centralworld, one of the largest shopping centers in Thailand. For a peaceful escape, stroll through Lumphini Park, 10 minutes south, or play a round of golf at the neighboring Royal Bangkok Sports Club.
The service
Every room comes with a polite butler, who anticipates your every need. When you check in, they’ll unpack your suitcase, press your clothes, and hang them neatly in the walk-in closet. If you’re landing after a long flight, the butler may surprise you by running a hot bubble bath and preparing chamomile tea to soothe your jet lag. For first-time visitors, the concierge can create a tailored itinerary of travel recommendations.
For families
While you won’t see many kids around the St. Regis, there are two-bedroom family suites with living rooms, dining areas, and kitchenettes that offer plenty of space to sprawl out.
Accessibility
Elevators are the main way to navigate the hotel, and there is an accessible Deluxe Room with a roll-in shower for easy access.
Anything left to mention?
There’s always something happening at the St. Regis. The Lounge hosts rotating art exhibits, spotlighting thought-provoking pieces like ceramics from Thai artist Bathma Kaew-Ngok and showstopping floral arrangements by Naomi Daimaru. It also has a collaboration with Singapore roastery Sarnies, serving decadent cakes and specialty coffee (try the crème brûlée honey latte). You can even choose the coffee beans you want to brew. Upstairs, seasonal pop-ups are common, especially around the holidays, when the St. Regis bar transforms into a Swiss alpine chalet, with Christmas trees, wooden skis, and a gondola you can drink inside.
