The St. Regis Cap Cana Resort: First In
The luxury brand's first foray in the Dominican Republic will satisfy your desire to be treated like a VIP

Why book?
This tranquil
Dominican Republic
resort features thoughtful design, a private beach, spectacular pools, and high-end dining. Come here if you want to get away from the crowds and experience quiet luxury in a refined atmosphere.
Set the scene
Located on the southern end of the resort enclave of Cap Cana, St. Regis’s debut in the Dominican Republic is the kind of high-end resort where you're as likely to see newlyweds on their honeymoon as you are well-heeled couples or single travelers relaxing by the pool or heading out early to play few holes of golf, as well as the odd celeb or dignitary. In fact, within two weeks of its opening, the St. Regis hosted the president of the Dominican Republic.
A squad of white-shirted staff await you at the gargantuan doors to welcome you into the lofty, expansive lobby. Chapi Design, responsible for several
Ritz-Carlton
and
St. Regis
properties, and the architectural firm Acebal Canney were enlisted to create a sumptuous resort that honors the unique characteristics of the Dominican Republic. While you’ll find the usual luxe materials (marble and leather), it's the local touches that ground the design: tropical plants, patterned brickwork, and curved ceilings adorned with wickerwork typical of the island. Beyond the monumental reception area, the indoor-outdoor resort maintains a serene atmosphere with minimalist decor, shaded corridors flanked by leafy gardens, and plenty of secluded sitting areas. Artworks by Dominican artists are peppered throughout the building, lending the resort a gallery-like vibe.
The rambling, low-slung building, containing 200 rooms, including 36 suites, is easy to get lost in. No matter—there’s always a staff member nearby with a smile to direct you. From afar, the structure and its roof of greenery (where you’ll also find a pool), blends into the surroundings. Its two wings (one is for private residences) hug the central outdoor area, with its manicured landscaping, placid pools, and a private beach. As you languorously wander from bed to breakfast, from the beach to the pool, from lunch to cocktails and dinner, you can relax knowing that everything will be taken care of.
The backstory
Upon your arrival, a member of the staff greets you with “Welcome to the House of Astor,” lest you forget the legendary history of the St. Regis name. The New York landmark was built by real-estate magnate John Jacob Astor IV in 1904, and while the brand has changed hands over the years and modernized, the same luxury that the Gilded Age families expected is on display. Suspended in front of a breathtaking cascade of water is the centerpiece of the reception area: a dramatic curving wood staircase from which to make a theatrical entrance. Wafting throughout is the brand’s signature scent, called Caroline’s Four Hundred, inspired by John Jacob’s mother, Caroline, famous for her love of roses and her lavish dinners where 400 of New York City’s elite would gather. And as in those days, extravagances like butler service and delicacies like caviar, lobster, and seafood towers are at the ready.
The rooms
The modern, plush accommodations—decorated in colors that evoke the sand and sea—have all the careful details you’d expect from the brand: smart lights that illuminate your way when they sense movement, Frette sheets and robes, full turn-down service that includes slippers by your bedside. Special ground-floor suites have swim-out areas when you don’t want to join other guests in the larger pools. The bathrooms—as large as many New York City living rooms—have double sinks, a separate toilet, a shower stall that could fit a small party, and a deep, deep tub (that many tall people will appreciate). You can ask your butler to draw your bath for you, which might be the ultimate luxury. Of course, room service and a stocked minibar and bar cart are available for when you want to stay put.
Food and drink
Options abound for food and drink throughout the premises. Enjoy the breakfast buffet every morning at the main restaurant, Cassava, or order à la carte (make sure to get a side of mofongo). Poolside, get your cocktails from the Carey Bay Seaside Bar and savor a lunch of grilled fish and other bites at Marola (or stay in your cabana and have it brought to you). If you’re at the rooftop pool, you can dine and eat at the modern tiki bar Cielo Mio.
For happy hour drinks and snacks, the famed St. Regis Bar—with a striking artwork of the Dominican “faceless doll”—has an extensive list of Blood Marys and other cocktails (one standout: the Damajuana, which highlights the Dominican spiced drink mamajuana). Afterward, keep it more casual at Cassava, which serves distinctly Dominican flavors (go for the ahi crudo islanded in a pool of mango sauce), or for something more upscale, there’s Nina, which boasts a menu created in collaboration with chef Diego Muñoz and a 100-bottle-strong organic-wine list. You can dine on the terrace as the sun sets or sit inside where a DJ provides a cool, loungy vibe. Both the St. Regis Bar and Nina are open to the general public for reservations.
If you’re looking for more bespoke experiences, you can reserve the small dining room and kitchen El Huerto for private meals by guest chefs. Wine enthusiasts can reserve the Uma Wine Cellar Room in Nina for wine tastings. And if you’re a cigar aficionado, you’ll dig the cozy Amber Room, where a cigar sommelier will select a special roll for you and a “rummelier” will pair it with two fingers of the Caribbean spirit.
The spa
Decked out in warm woods and cool marble, the spa has 14 treatment rooms as well as separate men’s and women’s areas with indoor and outdoor lounges, hot and cold baths, steam rooms, and saunas. Couples can book treatments in rooms that have their own terraces for the ultimate private indulgence.
The neighborhood/area
Located in the resort enclave of Cap Cana, the St. Regis is one of the most exclusive properties in this lush corner of the DR. Because of its setting between the sea and the golf course, this is a place where only the sea and trees make a sound. A short drive away are tennis courts and a marina from which to embark on snorkeling expeditions.
The service
As one would expect from a hotel that looks to the Gilded Age for inspiration, the service here is meticulous. Butlers are on call (and WhatsApp) for your every whim and can arrange tennis lessons, boat excursions, and horseback riding. Try to score a private cabana and have servers dote on you while curtains billow in the sea breeze.
For families
Aside from the pools for families, there’s a kids’ and teens’ center complete with toys, video games, and a cozy nook for movie watching. There’s also a separate room for teens’ to socialize and chill while scrolling on their phones, as is their wont.
Eco effort
As part of the Cap Cana resort community, the St. Regis is under the umbrella of the Cap Cana Foundation, which oversees the protection and preservation of marine and land wildlife and their habitats. Solar energy is used throughout the enclave, and 100% treated wastewater is used to irrigate the golf course. In partnership with NUVI plastic recovery, Cap Cana recycles plastic used at the resorts to reintegrate it into the production cycle.
Accessibility
There are fully accessible rooms, parking, and entrances as well as ramps and elevators throughout the property.
Anything left to mention?
Know that it’s not an all-inclusive stay, so be prepared. The Jack Nicklaus Signature Punta Espada golf course is right next to the St. Regis, so you’ll find plenty of golfers in your midst. Lastly, when the resort first opened, work was still being done on some land approaching the resort and on the permanent residences in the north wing.
