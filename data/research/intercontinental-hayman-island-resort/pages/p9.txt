Why book?
For a luxury private island getaway with Australia’s Great Barrier Reef at your fingertips. Water is a central theme here; you’ll arrive by private boat to this sprawling property where your room overlooks the largest pool in the Southern Hemisphere. The colossal 6.5-million-liter saltwater lagoon is ideal for daytime lounging, but days can also be spent in the ocean snorkeling, jet-skiing, or fishing. You can also move at a slower pace with sunbathing, getting pampered at the expansive spa, or eating at one of the resort’s four restaurants.
Set the scene
Hayman Island is the most northerly of the Whitsunday Islands and is located in the heart of the Great Barrier Reef. The island was designated a national park in 1946 and is also a bird sanctuary—as such, you’ll encounter many cockatoos and other feathery friends during your stay. For decades, this private island property has been considered the pinnacle of Australian luxury travel, and you’ll find couples on romantic vacations celebrating anniversaries or honeymoons. Given its size, Intercontinental Hayman Great Barrier Reef is also perfect for friend vacations or multigenerational trips. Guests are ready for the beach and the tropics, wearing breezy cover-ups and colorful dresses for dinner. Australia does resortwear very well, so if you want to stock up on some caftans or swimwear, the on-site retail hub Grove Boutique is worth a browse.
The backstory
In 1947, aviation pioneer Reginald Ansett acquired Hayman Island and, in 1950, opened the Royal Hayman Hotel, which was renowned for its lush foliage and famous “Hollywood Pool.” In 1985, a $300 million project began to turn the hotel into a world-class luxury resort, a reputation it’s maintained over the past few decades. In 2019, the property rebranded and reopened as the InterContinental Hayman Great Barrier Reef, where it’s part of the IHG Hotel portfolio.
The rooms
The InterContinental Hayman Great Barrier Reef has a wide variety of rooms, as well as pavilions, villas, and residences. Every room offers a sense of escapism; the courtyard rooms have outdoor showers to bring outside in, the pool access suites allow you to wake up and dive headfirst into the water, and the beachfront villas offer floor-to-ceiling views of the Whitsunday Islands. The residences lend themselves perfectly for a group trip or family vacation. I stayed in a sprawling 807-square-foot 1 Bedroom Suite Pool View suite, which had a separate living room and bedroom, a palatial bathroom, two separate closet spaces, and a large balcony. The design is minimalist with beachy motifs; touches of blue, rattan lamps, and a canopied bed elevate the space to remind you of the surrounding marine environment.
Food and drink
There are four restaurants on the property offering plenty of variety for a private island. Start your day at the juice bar before settling in for an extensive breakfast spread at Pacific, the resort's signature restaurant. For dinner, make sure to try the prawns and premium beef, both sourced locally from Queensland. At Amici, you’ll enjoy antipasti, pizzas, and pasta while sipping Australian wines, and at Bam Bam, you’ll have Pan-Asian cuisine with dishes like Thai beef salad and hibachi grilled beef. Aqua is the poolside restaurant where you’ll enjoy a Beachfire Margarita and fish tacos to refuel in between beach walks and swimming laps. Cap your lunch off with a refreshing ice pop before you take an afternoon nap in a nearby cabana.
The spa
The property’s Top-to-Toe Massage treatment is the ideal way to unwind, especially after a long travel day. It combines a tension-relieving massage with a rejuvenating lime-and-ginger scrub. You’ll also experience a pro-glow facial, foot scrub, and scalp frangipani oil treatment—when they say top to toe, they really mean it.
The neighborhood/area
The Ngaro Aboriginal people are the original inhabitants of the Whitsunday Islands and have lived there for at least 9,000 years. InterContinental Hayman Island Great Barrier Reef has partnered with Yooribaya Cultural Experiences to give guests a chance to see Hayman Island through the eyes of the natives with ceremonies, cultural education workshops, and traditional dance lessons, allowing visitors to fully immerse themselves in their customs.
The service
Low-key yet seamless—open for decades, this property is a well-oiled machine. Food and drinks arrive quickly, golf cart service is readily available to take you to the far reaches of the resort, and if you want to do off-site activities like a sunset cruise or a helicopter tour, everything is carefully arranged for you. If you have the time, a flight over the Great Barrier Reef is an unforgettable way to see this bucket list destination.
For families
This is a family-friendly resort, and the residences are ideal for multigenerational travel. They fit up to eight people across four rooms with two golf carts, making the restaurants, gym, spa, and bars easily accessible. Private chef and catering options are available should you want to eat in residence.
Eco effort
After Cyclone Debbie destroyed much of the resort, InterContinental Hayman Great Barrier Reef was rebuilt with sustainability in mind. The property has a reverse osmosis desalination plant that recycles saltwater into drinking water and a liquid food composter that diverts what would otherwise be 450 tons of landfill waste. It also adheres to the 2021 Queensland ban on single-use plastics.
Accessibility
The paths on the property are wheelchair accessible, and there are two accessible room types on the island: the One Bedroom Accessible Suite, Pool View, and the Two Single Premium Accessible Lagoon View Rooms. Both are equipped with stability rails.
Anything left to mention?
The jet-ski tour is an exhilarating way to learn more about the flora, fauna, and history of the island while whizzing around the pristine waters. Speaking of fauna, make sure to close your doors and windows, otherwise a cockatoo will enter your room in search of food.
