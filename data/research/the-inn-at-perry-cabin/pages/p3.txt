Inn at Perry Cabin
A Chesapeake Bay gem that combines small-town charm with elevated luxury

Why book?
For an elevated East Coast waterfront stay in a quiet corner of a bustling summer town.
Set the scene
Just a short walk from downtown St. Michaels, this property is surrounded by the historic charm and natural beauty of the Chesapeake Bay. The neighborhood is a combination of small-town locals and suave guests summering in the East Coast gem. The Inn at Perry Cabin itself is a dreamy waterfront retreat—any stress from the real world simply melts away when you arrive at this serene collection of suites and guest rooms. Everything is nautical, from the diverse fleet out back (including a 1902 skipjack,
Stanley Norman
, which is listed on the National Register of Historic Places) to the historical decor, like the classic paintings and plaques explaining the history behind the name of each building.
The guests are linen-wearing couples who walked out of a Ralph Lauren ad, enjoying a break from daily life to indulge in the golf course,
tennis courts
, and waterfront views of the Inn. Those who aren’t on a romantic getaway are likely part of some sort of celebration—birthdays, babymoons, anniversaries, engagements, and weddings are all frequently hosted on the property, and most summer weekends are already booked with one or the other. In fact, the hotel serves as the backdrop for the wedding reception in the 2005 movie
Wedding Crashers
.
The backstory
One thing this Inn, and the town surrounding it, has in spades is historical significance. The original Manor House, which was later turned into the Inn at Perry Cabin, was built in the early 1800s by Samuel Hambleton, the purser and aide-de-camp to Commodore Oliver Perry in the Navy (the property’s namesake). When Hambleton retired to St. Michaels, he designed the area of the manor that is currently known as the Morning Room after Commodore Perry’s cabin on his flagship, hence the name, Inn at Perry Cabin. Since that time, the manor has been drastically renovated to become the five-star hotel it is today. After many redesigns and changes of hand, Sir Bernard Ashley purchased the property in 1989 to renovate it further, including the addition of the restaurant STARS, which garnered it a reputation in fine dining. Orient Express took over the property in 1999 and enlarged the dining room, added the Purser’s Pub, and created three new buildings of studio rooms and suites, bringing the total number of guest rooms and suites to 78. The Inn at Perry Cabin has been independently owned since 2019.
The town itself has a particularly notable history as the home of Frederick Douglass for three years, as well as its notoriety as “the town that fooled the British.” This reference is from the War of 1812 when local residents were able to save their town from a British bombardment by hoisting lanterns into the treetops at night to fool the British into shooting wide over their homes. If you choose to take a historic tour of the town, you’ll be able to see the only house in town that was hit during the chaos, and which still has the cannonball that hit it on display inside.
The rooms
There are 30 suites and 48 guest rooms, each of which has a waterfront view or a view of the property’s garden, where vegetables and garnishes are grown for guests to enjoy at the restaurant. The rooms are all modern, airy, and open, designed by renowned designer Alexandra Champalimaud to incorporate modern furnishing alongside nautical decor. The bright white tones combined with gentle splashes of light colors feel both luxurious and understated. French doors open to the private decks, patios, and verandas, perfect for guests to indulge in a morning coffee surrounded by the quietude of the area. The majority of rooms are in the main building and an additional few dozen are in the two detached Ariel and Caledonia Buildings. While the layout of the property can get a bit confusing, guests are provided with a map of the grounds as well as the town.
Food and drink
While the property is close enough to St. Michaels to walk into town and sample one of the many tasty restaurants, the Inn itself is well-known for its culinary delights. The main restaurant, STARS, is an elegant, waterfront eatery that overlooks the docks with the Inn’s fleet—and is the perfect place for guests to indulge in local seafood and Chesapeake Bay flavors. The menu changes seasonally and based on the local catch of the day. The Luxe Chef’s Tasting Menu, paired with a drink crafted to your taste by the restaurant's mixologist (just answer three simple questions to help them create something personalized to you) is a luxury well worth it for your tastebuds. The St. Michael’s Oyster Stew is deliciously imbued with delicate flavors of ginger and lemongrass, while the must-try dessert is the local Smith Island Cake with ever-changing flavors, from Irish coffee to lemon. For more casual cuisine, head to the Purser’s Pub & Courtyard, where locals are known to grab a drink or two, and indulge in local dishes and craft cocktails. You can also grab a bite poolside at the High Tide Pool Bar.
The spa
The Spa at Perry Cabin which was completed in 2007, features a variety of different treatments in its seven treatment rooms, including plant-based ones inspired by the Eastern Shore, made from herbs and aromatics grown in the Inn’s own gardens or on Eastern Shore farms. The most popular treatments are those for couples, including the “Muddy Buddies” treatment—a side-by-side full body scrub, warm mud masque application, and neck and scalp massage followed by a shower for two. Those looking for a solo treatment may prefer the IPC Ultimate Results Hydrafacial—a 75-minute soothing facial that is ideal for all skin types.
The neighborhood/area
A little over an hour and a half away from both Baltimore and Washington, DC, St. Michaels is a quaint summer town in the Chesapeake Bay, perched on the water and surrounded by history. You can expect tranquil solitude and small-town life outside of the peak summer season, but once Memorial Day hits, the city is awash with visitors indulging in their East Coast summer fantasies. We recommend taking the guided history walking tour (also offered as a bike or kayak tour depending on the season), since the city is so rich with history. Aside from the typical tourist-geared boutique shops you’ll find in any summer town, there are also a variety of gorgeous parks worth checking out, as well as the Chesapeake Bay Maritime Museum which is a direct neighbor of the Inn at Perry Cabin. And of course, it’s worth taking a trip on the water in the Inn’s fleet to really see the whole area.
The service
The staff simply couldn’t be more accommodating. When walking around the grounds, you’ll be greeted with a friendly smile and chat. From the ship’s captain to the concierge to the property's driver, everyone on staff is interested in making your stay as comfortable as possible.
Eco effort
At the on-site restaurants, guests can enjoy locally sourced seafood and produce, as well as honey and vegetables from the on-site hives and garden. There is an on-site composting program in place as well as a concerted effort to recycle. Much of the single-use packaging, like the beauty products in the bathroom or the to-go containers, is recyclable.
Accessibility
The property is in full ADA compliance with many accessible rooms on the first floor as well as elevators for accessing additional floors. The common spaces, restaurants, and lobby are all accessible without any stairs. There are also some in-room modifications available.
Anything left to mention?
Dogs are more than welcome here. Guests with pets will be given a first-floor room with direct outdoor access to make their stay as easy as possible. There is a $200 fee for the first pet and an additional $75 fee for a second pet.
