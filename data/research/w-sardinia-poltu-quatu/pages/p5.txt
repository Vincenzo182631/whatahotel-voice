W Sardinia Poltu Quatu
Seemingly carved out of the rocks of Poltu Quatu, this sleek hotel brand’s first foray into Sardinia is high-design meets high-living.

Reviewed by
Matt Charlton
Why book?
W is bringing a bit of high-end pizzazz and party to the already very classy province of Sassari, the second-largest city in Sardinia. Roll with the beautiful people, from the indulgent breakfast with stellar views to quality nighttime tunes around a picturesque pool.
Set the scene
Could this be what Sassari has been missing? Adding a youthful sizzle and Balearic beat to an area already well-known for its luxury offering could be the injection of life that the province needs.
The aesthetic is high-end from the moment you walk into the spacious, quirky, and colorful lobby festooned with local art. A thoughtful approach to the design—inspired by the island's rich Mediterranean, Moorish, and Greek heritage—creates spaces that give authentic island vibes whilst adding a contemporary edge, offering a fresh take on Sardinian luxury from the moment you enter.
I was there at the end of the soft-launch season, so the hotel was winding down—as was the locale—but still, there was a constant flow of guests. Imagine nonchalant, beautiful people wearing Orlebar Brown and Heidi Klein, Persol shades perched on their heads, peacocking around the pool area to the daytime thrum of Café Del Mar–esque chill.
The backstory
Tucked into the resort village of Poltu Quatu (meaning Hidden Port), the W Sardinia is moments away from the craggy cliffs, sandy beaches, and turquoise waters…la dolce vita on your doorstep. With the aforementioned soft-opening in summer 2025, around 80% of the property was open at the time of my visit. W Suites, the Spa, and W Beach Club will remain closed until the full launch in April 2026.
This is W’s third Italian property, with other outposts in Rome and Florence. Originally known for being a “party” hotel brand, it has pivoted to tailor to a more refined, luxurious, cultural, and discerning demographic since its acquisition by Marriott, but still with a party in its heart. Today, W Sardinia immerses patrons in color, culture, and stylish informality.
The rooms
Split into two sections, the accessible “2000" block and the labyrinthine “4000" block (the stepped corridors reminded me of Escher’s
Relativity
), the property offers sophisticated Balearic accommodations with a Sardinian twist—think whitewashed walls and cedar joinery, with interior touches that reflect the island through sea blues, local arts and crafts, and warm tones.
Guestrooms range from cozy single rooms with no balcony and a garden view to the W Suites. In the Deluxe Suite, the sofa area is a great chill-out space, divided from the closet and high-spec bathroom areas by a sleek bar and coffee counter, with a minibar, mixers, and tea and espresso making facilities. A touch more personality wouldn’t go amiss in the soft furnishings, but this is a minor kink which may be ironed out before the hard launch.
The bed is huge and comfortable, and your music needs are met by an excellent Marshall Bluetooth speaker, which can fill the entire space with sound if needed. The turn-down service included some truly delicious chocolates made by the resident pastry chef, which felt very considered and special.
Food and drink
Showcasing “the best of Mediterranean cuisine with a contemporary twist,” Tanit stakes its reputation on seafood and thankfully comes up trumps. That, paired with the evening views over the picture-perfect bay, made for quite the dining moment. I recommend ordering the squid tagliatelle—it both messes with your mind and satisfies your stomach.
Beyond Tanit, where an excellent breakfast is also served (the pastries are the star), guests can enjoy the pool-adjacent W Lounge, a Soho House–esque space designed for socializing and casual gatherings. The poolside wet deck provides the focal point for the hotel—the daytime music progresses to more of a starring role as the night encroaches, and I was very pleasantly surprised by the fries on the poolside menu, which were served with a lime zest. With that said, the pool and room service menus could have been a bit more extensive and a touch more local.
The spa
The spa opens in 2026, but the excellent gym has all the latest Technogym equipment. It does not feel like an afterthought, like so many others.
The neighborhood/area
Poltu Quatu is very much a resort town, and again was winding down when I visited, but it is literally a minute away from the hotel and offers eateries, gift shops, bars, and hiking. Its English meaning, "Hidden Port," is accurate—a cove so narrow that it is not visible from the open sea.
There is a Land Rover Defender Experience Arena nearby if you wish to test your 4x4 skills, but beyond that, you will need a rental car to explore the island further—taxis are readily available, but extremely pricey.
The service
Informal, youthful, and knowledgeable—but it's the playful uniforms that first strike you. I was particularly taken with the Roger Moore–style blue safari jackets the male reception staff wore, and I wish I asked for one. You are treated as a friend here, and nothing seems to be too much trouble, from bar staff who know their wines and cocktails to the concierge who can arrange a day trip or a luxury car to the airport.
Accessibility
The 4000 block has accessible rooms and facilities, and there is a ramp up to a separate pool area.
Anything left to mention?
The grand bridge over the pool leading from the 2000 block into the communal area feels like a catwalk, so dress to impress. A lot of the staff here also work in Rome in the low season, so they bring their big-city style, swagger, and know-how to this small island.
