Grand Hyatt Seoul
In the heart of the city, but away from the fray

Reviewed by

Why book?
You want to be in the heart of the city, but away from the fray.
Set the scene
Following two flights—including a 12-hour one with delays that forced me to scrap my dinner plans—and a careening 90-minute taxi ride, I pulled up to Grand Hyatt
Seoul
, a 20-floor glass structure nesting on the slopes of Namsan (Seoul’s most iconic mountain) in Hannam-dong, looking as rough as I felt. No less than five seconds after I slumped out of my taxi, a young man in a black suit requested my name and said my luggage would be sent up to my room.
This thoughtful gesture shifted my gears, as did the sights and sounds of the vast, high-ceilinged lobby enveloped in stone, wood, and soft light. Despite the late hour—it was nudging close to 11 pm—the hotel’s timeless elegance was on full display. Live jazz drifted from the Gallery, the hotel’s always-lively lobby lounge, where well-heeled Seoulites and out-of-towners sipped cocktails and soaked in the city views beyond the outsize windows.
The backstory
Opened in 1978 as Hyatt Regency and repositioned as Grand Hyatt in 1993, this historic hotel heralded South Korea’s emergence as an economic powerhouse. Unlike other hotels in town, Grand Hyatt Seoul spreads across 23 landscaped acres—lending it the feel of a leisure resort in the heart of the city.
Packed with top-of-the-line amenities, including Club Olympus, a mammoth wellness complex with saunas, tennis courts, and an outdoor pool that transforms into an ice skating rink come winter, the hotel is a rare spot that business travelers—every U.S. president since George H.W. Bush has hunkered down here—tourists, and staycationers can enjoy in equal measure.
The rooms
The accommodations have held up well since being renovated in 2019. My King Bed room with Club Access—the exclusive space includes a dedicated concierge, daily breakfast buffet, and evening cocktails and canapes—nods to South Korea’s rich artistic heritage with black-and-white fish paintings, hanok (traditional Korean houses) photographs, and geometric wood patterns reminiscent of bojagi (traditional Korean wrapping cloths), while the well-placed outlets and purified water dispenser—which streams boiling-hot water, so I don’t have to wait for my morning tea—feel very much here and now. The strategic placement of lights high above my bed casts a soft, lit-from-within glow, while mirrors along the wall make my room feel even more open and brighter. The focal point, though, is the sweeping views. Book a room with a mountain view; watching the cityscape shift from morning to night is a treat.
Food and drink
Grand Hyatt Seoul spoils guests with a whopping eleven restaurants and bars, ranging from the swanky and jazz-filled The Paris Bar, to The Deli (a bakery and wine shop), to my pick, Kauri, which is situated in 322 Sowol-ro, the hotel’s food “alley” featuring Japanese restaurants. This intimate Japanese spot, done up with neutral stone, a counter crafted from its namesake wood, and paper bags painted with expressive faces, is renowned for its omakase. As I sat between a reserved solo business traveler from Hong Kong and two rowdy locals donning matching Van Cleef Alhambra bracelets and double-fisting beer and Dom Perignon one evening, the sushi chef presented me exquisite small cooked plates—the steamed abalone dolloped with a green, umami-packed sauce made with the intestines was a knockout—and bite after bite of premium raw seafood. Unlike other omakase spots that stick to the same script for every diner, Kauri will play off your preferences. After professing my deep love of mackerel, the chef whipped up an otherworldly bite: a hand roll heaving with a slick, fat slab of mackerel from Jeju Island.
The neighborhood/area
Grand Hyatt Seoul’s isolated perch up on Namsan makes it easy to forget that you’re technically in Hannam, one of Seoul’s most affluent neighborhoods. The ascent up the hill is steep and takes about 20 minutes if you’re walking at a brisk pace. Thankfully, complimentary shuttle buses are available to and from the closest subway stop, Hangangjin Station, as well as Myeongdong, during daytime hours. Taxis are readily available, too.
The service
From the moment my suitcases were whisked upon arrival, the service was consistently polite and professional—but never stuffy. Every inch of the hotel is well-staffed, from the indoor pool to the entrance, where uniformed men are happy to help you get where you need to go.
For families
The hotel has a dedicated kids’ lounge. Parents should be aware that the space is unsupervised.
Eco effort
The hotel holds an EarthCheck Silver Certification and collaborates with UL Solutions to independently verify its sustainability practices.
Accessibility
The hotel is equipped with ramps, elevators, and accessible guest rooms.
