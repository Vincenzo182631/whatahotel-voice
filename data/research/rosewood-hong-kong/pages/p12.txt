Reviewed by

What’s the backstory?
The Cheng family owners originally came from Shunde in mainland China. They did well in jewelry and real estate, and redeveloped the site to accommodate an office tower, a mall, and a
Rosewood
(they bought the group back in 2011, with Sonia Cheng, then 30, as its CEO). The hotel is a trophy project, for sure, but one that’s been achieved with more than a little panache.
What can we expect in our room?
The views—mainly harbor but some over the city—can be enjoyed from plush velvet sofas, while in the more expensive rooms, bottles of spirits line the drinks table and arresting artworks speckle the walls. That the soaps (Votary) and similar amenities change with the seasons is the best indicator of the attention to detail that goes into putting together a very superior home away from home.
How about the food and drink?
There’s nothing random here—everything has been carefully thought out to cater to all possible tastes. Breakfast at Holt’s Café skips about between Western, Asian, and the ultra-healthy; the list of teas at The Butterfly Room runs to dozens of pages; breezy Bayfare Social is seemingly but half a step down from the Food Hall at Harrods; CHAAT plays with Indian street food; Marmot Bistro and BluHouse transport you to France and Italy, respectively, and then there’s Henry, a meat-centric grill with sublime artisanal sausages. The list goes on, though; roast goose and crispy fried pigeon star on the menu at The Legacy House, and stylish couples swish into DarkSide for live jazz even on most evenings.
What’s the crowd like?
Hong Kong’s elite and international travelers mix seamlessly here. You’ll find CEOs, artists, and designers rubbing shoulders over whisky cocktails at DarkSide or business lunches overlooking the harbor.
What’s the neighborhood scene like?
The Rosewood’s opening marked the final act of a highly theatrical renaissance in the area, which has seen the nearby Hong Kong Museum of Art and the harborfront promenade revamped, as well as the advent of K11 Musea, an inspiring mix of retail and entertainment that is so much more than a mall. Both the metro and ferries are more or less on the doorstep.
Anything else to add?
Asaya, occupying an entire floor, describes itself as an "integrated wellness concept," which, although it sounds rather gimmicky, is fair enough as "spa" would be woefully inadequate. Nestled within the 40,000-square-foot space, Asaya by Guerlain offers holistic treatments delivered with genuine care and expertise. Choose from tailor-made facials, deep massages, expressive arts therapy, hypnosis, plus two exclusive treatments inspired by Chinese culture—like a face and body massage performed with gentle calligraphy brushes. On the same floor, you'll find a harbor-facing gym, 25-metre infinity pool, and Rossano Ferretti HairSpa. The option to stay overnight in a tranquil Asaya Lodge is a clever addition.
Is it worth it?
It’s one of the smartest hotels in Hong Kong, and likely to remain so for some time to come.
