The Kensington
A handsome hotel set in Victorian buildings in elegant South Kensington

Reviewed by
Steve King
Set the scene for us.
Something of an unsung hero of the London hotel scene but, to those who know it, one of the loveliest, warmest, and most unobtrusively delightful places to stay in the capital, combining classic good looks and impeccable manners with an irrepressible sense of fun.
It's a classic stucco-fronted Victorian pile a dinosaur’s stride from the Natural History Museum, Science Museum, and V&A. Inside the front door, two immediate impressions: the tremendous breadth of the place, extending for a full block to your left, with no obstacle apart from common courtesy to prevent you from roller-skating all the way down it if you felt like it; and the immensely cozy yet entirely unstuffy Englishness of it all.
What’s the story with this place?
One of the Doyle Collection’s interesting and eclectic portfolio of hotels in England, Ireland (where the family-run company is based) and the U.S. Each of the three London ones—
The Bloomsbury
,
The Marylebone
, and
The Kensington
—has its own particular character and tone. The Kensington, though neither the youngest nor the raciest of the three sisters, is the most charming and sweet-natured, with excellent manners and a keen sense of humor.
Adorable. Tell us about the rooms.
Considerable variety. The small, contemporary rooms on the top floors are actually very chic and streamlined; the larger rooms more traditionally and sumptuously decorated (heavy fabrics, claw-foot baths, four-posters, working fireplaces). The two signature suites, the Kensington and the Brompton, on the first floor, are the pick of the litter.
How about the food and drink?
The K Bar is sleek and swishy and takes its cocktails seriously; the Town House restaurant is a sprawling and remarkably flexible, with a sort of charmingly split personality—bright and cheerful and welcoming in the morning and throughout the day, rather more cosseted and intimate and enveloping after dark.
Anything to say about the service?
Warm, enthusiastic, youthful, on it. Neither pompous nor matey. Really impressive. The liveried Pashley bikes parked outside, their baskets festooned with flowers, were tempting but, in the final analysis, marginally less so than the golden glow of the K Bar inside.
What sort of person stays here?
Though it may cause that Frankie Goes to Hollywood song to get stuck in your mind, this is a Two Tribes sort of place. Basically, well-heeled Americans (probably on holiday with their kids) and well-heeled Brits (probably in town for work). But the tribes rub along nicely and I saw no hint of friction, let alone imminent war.
What’s the neighborhood scene like?
You are smack-bang in the middle of South Kensington, and there are few more amusing, diverse, and agreeable parts of London to be smack-bang in the middle of. Though there is no shortage of hotels in the neighborhood, the Kensington is right up there with the best of them, including the
Pelham
, the
Franklin
, and the
Gore
.
Anything else we should know?
Keep an eye on the space in the basement and ground floor on the Old Brompton Road corner of the building, currently under development. This is a prime bit of South Kensington real estate and ripe for imaginative repurposing. Exciting plans, I was told, are in development. Watch this, or rather that, space.
And anything you’d change?
The original artwork throughout the hotel is terrific; more of that, please, and less (as in none) of the knock-offs on the otherwise wonderful spiral staircase.
So is it worth it—and why?
This is a little gem that deserves to be better known. In its unobtrusive way, it compares favorably with more familiar London hotels of a similar ilk—including, of course, its more famous sibling-properties the Marylebone and the Bloomsbury.
