Palazzo Ripetta
A sophisticated, discreet five-star hideaway on chic Via del Ripetta

Reviewed by

Why book
One of the few remaining independent five stars in
Rome
, Palazzo Ripetta embodies the kind of edgy, unshowy elegance Italian design is famous for. This former convent was transformed into a five-star hotel by Italian architect and interior designer Fausta Gaetani in 2022, who cleverly contrasts the ancient 17th century digs with wild wallpapers, bold color schemes, and gilded sculptures. Located on Via di Ripetta in the historic center, the hotel is a stone’s throw from cultural sites like the
Ara Pacis
,
Trevi Fountain
, and
Pantheon
.
Set the scene
There’s no shortage of pattern or color here. From the moment you enter the lobby, it’s a big visual treat, with meticulously restored artifacts, like the Arnaldo Pomodoro—just one of a few in the world—and a contemporary art collection (the owner’s own) featuring an original Warhol. They’ve even transformed a 3rd-century Roman sarcophagus into a fountain in the courtyard patio. While you’re there, stop by the 18th-century Salone Bernini, which was originally consecrated as the Conservatory's church by Pope Benedict XIII, and has a ceiling adorned with The Triumph of Divine Providence fresco by Giacomo Triga. If you visit during spring or summer, you might see couples exchanging vows; in the past few years, Palazzo Ripetta has become a popular place for weddings.
The backstory
The Palazzo has been in the same family for generations, and they tend to it with all the care and love you’d expect. This is one of the most important buildings in Rome; a 17th-century complex that has gone from convent to art deco design icon, thanks, in part, to leading Italian architect Luigi Moretti, who worked on the building in the 1960s.
The rooms
The 78 rooms and suites are exquisitely decorated: there are big Murano glass chandeliers dangling from high ceilings; colorful bed headboards; and Frette linens so soft that you’d be forgiven for staying under the covers all day. They range in size from the compact—but cleverly designed—deluxe rooms to the slightly larger prestige rooms, and then onto the spacious family room, which is essentially two connecting rooms with enough room for four adults.
Suites have their own spa or
Turkish baths
, while the luxury apartment runs across two floors and has its own kitchenette. For the ultimate blowout, book into Suite Ripetta, which combines two suites to create a three-bedroom, three-bathroom apartment, complete with a dining table, kitchen area, and walk-in wardrobes. Across all rooms and suites, amenities are appropriately luxurious, with Ortigia shampoos and moisturizers in the bathrooms and Alessi kettles and Nespresso machines.
Food and drink
Head up to the rooftop terrace, Etere, for Champagne cocktails and a seasonal menu, or stop by Piazzetta Ripetta for something more casual. Tables are arranged amongst olive trees, and the menu includes trays of oysters, white pizza topped with slivers of mortadella, hummus, and crudites. The crowd here is bone-deep chic: no one’s taking selfies or pictures of their food. They come here because it’s sophisticated and discreet.
But it’s San Baylon, the flagship eatery, that packs the biggest punch. It harks back to the Roman trattorias of old, with terra-cotta tiles and lanterns throwing soft light and deep shadows. Chef Christian Spalvieri combines top-notch ingredients to put a new twist on old Roman and Abbruzzese classics. Don’t miss the smoked artichoke with black garlic and marjoram, or the monkfish saltimbocca with turkey bacon (no pork is used in the restaurant). San Baylon is also an (unexpected) godsend for children, with coloring activities and connect-the-dot games, and a menu of kid-friendly options like crispy chicken nuggets and mashed potatoes, with chocolate chip cookies with ice cream for dessert.
The concierge can also arrange market visits, hands-on cooking sessions, wine excursions, and seasonal truffle hunting experiences.
The neighborhood/area
The location makes Palazzo Ripetta a perfect urban respite—bang in the heart of Rome’s historic center, easily walkable to the main sites (five minutes to Piazza del Poplo, 10 minutes to the Spanish steps) but also close to public transportation (Flaminia and Spagna, depending on your destination) and some of the city’s best eateries. The Tiber is just across the road, beckoning you across the bridge to upscale (and crowd-free) Prati in just 15 minutes.
Eco effort
Palazzo Ripetta is upfront with its commitment to sustainability and minimizing its carbon footprint. And it’s not just lip service—the property holds a Green Key certificate for meeting rigorous sustainability standards.
Bottom line: worth it, and why?
Palazzo Ripetta is the epitome of quiet luxury and enduring good taste. It’s crisp, elegant, and smart—and will be today, tomorrow, and in a decade’s time.
