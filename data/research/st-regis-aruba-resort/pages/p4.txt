The St. Regis Aruba: First In

Why book?
For a luxe stay among the toniest on the island known for mild weather and calm waters year-round—and along one of
Aruba
’s
loveliest stretches of beach
.
Set the scene
Positioned at the south end of Palm Beach, the St. Regis is fairly nondescript on the outside, but stands out as the most modern of the hotels among the strip. It’s built in a way to maximize beachfront rooms, which is of course what you’re here for—and the impressive maze of pools (seven all together) spilling into the ocean is a sight to behold from up above. Thanks to its position on the western side of the island, the water is quite calm, even during the windier months. The moment you step inside, nature is on full display here, from the lobby's focal greenery arrangement to the floor-to-ceiling lobby windows looking out to the water.
The backstory
Now part of Marriott’s luxury portfolio, the first St. Regis was opened in NYC at the turn of the 20th century by John Jacob Astor IV, a member of the wealthy Astor family. The Aruba property, one of over 60 St. Regis’s around the world, is the first to have a casino, and is part of the brand’s further expansion into resorts. Like its sister properties, it’s centered around extravagant daily rituals and traditions: an elaborate afternoon tea, the evening Champagne sabering (formally dubbed The Art of Champagne Sabrage), and a specialty Bloody Mary—this one with papaya and lime juice (the original Bloody Mary was invented at the New York property).
The rooms
Of the property’s 252 rooms, 52 are suites; all lean into the island’s Dutch-Caribbean aesthetic mixed with the expected poshness of a St. Regis. Rooms start at the deluxe category, which are still plenty sizable with a big balcony, both a shower and a bathtub, and a small seating area. From there, they go up to a two-bedroom suite (three via a connecting room, if needed, for families or group travel). I stayed in an executive suite, which had a huge living area, a balcony stretching the full length of the suite, a gorgeous platformed four-poster bed, and an oversized bathroom with double sinks, a large soaking tub with a wooden tray perfect for readers, and a roomy walk-in shower.

Food and drink
The hotel’s standout, don’t-skip-it restaurant is the rooftop Akira Back, helmed by its namesake chef (of Michelin-starred Dosa in
Seoul
). Here, you’ll not only get stunning views of Palm and Eagle Beach on your way in and from the terrace, but a delicious Japanese and Korean-inspired menu—the tuna pizza and AB cucumber salad were highlights, as was the miso butterfish. Across the hotel’s restaurants, fresh seafood abound; enjoy poolside bites like ceviche and tuna poke bowls at Nuba. If you find yourself looking for a sweet treat during the day, head to Grano Café for gelato or the key lime pie.
The spa
Steal some time away from the pool or beach to get a treatment at the St Regis Spa. The peaceful sanctuary has saunas and steam rooms inside the locker rooms and a spacious relaxation area with big archways, midcentury modern decor, and a neutral color palette (whites, creams, woods), with little alcoves to tuck yourself away in for a bit more quiet time pre- or post-massage. I enjoyed a classic relaxation massage, particularly the heated tables, tropical scents to choose from (I went with orange blossom), and incredibly comfortable Coop robes (
these ones
, according to the tag).
The neighborhood/area
There’s plenty within walking distance here, which isn’t always the case at resorts. If you feel like mixing up your breakfast routine, stroll down the beachside walking path to pick up a fresh juice, smoothie, or breakfast burrito at one of the many stalls along the way. The path runs all the way down to the
Ritz-Carlton Aruba
, where guests have signing privileges at its restaurants, including Casa Nonna New York, Divi Sushi Bar & Lounge, and BLT Steak. Beyond the beach, stroll over to the
Butterfly Farm
to see 35 species of butterflies in every stage of the life cycle, from egg to caterpillar to inside the chrysalis. To see even more of the island (which is quite small at just 21 by 6 miles, and has a unique desert landscape), arrange a tour with De Palm Tours, who can bring you to sites like the Natural Bridge, California Lighthouse,
Ayo rock formations
, and the National Archaeological Museum.

The service
St. Regis’s butler service is core to its offering, and something that really makes the hotels stand out. Aptly described as bespoke and anticipatory, you’ll feel well taken care of from check-in to check-out—my butler came to find me as I was checking in at the spa to ask if I needed anything pressed or shined before my dinner reservation. Here, the key cards read "welcome to the house of Astor,” and for the length of your stay, you really will feel like a member of the Gilded Age; ask your butler to pack and unpack for you, bring you fresh coffee or tea, press your garments, and more. Restaurant service does seem to run on island time; sit back and enjoy the views and you’ll be just fine.
For families
There were plenty of families at the resort during my stay—between the children’s club, pools, beach access, and water activities, it’s a great spot for active families.
Eco effort
A card is left next to your bedside with instructions on reusing sheets and towels—though if you’d prefer fresh ones, there’s an option to do so. Full-size toiletry bottles are placed in the showers instead of minis. Additionally, in line with Marriott’s
Serve360 Goals
(one of which is to better the communities where they operate), much of the staff are native Arubans.
Accessibility
All public areas, including the main entrance, pool area, spa, fitness center, restaurants, and meeting spaces have accessible routes. Accessible on-site parking is available, as are accessible rooms for those requiring mobility accessible rooms, hearing accessible rooms, non-slip grab rails, bathtub grab rails, and/or a roll-in shower; call (+297) 281-2222 with any specific questions.
Anything left to mention?
This property boasts the first St. Regis casino, one that hotel staff expects will draw in both guests and locals. Along with around a hundred slot machines and 10 table games, there’s a funky, well-decorated bar shrouded with greenery for players and people-watchers alike to enjoy a cold beverage. It's also home to the second
Okaicos
boutique (find the flagship store at The Ritz-Carlton, Turks & Caicos), which sells classic linen pieces, cozy hoodies and graphic tees, and fun, brightly-colored crewnecks that offer a more modern take on the spirit jersey you're likely to find in other hotel gift shops.
