One & Only Royal Mirage
You’ll be in awe of the Royal Mirage from the moment you drive up the long palm-lined road and into the stunning courtyard.

Reviewed by

Why book?
This is one of the few resorts in the city that immerses you in local culture via architecture, design, and cuisine in the most authentic (unforced) way. And because it is divided into areas for families and adults, it doesn’t have the zoo-like vibe you can find at some of the other properties on Palm Island or along Jumeirah Beach.
Set the scene
Trickling fountains, beams of sunlight, and the sound of the traditional One & Only gong being sounded (that indicates the beginning of someone’s stay at the brand’s properties around the world) are what you experience the moment you arrive. Next up: Arabic coffee and dates as the next part of the local welcome.
As you walk to your room, pay close attention. It is easy to be wowed by what you will see, but it is just as easy to get lost (believe me). That’s because the property is divided into three sprawling, but distinct sections: The Palace, Arabian Court, and The Residence. They are each designed for different experiences.
The Palace is the family-focused section, while The Residence belongs to adults only. Kids can only visit designated parts of the resort, while Residence guests can roam freely. And there is a lot to explore for the United Nations-like guest list that checks in here.
In the hallways, you will hear different dialects of Arabic (this is a family favorite of regional travelers) as well as dozens of European and Asian tongues, as this is a vacation hot spot. More Americans are finding their way here, too, as very few of the familiar brand names in town can match the range of amenities on offer here.
The backstory
The property opened in 1999 as The Palace Hotel before later adding the Arabian Court and The Residence & Spa. Shortly thereafter, it joined the One&Only collection.
The rooms
Each area of the resort has its own defining features. The Palace is the largest section and is dramatic in its Arabic design. This is the most family-friendly area of the hotel, where you will find kids’ play areas as well as pools and recreational facilities like tennis and paddle courts, a putting green, and a watersports desk.
The Palace is home to all of the Superior rooms, which are sizable with patterned headboards and views of the gardens or pool areas, but also Executive Suites (twice as large as the rooms and great for those traveling with kids). If you check into one of the Royal Suites, prepare for a jaw-drop with the fabulously canopied four-poster beds and wide balconies.
If sea views are important, Arabian Court ensures that all rooms have a water view from their balcony. This section strikes a chord with multi-generational travelers for its numerous suites and connecting room categories, many overlooking the section’s own pools.
The smallest and most sophisticated part of the property is The Residence. It has its own reception and recreational area with pool and spa facilities. This is also where you will find most suites, including 16 in the junior category and two enormous villas. The latter are especially popular with regional guests, who often travel with their extended family and sometimes even nannies in tow.
All of the premium suites here come with an array of extras like free afternoon tea and complimentary evening drinks, access to the private beach club area, and, for those staying at least three nights, a 60-minute massage.
Every accommodation here has a separate shower and soaking tub, plus vanities stocked with Montroi products.
Food and drink
One thing that really sets this property apart is that all rates come with breakfast, but you can also opt for a half-board concept to add dinner in certain restaurants. With such a wide variety, it can prove a great value for families and those who don’t want to sit in the city’s infamous traffic jams, traversing highways to get to other restaurants or resorts to try out what they have to offer.
Trèsind is the signature Indian restaurant at Royal Mirage, known for its modern twist on Indian classic dishes under the helm of Chef Himanshu Saini. It’s not your everyday Indian spread. For example, you can order an A5 Wagyu steak served with five different curries or even an achari-spiced Tomahawk steak if you’re seriously starving. There’s also a tandoori portobello dish for the veggie-minded and an Alleppey lobster curry that earns rave reviews.
At Drift, the beach club menu swings the other direction with lighter salads and burgers, while Tagine leans into more traditional Moroccan fare at dinner. In total, there are 17 food and beverage options to tempt you, depending on where and when you want to spend your time across this sprawling property.
The spa
Located at The Residence section, the spa is an oasis within an oasis so to speak. While I did not sample a treatment, some colleagues told me of their experience. In one word, decadence, as they described the holistic hamman and sound ritual that involved a traditional full-body scrub followed by a shea butter massage with sound therapy.
There’s also a Tibetan sound massage following a similar sequence (without the scrub) and the “B Silent” treatment that incorporates Japanese Shiatsu rocking, back massage, and body stretching to improve your overall sleep.
The neighborhood/area
Hotel guests also have access to a private stretch of sand, where they can play in the sea or relax in the sunshine on towel-covered chairs with umbrellas. But, the property’s position at the base of the Palm Jumeirah island means that you are facing most of the buildings along The Palm itself rather than the unobstructed sea. And while that is the case in a lot of Dubai, it is more notable here. With that said, getting to places like Burj Khalifa, Dubai Mall, and even the airport is faster since you don’t have to deal with the traffic along Palm Jumeirah.
The service
An international team cares for the international clientele. Like many hotels in town, the staff is almost entirely foreign, but the city’s standards (as are One&Only’s) are very high. The extra steps I noticed they take to cater to families with kids really stood out.
For families
The hotel has a kids club with supervised activities so that parents can dine, have spa treatments or shop to their hearts’ content. At The Palace, the main pool is dedicated to families, where kids can splash and scream without worry (and they do).
Anything left to mention?
Dubai may have been off the grid for tourists for a while, but it has always managed to get its groove back. My visit was a couple of years ago, but this resort is among the city’s finest.
