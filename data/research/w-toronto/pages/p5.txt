W Toronto
A high-design hotel in Yorkville where velvet-draped rooms, trendy Nikkei dining, and lively public spaces pull you straight into the city’s social orbit.

In a nutshell
: W Toronto brings the brand’s refined irreverence to Yorkville, pairing theatrical interiors with a strong sense of place. This is a high-design, high-energy hotel in the heart of the neighborhood, with lively public spaces animated by music, art, and nightlife. It suits travelers who want somewhere stylish, convivial, and firmly in the middle of the action.
Jessica Huras
Jessica Huras
Rooms
The hotel’s 255 guest rooms and suites are unabashedly fun, drawing on the W’s long-standing connection to music and nightlife as well as Yorkville’s bohemian past. Jewel-toned accents, sapphire velvet curtains, and beds with an ambient underglow create a mood that feels closer to a backstage lounge than a standard hotel room. The setup favors pleasure over productivity. There are no workstations, and the minibar expands on the usual snack-and-spirits lineup with tools and ingredients that invite guests to play bartender.
Open-concept bathrooms with terrazzo vanities and illuminated mirrors blur the line between getting ready and hanging out, which suits the hotel’s social energy. If you can, book one of the Urban Suites on the 11th floor: The oversized window walls flood the room with light, making every vivid detail hit a little harder.

Jessica Huras
Food and drink
Centered around a circular bar and floor-to-ceiling windows that keep Bloor Street permanently in frame, W Café & Bar starts the day with Barocco coffee and pastries before easing into cocktail mode by late afternoon. Up one level, the Living Room lives up to its name as the hotel’s social spine, carrying the action from brunch through late-night drinks. Its menu leverages a tight roster of local suppliers, including honey from Bee Local 416, fish and meat from Woodward Meats, and produce from Muddy Crops for elegant, easy-to-share plates.
Upstairs, Tono by Akira Back is the headline act. At this rooftop spot with expansive city views, the Michelin-starred chef, known for restaurants from Seoul to Las Vegas, turns to Nikkei cuisine, balancing Japanese precision with Peruvian brightness. Opening in May 2026, the hidden Toki Listening Bar tucked inside Tono will offer an intimate, vinyl-led speakeasy experience, with whisky-forward cocktails and a deeper focus on sound.
Amenities and experiences
The Living Room doubles as the center of gravity, transforming from a casual daytime workspace into a lounge with live performances by evening. Daily programming, from DJ sets to vinyl sessions and pop-ups, gives the space a sense of rhythm that carries through the hotel.
Downstairs, the 3,300-square-foot gym is well above average for a city hotel, with cardio machines, free weights, a boxing setup, and space to stretch. Even the event spaces feel geared toward spectacle, with flexible rooms designed for shoots, launches, and anything that benefits from a bit of stage presence.
The three-story W Atrium, with its floor-to-ceiling windows and natural light, pulls double duty as an event space and production backdrop, attracting film and content crews. The W Sound Suite makes the hotel's music credentials concrete, open to Toronto creatives as much as hotel guests. This fully equipped recording studio is available to rent for everything from content creation to impromptu jam sessions.
What to do nearby
The hotel’s Yorkville address gives guests a straight shot to some of Toronto’s biggest cultural draws. The neighborhood has evolved from its 1960s bohemian roots (musicians Neil Young and Joni Mitchell used to play the local coffeehouses here) into one of Toronto’s most posh pockets without entirely losing its artsy edge.
Bloor Street’s “Mink Mile” delivers the expected luxury names, from Hermès and Chanel to Holt Renfrew, while Yorkville Village mixes Canadian designers with pop-ups and dining. Just off the main drag, Hazelton Avenue’s gallery row offers a strong lineup of contemporary Canadian and international art. The
Royal Ontario Museum
is a short walk away, and W Toronto guests get complimentary passes.
The whole neighborhood is compact and walkable, with patios, cafés, and seasonal events like Yorkville Murals filling in the gaps. The hotel offers direct access to transit and the PATH system if you want to roam further.
The backstory
W Toronto opened in 2022, but the version guests see now was shaped by a major 2025 refresh that brought it more clearly into line with the brand’s broader reinvention. Toronto now stands as one of the sharper expressions of where W is heading next, as it aims to redefine itself for a new era of lifestyle hospitality.
Insider tip
If you’re eating at Tono, let the knowledgeable sommelier lead. The drink list has range, but the more interesting experience is in the rotating offbeat pours. I was steered toward a rare red sake, NV Mukai Shuzo “Ine Mankai” Junmai Genshu, during my stay, and it ended up being one of the more memorable things I drank there.
Final considerations:
Family friendly
: Children are welcome, though the hotel's after-dark energy and open-concept room layouts make it better suited to a trip without the kids
