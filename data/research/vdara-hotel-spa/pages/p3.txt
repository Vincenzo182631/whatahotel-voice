Vdara Hotel & Spa
A glassy, condo-style tower in CityCenter that functions as a smoke-free, casino-free sanctuary with a destination-level spa and rooms that feel more like small apartments than typical hotel keys.

Reviewed by
Andrea Bennett
Best for:
Travelers who want Strip access and Aria/Bellagio as their “local” casinos, but prefer to sleep and recharge in a calmer, spa-forward environment.
Location:
In the CityCenter complex between Bellagio and Aria, set slightly back from
Las Vegas
Boulevard with walkway connections to both.
Can’t-miss feature:
The Spa at Vdara and the all-suite, residential layout—kitchenettes, usable living rooms, and, in higher categories, in-unit laundry.
In a nutshell:
A lovely, low-key sanctuary for people who want to choose when they enter a casino, not stroll through one every time they leave their room.
Rooms
Every room at Vdara is a suite, which changes the whole feel of a stay here. Even standard studios start around 500–600 square feet and come with a sitting area, dining table, and kitchenette equipped with a cooktop, microwave, sink, and empty refrigerator you can actually stock yourself. Larger categories, including City Corner Suites and owner/condo units, add more square footage, full kitchens, and often in-suite washer–dryer setups that make longer trips or work stays much easier. Floor-to-ceiling windows frame views of the mountains, CityCenter, or, in specific “fountain view” layouts, the Bellagio Fountains across the way. The overall look is modern and somewhat understated—more sleek condo than themed resort—which is part of the appeal. Many execs and casino companies use Vdara as their hub for long-term stays.
Food and drink
On site, the food and drink lineup is intentionally compact. Market Café Vdara handles quick breakfasts, salads, sandwiches, and grab-and-go meals, plus groceries and frozen items you can heat up in your kitchenette. Vice Versa Patio & Lounge functions as the main bar and social space, with indoor-outdoor seating and cocktails that lean more craft than yard-stick. For full-scale dining, most guests wander next door to Aria or over to Bellagio, where the restaurant rosters are among the best on the Strip; Vdara’s role is to give you a calm home base in between those meals rather than compete with them.
Amenities and experiences
Vdara’s pool deck continues the spa-hotel feeling: it is compact, design-forward, and significantly more relaxed than the party scenes at many nearby properties, with cabanas and semi-private plunge areas rather than a DJ-driven dayclub. The Spa at Vdara is one of the resort’s signatures, spread across two floors with European-style heat experiences, quiet relaxation areas, and ESPA-branded treatments that make it feel more like a true urban spa than just a hotel add-on. The fitness center is solid for an all-suite property, and the overall lack of gaming, smoke, and heavy nightlife helps maintain the wellness-adjacent atmosphere. Practical perks like access to laundry facilities in many suites and strong in-room Wi-Fi add to the residential feel.
What to do nearby
From Vdara, you are effectively in the middle of modern Strip life without being front and center in it. Pedestrian paths and interior walkways connect you to Aria’s casino and restaurants, Bellagio’s casino and fountains, and the larger CityCenter complex, including the Shops at Crystals. It is an easy walk to T-Mobile Arena and Park MGM for concerts and games, yet when you come back, the lobby and elevators feel noticeably calmer than those of the surrounding megaresorts. If you do want to roam farther—down to Caesars and beyond—ridehares and taxis are straightforward from the porte cochere, and you do not have to thread your way through a casino floor to find them.
The backstory
Vdara opened in 2009 as part of CityCenter, conceived from the start as a condo-hotel hybrid and one of the Strip’s only purpose-built non-gaming, non-smoking towers. About 1,150 of its roughly 1,500 suites are in the nightly rental pool, with the rest held as residential condominiums, which is why the building feels more like a high-rise residence than a traditional resort. That setup influenced everything from the floor plans to the amenity mix: kitchens, laundry, and a serious spa for the hotel side, with casinos and big-ticket dining left to Aria and Bellagio next door. Over time it has become a favorite of guests who know Vegas well enough to value space and calm over slot-machine proximity.
Insider tip
If the Bellagio fountains are on your must-see list, look specifically for Studio Fountain View or higher categories and request a high floor; the diagonally framed view from Vdara is one of the more underrated angles on the Strip. For longer stays, make full use of the residential features—stock the fridge from Market Café, ask housekeeping for cookware and extra K-cups, and take advantage of in-suite laundry instead of sending everything out at Strip prices. And if you want a full spa day, book treatments at the Spa at Vdara rather than elsewhere; the combination of a quieter facility and the hotel’s no-smoking policy makes the whole experience feel cleaner and more restorative.
Final considerations
Family friendly:
Works well for longer stays and families who value space and kitchenettes, though there is no dedicated kid zone and you will head to neighboring resorts for big pools and attractions.
Accessibility:
Good; elevators, accessible rooms, and the all-suite layouts are supported by accessible parking and a relatively compact lobby footprint.
Wellness:
Strong for a Strip property, with a serious spa, relaxed pool deck, fitness center, and a non-smoking, non-gaming environment that actually feels like a wellness choice.
Loyalty group:
Part of MGM Rewards through its connection with Aria and CityCenter, useful if you are earning or redeeming across multiple MGM properties.
