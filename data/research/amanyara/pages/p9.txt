Reviewed by
Erin Florio
Why book?
There are few resorts in the Caribbean that can claim to lead in design, food, privacy, beaches, wellness, and family retreats. Set across an 18,000-acre nature reserve, Amanyara manages to do all of the above in a way that never distracts or deters from what you have set out to achieve—a tranquil, restorative holiday in one of the most beautiful spots in the area.
Set the scene
The travelers who check into Amanyara may have different backgrounds—perhaps they are a family of four who have dropped in for the school holiday week from Atlanta; honeymooners in from London; a group of girlfriends reconnecting while on leave from their busy New York City jobs; the odd A-list celeb almost blending in…. But regardless of where they’ve come in from or why, they all make sense here. A soundtrack of calming, gushing water accompanies the airy arrivals pavilion, where golf carts are at the ready to whisk guests, cocktail or welcome drink in hand, to their villas where it’s entirely likely the butler has already begun unpacking their luggage. From that point, the cycle of roaming from beach to pool to spa to catamaran to tennis courts takes over, moving at the speed set entirely by you, the guest. Should you get stuck at just one spot and not want to move, well, that’s no problem either. I assure you the resort will ensure all cocktail, snacking, and reading needs are delivered to wherever it is you and your crew decide to stick to.
The backstory
Amanyara is a portmanteau of Aman, meaning ‘peace’ in Sanskrit, and Yara, meaning ‘place’ in the language of the Arawak people who once lived on Turks and Caicos. And the name nails it. This quiet corner of the island is laced with pink-sand beaches and impossibly calm, glowing blue waters. It opened as the first property in the Caribbean from luxury resort portfolio Aman back in 2006 (it has since been joined by
Amanera
in the Dominican Republic). The sprawling resort just outside Providenciales channels the brand’s Asian roots in its tranquil structures, heavy on timbers and bamboo, as well as its signature spa.
The rooms
Quiet luxury is the name of the game at Amanyara and the resort consists of one-to-two bedroom pavilions and three-to-six bedroom villas, each surrounded by forest, with park or ocean views. The layout means they all feel incredibly private—your kids could be splashing in your villa pool, and their squeals are unlikely to disturb your neighbor trying to catch an afternoon nap on their outdoor lounger. Each villa has its own pool, golf cart parking, and outdoor area. Beds are plump, bathtubs are deep, and all are designed to optimize the island’s brilliant natural light. My family of four stayed in a one-bedroom pavilion, which was shrouded by natural flora. It kind of made the pool feel like a backyard pool, which I loved. My kids could scamper around the grass and then jump into the pool. We really enjoyed spending lazy afternoons there as my daughter napped. One thing to mention—the floors are made of stone and extremely hard. So I was always on extra alert when there were wet feet going back and forth, be it from the pool or bath.
Food and drink
Though you could head off to a vibrant beach bar on Providenciales, the reality is pretty much all your eating and drinking is happening across the resort’s 18,000 acres—and that’s a good thing. Executive chef Jovan Uksanovic, who arrived at Amanyara from Aman Sveti Stefan in his native Montenegro, oversees a team across three main places to eat, including The Restaurant, where tables spill out from an open-walled dining area across terraces with fantastic ocean views, The Bar, and the seasonal Beach Club (in-villa dining is also available for any meal, at any time). The food is phenomenal—from the Asian-inflected Restaurant menu, we devoured black cod gyozas and green eggplant curry. Hotel breakfasts should be a dining highlight of any respectable property, and Amanyara’s measured up. We would start our day outside, with baskets of fresh breads and pastries, followed by whatever we wanted cooked fresh to order. For my kids, that often meant pancakes with mountains of strawberries while I enjoyed eggs, salmon, and avocado. A real challenge of Caribbean dining can be the sourcing of ingredients and maintenance of freshness, but as chef Jovan explained, a lot of work goes into sourcing as locally as possible and strategizing on the best way to bring in everything else. Suffice it to say, my salmon and avocado toast could have been made with stuff caught and plucked that day.
But, a highlight: The Bar. Though our days were a happy rotation of beach, pool, and villa pool, they always ended before sunset at The Bar, which is an enormous semi-open-air space with gorgeously large cushions to lounge on with views across the Caribbean. Board games and cards are on hand should you want to play a little something with your Negroni and martini. The four of us could take over just one of these ginormous pillows and my kids would play games, and my partner and I would sit and sip and genuinely relax. The sunsets turn the sky into a sherberty fire, too, and being there at cocktail hour was a great opportunity to watch the camera angles and outfits of all the ’grammers who showed up to get the shot for the feed.
Finally, and most importantly, though the scope of the food production here is naturally vast, it’s all executed extremely thoughtfully and personally. My daughter has extreme food allergies, which the property was aware of. Chef Jovan personally saw to it that his kitchen made plenty of kid-friendly snacks that were safe for her to enjoy during our whole trip, which helped make me and my partner’s vacation just a little more relaxing. And while we were enjoying the cod and curries, my kids were treated to huge plates of pasta to rival any Italian restaurant because sometimes vacation just isn’t the place to fight with your kids about their food.
The spa
Aman’s approach to wellness is holistic and advanced, drawing on nearly 40 years of well-being practices across its entire portfolio. In other words, even with the glorious beaches, Amanyara has such a robust approach to physical, spiritual, and mental wellbeing that plenty of travelers come here specifically for the wellness program and the resort rotates through a sterling list of guest specialists to keep the quality competitive. The spa itself sprawls 64,000 square feet and has a cluster of treatment pavilions, fitness centers, and outdoor pools where hydrotherapy sessions are performed (my partner turned to jelly after a particularly effective water shiatsu session). Drawing on Aman Thai roots, the massage menu is long and varied; favorites include the Stone Therapy which uses Himalayan salt stones. There are also tennis courts and a cool Clubhouse to hang out in after lessons and matches. For me though, a highlight was Amanyara’s more intense active training. I’m not one for sitting still through massages, yoga, and more (sorry!), so I opted for a personal boxing session with one of Amanyara’s extremely knowledgeable and skilled resident wellness specialist, who whipped my butt into shape during an hour session in the private gym. My muscles ached the next day in a good way (and it was nothing a quick swim in the ocean couldn’t cure).
The neighborhood/area
Amanyara is located about 25 minutes from Providenciales, on the quiet northwest tip, making for extraordinary sunsets. It borders Northwest Point National Park, which is excellent for birdwatching and exploring a network of underwater caves.
The service
It’s low-key but polished. Amanyara benefits from the extensive Aman network of highly trained and extremely friendly staff. We had lovely waiters from the Philippines and eastern Europe. But crucially, the resort employs plenty of locals from the Caribbean, which lends a nice degree of locality to the place.
For families
This place is excellent. On top of the pool and beach, where the sea is calm and swimmable, the resort has an incredibly engaging kids program called Nature Discovery, where older kids can go sailing and mountain biking while younger kids can learn to sail and take kitesurfing lessons. Kids are also able to play a ton of sports and run around at the Clubhouse. For those families with really little kids (like mine), there are plenty of spaces on the property to keep kids entertained, including the playground, kiddie pool with basketball hoop, and oversized chessboard (what is it about an oversized chessboard that makes kids go nuts?). The staff, too, doted on all the kids endlessly. But I have to say, my kids were almost always happy splashing around in our villa’s private pool, or if we were at the beach, I would plop them on the paddleboard, paddle out to sea, and let them use it as a platform for endless cannonballs into the ocean which they loved (with lifejackets secured, of course).
Accessibility
The resort is wheelchair-friendly and flat; golf carts are on hand to escort guests around the property, and villas are almost all single-level. There are no notable stairs to tackle.
Anything left to mention?
I have to take a moment to appreciate the beach setup. The sand is powder fine, the sea itself a brilliant, glowing shade of blue and wonderfully still. The resort has a stash of water toys at the ready, including snorkels to admire the turtles and beautiful fish, as well as paddleboards, which I looked forward to every day.
