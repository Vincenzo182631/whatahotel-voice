Villa San Michele, A Belmond Hotel, Florence
A once-in-a-lifetime kind of stay located in Fiesole, in the hills outside of Florence, where views and super staff elevate everything just a bit more.

In a nutshell
: The iconic Belmond Villa San Michele re-opened in May 2026 after an 18-month long renovation transforming it from just another luxury hotel inhabiting a historic building (albeit in a peerless position) into something truly special. And magically, the architects and designers have achieved this without compromising its unique character. It offers luxurious bedrooms and suites, a magical garden, great food, a pool-with-a-view, and now that all- important spa. The privileged hillside location is well removed from the crowds, yet Florence and all its Renaissance glories is a mere 20-minute ride on the complimentary shuttle bus.
Rooms
The style is monastery chic with the recent restyling raising the stakes considerably adding a new sense of design that was absent before. The 39 bedrooms and suites—split between the main villa and the garden annexes—showcase Tuscan artisans and are supremely comfortable with antique furnishings, fine fabrics by the likes of Rubelli and Loro Piana and exquisite Rivolta Carmignani linens. I stayed in one of the garden suites which have undergone a transformation into botanical wonderlands in shades of green with terracotta floors, rattan furniture, floral tapestries and paintings by local artist Elena Carozzi. Huge bathrooms are done out in stunning swirly green and white ‘cipollino’ marble from Carrara with large tubs and separate walk-in showers. All have private terraces framed by fragrant jasmine; some have gardens.
Food and drink
Executive chef Alessandro Cozzolino oversees three restaurants; the poolside San Michele Grill serves lunch and dinner (pasta, grills, pizza, salads) while tables under the graceful arches of the fabulous Renaissance loggia overlooking the city are split between Michelin-starred Antesi and the more casual San Michele. Antesi offers three multi-course tasting menus based on meat, poultry and game, fish and seafood and vegetables and it is here that Cozzolino puts his creative spin on the traditions of Tuscany and his native Campania. We ate at San Michele; chilled gazpacho topped with raw red prawns, fusillone pasta with wild fennel and anchovies and seared sea bass fillet were all delicious. Carnivores can order a mighty bistecca alla Fiorentina, Florence’s signature dish, char-grilled and carved at your table. Before dinner, we joined other hotel guests and smart Florentines for cocktails on the fabulous garden terrace as we watched the sun go down over the city; it’s achingly romantic.
Breakfast served under the loggia is a sumptuous buffet laid out on a huge oval table with endless sweet and savory options plus avo toast topped with a poached egg, French toast, eggs Benedict and eggs ‘Royale’ with smoked salmon.
Amenities and experiences
The one thing that had always been missing at Villa San Michele was a spa, but with the recent renovation, that has been remedied and now the hotel hosts Italy’s first Guerlain spa, a jewel box of a space decorated with floral murals painted by a local artist. The lovely pool at the top of the property has a generous sunbathing area and fabulous views over the city and there’s a 24/7 gym up there with an al fresco area kitted out with brand new TechnoGym machines. Footpaths wind through the 30,000 square meters of wooded grounds that climb up the hill behind the hotel; there‘s a secluded ‘Energy Chapel’ and Sungazing Terrace (designed in collaboration with JJMartin) where you can take a yoga class or simply enjoy the peace and quiet. Belmond are always brilliant at inventing off-site experiences for guests, and this is no exception; you can explore the local artisan scene, learn about paper marbling and discover the city from a Grand Tour perspective.
What to do nearby
With a history that pre-dates Florence—it was founded by the Etruscans -Fiesole is a pretty hilltop village that lies a 20-minute shuttle bus ride from the centre of Florence. With its caffé-lined piazza shaded by plane trees, Roman amphitheatre, impressive Duomo and several museums, you could easily pass a pleasant day here, and it’s cooler than the city centre. But down the hill lies Renaissance Florence in all its glory; you can nip down to the Uffizi Gallery in the morning, enjoy lunch in town and be back up at the hotel in time for swimming and sunbathing before cocktails and dinner.
The backstory
Dating from the 15th century, and with a facade that was reputedly designed by Michelangelo, Villa San Michele was originally the property of the Davanzati family and then a Franciscan monastery. At one point used by Napoleon as his wartime base, it became a luxury hotel in the 1950s since when it has hosted a constant stream of illustrious guests. It was re-named the Belmond Villa San Michele in 2014.
Insider tip
The 2026 opening season will extend through the Christmas and the New Year holidays for the first time, and the inside spaces have been adapted accordingly. While it’s not unusual to be able to sit outside and enjoy a lunchtime Spritz in November, evenings can be chilly so there are plenty of cosy corners for cooler weather stays.
