### Save your money and sanity—book the Grand Hyatt or literally anywhere else in D.C.

I booked a Park Executive Suite for three nights, expecting the flagship luxury experience the Park Hyatt brand is known for. Instead, I received hardware and service that were genuinely worse than a roadside motel.

The nightmare started with the plumbing: the shower water pressure was practically non-existent, functioning more like a toy water gun. The hairdryer in my "Executive suite" was visually repulsive—the soft-touch handle had degraded into a sticky, peeling mess, and the rear filter was heavily clogged with years of dust.

But the absolute lowest point was the "body wash incident". Before a late-night shower, I realized the large-format body wash bottle was empty. I texted the front desk, and their response was unbelievable: they had no small samples and told me I would simply have to "wait for housekeeping in the morning". Yes, a Park Hyatt told an Executive Suite guest to just go without a shower.

Left with no choice, I had to order my own soap via Uber Eats from a local CVS. Only then did the hotel send their security team on a "scavenger hunt" across departments. What they eventually delivered to my door was shocking: a visibly USED, dirty Le Labo bottle (less than 1/4 full) scavenged from another room, with a pump encrusted in dried soap grime. Distributing used, unhygienic bath amenities to guests is a blatant health and hygiene violation.

To add insult to injury, the front desk explicitly promised via text that management had been notified and would reach out to me. I was in the hotel until 2:00 PM the next day. I was completely ghosted. No response, no apology, nothing. Just a generic, automated "hope you enjoyed your stay" email from the GM days after checkout.

This property is a complete disgrace to the Park Hyatt flag. Save your money and sanity—book the Grand Hyatt or literally anywhere else in D.C.
