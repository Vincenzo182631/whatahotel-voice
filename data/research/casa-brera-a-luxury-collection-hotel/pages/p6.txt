Casa Brera, a Luxury Collection Hotel, Milan
This new refuge in Milan brings together art, design, and a certain sophistication all with the unique style of Patricia Urquiola.

Why book?
The list of reasons to stay at Casa Brera is long but perhaps the best place to start is with the fact that the hotel is a masterpiece of Italian design, albeit with a Spanish pedigree as well since it bears the stamp of
Patricia Urquiola
. The architect and designer has perfectly fused Milanese traditions with contemporary style. In addition, the hotel offers a perfect location to explore the city.
If you need more reasons, we have them: The hotel’s culinary offerings are stellar; the hotel’s bar has mastered the art of the aperitif; and on the rooftop, you’ll find a swimming pool, restaurant, and terrace all with panoramic views.
The neighborhood
The hotel is located on the Piazzetta Maurilio Bossi, a charming pedestrians-only corner of the Brera district, just a few steps from the Teatro alla Scala and the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II. And when we say steps, we mean that literally—it’s a two-minute walk to both. The famous Piazza del Duomo is only about eight minutes away and the Castello Sforzesco and Parco Sempione are about the same distance.
As for the neighborhood, the Brera district is a favorite of both locals and visitors with its unique atmosphere that combines the best of historic Milan with the restless and infectious energy of the city’s creative scene today. The cobblestone streets may be challenging if you’re in stilettos but the reward for navigating them is the opportunity to explore art galleries, cozy restaurants and cafes, designer boutiques, exclusive perfumeries, and must-see sights such as the Pinacoteca di Brera, the Chiesa del Carmine, the Orto Botanico di Brera, and the Biblioteca Nazionale Braidense.

Set the scene
The acclaimed Spanish architect and designer Patricia Urquiola has carried out a careful restoration of the historic 1950s rationalist building by architect Pietro Lingeri, masterfully fusing her characteristic style with the severe style of the original architecture. At Casa Brera, her colorful and sophisticated design appears more timeless than ever, establishing a strong link with the Milanese soul of the place as well as creating a seamless connection between the interior and exterior of the hotel. Urquiola paid homage to the existing building by highlighting its external structure and ensuring that the interior design engages with the orderly geometry of the façade’s vertical lines.
From the lobby to the guest rooms, there’s an exquisite attention to detail with fine finishes, appealing textures, and a color palette carefully chosen to evoke the refined spirit of Milan.
The building’s history is reflected in the geometric patterns that decorate the walls, the floors which look like carpets made of marble, and the distinctive ceilings. Its rationalist pedigree can also be perceived in the careful selection of materials and finishes, with an abundance of Italian marble and granite, a common theme of the design which reflects the aesthetics of the façade.
The floors and ceilings of the Living lounge are a nod to the rationalist geometry and checkerboard pattern of the facade, while strategically placed mirrors amplify the natural light and bring a vibrant dynamism to the space. Large semi-circular sofas, dark blue and light green armchairs, and the leather stools lined up at the large green marble bar create a cozy and sophisticated ambiance. Most of these carefully selected pieces were designed by Urquiola for firms including Cassina, Andreu World, and Moroso.
Casa Brera also celebrates the link between art and design as is evident in the list of works on display at the hotel. There are images by fashion photographer Tim Walker, who captures the essence of the Milan aesthetic, as well as pieces by other contemporary artists who engage with the history of the space and its place in contemporary Milan.
The Cara Brera story continues on the rooftop, where guests will find the Etereo restaurant and a heated outdoor swimming pool with 360º views of the city. There’s also a Japanese restaurant, Odachi, and an Italian one, Scena. On the street level, there’s an outdoor terrace.

The rooms
The hotel’s 101 rooms and 15 suites follow the same philosophy of balancing past and present and celebrate Milan’s art, culture, and design, by combining timeless aesthetics with contemporary nods.
The rooms are noteworthy for their use of fine materials including walnut, Fior di Pesco marble, brass, and patterned glass. Deluxe and Premiere rooms feature Poliform modular furniture and other comfortable pieces, as well as signature artworks.
The Studios with balconies are ideal for families as they offer the option to reserve interconnecting rooms. Some of the touches we liked the most include the mushroom-shaped green ceramic lamps, the geometric wool rugs, and the terra-cotta vases. Junior Suites have a balcony or terrace and feature a modular corner sofa and a mirrored bar. If you are in search of an even more exclusive experience, the Brera and Milanese Suites have private terraces with rooftop views of the city.
The Brera Suite offers the luxury of an exquisitely decorated apartment. There’s a spacious bedroom, a separate living room, and a stunning 1,300-square-foot terrace. Through its imposing floor-to-ceiling glass doors, guests can enjoy the views from the bright blue semi-circular sofa.
The Milanese Suite combines elegance and spaciousness with absolute comfort. Its luminous living room includes a dining area with Dudet armchairs, a signature design by Patricia Urquiola for Cassina. The fluidity between indoors and outdoors is one of the suite’s principal attractions, with two magnificent, interconnected terraces connected to the suite through large glass doors.
The terraces include a cozy sitting area and a dining table for 10 surrounded by lush plantings. On days when the weather is good, the option of an al fresco meal awaits. Inside, the luxurious touches include an en-suite bathroom with walk-in shower, whirlpool tub, and double vanity.

Food and drink
Casa Brera’s culinary direction is provided by the renowned Michelin-starred chef Andrea Berton who created the menus for Living, Scena, and the rooftop Etereo bar and restaurant. Celebrity chef Haruo Ichikawa, the first Japanese Michelin-starred chef in Italy, oversees the menu at Odachi.
The decor at Living is dominated by a palette of orange and dark blue. It’s a lively space where diners can enjoy haute cuisine light bites at any time of day. In the center of the lounge there’s an imposing green marble bar—a tribute to the celebrated Italian architect Piero Portaluppi—that serves as the focal point of the space.
Visitors to Living can enjoy an authentic Milanese aperitivo, either indoors in the elegant atmosphere of the lounge, or outdoors, on the lush terrace overlooking the charming Piazzetta Bossi. Living also hosts a series of events dedicated to the art of the aperitivo, an opportunity to chat, meet, drink, and taste its new Milan-inspired drinks menu titled “Art of Living.”
At the opposite end of the hotel from Living is Odachi, an intimate gastronomic retreat that pays homage to Japanese cuisine. It has a warm and welcoming design thanks to Canaletto walnut floors and walls paired with a mirrored ceiling that evokes the ancient art of Japanese lacquering. Delicate handcrafted lamps hang above the restaurant like floating lanterns, casting a soft orange glow.
In addition to its à la carte menu, Odachi offers an exclusive omakase experience, available by reservation only, where the culinary mastery of its staff is displayed in dishes such as yellowtail tartar; the Korokke Chashu (Japanese-style croquette); the Ika Somen (raw squid noodles); and a varied selection of the very best sushi and sashimi.
At Scena, led by Executive Chef Francesco Bonato, Italian haute cuisine is masterfully reinvented with dishes that highlight the flavors of Italy and, more specifically, Milan. Each carefully prepared offering is paired with a wine selection that ranges from celebrated labels to exclusive options for the most demanding palates. Scena’s interior design fuses rich and warm browns, Persian orange, off-whites, and black tones. Its artistic details include mirrors by Giovanni Botticelli and Paola Paronetto, and an imposing sculpture on one wall by Hans Schule.
On the eighth and top floor of the hotel, Etereo is an exclusive bar and restaurant where cherry red, brown sugar, and sand tones envelop the interior space, which gives way to a breathtaking 360-degree panoramic view over Milan, spanning from the Duomo and the Galleria to the Sforzesco Castle and Porta Nuova. The spectacular rooftop swimming pool is surrounded by chaise lounges and umbrellas. It’s a perfect setting for relaxing while gazing at the city skyline.
Inside the restaurant, the design harmoniously connects the terrace to the impressive red marble bar.
Etereo is open every day from 5:00 p.m. to 1:00 a.m., and serves Italian seafood specialties and signature cocktails, inspired by art, design, and Milan’s fashions.
Health and wellness
The hotel has a fitness center for hotel guests with cardio equipment, weight machines, free weights, and other fitness accessories.
Accessibility
The property has accessible elevators, accessible areas with routes from the main entrance, and accessible entries to the hotel pool and fitness center. The hotel’s restaurants and rooms are also accessible. Casa Brera has accommodations for the hearing impaired and wheelchair accessible showers.
