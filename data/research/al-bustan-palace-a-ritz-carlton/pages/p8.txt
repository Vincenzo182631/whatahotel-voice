Reviewed by
Ramsey Qubein
Why book?
Stay here if you want the trappings of a beach resort close to the Omani capital. For families, especially, it’s an ideal home base for tapping into the country’s cultural and dining scene while balancing it out with a little splash-worthy entertainment.
Set the scene
It is as if you have stepped into a movie set with the Al Hajar mountains as the backdrop and the Oman Sea at the property’s edge. The mix of Arabic and traditional Omani architecture sets this palace apart from almost any other hotel in or near the capital.
Checking in are vacationers from Europe, the Middle East, and a handful of Americans looking for an alternative to glitzy Dubai (or perhaps as a complement to it). The archways, chandeliers, delicate lighting, and intricate design patterns elicit oohs and ahs from all who enter (myself included).
The backstory
The place feels like a palace because it actually is one. Built nearly four decades ago to welcome visiting heads of state, it was converted into a hotel in 1985. That’s when The Ritz-Carlton entered the scene, snapping it up for its portfolio while keeping its original name, Al-Bustan Palace.
Dripping in chandeliers, marble, and gold surfaces, the lobby is luxury at its finest, but you definitely feel a sense of place here. Some might say that many other resorts in the region are fancy for the sake of being fancy. Here, the designers honored the history of this building and the Omani people.
Just glance up to take in the 125-foot dome that towers over arrivals; look back down to discover a tray of welcome Arabic coffee and dates to set the mood.
Outside, gardens burst with blossoming trees or palm fronds, splashing fountains, rows of flowers, and cozy cabanas on the grass for impromptu relaxation. They seemingly go on forever, which should come as no surprise for a hotel named the Garden Palace (“al bustan” means garden in Arabic).
The rooms
Designed with one rounded central building and a wing on either side, each wrapped with a lagoon pool, this building looks like a palace. Inside, its hallways and public spaces feature the resort’s signature, woody oud scent. It’s an enchanting fragrance that almost lures you into different corners of the property to take a look.
While updates are coming that will change the interior look of the rooms, most guests are scanning the exterior skyline for those incredible views. On one side of the building, the dramatic rockface of the mountain range is other-worldly, as if you have landed on another planet. Those on higher floors are the best options, with lower floors typically looking out towards the verdant gardens being the base category (and hardly a poor choice in my opinion, either).
On the other side of the building, you have sea-facing accommodations that, for many people, are the real prizes. Furnished balconies are a lovely spot for morning coffee or evening sundowners. Families will love the ground-floor, lagoon-access rooms that open directly to the lagoon pools.
The resort sees a high number of travelers from GCC (or Gulf Cooperation Council) countries, like Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, and the UAE, and they tend to arrive with big families and even bigger wallets. As a result, there is a higher percentage of suites here, ranging from junior models to full-blown Presidential Suites with a pair of bedrooms plus dining rooms and kitchens. The largest suites can sleep as many as 10 people, but the hotel has plenty of connecting rooms around the building, too.
Bathrooms stock Diptyque products in pump bottles to enjoy from the marble vanity or in the combination tub or shower. A fresh rose in a flower vase is an elegant touch.
Food and drink
If the lobby space impressed you, this is also where afternoon tea and all-day drinks are available. There’s something magical about sipping a drink surrounded by these soaring ceilings, enormous flower arrangements, and crystal chandeliers.
Dining options are expansive here; it seems like many guests hang around for three meals a day. There’s enough variety for that with Chinese, Omani and Italian restaurants plus a few international and buffet venues. There is also one restaurant just for women 18 years and older (another indication of the large GCC contingent that stays here).
The quality and presentation is top-tier, something evident in the number of non-guests that often book in for reservations here, too.
The spa
Following the same design style as the public areas in what looks like an Omani fort, Al Bustan Spa offers six hammam treatment rooms plus separate relaxation areas for men and women. On the menu is the Sense of Oman service, which is a full-body massage that comes with a date body scrub, and Royal Arabian Splendor, which packages together an authentic hamman experience and a facial. There is also a 24-hour fitness center.
The neighborhood/area
This seaside resort has a whole beach vacation vibe, but you are close enough to Muscat (a 20-minute car ride) to make day trips in and around the city to museums and for shopping. Smart travelers will split their days, especially during the warmer summer months, heading into town in the morning for exploration and returning to the hotel to relax by the pool when the sun is at its most scorching.
One floor below the lobby is a shopping arcade for those who prefer not to visit the city’s souk itself.
A private beach comes lined with umbrellas and towel-covered chairs with doting service from Ritz-Carlton staff. You can order all the cocktails, mocktails or food items you want here, and guests at the main pools or beach tend to be more liberal than conservative in their bathing suit choices.
There are six pools in all at the property, one of which is reserved for ladies (another reason many GCC guests really like this resort). Women also have their own private beach area.
Lagoon pools wrap around the bottom floor of hotel rooms, ringing the building, while the main pool features palm tree islands and flowing fountains, stretching all the way from the central building to the sea. The family-friendly pool is the most lively, while the infinity-edge pool attracts mostly adults reading books, napping, or snapping photos. Honestly, the backdrop could turn almost anyone into a social media influencer.
The service
Doting, but never overly so. The Omani people are some of the most hospitable you will meet, and that is just as true here. I was especially impressed with the number of languages the staff speaks here (everyone I met spoke excellent English), but there is also a large international contingent of employees.
For families
Their own pool, a private beach, and acres of gardens…what more could you ask for than that? There’s also a kids club to keep them busy, and a playground and kids' splash area, which sees a lot of action.
Anything left to mention?
The hotel is closing for the second half of 2026 to complete a full renovation of its interior spaces with plans to open by 2028.
