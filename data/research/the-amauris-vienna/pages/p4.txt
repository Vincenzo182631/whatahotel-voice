The Amauris Vienna - Relais & Châteaux
An intimate, built-in-1860 palais on Vienna’s landmark-strewn Ringstrasse

Reviewed by

Why book?
For a glorious home base to return to after long days spent hopping between
Vienna
’s museums and opulent coffeehouses. Numerous hotels line the grand, leafy boulevard of Ringstrasse—but The Amauris, part of the Relais & Châteaux collection, beckons with an Italian marble-swathed interior, a trove of Austrian art from the early 1900s, and a destination-worthy restaurant beloved by locals.
Set the scene:
Beyond the corner building’s majestic façade, Croatian designer Nikola Arambašić embraced more than 160 tons of sleek marble—a mix of Carrara Bianco, Calacatta Viola, Guatemala Verde, Nero Marquina, and Rosso Levanto. It’s a fittingly hushed, yet elegant backdrop for artistic marvels like the pristine reproduction of Michelangelo’s Venus in the lobby that recalls the sitting room of a private manse or Croatian artist Klara Rusan’s fantastical mural meandering up the stairwell.
Serge Mouille lamps and furniture from the likes of Roche Bobois and Cassina infuse The Amauris, an ode to the butterfly species of the same name, with a decidedly contemporary air, but its Old-World appeal is undeniable. Take the elevator, a turn-of-the-century cast-iron treasure or the mélange of paintings set in gilded frames plucked from the owner’s personal collection—including works by the prominent Alexander Rothaug—that give the two restaurants and select suites the feel of a cozy parlor. This graceful fusion of past and present beckons to couples in search of a romantic urban holiday, chilled-out families and discerning business travelers alike.
The backstory:
Most recently, The Amauris was formerly known as The Ring Hotel, but entrepreneur Johann Breiteneder, CEO of family-run Breiteneder Immobilien Parking AG, saw the potential of transforming it into a swank boutique getaway smack in the center of the city. Vienna is just the beginning for The Amauris brand; its second European outpost is already under construction in Split, on Croatia’s sunny, scenic Dalmatian Coast.
The rooms:
Hefty marble doors open onto the 62 rooms and suites at The Amauris. Devoid of handles, they deftly blend into the walls, instilling the corridors with the reverent aura of a museum. Inside these accommodations, crisp hues of black, white, and gray are invigorated with bold splashes of blue, red, or green and eye-catching sculptures mingle with board games like chess, coffee table tomes dedicated to such visionaries as Albrecht Dürer, and striking wall coverings courtesy of Arambašić that magnify some of the city’s architectural wonders, including Schönbrunn Palace. Plush canopy beds and roomy bathrooms brightened with fresh blooms and Acqua di Parma toiletries promise much-needed rejuvenation.
There are superior, deluxe, and grand deluxe rooms to choose from as well as an array of suites, the most coveted of which are the duplex Grand Maisonettes starring freestanding bathtubs, the Loft suite that looks onto St. Charles Church, and the nearly 1,300-square-foot Opera suite. Complete with a monumental Murano chandelier, centerpiece balcony, and elaborate, original stucco reliefs, the space once housed the manager’s office when The Amauris welcomed Viennese as a bank.
Food and drink:
There will be plenty of time to gorge on schnitzel and tafelspitz. For a more refined and adventurous introduction to Viennese cooking, a dinner at fine dining restaurant Glasswing is essential. Here, chef Alexandru Simon presides over the open kitchen, centering the seasonal tasting menus on ingredients largely sourced from local farmers and producers. Look for delightfully clever contrasts like zucchini and cherry, traditional Austrian dumplings reimagined with shabu shabu-style Wagyu, and a bountiful bread service with roasted onion and caramel-tinged butters. Come morning, the tables and curving gray booths are where à la carte breakfasts (try the truffled scrambled eggs and a grape and spirulina smoothie) unfold. More casual meals are the domain of Glasswing Bar & Bistro, which also whips up classic cocktails like the Negroni, best savored as a nightcap at one of the tables nestled into the Ringstrasse-facing windows.
The spa:
Capped in a glass roof, the serene guest-only spa is found in The Amauris’s courtyard. While there are treatment rooms, as well as a fitness center with Technogym equipment, sauna, and steam bath, the jewel is the wellness pool buoyed by massage jets. Revive on one of the loungers after winding your way through the nearby Albertina’s heady exhibitions.
The neighborhood/area:
Conveniently straddling the Renaissance Revival Vienna State Opera and the Musikverein hall, site of the Vienna Philharmonic’s famed annual New Year’s concert, The Amauris is an ideal base for classical music fans. Must-visit sites, like the newly revamped Wien Museum, red sofa-lined Café Museum, and perpetually busy Bitzinger Sausage Stand, are also mere minutes away, as is Kärntner Strasse, the tourist-thronged shopping street leading to the Gothic St. Stephen’s Cathedral. The surrounding hubbub amplifies just how quietly inviting the hotel is.
The service:
Reception desks are low-slung and seamlessly integrated into the lobby, setting a tone of discretion that permeates The Amauris. Professional staff are affable but not intrusive (at Glasswing they are impressively knowledgeable about the dishes), and conscientious concierges are eager to bring guests’ dreams to life. Once, to celebrate a 50th wedding anniversary, The Amauris organized a horse-drawn carriage ride, stopping at the locations the guests visited on their first date decades ago.
Eco effort:
Sustainability is taken seriously at The Amauris, which is outfitted with energy-efficient lighting and a hydrating and cooling system that reduces CO2 emissions. Keycards are fashioned out of cardboard and water is served in glass bottles. The kitchen’s zero-waste approach is also showcased in the former wine cellar, a subterranean space converted into a pantry for dry-aging meat and fish that holds plentiful jars of preserved produce to satiate diners all year long. A forthcoming rooftop garden dotted with beehives will strengthen the close-knit food system.
Accessibility:
In addition to the antique elevator, there is a commodious modern one along with a smattering of ADA-accessible guest rooms.
