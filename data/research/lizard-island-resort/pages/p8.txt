Secluded on 1,000 hectares of tawny bush, and ringed by sugar-white beaches

Reviewed by
Chloe Sachdev
Why book?
To be on one of Australia’s most exclusive islands with easy access to the Great Barrier Reef.
Set the scene
Totally secluded on its on 1,000 hectares of national park, ringed by sugar-white beaches, Lizard Island is one of the only resorts In Australia located literally on the Great Barrier Reef. An easy 10-minute swim will have you on the fringing reef, with slivers of multi-coloured fish, gliding turtles and epic coral formations. There are manicured gardens and tawny bush, a turquoise lagoon, and spectacular hikes. The whole place vibrates with scuttering critters, including its namesake dragon-like monitor lizards, which are gentler than they look.
The backstory
Lizard Island was declared a National Park in 1937, the surrounding waters were recognised as a National Marine Park in 1974, and the Great Barrier Reef was listed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 1981. In 2009, Delaware North purchased the resort from Voyages Hotels. In 2021, Andrew “Twiggy” Forrest, the same billionaire who also owns R.M. Williams, purchased the land in 2021, while Delaware North continues to operate all aspects of Lizard Island Resort. The House on Lizard Island is owned privately by Steve and Jane Wilson. Delaware North and Lizard Island Resort operate and service The House, looking after all operational requirements and guests who come to stay.
The rooms
Each of the 40 villas and suites are bright and airy with high ceilings, some with wide-frame views out to the lapping blue sea, others wrapped in a tropical garden. The bigger suites are standalone white-weatherboard cabins on the oceanfront manicured lawn and framed by swaying palms. Out of sight, on its own jagged outcrop of granite rocks, stands ‘The House’. A brutalist concrete and copper 4-bedroom private villa, surrounded by its own slivers of white-sand beach and beyond that the Coral Sea, this is Australia’s plumiest beach house.
The neighborhood
Two hundred kilometers north of Cairns and totally secluded, the island is the neighbourhood, and the native birds, critters, and sea life are the locals.
Food and drink
Excellent. This is an all-inclusive island. Most meals are in the open-air Salt Water restaurant, which feels a bit like a breezy wraparound veranda. The menu is top-tier and uses as much local produce from the ocean and nearby hinterland as possible. An a la carte breakfast with everything from avocado on toast, eggs done all ways, and fluffy wattleseed pancakes is followed by long multi-course lunches. Dinner – a la carte or degustation – is a smart affair with guests gussied up in neatly pressed shirts and floaty dresses. For more down-tempo casual, at the end of the boardwalk is Marlin Bar. Here, it’s all about casual bites like burgers, sandwiches, frosty beers, and cocktails. It’s also a public bar, so as well as off-duty staff, you’ll also find seafaring passersby enjoying a night on dry land.
The service
Friendly but professional. This is a luxury resort, and the service reflects this. A lot of staff have worked at other Luxury Lodges, and exude the finesse of career hospitality staff.
Who comes here?
Well-heeled retirees, loved up honeymooners, and couples celebrating anniversaries.
For families?
Lizard Island caters to guests aged 10 and above.
Eco-effort?
Due to its UNESCO status, the resort adheres to some of the most stringent operating requirements in Australia, managing and reporting daily waste management, potable water testing, administering strict biosecurity measures, monitoring water consumption, and managing annual visitor numbers. 90 percent of all food supplies on Lizard Island come from local Queensland suppliers. Around 60 percent of all waste from the resort operation is either recycled or deployed for alternative use. Biodigestors have been installed across the resort, eliminating the need to transport food waste to the mainland.
Accessibility?
The Beachfront Suite is accessible – i.e. wheelchair access, handrails, and accessible toilet and shower.
Anything else to mention?
Best to reserve your activities before you get there, including snorkelling, diving, and touring the research facility.
Is it worth it?
Yes. This feels like your own private slice of paradise.
