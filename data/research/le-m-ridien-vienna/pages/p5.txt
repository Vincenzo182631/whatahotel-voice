Reviewed by
Sophie Friedman
Why book?
To be able to walk to nearly every museum in Vienna and be flanked by green spaces, and rack up (or use) Marriott Bonvoy points, book Le Meridien Vienna. Don’t book for personalized service; this is a business hotel that’s as busy as Vienna Central Station.
Set the scene
This is a nearly 300-room property in the UNESCO-listed Innere Stadt, Vienna’s Old Town, and as such, you have every stripe of traveler staying here. On a morning walk through the breakfast buffet, we heard Arabic, Mandarin Chinese, Hindi, German, French, Spanish, and English. Some people came down in suits, another in a bathrobe.
There are clearly repeat guests—business travelers from the looks of their newspapers and tablets—who know the staff. This is a busy hotel; people are streaming in and out nonstop, and it definitely falls into the “comfortable place to sleep” camp rather than “a place to hang out.” There is essentially nowhere to sit in the lobby, though guests waiting for someone could conceivably perch in the adjacent YOU restaurant.
The backstory
The hotel opened in 2003, a combination of a 1960s building and a palais from 1860. The only difference between rooms in the historical wing and the others is ceiling height—10 feet vs 8.5 or so.
The rooms
When you book a mega chain hotel, you go in with a certain guarantee of uniformity. That’s certainly the case at Le Meridien Vienna, where the tidy, modern decor is reassuringly identical in every room: an island of blond hardwood under the platform bed flanked by black and white abstract carpet, greige armchair, chaise, and/or bench.
The headboards, however, are unique to the hotel and quite cool: behind each bed is a panel of glass etched with an iconically Viennese image (a coffee cup, a horse from the Spanish Riding School, a window at St. Stephen's Cathedral). These double as a lamp and add a nice sense of place.
Rooms overlook either the cityscape (including onto Schiller Park and the late-19th-century Academy of Fine Arts) or the “courtyard”, aka the opposite side of the building and the roof of the lobby/restaurant. “Courtyard” view rooms are quite dim. If you’re not too tall and want a cozier feeling room, we recommend those on the top floor with garret windows. The Rooftop Terrace category rooms offer their namesake amenity, though note that the windows can be opened in all rooms, so you can still get a bit of fresh air.
There is definite room for improvement: there’s little storage for clothing, the rooms lack outlets—especially next to the bed—and the bathroom design is not ideal for anyone sharing a room. The frosted walls mean that if you turn on the light at night, the light shines into the bedroom. The sink is an oversized glass bowl, and in the entry-level rooms, there is barely any counter space for toiletries. There are no hooks for towels, just a narrow metal rack. We put up the do not disturb sign on arrival, but the room was still tidied the next day—a small faux pas, no harm done, but then what’s the point of do not disturb? Some suites have Dyson hairdryers, the holy grail for anyone with long, thick hair, but unfortunately, there is no way to book a suite with one.
Food and drink
Expect an ample breakfast buffet, with an impressive bread corner (unfortunately, its popularity and corner location mean access to the bread and toaster is a plate-juggling dance). Coffee is from a machine, and it’s not bad, especially as only instant is available in the room. Despite the steady stream of guests plowing through the buffet, the staff have it under control.
The hotel’s restaurant and bar, YOU, serve standard Continental fare with some Austrian dishes like schnitzel, and it’s all solid enough if you’re too tired to go out. And, perhaps owing to the many business travelers who stay here, the restaurant was
packed
at 10 p.m. on a Friday, every one of the forest green velvet seats filled.
The neighborhood/area
You’re staying at Le Meridien for the ultra-central location. Though Vienna has an excellent metro system (and the Karlplatz station is near the hotel), you’ll scarcely need it; nothing is more than a 20-minute walk from the hotel (that’s Belvedere Castle), with most sights less than 10 minutes away.
And though the Innere Stadt is where most tourists stay, Vienna isn’t rammed like Paris; there are plenty of Viennese out and about, especially at the neighborhood’s cultural heavyweights. The Opera House is scarcely five minutes from the hotel, as are the Albertine museum and the contemporary Heidi Horten Collection. The city regularly takes the cake for the world’s most livable in part thanks to its parks, and there are four surrounding the hotel.
Coffeehouse culture is intrinsic to Vienna, and 10 minutes from the hotel is perhaps the city’s most unique cafe, Vollpension. Here, the bakers are seniors, the decor is straight out of your grandparents’ house, and the idea is that these silver-haired bakers flex their culinary skills and that younger and not-so-young people can chit-chat. Fighting loneliness while eating cake (truly a win-win).
The service
This is a super-busy hotel in Vienna, and the service reflects that: It’s pleasantly efficient. No one books a 300-room Marriott for personalized service. The staff power through the waves of check-outs and check-ins each day as quickly as they can, but if you have an early flight or train in the morning, leave an extra five minutes for checkout.
Accessibility
The hotel’s public spaces are all wheelchair accessible, including a registration/concierge desk, and the Executive King rooms are accessible. Cabs can pull right up outside the hotel, which is on a flat street, and there are no obstacles between the entrance and the elevator. On our visit, we saw the concierge call a wheelchair-accessible taxi for a group.
All of the rooms can be reached using the elevator except for a handful on the top floor, so double-check with the hotel if you want to avoid stairs completely.
Anything left to mention?
The rooms that face the so-called courtyard are pretty dim, but if you plan to be out all day, they’re good value for money.
