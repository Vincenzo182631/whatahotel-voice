The Palace, a Luxury Collection Hotel
The iconic Palace hotel in Madrid returns to its grande dame status following a complete renovation by Ruiz Larrea Arquitectura with Lázaro Rosa-Violán entrusted with the interiors. The end result is a beautiful love letter to Madrid.

We’ve said it before.
Castizo
—a term used to refer to the places, people, and objects that embody
Madrid
’s unique culture—should be included on UNESCO’s list of Intangible Cultural Heritage. Perhaps it could help save Spain’s capital from the homogenizing influence of a certain ubiquitous international cosmopolitanism. While the opening of hotels from many global luxury brands in the last few years has been exciting and has helped to put the city on the maps of even more travelers, there’s a danger that the much-celebrated character of Madrid may be lost.
The Palace hotel, on the other hand, has long been and continues to be a perfect example of castizo. And it has also now become, once again, a luxury hotel the lives up to the high bar set by its original incarnation. Following its complete renovation, it’s time to welcome the reborn Palace, a Luxury Collection Hotel, a property that’s been a protagonist and a witness to many moments in Madrid’s history since it opened its doors in 1912.
Why book
Because of its location, in the heart of Madrid and more specifically in the middle of the Paisaje de Luz, or Landscape of Light, which has been recognized by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site. Opposite the hotel stands its sibling and rival, the Mandarin Oriental Ritz, which opened a couple of years earlier (in 1910). It also faces the recently restored Neptune Fountain, and the Thyssen-Bornemisza and the Prado museums are also nearby. It is not only surrounded by buildings that have been the stages where much of Madrid’s history has played out, when you walk through the front door of the hotel, you are entering an establishment that has been part of that history, too.
The backstory
The story of the construction and the early years of The Palace could be the basis for a very long article on its own. The hotel’s reinforced concrete construction was revolutionary for its time and it was Spain’s first hotel (and the second in the world) to have private toilets in every room which was a colossal feat of engineering (and plumbing). It was also the first hotel in Spain to have telephones in every room. Soon after opening, it would become one of the favorite places to stay in Madrid for Europe’s upper classes. During World War I, Spain’s neutrality meant that the hotel welcomed royalty and the elite seeking an escape from the conflict.
Later the hotel’s bar would become a gathering place for the leading intellectual figures of Spain in the 1920s and 1930s. Poet Federico García Lorca, painter Salvador Dalí, and film director Luis Buñuel were among the luminaries to raise a drink at the Palace. During the Spanish civil war, from 1936 to 1939, it became a field hospital and its beautiful stained-glass dome with its vibrant rainbow of colors illuminated the make-shift operating rooms. In the post-war period it shone again as a glittering hotel, with Hemingway (practically an adopted son of Madrid) wandering its halls. Numerous Hollywood stars also made The Palace their home on visits to Spain. If these walls could talk, they’d have impressive tales to tell.
The hotel
If you’ve been to Madrid recently, you may have already noticed the meticulous restoration of the hotel’s façade by
Ruiz Larrea Arquitectura
. The effort involved work on an area of more than 86,000 square feet, including a thorough scientific analysis to determine the original colors and materials. The iconic “Palace color,” is a soft beige that contrasts with the terracotta-painted details. All the garlands and floral decorations have been individually restored for the first time in decades.
Once inside, the other great jewel of the building is its wrought iron and glass dome, designed by Eduardo Ferrés i Puig in 1912, and executed by the master glassmakers at the Mauméjean Brothers studio who also collaborated with the architect Antoni Gaudí. It now shines thanks to the painstaking restoration, which required dismantling 1,875 pieces of glass, restoring each one, and cataloging the pattern of the dome. The project included more than one hundred specialists in a workshop created on site. The most complex pieces to repair were sent to a studio in Barcelona that is also working on the stained-glass windows of that city’s Sagrada Familia basilica. A detail that every guest should look for: The emblematic glass lamp in the shape of a palm tree, which in recent years has welcomed visitors in the lobby, has been returned to its original place, under the center of the dome.
Acclaimed Barcelona interior designer
Lázaro Rosa-Violán
was responsible for the interior spaces and he was clearly inspired by the legend of the hotel and some of its most famous guests, including Picasso, Mata Hari, Dalí, Lorca, and Hemingway. The carpets, custom designed by the studio’s artistic team, allow guests to walk through a dreamlike world in the corridors, with art works hanging on the walls completing the scene. Rosa-Violán has created a mythological Madrid that draws inspiration from the earlier era of Goya as much as it does from the avant-garde of the 20th century.
The rooms
With 470 rooms and suites, The Palace is the largest luxury hotel in Madrid. Every one of them has been reborn thanks to the intervention of Rosa-Violán. For this First Check-in, the first visit by any media outlet in the world, we stayed in room 523, the Neptuno Suite, and the sensation was that of traveling back to an idealized past. It felt like we stepped inside an elegant bourgeois Madrid house from the mid-20th century with its restored marble fireplace, wood paneling, books perfectly lined up on the shelves, Art Deco-inspired furniture, and beautiful wallpaper above the headboard of the bed. All the wallpapers are inspired by the landscapes of the nearby Retiro Park, just as the mosaics in the showers evoke a bird's-eye view of the Royal Botanic Gardens. From the reddish velvet furnishings to the parquet herringbone wood floors, everything exudes warmth. The traditional elements are combined with the latest in-room technology.
Frette
sheets from Italy line the beds and the bathroom amenities are from Le Chemin by Byredo, created exclusively for Marriott’s Luxury Collection hotels. You’ll likely want to take some home with you, and you can: The Luxury Collection has a wonderful
online shop
for anyone who wants to turn their home into a hotel suite. There you can buy everything from mattresses to bed linens, pillows, products by
Byredo
, towels, bathrobes, and more.
Food and drink
Portuguese chef
Nuno Matos
makes his debut as an executive chef after stints at Portuguese hotels including Six Senses Douro Valley and Aethos Ericeira. He has the task of breathing new life into La Cúpula, which now more than ever is the social epicenter of the hotel. The menu is defined by its use of local produce, simple preparations, and embrace of tradition. Classic snacks such as Russian salad, croquettes, gildas (a Basque pintxo of olives, anchovies, and pickled peppers), and Iberian ham coexist with an excellent selection of caviar, fresh tomato salad with lobster, peppers, and strawberries and perfectly prepared fish of the day such as sea bass and turbot. Pork tenderloin with clam sauce, excellent cuts of meat, and a beef Wellington intended to be shared are classic dishes befitting a classic hotel.
Cocktails were central to life at the original Palace and the hotel’s bars remain committed to the classics, with some updates. The superb dry martini includes a splash of chamomile while La Violetera pays homage to Madrid’s traditional violet candies. These drinks can be enjoyed not only at the bar in La Cúpula, but also in the renovated and legendary bar, now called 27 Club—a tribute to the Generation of ‘27, a name for the group of Spanish intellectuals who regularly frequented the bar in the years before Spain’s civil war. The Palace plans to return to those heady days with talks and gatherings with artists, writers, painters, and other cultural figures.
Wellness
The gym, which is a little over 1,200 square feet, is located on the top floor, almost touching the Madrid sky, and includes stationary bikes, treadmills, stair climbers, rowing machines, as well as strength equipment, weights, saunas, and changing rooms.
Anything else to add?
The Palace is known for its event spaces, roughly 27,500 square feet of them spread over 20 rooms (the largest is more than 7,500 square feet). The recent refurbishment included all of them as well. Finally,
Juanjo Oliva
designed the stylish new uniforms for the whole team, which now numbers around a hundred employees.
