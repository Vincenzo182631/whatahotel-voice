## The Building Behind the Hotel

In 1927, architect Emery Roth completed a 26-story building on Lexington Avenue designed to feel more like a Manhattan residence than a commercial tower. A year later, Georgia O’Keeffe captured its silhouette in New York Night, one of her most celebrated works.

Nearly a century on, The Benjamin Royal Sonesta Hotel occupies that same building, renovated by DLR Group in 2024 around two design inspirations: Benjamin, represented in bold architectural details, and Beverly, the building’s original 1927 name rendered in softer, gilded curves. The hotel’s mascot, Oscar, The King of New York, is drawn from the original building facade.

#### What neighborhood is The Benjamin Royal Sonesta in?

The Benjamin Royal Sonesta is located on Lexington Avenue in Midtown East, one block from Grand Central Terminal, within walking distance of Fifth Avenue, Rockefeller Center, and the Theater District. It’s the quieter, more residential side of Midtown.

#### What is the Rest & Renew Sleep Program?

The Rest & Renew Sleep Program is The Benjamin Royal Sonesta’s signature in-room wellness experience, designed for guests who want to sleep better in New York City than they do at home.

Choose from 12 pillow options (down, memory foam, buckwheat, and more). For guests who want a deeper level of support, a one-on-one sleep consultant program is available, as is the Suite Dreams Royal Sleep Retreat, a bookable package that brings the full Rest & Renew experience together.

The program has been featured in Bloomberg and amNewYork. Adjacent wellness offerings include the Royal Reflections Wellness Spa and the Kids Sleep Program, developed with sleep expert Dr. Rebecca Robbins for younger guests.

#### Is The Benjamin Royal Sonesta good for families?

The Benjamin Royal Sonesta is one of the most family-accommodating hotels in Midtown Manhattan, starting with the rooms themselves.

Because the building was originally designed as apartments by architect Emery Roth in 1927, the guest rooms are genuinely larger than standard Manhattan hotel rooms.

In-room kitchenettes, pull-out sofa beds, and suite configurations that separate sleeping and living areas make longer family stays comfortable and practical.

#### Are the rooms at The Benjamin Royal Sonesta quiet?

The Benjamin Royal Sonesta is located on Lexington Avenue, and like most Midtown Manhattan hotels, some rooms face an active city street.

Guests who prefer a quieter room are encouraged to request an interior-facing room or a higher floor at the time of booking, both of which significantly reduce ambient street noise.

The hotel’s 2024 renovation by DLR Group included updated window treatments and blackout shading throughout, and the Rest & Renew Sleep Program’s white-noise options provide an additional layer of sound management for light sleepers.

#### What makes The Benjamin Royal Sonesta different from other Midtown Manhattan hotels?

The Benjamin Royal Sonesta occupies a 26-story building designed by architect Emery Roth in 1927, originally conceived as apartments, which is the reason the guest rooms are genuinely larger than those found in most Midtown hotels.

That scale is structural, not incidental. The 2024 renovation by DLR Group modernized the interiors while preserving the building’s residential character, adding herringbone wood floors, walk-in showers, and motion-sensing lighting throughout.

The Rest & Renew Sleep Program, featuring a 12-option pillow menu, plush linens, and a sleep consultant program, is a further differentiator that reflects the hotel’s focus on restorative, unhurried hospitality in the center of Midtown East.

#### Does The Benjamin Royal Sonesta have meeting space?

Yes. The Benjamin Royal Sonesta offers dedicated boardrooms and flexible multi-use event spaces equipped with AV support and in-house catering — one block from Grand Central Terminal, making it straightforward for attendees arriving from across the city or the region.

The spaces are sized and staffed for corporate groups that want the focus and atmosphere of a boutique property rather than the impersonal scale of a convention hotel.
