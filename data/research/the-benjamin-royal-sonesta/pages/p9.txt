The Benjamin Royal Sonesta New York
A landmark Midtown East hotel where the suites are genuinely spacious, diplomats check in for the unbeatable location, and everyone sleeps better than they do at home.

Best for:
Business travelers, diplomats, girls weekends, families, anyone staying longer than a night or two in Midtown
Location:
The corner of 50th and Lexington in Midtown East, one block from Grand Central and a short walk from Fifth Avenue and Rockefeller Center
Can't-miss feature:
The Royal Reflection Wellness Spa, where the treatments are both therapeutic and pampering
In a nutshell:
A 1927 Emery Roth landmark reborn after a top-to-bottom renovation, The Benjamin pairs genuinely spacious suites with one of Midtown's more charming restaurants, The Parliament. The location is unbeatable, one block from Grand Central, and the hotel counts UN delegations, international executives, and the occasional head of state among its regulars.
Pallavi Mohan Kumar
Pallavi Mohan Kumar
Rooms
I've stayed at a few
New York hotels
, and can vouch for space being the real gift at The Benjamin. I checked into the Balcony Suite with my sister, and it was more generous than almost anything I've encountered in this city at this level. It features a separate living area, a kitchenette for guests who like to be a little more hands-on, and a private balcony looking over Midtown, where we did the full girly evening, with robes, Champagne, face masks, and
Jurassic Park
on TV. A two-year, multi-million-dollar renovation touched everything from the lobby to the 209 rooms, adding herringbone floors, custom desks, and a soft palette that fits right into the neighborhood.
If you're splurging, the two penthouses are named for the building's twin personalities. The Benjamin is the larger and more architectural; The Beverly is the artistic one, all marble with gilded curves and a corner terrace—it's the one I'd book.

Food and drink
The on-site restaurant,
The Parliament
, is an ode to the hotel's royal mascot, Oscar the Owl (in case you didn't know, a group of owls is called a parliament). With its own entrance on Lexington, red leather banquettes, and owl-shaped motifs throughout, it's a proper destination of its own. Chef Rakmin Lee cooks modern American with Korean, French, and Italian threads running through it. At dinner, we shared the warm artichoke dip and San Marzano margherita flatbread before the comforting herb-roasted chicken and grilled branzino.
We finished with the old-fashioned Parliament Sundae, which arrives with warm malted chocolate over Il Laboratorio del Gelato vanilla, a crunch of peanuts, and cherries on top. Weekend brunch is also a main event, pulling locals, bridal parties, out-of-towners, and visiting delegates; the decadent breakfast sandwich alone is worth the trip.
Amenities and experiences
We tried out the
Rest and Renew program
, the hotel’s complimentary sleep program, throughout our two-night stay. It includes an on-demand meditation channel, a stack of books left by the bed, and a pillow menu (which I ordered from with housekeeping twice).
The spa delivers serious bodywork along with an impressive menu of therapies: My 60-minute myofascial release involved a wooden tool that worked firmly along the muscles, closer to physical therapy than pampering—I walked out feeling looser than I'd been in months. Book it if you want the knots gone, or book the lymphatic drainage, like my sister, if you want something more relaxing, or, as she described, “muscle melting.” There's also a 24-hour fitness center.
The kitchenettes in each suite make longer stays more comfortable in a way that feels like home.
What to do nearby
It doesn't get more central than this. You're one block from Grand Central, which puts most of the city and both airports within easy reach. Fifth Avenue, St. Patrick's Cathedral, and Rockefeller Center are a 10-minute walk; the
MoMA
is barely 15, and Summit One Vanderbilt, the mirrored observation deck rising directly above Grand Central, is glamorous and practically around the corner.
And when the city gets loud,
Central Park
's quieter southeast corner is 10 blocks north. Pick up provisions, let the kitchenette become a dance party as you assemble cucumber sandwiches (like we did), and picnic by the pond.
Pallavi Mohan Kumar
Pallavi Mohan Kumar
The backstory
The Benjamin opened in 1927 as The Beverly, an Emery Roth design and part of Terminal City, the constellation of hotels built around the new-at-the-time Grand Central. It's now a designated
New York City landmark.
A $25 million renovation was completed in 2024.
Insider tip
Order from the pillow menu. It's the sort of amenity most guests read about, smile at, and never use. But there are 10 robust options, from buckwheat to water-filled, and I mixed and matched them like I was building a tasting flight. Pair that with a bed so enormous that you could get lost in it, blackout curtains that turn noon into midnight, and the on-demand meditation channel, and you might find yourself sleeping better than you have in years. (I certainly did.) For a hotel that keeps an owl as its mascot, it is quite good at getting people to sleep.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Yes, with connecting rooms, cribs, and a kids sleep program.

accessibility page

offers serious therapeutic treatments. Plus, there's a 24-hour fitness center.
