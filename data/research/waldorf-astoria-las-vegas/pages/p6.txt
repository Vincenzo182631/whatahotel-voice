Waldorf Astoria Las Vegas
A hushed, high-service sanctuary in the middle of the Strip, now freshly renovated and better at arrival, cocktails, and lingering than ever before.

Reviewed by
Andrea Bennett
Best for:
Travelers who want serious luxury and service without a casino, smoke, or nightclub soundtrack following them into the elevator.
Location:
Center Strip at CityCenter, connected by sky bridge to The Shops at Crystals and an easy walk to Aria, Cosmopolitan, and T-Mobile Arena.
Can’t-miss feature:
The combination of a genuinely serene pool and spa scene with a revamped 23rd floor—Peacock Alley for tea and champagne, Hard Shake for cocktails with a view.
In a nutshell:
One of the Strip’s most polished quiet-luxury addresses, with a full property refresh, standout service, and the rare ability to let you dip into
Vegas
only when you feel like it.
Rooms
All 389 guest rooms and suites have been redesigned in a desert-inspired palette of soft neutrals, warm woods, and jewel-tone accents that make them feel more residential than hotel. Floor-to-ceiling windows remain one of the main draws, framing dramatic views over the Strip or the surrounding desert basin without bringing any of the noise inside. Standard rooms are calm and beautifully equipped, with intuitive bedside controls, tea kettles, and deep soaking tubs positioned to make the skyline part of the bathroom design. Bathrooms are especially strong after the renovation—lighter, brighter, and more spacious-feeling, with marble, natural light, and tubs that invite you to schedule your evening around them. If price is not the first concern, the larger suites are where the residential mood really lands, giving you enough space to settle in and treat the hotel less like a base and more like a retreat. This is where many locals book a staycation, because this feels the most like an escape of perhaps all the Strip’s hotels.
Food and drink
Waldorf Astoria is not trying to out-restaurant Bellagio or Cosmo, and that is part of its appeal. The biggest food-and-drink update is the 23rd floor, where Peacock Alley now serves the brand’s signature tea service in a glamorous setting, while Hard Shake gives the hotel a proper cocktail destination with a little theater and a lot more nightlife energy than it used to have. Tea here is still one of the best daytime rituals on the Strip—tiered trays, rare teas, and a room that encourages you to slow down on purpose. The upside of staying small is that you are also minutes from Aria, Cosmopolitan, and Crystals, so if you want Carbone-level reservations or a louder bar scene, they are right there without having to sleep above them.
Amenities and experiences
The pool has been and still is one of the most civilized in Las Vegas: no blaring DJ set, no aggressive bottle parade, just a compact, elegant deck with cabanas and loungers. The spa is another major draw, spanning two floors with treatment rooms, thermal facilities, and a quieter atmosphere than most Strip wellness spaces. Fitness is taken seriously here too, with a full gym and yoga studio plus classes included through the resort fee. Service is what consistently sets the place apart, though—personal concierges, twice-daily housekeeping, and that old Mandarin-era precision still lingering in small gestures that make the whole stay feel unusually considered.
What to do nearby
Waldorf’s location is ideal if you want access without immersion. You can walk straight into Crystals for luxury shopping, cross to Aria for dining and gaming, or continue toward Cosmopolitan and Bellagio without needing a car. T-Mobile Arena and the Park MGM side of the Strip are also within comfortable reach, which makes this a good choice for concert or hockey nights when you still want to come home to somewhere calm. The point of staying here, though, is that leaving the hotel always feels optional rather than necessary.
The backstory
The hotel opened in 2009 as Mandarin Oriental Las Vegas and became Waldorf Astoria Las Vegas in 2018, retaining its non-gaming, non-smoking identity even as the branding changed. For a while it still felt architecturally and emotionally like the Mandarin, especially with the old sky-lobby arrival sequence. The recent property-wide renovation finally completed the transition: the lobby moved to the ground floor, public spaces were rethought, rooms were redesigned, and the 23rd floor was turned from a pass-through lobby into a destination with Peacock Alley and Hard Shake. It now feels less like a beautifully mannered holdover and more like a fully modern Waldorf with a Las Vegas address.
Insider tip
Book a higher floor facing north or west if views matter; the windows are a huge part of the experience here, and the right room can make the whole hotel feel like a private aerie above the Strip. Build in at least one slow afternoon for Peacock Alley or the pool, because this is one of the few Vegas hotels that really rewards doing less. And if you are choosing between here and a bigger luxury resort, the deciding factor is simple: stay here when you want proximity to Aria, Crystals, and the rest of CityCenter without committing to their pace, crowds, or casino air.
Final considerations
Family friendly:
Somewhat, but better for adults; the tone is quiet and refined, and the amenities skew more spa and pool than kid-oriented entertainment.
Accessibility:
Strong overall, with elevators, accessible rooms, and a modern layout that is easier to navigate than many giant casino resorts.
Wellness:
Excellent; the spa, yoga classes, fitness center, and non-smoking environment make this one of the Strip’s best reset hotels.
Loyalty group:
Part of Hilton Honors through Waldorf Astoria, great if you are booking on points or going for elite benefits.
