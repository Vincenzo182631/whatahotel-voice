Alila Jabal Akhdar
A fortress-like resort that blends Arab, Persian, and Indian design influences

Reviewed by

Why book?
A remote, high-altitude hideaway offering spectacular scenery and serenity. Stepping into the hotel feels like entering an ancient fortress, with a limestone facade, fort-like doors, and a blend of Arab, Persian, and Indian styles. From the warm Omani hospitality to the unique via ferrata climbing path, located just beyond the hotel restaurant, Alila Jabal Akdar has a genuine sense of place.
Set the scene
Oman’s “Green Mountain” is a rugged highland plateau dotted with tiny terraced villages carved into stone. The hotel is plopped dramatically along a canyon rim, 6,500 feet above sea level. The journey isn’t the easiest; we’re talking about a 2.5-hour journey from Muscat by car. That road climbs steadily up into the mountains. (While steep, it’s a relatively straightforward drive, but a 4x4 vehicle is mandatory.) The crowd is decidedly mixed, from well-to-do and elegant European families to the occasional chatty American couple. I was surprised that the guest profile skewed relatively younger and Millennial, presumably due to the more adventure-based activities.
The backstory
Opened in 2014, Alila Jabal Akhdar was one of the first luxury properties to stake a claim in Oman’s mountainous interior. Designed to sit low against the landscape, the resort sourced local stone and earth-toned materials to echo its nearby surroundings. The Alila brand, which started in Southeast Asia and is now part of the luxury Hyatt portfolio, focuses on local immersion in more geographically remote areas. That ethos is clearly reflected at Jabal Akhdar.
The rooms
The 84 suites and villas are understated, earthy, and timeless. Intricately carved wooden doorways and window frames are juxtaposed with clean lines and stone finishes. Woven rugs and hand-painted juniper leaf murals supply a dose of warmth, as do built-in fireplaces. Most rooms are similar in layout, with the primary difference being the view. I spent plenty of time on my outdoor terrace, where I snacked on fresh pomegranate seeds picked from nearby trees while soaking in the unobstructed mountain vistas. Even base-level rooms are generously sized, with the smallest accommodations starting at over 700 square feet. For guests seeking more privacy, ask for a suite near the wooden deck used for yoga, away from the main pool.
Food and drink
Juniper, the hotel’s primary restaurant, serves hearty local dishes that are simple and satisfying. Think: spiced skewers with camel meat and herbed lamb kofta; slow-cooked tagines and rich stews; and bright Fattoush salads with cucumbers from the on-property garden. (Be sure to get a table outside on the terrace, if the weather permits.) Rose Lounge, the more casual of the two dining options, offers up lighter bites and is ideal for a pre-dinner aperitif or post-meal nightcap.
The neighborhood/area
This is a remote property, with not a whole lot around—but that’s the entire point, right? Guests can opt for guided hikes into nearby village communities, both for the dramatic canyon scenery and for the chance to see age-old agricultural practices still in use. The famed Nizwa Fort, a 17th-century castle, is less than an hour away and shouldn’t be missed (especially in the late afternoon when the walls are bathed in golden light). The town of Nizwa has plenty to do, too, from rooftop cafe hopping to shopping in the central souk.
The service
Most of the front office staff are local Omani and live in nearby villages, which adds an overall air of authenticity. Welcome formalities are carried out over Arabic coffee and dates with tahini. Service is well-meaning, if a bit slow at times. Nightly turndown amenities included small gifts like handpicked green olives and biodegradable branded pencils—a lovely touch. Complimentary bikes are also available to guests to meander around the resort grounds.
Eco effort
An organic on-property garden supplies a majority of the restaurant’s produce, while the hotel acquires rose and pomegranate harvests from nearby farms.
Accessibility
While there are no wheelchair-accessible rooms, visual alarms and TTY phones cater to hearing-impaired guests.
Anything left to mention?
If you’re staying at the hotel, check out the rotating list of complimentary activities, including sunrise or sunset yoga set on a platform overlooking the mountains. The via ferrata experience is an extra cost, but even nonexpert climbers can traverse the terrain safely using a system of fixed cables and bridges. Or just sip on a cocktail next to the infinity pool—you’ll see the sights either way.
