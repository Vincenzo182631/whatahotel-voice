The Dylan

Reviewed by

Why book?
On the widest canal in the city (Keizersgracht), The Dylan is one of Amsterdam’s best boutique hotels. At just 40 people, the staff here deliver the attention to detail necessary at this price point. And the location is hard to beat.
Set the scene
Entering through a small courtyard, guests discover a residential-style door where you feel as if you are visiting a friend’s home rather than a hotel. Recently updated, the small lobby with a new modern fireplace has a fancy, signature woody scent to go with it. Farther inside, you get a look at what so many canal houses are like around here: a maze of hallways, staircases, tiny gardens or patios, seating nooks, and plenty of cozy spaces in which to enjoy a drink, tap away on a laptop or chat with friends or fellow guests.
The backstory
This address was once home to the first theater in Amsterdam (dating back to the 1600s and Dutch painter Rembrandt van Rijn once worked as a production assistant here) before becoming Hotel Blakes, its preceding name to The Dylan. The property’s main dining room now boasts two Michelin stars and belongs to The Leading Hotels of the World. Rembrandt’s most famous work “The Night Watch” has been rumored to be based on the opening scene of the Gijsbreght Van Aemstel play that took place here rather than the civic guard, something experts noted based on his use of light in the painting.
The rooms
Pay attention to your room number and how to get there because it can be a puzzle to find your way back to the lobby. This is common among Amsterdam canal house hotels, where several buildings have been connected via various stairs and pathways to form a single hotel. And this is all part of the charm. I love how each room is different from the next and that you will find unique views or layouts behind every door. My suite had an adjacent living area that looked directly over the front entrance courtyard, a special view that I had not been lucky enough to try on my previous visits.
The rooms showcase comforting furniture, with sofas you can curl up in while looking out towards the canals or beds that have pillows large enough to engulf you after a long flight. The duvets are cloud-like, and this hotel likes to surprise and delight some guests with unique touches like monogrammed pillowcases that you can take home (like if you’re celebrating a special occasion). You don’t have to reach far for a power or USB port here, although every room does have a slightly different setup. Bathrooms are bright, sometimes with separate water closets, and always with a lovely stash of Aesop toiletries from Australia, using organic and floral ingredients. The drenching rain pressure in the glass showers is something I still think about as part of my remedy to wash away jet lag. In premium rooms and suites, there’s a take-home leather amenity kit stuffed with even more Aesop goodies.
It’s hard to determine what a standard room here is, but it’s probably one that looks inward towards the courtyard. The best views are of the famous canal where the city glides along boat by boat (or bike by bike). Turndown service delivers delicious Amsterdam stroopwafels, produced by nearby Lanskroon bakery using the local recipe, adding honey syrup. Otherwise, housekeeping seems to be invisible in the hallways, but ever-present in keeping the accommodations looking at their finest from morning to night.
Food and drink
Of the main dining options here are Bar Brasserie OCCO, where you can enjoy a tasty breakfast, craft cocktail or light bite to eat. It’s spread across various rooms and spaces so that every table seems private or at least has an interesting view of the nearby street. At breakfast, I was truly impressed by the quality of the buffet, which featured appetizers like beetroot gazpacho and multicolored cherry tomatoes drizzled with olive oil, served in small-portion containers. All of the yummy Dutch cheeses were perfect to pair with house-baked breads, and there were all types of yogurts, fruits, and deli selections to peruse. A freshly prepared egg or sweet dish menu was at every table and part of the offering.
Later in the day, this hotel has a real feather in its cap: Restaurant Vinkeles, now with its second Michelin star under Executive Chef Jurgen van der Zalm. His dishes are sought after citywide, with reservations here hard to come by since guests are competing with locals for a table. The chef’s signature menu includes dishes like Berton oysters with beurre blanc and N25 Kaluga caviar or the Wagyu A5 Nakayama steak with fermented garlic and dashi. A wide selection of vegan and vegetarian options is also on offer. There’s even a fun bubble gum-style machine at the bar that spits out a plastic ball with a random cocktail inside. If you don’t know what you want to order, try your luck to see what you get, and then the bartender will prepare it for you. That’s something creative and fun for indecisive imbibers or those eager to try something new.
The spa
Hand-crafted Roetz electric bikes are available for guests to borrow free of charge to cycle around town like a local. But take note, this is not as easy as it might be at home, as Amsterdammers are known to be serious, if not almost vicious, at their navigation around tourists and novice riders. Otherwise, there is an onsite fitness room and ensuite massage services available.
The neighborhood/area
In central Amsterdam, you can walk or bike almost everywhere, and this hotel’s prized location assures you of that. But, remember that the city (and this hotel) are not as easy to maneuver around for those with mobility issues.
The service
Expect guest name recognition from staff, the offer of an eco-friendly bottle of water before heading out, housekeeping attention to detail (hanging a jacket or folding clothes otherwise strewn about (oops), and an overall keen focus on making the experience personalized.
For families
Kids are not always common, but welcome and well cared for here.
Anything left to mention?
Leaders Club loyalty program members receive free breakfast for two and late checkout considerations. Since it’s free to join, this immediately tacks on plenty of added value.
