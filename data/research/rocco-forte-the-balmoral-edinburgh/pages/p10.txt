The Balmoral, A Rocco Forte Hotel
In Gaelic, the word "balmoral" translates to “majestic dwelling”—and that’s exactly what this classic, luxurious hotel feels like.

Why book?
This grand dame is a fitting stay in a city known for its history and beauty.
Set the scene
In Gaelic, the word "balmoral" translates to “majestic dwelling”—and that’s exactly what The Balmoral is, with Baronial architecture style, Victorian influences, and classic kilted doormen welcoming guests. Inside, Olga Polizzi's spaces are clean and elegant. The reception area is gorgeous with latte-colored walls, crown moldings, ornate chandeliers, dramatic staircases, and artistic floral statement centerpieces that change seasonally. At a time when many hotels follow the same few stylistic plays—neutral and mid-century, or perhaps OTT and designed for the internet—the Balmoral feels like a tale as old as time, set firmly in
Scotland
. If it ain’t broke, don't fix it.
The backstory
This hotel was originally built in 1902 for the North British Railway company; porters would take guests from their trains to an elevator that led straight to reception. The journey from Waverley train station to your plush bed is just as convenient these days—and the digs are presumably even better since Rocco Forte took over—though bellmen will still meet you right off your car to bring your luggage up (no wonder this hotel is the jumping off point for Belmond's lush
Royal Scotsman
train).
The rooms
Expect comfy furniture and contemporary color schemes that nod to Scotland: There are rustic off-whites and browns, bursts of spring green and fiery red-orange, and, in the suites, royal reds and blues. Tartan drapery and art depicting the Scottish countryside remind you of exactly where you are, or where you're headed. Classic rooms—the lowest tier—aren't the most conspicuously swanky, nor are they very large; if you want the five-star experience, splash out on one of the higher-category rooms, which have stunning city views and lofty ceilings. Harry Potter fans (or, their children) continue to clamor for the J.K. Rowling suite, where the infamous author finished the final book in the series—and signed a marble bust of Hermes to prove it.
Food and drink
The hotel is a destination not just for its grandeur but for its restaurants and bars, with five total venues. At the Michelin-starred Number One, chef Mathew Sherry turns fine Scottish ingredients into creative, seasonal dishes with refined presentation; the wine program, meanwhile, is a reason to visit alone (step into the cellar to ogle the collection, and maybe try something new). This is fine dining at its finest; if you prefer to roll up your sleeves and chat a bit louder, head upstairs. The Palm Court, where a harpist plays from the balcony and the tea-time decor charms, is arguably the visual centerpiece of the hotel—and clearly a magnet particularly for snap-happy travelers from beyond the UK who want to indulge in a great cuppa. Because I prefer something a little stronger, I was partial to the beautiful Bar Prince, with its gorgeous wood bar, decadent wallpaper, and menu dedicated to martinis (there's also a seasonal supplement of rotating 'tinis, and the Parmesan-infused rendition I tried in
June
is still on my mind—not to mention the bartenders are packed with cocktail knowledge and city recs). The Brasserie beside it, a continuation of the same vibe, focuses on French dishes with some good vegetarian options, and hosts a breakfast buffet with lovely local products. Whiskey lovers have a bar just for them at Scotch—and in those elegant bottle lockers you see out front, regulars store the prized scotches they drink on return visits. If you’re new to the world of whiskies, pop in for a tasting.
The spa
The spa and fitness center just received a major facelift. Summer 2025 guests will be among the first to enjoy the gorgeous new space, which feels like a nod to the Italian roots of the Rocce Forte brand. Case in point: a mural just behind reception transport you to the Sicilian countryside, with trees weighed down with lemons, and rolling hills unfolding behind them. With the vegan botanical products from Irena Forte’s line, treatments focus on both relaxation and results. The pool (underground, with the rest of the spa) feels like a reflection pond in spirit: It’s quiet here, and plush daybeds with tea offered might lull you into a post-treatment nap. Whether you’re after a workout or not at all, pop your head into the gym, where a wallpaper decorated with bendy acrobats makes this, dare I say, a fun place to be. (The shiny new Technogym gear promises you can get a real workout in too, if you so choose.)
The neighborhood
The hotel's historical ties to Waverley Station—a hub for those coming and going from
Edinburgh
—make this hotel a vital part of Princes Street, the high street of the Scottish capital. In fact, the building's almost 200-foot-tall clock tower is set three minutes fast so guests and railway passengers don't miss their trains. There's only one exception: During Hogmanay, the city's New Year celebrations, it marks the turning of the year precisely on time.
When you do leave the hotel, you’ll be delighted to find that many of the city’s hottest restaurants tend to be close by, in bordering New Town; meanwhile, the picture-perfect Old Town (and home to the Royal Mile, the heart of Edinburgh since the Middle Ages and street upon which many landmarks lie), is just on the other side of Waverley. You couldn’t be more central if you tried.
The service
Staff is five-star all the way, with everyone going above and beyond in the little ways that matter. Quickly, you’ll know the kilted men at the door by name—like Mike, an Edinburgh local who has worked here for decades.
Anything left to mention?
This hotel carries on the best of Edinburgh traditions while going above and beyond with 21st-century comforts.
