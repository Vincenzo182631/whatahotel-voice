ANA Intercontinental Tokyo
Between Akasaka and Roppongi, where you're steps from the best of both neighborhoods but far enough to avoid the late-night crowds

Why book?
For a convenient, freshly renovated base between Akasaka and Roppongi, where you're steps from the best of both neighborhoods but far enough to avoid the late-night crowds.
Set the scene
The lobby is a vast, bubble-era set piece—soaring double-height ceilings gridded with geometric lights, warm wood paneling, and a glowing staircase. At 3 p.m., pilots and cabin crew roll carry-ons past families at the check-in counter, business travelers make a beeline for the Club Lounge upstairs, and women linger over afternoon tea in the Atrium Lounge.
The backstory
The hotel opened in 1986 as the
Tokyo
ANA Hotel, timed to the airline's debut as an international carrier, and took the InterContinental name in 2007 after ANA's hotel business merged with IHG. Ahead of its 40th anniversary, the property closed roughly 90 percent of its 800-plus rooms for a 15-month overhaul, relaunching in 2025 with a lighter, more polished look.
The rooms
Renovated rooms lean into subdued Japanese modernism: origami-inspired geometric patterns, custom carpets with a cherry blossom motif in grey with soft pink and indigo, and touches like a wine-colored cast-iron teapot or tea. My Mizu Suite—essentially a junior suite at 560 square feet—had a comfortable sofa and a view across to the National Diet Building's distinctive pyramidal tower. The bathroom came with a rain shower, Thann amenities, and a tub that was deep enough to sink into, though anyone over six feet may find it short. The renovation expanded the Club InterContinental inventory to 165 rooms and suites, and the perks are worth the premium.
Food and drink
The hotel boasts a whopping 13 restaurants and bars. Unkai serves precise, seasonal kaiseki; Karin turns out polished Cantonese (the popular Chinese afternoon tea is a colorful array of dim sum and bite-sized desserts), with Kenzan as the hotel’s sushi restaurant. Downstairs, the new Genever bar centers on gin, while Mixx Bar and Lounge on the 36th floor trades on the view. Club guests, though, will do much of their eating in the lounge, which runs from breakfast through afternoon tea to evening cocktails: order the trio of hot tapas, and don't miss the spicy tuna roll.
The spa
Thann Sanctuary Spa is a tranquil pocket of the property. After a cup of lemongrass tea, my 60-minute Swedish massage began with a relaxing foot bath and scrub. The therapists are skilled and blessedly unchatty. Club guests receive a complimentary 10-minute head massage with their treatment, so be sure to mention it. The fourth-floor outdoor pool is a real asset, rare for central Tokyo; order a glass of champagne and drink it in the sun. The one miss: a sauna in the men's changing room only.
The neighborhood/area
The address is technically Akasaka, but you're on the Roppongi side of it, a short walk to that neighborhood's restaurants and bars, and around the corner from the US Embassy (should diplomatic business arise). Suntory Hall is two minutes away, which makes this the obvious choice on a concert night. Toranomon Hills and Azabudai Hills are both walkable for shopping and dining.
The service
Service is polished and professional throughout.
For families
Family suites sleep four with the sofa bed and can be booked without lounge access. Children 12 and under stay free when sharing a bed with an adult (one child per bed). Breakfast is complimentary for children 5 and under.
Accessibility
Wheelchair-accessible guest rooms and access routes meet ADA standards, with grab bars and raised toilet seats available on request.
Eco effort
A bio-treatment system recycles kitchen wastewater, rainwater, and pool water for use in flushing toilets, and heat recovered from the main kitchen's dishwashers is reused to warm the water for the next load.
Anything left to mention?
The Club Lounge is at its quietest between 4 and 5:30 p.m., when the window seats become the best place in the hotel to work or read. On weekends, a second lounge opens on the 36th floor for breakfast.
