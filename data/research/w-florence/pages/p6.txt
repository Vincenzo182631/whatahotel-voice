W Florence: First In
A spirited, contemporary stay in the heart of Italy’s Renaissance capital.

Reviewed by
Laura Itzkowitz
Why book?
For the buzzy, youthful energy, inspired design, and must-try dining that characterize the W brand. Lately, waves of contemporary-minded hotels have been washing up in the home of Dante and Donatello and the W Florence feels like the apex of that trend. It aims to be a social hub where cosmopolitan travelers can meet and mingle with locals who come to the restaurants and bars.
Set the scene
A five-minute walk from the Santa Maria Novella train station, the W Florence sits on Piazza dell’Unità facing the Basilica di Santa Maria Novella. If you’re coming from the train station, as I was, you might mistake the entrance to the restaurant, Tratto, for the hotel’s entrance, which tells you a lot about the W’s emphasis on dining and drinking.
The reception area, just around the corner on Via del Melarancio, welcomes you in with handpainted wallpaper featuring tigers, peacocks, and other exotic animals that might seem out of place in Florence but are actually a nod to the Medici family’s menagerie. It’s far from the only example of bold, eye-catching artwork in the hotel, whose 1970s-inspired interiors were designed by AvroKO. I especially loved the abstract mural in the plant-filled courtyard, which reminded me of Matisse’s cutouts.
The backstory
Abandoned for more than 15 years, the building that houses the hotel is one of the few examples of 20th century architecture in this Renaissance city. It was designed in 1968-1972 by Lando Bartoli and housed the Grand Hotel Majestic as well as the Banca Poplare di Novara. When designing the interiors, AvroKO drew inspiration from Bartoli’s era, creating sinuous banquettes in the lounge and restaurant, layering pastel colors and bold Mediterranean hues, and adding lighting that recalls the hotel’s original disco heyday.
This is the W brand’s second hotel in Italy, following the 2023 debut of the
W Rome
. That hotel signaled a bit of a reinvention of the brand, which doubled down on designing hotels rooted in their destination for millennial and Gen Z guests with a global worldview. The W Florence feels very much in line with that approach to hospitality.
The rooms
It wouldn’t be a W without some attention-grabbing design details and you definitely can’t miss the headboards with an optical illusion effect made by lights and mirrors. All of the 119 rooms and suites have them, in addition to minibars filled with local wine, premium spirits and juices, Lavazza espresso machines, Smart TVs, and marble and terrazzo-style bathrooms stocked with bath products by Davines.
My suite had a spacious living area with a mustard yellow sofa and cobalt blue cushions, a fireplace feature dividing it from the bedroom, and a separate dressing room with a soaking tub. I loved the custom Monopoly board featuring the names of Tuscan towns like Porto Ercole and Capalbio. For the crème de la crème, book the Penthouse Suite, which has a huge private terrace overlooking the Medici Chapel and can be combined with two other rooms to create a three-bedroom suite.
Food and drink
At Tratto, the ground-floor restaurant that serves breakfast, lunch, and dinner, the talented young team of chefs from the Michelin-starred Trattoria Contemporanea near Lake Como has created a contemporary Italian menu heavily influenced by their southern roots. A highlight is the parmigiana pie baked (perhaps blasphemously, depending on who you ask) in a crispy pastry shell. Close your eyes as you take a bite of the risotto pizzaiola style and memories of margherita pizza will come flooding back to you. Be sure to leave room for dessert, which comes wheeled out on a cart. Trattoria Contemporanea’s young pastry chef was named one of Italy’s best pastry chefs in 2024 and at Tratto she offers up a sublime tiramisu in addition to delicious cakes and gelato.
At the adjacent W Lounge, the bartenders play with classic cocktails, adding unexpected twists. Vespucci’s Voyage is a gimlet riff inspired by the sea, with gin, Italicus, and celery that gives it a slightly salty taste and an ethereal foam that recalls the Mediterranean sea. The Unordinary Spicy Margarita—a clarified blend of tequila, mezcal, lime, mango, yerbito liqueur, and green peppers—is dangerously smooth.
Coming soon are the signature restaurant by Korean-American chef Akira Back, which will bring a welcome taste of contemporary Japanese and Korean cuisine to Florence, and Zefiro Rooftop, which will host DJs, live music, and other events on the spacious rooftop terrace, which boasts views of the Duomo, Palazzo Vecchio, and the Medici Chapel.
The spa
There’s no spa per se, but the signature FIT gym equipped with Technogym equipment is open 24/7 and the wellness area has a sauna, steam bath, and relaxation room. In-room massages are available upon request.
The neighborhood/area
The W puts you right in the heart of Florence, a five-minute walk to the Duomo, Santa Maria Novella Station, and the Medici Chapel. In this walkable city, you can go just about everywhere on foot.
The service
The young staff are full of creative energy, especially in the Lounge and Tratto, where they seem proud to be a part of such a cool new hotel. The W’s signature Whatever/Whenever service ensures high standards around the clock.
For families
Kids and pets are welcomed with their own set of amenities, including bath products and dog bowls.
Eco effort
The hotel has obtained LEED Gold and WELL Silver sustainability awards. In the rooms, you’ll find glass or carton containers instead of single-use plastic. The restaurants and bars source as much as possible locally.
Accessibility
The public areas are completely accessible and there are five handicap-accessible rooms available.
