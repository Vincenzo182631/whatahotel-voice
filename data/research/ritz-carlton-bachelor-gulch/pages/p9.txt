The Ritz-Carlton, Bachelor Gulch
If you want a celebratory stay that never quite loses its family-friendly atmosphere, this Ritz-Carlton is perfect.

Reviewed by

Why book?
This is ski-in, ski-out luxury at its best, without the see-and-be-seen circus. An unbeatable location at the base of Beaver Creek’s Bachelor Gulch base, soup-to-nuts amenities, and classic Ritz-Carlton service make this a turnkey approach to skiing.
Set the scene
Tucked away in Beaver Creek’s Bachelor Gulch base, this stunning Ritz-Carlton is an ode to “parkitecture,” with soaring timber beams and sturdy natural stone nodding to some of the iconic lodges of our National Parks. Driving up the access road from the town of Avon, you feel every inch of this hotel’s grandiosity. The crowd is certainly upscale–families in matching Moncler jackets, lots of Texas accents, finance types zipping in on the company jet for a ski weekend. But despite the high price tags and well-heeled clientele, the hotel manages to feel intimate, familiar, and warm.
The backstory
Opened in 2002, this resort was one of Ritz-Carlton’s first true alpine resorts in North America, and put Beaver Creek on the map as a quieter, more upscale alternative to the energetic Vail Village, which is about twenty minutes down the highway from here. Bachelor Gulch is one of the three main base areas of Beaver Creek, named after a group of men who settled the area and, over time, turned it into a lettuce-farming hub.
The rooms
Think Colorado luxury: dark woods, stone fireplaces, leather accents, and balconies with views over the Vail Valley or onto the slopes. I stayed in a Lounge Level King, which provided access to an all-day serviced lounge with gratis drinks and snacks–gratis once you’ve paid the steep room rates, of course (holiday weekends can soar upwards of $3,000/night). During the day, these rooms are bathed with natural light to near snowblindness proportions, but can be a little dark in the evening. There are a number of multi-bedroom suites and interconnecting rooms available, making this one a no-brainer for multigenerational groups and those traveling with little ones. Looking to splurge? Book one of the truly decadent, privately owned residences on the upper floors—no two are decorated the same, and they come with sprawling layouts and full kitchens, taking this Ritz-Carlton experience to another level.
Food and drink
There are three fantastic restaurants here, in addition to a main floor grab and go café: Wyld, which serves fantastic, upscale alpine cuisine like truffled pastas, slow-roasted lamb, caviar service, and a chef’s tasting menu; lively and contemporary sushi and izakaya at Sakaba (which has a glorious, heated outdoor dining room that is nothing short of magical when snow is falling); and Buffalos, a Mexican-inflected steakhouse. Across the board, the food is fantastic, the dining spaces are elegant and effervescent, and you really feel like your satisfaction is the service’s top priority.
Make sure to dedicate some time for après-ski drinks and snacks in the Great Room, a cozy space built around a three-story fireplace with tons of chairs and couches for the whole gang to spread out. Sure, it’s pricey—a fairly modest fondue at $78 felt rather steep—but can you really put a price tag on quintessential Colorado ski vibes?
The spa
Spa facilities are spacious and well-maintained, spanning large relaxation rooms, saunas, steam rooms, as well as both single-sex and co-ed grotto spaces. Treatments are among the best you’ll find in the Vail Valley, and so is the wet deck. It features a huge lap pool heated to 82 degrees year-round, a larger hot tub, and two smaller hot tubs tucked behind some birch trees. Even at the prime après-après-ski, pre-dinner hour when the area was flooded with children, it was still possible to find some peace and quiet here. My one gripe? The oxygen therapy machines—which here at 8,000 feet are a nice touch for those of us coming from sea level—are bafflingly tucked unglamorously into an elevator vestibule.
The neighborhood/area
Bachelor Gulch is quiet and secluded, which is the point. There are a number of shuttles and taxis available, but just know that when you’re here on property, you’re pretty isolated from everything else. That being said, the hotel operates a house car for quick transfers down the mountain into Avon or, pending availability, over to Vail.
The service
As a Ritz-Carlton, the service is warm, polished, and expectedly on the more formal side. This hotel has what seems like ten thousand individual moving parts—from the restaurants, to the spa, to the Lounge Level, to the front desk, to the ski valet, to the
valet
valet, so forth—and they all seem to move in unison. When you’re here, you really feel like you’re part of something special.
For families
This hotel is an excellent choice for families–and it starts upon check-in when kids are invited to select a toy from a giant chest inspired by the hotel’s log-constructed exterior. The ski shop is a whiz at fitting boots onto the tiniest feet, and the multi-bedroom layouts ensure everyone has enough space to stretch out.
Accessibility
ADA standards are met. Elevators reach every floor of the property, and accessible rooms are available.
Anything left to mention?
The outdoor bar here is fantastic at après-ski time. The vibe is cozy and refined, never chaotic. There are live DJs spinning, and the cocktails are certainly flowing, but it doesn’t have the obnoxious see-and-be-seen energy that has overtaken some of Colorado’s other resorts. If you want shotskis and bottle service, go elsewhere. But if you want something celebratory that never quite loses its family-friendly atmosphere, this Ritz-Carlton is perfect.
