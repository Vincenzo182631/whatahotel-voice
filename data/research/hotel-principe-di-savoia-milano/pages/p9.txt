In a nutshell
: An unapologetically decadent hotel that excels at white-glove hospitality, guests will find no shortage of indulgences here. From crystal-draped private dinners and theatrical cocktails to elegant rooms and a rooftop wellness retreat, Hotel Principe di Savoia knows exactly what kind of five-star service it wants to deliver—and boy, does it deliver.
rooms

Rooms
There’s no quiet luxury here. Instead, old-school Milanese glamour decorates every one of the 301 rooms and suites. Expect a rich palette of creams, golds, seas of blues, and pops of burgundy nestled against dark wood furniture and an array of textured fabrics.
If you’re looking for a room recommendation and love a generous soaking tub, my husband and I stayed in one of the Mosaic Rooms and it was the perfect home base. With a long entry foyer, dedicated sitting space, and a bathroom with marbled his-and-hers sinks (plus that aforementioned glorious tub), I was never bummed to head “home” at the end of a long day exploring the city.
Lara Kramer
Lara Kramer
Food and drink
While the hotel’s recently renovated lobby lounge is a chic stop for light bites and excellent people-watching, if you’re looking for a more complete dining experience, you’ll want to break up your evening into two stops: first, drinks at Principe Bar and second, dinner at Acanto Restaurant.
It’s all about heavy drapery and mood lighting at Principe Bar, where you’ll find a central piano encircled by dining tables and deep lounge chairs. The inventive cocktail menu includes a dedicated collection of drinks inspired by the events that shaped the world—like Blue Sky, which highlights Italy’s team color from their 2006 World Cup victory and Dial-Up Dream, which dives into the early days of the Web, using coffee soda to mimic digital pixels and whiffs of vanilla to hint at the first concept of a “cloud.” In true Italian fashion, expect a trio of aperitivo snacks to arrive alongside your drinks, and be sure to stay long enough to hear the piano come to life.
If drapes and darkness are befitting of Principe Bar, it’s all about crystals and caviar at Acanto Restaurant. Led by chef Matteo Gabrielli, you’ll find dishes that celebrate traditional classics with modern (and sometimes whimsical) twists. The chickpea farinata was a favorite bite of the night, made with baked ricotta, baby greens, and sweet and sour turnips drizzled with a reduction of grilled vegetables. You also can’t go wrong with an über-comforting bowl of warm linguine with Calvisius caviar or the spaghetti, made with the freshest tomato sauce I’ve ever had. Sparing no detail, as my spaghetti arrived, my waiter fixed a bib over my dress to protect it from rogue splatters. For dessert, the Symphony of Peaches is a must, featuring fresh peaches and almond semifreddo accompanied by an anise granita that brought me the closest I’ve come to licking a bowl in public.
Lara Kramer
Lara Kramer
Amenities and experiences
Hotel Principe di Savoia does not shy away from indulgence. And for the ultimate splurge, guests can book a private dining experience for up to six people at Acanto Restaurant surrounded by Swarovski crystal curtains. The intimate setting, tucked into a secluded corner of the dining room, makes the multicourse meal feel like a Milanese fantasy come to life.
But the hotel knows how to have fun too—you’ll find programming that ranges from
guided walking tours
through Milan’s historic Cinque Vie district to
board-game nights
at Principe Bar every Monday and Tuesday.
Lara Kramer
What to do nearby
The hotel sits in a prime position between the sleek towers of the Porta Nuova neighborhood and the city’s historic center. For easy access to the latter, the hotel provides a daily complimentary shuttle service that runs from 10 a.m. to 6:40 p.m. and services two drop-off/pick-up points near the city center. If you find you’ve just missed the shuttle or don’t want to wait, you can also reach iconic landmarks like the
Duomo di Milano
and
Teatro alla Scala
in about 10 to 15 minutes by metro or tram.
The backstory
Hotel Principe di Savoia has been a fixture of the city for nearly a century. Originally opened in 1927 as Principe e Savoia, its strategic location near Milan’s central railway station made it a convenient hub for the businessmen who were increasingly flocking to the city’s ever-growing economy. It soon became celebrated for its distinguished service, drawing royalty and celebrity guests alike. The property took on its current name in the 1980s, with subsequent renovations adding the 10th floor, home to the Presidential Suite and spa. Today, the hotel remains a grand dame of Milan, offering a perfect mix of old-world luxury with modern amenities, all delivered through impeccable hospitality.
Insider tip
The hotel will be closing on January 1, 2027, for an extensive restoration and is expected to reopen in 2029. So, if your travels find you in Milan before 2027 kicks off, consider this your impetus to go ahead and book that room.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
While you could theoretically bring your kids here, I wouldn’t recommend it.
Accessibility:
Yes; more information available
here
Wellness:
Located on the rooftop with sweeping views of Milan, the property’s wellness refuge includes a fitness center, pool, sauna, hot tub, and private treatment rooms. I cannot recommend my facialist, Giada, with enough fanfare. Her focus, expertise, and ability to make me sleep so deeply that I woke up to the sound of my own snoring, made this a 10/10 experience.
