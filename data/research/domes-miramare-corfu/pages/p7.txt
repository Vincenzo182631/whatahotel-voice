Domes of Corfu, Autograph Collection
Situated on a breathtaking stretch of the Ionian coastline but only 30 minutes from Corfu’s airport and Old Town, Domes of Corfu has everything you need for a luxurious, beachfront vacation—with or without kids.

Ben Richardson
Ben Richardson
Rooms
The rooms and suites throughout Domes of Corfu are chicly styled and feature balconies or patios, making it easy for guests to enjoy the breezy climate the west side of Corfu is known for. Each room is thoughtfully designed for travelers looking to unwind—luxurious beds, tasteful lighting, boho-minimal decor, and plentiful smart storage. I stayed in a swim-up suite facing the ocean with a sitting room and deck that was the perfect size for two people or a small family—a great value for this class of resort and well worth the upgrade.
Base rooms are compact (around 235 square feet) with larger-sized suites above 300 square feet; however, the size wasn’t an issue for me—this resort is best experienced outside on the sprawling, scenic property. Book facing the ocean to catch the dramatic sunsets, and choose the Sapphire Junior Suite or any room category above it to gain access to Haute Living, a private lounge stocked with complimentary snacks and drinks throughout the day.
Food and drink
The resort has four distinct dining options. Meze (Greek), Yaosai (Asian), and the Agora Buffet all give guests variety and choice throughout their stay. The fourth is the lively outdoor dining section called Spianada with three different venues: an Italian trattoria, a celebration of Italian and Mediterranean culinary traditions; Souvlaki, a Greek street food cart; and Honaki, a premium gelato bar. I found the food to be fresh, high quality, and tasty at both the buffet and à la carte dining options.
Base room rates offered by the property include breakfast at the Agora Buffet, with options to upgrade to half or full board (meaning breakfast and dinner or all three meals are included), which I’d recommend given there aren’t plentiful dining options in the small nearby town. The best part? Dining options all take advantage of the indoor-outdoor living the island thrives on, and are framed by beautiful views highlighting the craggy stretch of coast Domes sits on.
Ben Richardson
Ben Richardson
Amenities and experiences
While Domes of Corfu is well situated to easily navigate the rest of the island, everything you need to relax, unwind, and have fun is on-site. The defining feature of the property is its location directly on the Blue Flag–awarded Glyfada Beach, at the base of dramatic cliffs dotted with pine forests. It’s a truly breathtaking stretch of the Ionian coastline that most resorts on the island could only dream of. Once you’re on the wide, sandy beach, loungers and umbrellas await. The west side of the island tends to be a bit breezier—while the sea can get a bit rough for small children, it keeps the climate cool at the peak of summer compared to other parts of the island.
The sprawling, lushly landscaped grounds feature a large main pool as well as a separate, quieter adults-only pool, all overlooking the ocean. There’s also a kids water park for the more rambunctious members of the family to get their energy out. The property is well-proportioned, with plenty of space and amenities. Even at the peak of Euro summer, I had no trouble finding a place to sit in the middle of the day (so feel free to sleep in) or getting seated for meals without reservations. There’s a host of activities offered on-site, from fitness classes and activities for kids and families to tours of the surrounding olive oil farms and live music or DJs.
What to do nearby
The proximity to Corfu Old Town and the airport were both big pluses for me—both are around a 30-minute drive. The hotel can arrange taxis but also offers free parking for those who choose to rent a car—I’d recommend doing so if you want to explore the island. I found the roads to be easy to navigate, although small and twisty at times.
There’s also a set offering of pre-planned excursions offered by the hotel each day—think visits to nearby olive oil farms, artisans, or Corfu Old Town. If you’re leaving the resort for one excursion while you visit, it has to be Corfu Old Town. It’s a UNESCO World Heritage Site filled with history, shops, restaurants, and architecture, which skews Venetian given the island’s dynamic past. The concierge can also arrange boat tours of dramatic beaches nearby accessible only by boat.
Ben Richardson
The backstory
Domes of Corfu is part of the Marriott Autograph Collection and
Domes Resorts group
. Domes Resorts began in the mid-2000s and has since breathed life into properties throughout Greece and the Mediterranean. Their two hotels on Corfu, Domes of Corfu and
Domes Miramare
, were first opened in the ’60s and ’70s but have since received major facelifts and modern upgrades.
From my experience, what really sets Domes Resorts apart from competitors at this price point is the incredibly warm hospitality and service and thoughtfully designed properties for travelers of all types who are looking to unwind. These resorts make relaxation as easy as possible, without losing sight of what makes each locale worth visiting and offering plenty of opportunities for those looking to experience what makes the destination special.
Ben Richardson
Insider tip
From check-in onward, the staff's impressive hospitality and warmth stand out. There are thoughtful local touches throughout, like welcome gifts of local kumquat candy and liqueur or specialty spritzes using locally infused spirits. This is the perfect destination for travelers with a short amount of time on their hands looking to spend a long weekend relaxing and soaking in the beauty of their surroundings, or for longer trips as a base to explore all that the green island of Corfu has to offer.
Final considerations:
Family friendly:
Very—when I visited, the guests were around a 60/40 split of families and couples, but there’s enough space, separation, and family-focused activities where those traveling without children won’t feel that the property is dominated by families. If you prefer a child-free experience, the hotel's sister property, Domes Miramare, is adults-only and likely a better fit.
Accessibility:
The property is on a hillside, so expect some grade and need to navigate around levels via stairs, ramps, and elevators between restaurants, rooms, and pools. While there is a small boardwalk on the sand in between loungers, it’d be tricky to access for anyone with mobility issues.
Wellness:
The on-site Soma Spa offers massages, facials, and more with a local twist. The “Gym Pavilion” is equipped like a standard hotel gym but is decently sized—I’d love to have seen more equipment offered. There are also a few fitness classes (yoga and HIIT) throughout the day for an extra €10 fee (at this price point, I wish it would have been complimentary, but imagine it keeps capacity manageable for the classes).
