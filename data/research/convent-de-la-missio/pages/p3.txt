No place better embodies Mallorca’s transformation from sixties-package-tour horror into one of Europe’s most delicious holiday destinations than this whitewashed 14-room former convent, dating from the seventeenth century. Natural-linen-covered beanbag chairs in front of a wood-burning fireplace, waxed oak-parquet floors, pine beams, and off-white tufted area rugs echo the simplicity of the fincas (or farmhouses) that are the source of Mallorcan style. Bathrooms have eggcup-shaped sinks and big tubs, and most rooms have a private terrace shaded by teak shutters. There’s also a sauna, a steam room, and a small pool, along with a rooftop sun terrace. The Refectori has turned into one of the island’s hippest eateries with haute-Mallorcan health food: corn-fed chicken ravioli in truffle broth, mackerel fillets, and a chilled mango and cardamom soup that’s to murder for.
