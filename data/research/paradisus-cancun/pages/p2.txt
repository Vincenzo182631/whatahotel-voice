In a nutshell
: With recently redesigned guest rooms, broad dining options, and enough activities to fill an entire vacation, Paradisus Cancún delivers exactly what many travelers seek from an all-inclusive: convenience, variety, and fewer out-of-pocket expenses in one of Mexico's most popular beach destinations.
Paul Rubio
Paul Rubio
Rooms
Along Cancún's Hotel Zone,
all-inclusives are having a moment
once again. As tropical resorts push nightly rates into eye-watering territory (while charging excessive rates for cocktails, kids clubs and more), the appeal of paying for nearly everything upfront has become increasingly attractive. Paradisus Cancún, which reopened in April 2026 following an extensive renovation, is among the latest to capitalize on that renewed interest.
Even before checking in, Paradisus Cancún makes an impression. Spread across five interconnected glass-topped pyramids inspired by Mayan legend and structural geometry, the resort remains one of the most recognizable silhouettes along Cancún's shoreline. It's bold and dramatic and, to many, iconic—a reminder that this was one of Cancún's defining luxury resorts when it opened in 1989 as Gran Meliá Cancún, even welcoming Spain's royal family just two years later. The property's 2026 renovation wisely preserves that distinctive architecture while thoroughly modernizing what matters most: the suites and common spaces.
The renovation goes well beyond a cosmetic refresh. Triangular atriums illuminate an open-air design inspired by local cenotes, in which live vines cloak concentric hallways from the top floor down, landing at ground level to a wealth of fountains and limestone-inspired pools. All 773 guest rooms have been completely redesigned in a calming palette of white, cream, and warm woods, accented by woven macramé wall art and light fixtures cocooned in golden beige hilo de yute. The orientation of the room itself is key: the beds look straight through floor-to-ceiling glass toward the balcony, making the view the focal point the moment you wake up. Thanks to the resort's stepped-pyramid design, every furnished balcony enjoys an unobstructed view of either the Caribbean Sea or Nichupté Lagoon, regardless of category, allowing outdoor living to feel like an extension of the room itself.
Most accommodations fall into either Junior Suite or Suite categories, although calling my 474-square-foot Junior Suite a suite felt a touch generous. The bigger decision is choosing the room type that best suits your travel style: Standard rooms place guests in the middle of the action, steps from the main lobby, restaurants, and pools. Those looking for a quieter, elevated alternative can opt for Family Concierge or The Reserve; The Reserve offers an adults-only experience with a exclusive privileges, including dedicated concierge service, private dining and lounge spaces, and access to a dedicated adults-only pool and beach area. For those with kids, Family Concierge is designated for families who want the non-Adults-Only extras.
I stayed in The Reserve, the resort’s adults-only experience, which offered a welcome change of pace from the nonstop activity elsewhere. Rather than rushing out each morning, I often lingered over coffee watching frigate birds drift above the water at sunrise. I enjoyed evening cocktails under the atrium, where fountains echoed through the hanging vines, creating an almost ASMR-like calm.
Food and drink
One of the biggest advantages of a resort this size is variety, and Paradisus Cancún certainly delivers. Between nine restaurants, seven bars, an artisan gelato shop, and a Nespresso café, it's easy to spend a week here without feeling like you're eating the same meal on repeat. While the menus span everything from sushi to steakhouse fare, I found myself gravitating toward the restaurants that celebrated Mexican flavors rather than trying to imitate somewhere else.
Taqueria Blue Agave quickly became my favorite, serving freshly pressed corn tortillas piled with mushrooms, and nopales (cactus) alongside excellent salsas and guacamole (My meat-eating friends raved about the tacos al pastor). Seafood-centric Agua Marina also shined, particularly its flavorful tuna aguachile and delicate scallop ceviche.
Paul Rubio
Dare I say, I even found myself eating at the buffet. Impeccably maintained and constantly replenished, it offered enough Mexican, Asian, Italian, and American dishes that it quietly worked its way into my regular rotation.
The wine selection could be better, so after the first evening I happily pivoted to cocktails instead. Fortunately, the bars are one of the property's strengths: Premium spirits like Grey Goose, Ketel One, and Patrón are poured generously without additional charge, making margaritas, cosmos, and espresso martinis a better choice than vino. With bars surrounding the pools and attentive servers circulating throughout the beach and pool decks, saying “just one more” becomes remarkably easy.
Amenities and experiences
One thing Paradisus Cancún will never leave you with is idle time. This is the classic all-inclusive playbook executed on a grand scale for travelers who like their vacations full, whether that means bouncing between pools, joining the next activity on the schedule, or keeping the kids entertained from breakfast until bedtime. If your ideal beach vacation involves a paperback by the pool and little more than deciding when to order your next margarita (that's me, hi!), you can find that, too, especially at The Reserve.
The resort revolves around several expansive pools. One pool caters squarely to families with younger kids, anchored by a colorful splash park with pint-sized slides, tipping buckets, fountains, and a shallow wading area for toddlers. The second draws multigenerational families and couples, featuring a swim-up bar and a steady rotation of activities. During my stay, afternoons included everything from salsa lessons and aqua aerobics to aquatic Olympics and, yes, bingo. It's energetic and loud, but participation is entirely optional—and it's what most of the crowd here wants. If organized group fun isn't your thing, there's always a lounge chair waiting somewhere quieter, and The Reserve pool feels miles away from the frenzy.
The children's programming is particularly ambitious, extending well beyond a supervised kids club. Daily schedules might include Mario Kart tournaments, water polo, yoga, arts and crafts, story time, face painting, or paper airplane competitions, making it easy to understand why so many families spend an entire week here without venturing beyond the gates. Older kids and adults can head to the Play District, where Formula 1 and golf simulators share space with arcade games and a sports bar. Just know that these attractions aren't included in the all-inclusive rate. Same goes for Kanna, the property's stylish beach club, where even hotel guests pay $255 extra for a Balinese daybed (albeit with a bottle of Moët and a seafood platter). While neither of these experiences is essential to enjoying your stay, the additional charges do chip away at the all-inclusive promise. Thankfully, complimentary beach loungers, pool chairs, and attentive drink service remain plentiful elsewhere on the property.
The amenities I appreciated most were the ones that encouraged wellness and fitness. Four pickleball courts were consistently available, the spacious Life Fitness gym rivaled many standalone health clubs, and the daily fitness schedule kept my late mornings busy. Guests staying in Family Concierge and The Reserve—as well as anyone booking a spa treatment—also receive one hour of complimentary access to the YHI Spa's hydrotherapy circuit, complete with a steam room, sauna, plunge pool, and hydrotherapy pools.
Paul Rubio
What to do nearby
Most guests book an all-inclusive because they have no intention of leaving. That's the appeal, and it's why this style of vacation isn't for everyone. Paradisus Cancún embraces that mindset, but it also tries to connect guests with the destination through its new Destination Inclusive program's curated experiences.
One of the signature experiences is a sunset cruise through Cancún's Nichupté Lagoon, exclusively for The Reserve and Family Concierge guests. The scenery is beautiful, though the onboard entertainment leans into classic all-inclusive territory, complete with a digital saxophone performance, audience participation, and the inevitable Macarena throw down. It wasn't quite my speed, but judging by the enthusiastic crowd, I was clearly in the minority—and so I soon warmed up to it. I found the on-property Alebrije workshop more rewarding, spending an afternoon learning the craft behind Mexico's brightly colored folk art from local artisans.
If you do decide to venture off-property, make time for a Río Secreto eco tour. Roughly an hour away, this remarkable underground river winds through one of the world's largest cave systems, where thousands of stalactites and stalagmites create a landscape unlike anything you'll find at Cancún's better-known theme parks. Floating through crystal-clear water before hiking silently through cathedral-like caverns was easily the highlight of my time in the Mexican Caribbean and a reminder that one of the region's greatest attractions lies well beyond the beach.
Paul Rubio
Insider tip
If you're traveling
without
children, book The Reserve. You’ll still have full access to the restaurants, pools, and activities throughout the property, but you’ll also experience an adults-only setting with its own pool, restaurant, café, dedicated concierge service, and additional privileges. It changes the feel of the stay entirely, offering a quieter retreat without sacrificing any of the conveniences of the larger resort.
And if you're visiting during the warmer months, keep your expectations in check when it comes to sargassum seaweed. Like much of Florida and the entire Caribbean, Cancún experienced significant blooms throughout 2026. The resort begins clearing the beach before sunrise each morning, but nature doesn't always cooperate. If a more pristine shoreline is high on your wishlist, consider traveling later in the year when conditions are typically more favorable, typically mid-October to April.
Final considerations
Family friendly:
Yes; families are clearly the property's sweet spot, thanks to Family Concierge, extensive kids programming, multiple pools, and a schedule of activities that keeps younger travelers entertained from morning until night.
Accessible:
Yes; contact resort before booking for more information
Wellness:
A spacious Life Fitness gym, four pickleball courts, daily fitness classes, and the YHI Spa
Hotel brand:
