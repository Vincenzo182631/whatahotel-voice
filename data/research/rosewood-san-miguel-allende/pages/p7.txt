Rosewood San Miguel de Allende
Without peer in a town that has its share of swank hotels

Reviewed by

Why book?
The Rosewood San Miguel de Allende captures an idea about faded colonial grandeur but presents it in a way that is bright, fresh, vibrant.
Set the scene
In San Miguel, boutique hotels seem to pop up like wildflowers, but the stately Rosewood feels like a city within a city—an estate, with well-tended gardens, a pool, airy courtyards, a destination rooftop—that you can retreat to after days spent out on the cobblestone streets outside. For groups and families, the ability to book rooms together and end nights in the spacious cocktail bar or on-site restaurants, is a valuable amenity. The guests here are affluent but not ostentatious; their demeanor and dress are relaxed. It's primarily a mix of families (who make full use of the hotel's world-class pool) and older couples. Most are passionate about food and/or art, probably the two leading draws for anyone visiting San Miguel.
The backstory
Opened in 2011, Rosewood's first city hotel in
Mexico
embodies the ultra-luxury brand's strategy of going deep into the character and culture of a place. Though it is a new build, the hotel feels deeply ingrained in the fabric of San Miguel, and you'd be excused for thinking it has stood since the 19th century. This is San Miguel, so naturally, the hotel has an extraordinary collection of artwork by
Mexican artists
.
The rooms
Across 67 rooms, there are three categories—all of which have a balcony or terraces—spacious suites, and even residences. The appointments, in keeping with everything else about the hotel, reflect the best kind of artisanal Mexican luxury, from the wood-beamed ceiling to exquisite hand-carved four-poster beds. My favorite feature was the bathtub—surrounded by alcoves for tea candles—which the team will happily set up before you return to your room in the evening.
Food and drink
Luna Tapas Bar is one of the best-known rooftop destinations in a city full of them. You won't find a better place to take in the sunset, and the views of the iconic
Parroquia de San Miguel Arcangel
are first-rate. The menu has some Mexican classics, but the new approach skews Mediterranean. The ground-floor fine-dining restaurant 1826 is home to successful twists on traditional Mexican cuisine. This is where you'll have breakfast, too—a nourishing bowl of chilaquiles, perhaps, that will keep you going through lunch no matter how active your morning. And over at Pirules Garden Kitchen, for which you'll walk through the manicured gardens to reach, is a change of scenery with locavore dining (Chef Odín Rocha sources ingredients within 60 miles of the hotel). The salads, bursting with local produce, and a long list of expertly grilled meat, are fantastic.
The spa
The spa offers all the classics: Massages, facials, manis, pedis. I nearly reached nirvana in a soothing massage, with the scent of lavender lulling me to the brink of sleep. A steam room and sauna are reasons to arrive pre-treatment, and relaxation rooms make the case for lingering.
The neighborhood
The location is central, but on a quieter side street: The church and the Jardin Allende are just a 10-minute walk away, and all of the city's great restaurants and galleries are immediately accessible, but there's a tranquility you may not expect in the city center. There are many great hotels in San Miguel, but none that is so good at being both in the city and set apart from it, which makes it an especially
good option for families
. That in mind, this really does feel like a resort experience, and as with any great resort, it's only natural to want to spend all your time on the grounds—which may not be what every visitor to San Miguel is seeking.
The service
The service throughout was smooth, gracious, friendly, and unfailingly helpful. In addition to all the great art around the property, the Rosewood SMA has an art concierge who can arrange painting classes and art experiences throughout the city.
Cooking classes
, at an outdoor kitchen if you'd like, are another key part of the Rosewood SMA experience, and can be paired with a visit to the local farm where the hotel sources much of its produce. The hotel can also set up bike rides, horseback riding adventures, you name it. Anything you can think to do in San Miguel, the hotel can make happen.
Anything left to mention?
Ask and you shall receive—whether you're after a custom cocktail in the lobby bar, a chef-selected menu at Pirules, or a final stop for barbacoa tacos en route to the airport during your hotel-arranged transfer. They'll make it happen.
