Hotel Gabrielli Venice
A literary grand dame brought back to life.

Reviewed by
Stephanie Gavan
Why book?
For a sense of Grand Tour glamour, made tangible in ancient stone and regal wooden beams, and a renewed appreciation of the city’s craftspeople, who have restored every inch of this storied building—from the frescoes to the glass chandeliers—with their skilled hands. To walk in the footsteps of literary greats, and arguably, for the best views of
Venice
.
Set the scene
Mist curls above the lagoon as boats pull up outside Hotel Gabrielli. Before stepping inside the hotel—a sprawling structure comprising seven palazzos, three of which directly face the canal—your eye is drawn upward across its terra-cotta façade lined with Moorish-style quadrifora windows. This four-lobed design serves as the hotel's emblem and is consistently referenced throughout the glitzy, Andrea Auletta–designed interiors. After a devastating episode of acqua alta flooded Gabrielli back in 2019, the hotel has been through an extensive five-year renovation, redistributing space and salvaging original features to create something that reflects the spirit of the city: resilient, enduring, and regenerative. Here, the focus is on Venetian craftsmanship. All 700 of the hotel’s original chandeliers have been restored by Seguso and Pauly & C, two of the most important family-owned studios of Murano. Rubelli and Fortuny fabrics are spotted throughout, and frescoes, wooden beams, and Istrian columns have been meticulously restored by local experts. This reverence for the island is echoed in thoughtful contemporary additions like the round carpets in the lobby, whose etched lines reference the ripples of the water, or the emerald green velvets in the bar and restaurant that recall the tonality of the lagoon.
The backstory
The noble Gabrielli family were well-known figures in Venetian politics and the original stewards of this 14th-century palazzo. It wasn’t until 1856 that ownership was transferred into the hands of Andreas Perkhofer, who had arrived in the city after a military career. He ran the site initially as a restaurant, adding rooms as its popularity grew. Soon enough, the hotel was established as a key stop on the Grand Tour, and became particularly popular among German-speaking cultural figures. Guests included everyone from Sigmund Freud and Adolf Loos to the dark allegorist Franz Kafka, who wrote a postcard to his lover (to dump her, no less) from his room on the third floor. Since then, the palazzo has been nurtured by five generations of the Perkhofer family, and now remains under the ownership of sisters Francesca and Johanna Perkhofer, though under the management of the Florentine hotel chain Starhotels.
The rooms
The hotel's restoration reduced the number of rooms from 105 to 66, making the remaining spaces significantly larger. While the prestige room I experienced was on the smaller side, it was ideal for a weekend stay. Guests planning a longer visit should opt for a suite, which offers more generous proportions and the added luxury of a bathtub. If you’re willing to splurge, the presidential suite, nestled in the eaves, spans 915 square feet, making it one of the largest in the city. It also boasts its own altana terrace with panoramic views across the city’s terra-cotta rooftops and the lagoon. Regardless of size, every room achieves a decadent feel without tipping into excess. The design deftly blends mirrors, damask fabrics, and a dash of gold with wooden accents and subtle hints of gray, recalling the island's winter hues. Walls are furnished with reproduction sketches of iconic pieces from Seguso and Venini (historic Venetian glass brands), made exclusively for the hotel and curated by Italian artist Ottavia Moschini. The recurring window motif is featured prominently in headboards and rugs that mirror their pointed arches, but the real crowning glory of these spaces is the views they offer, with most rooms overlooking the lagoon or the small canal to the side of the building where you can watch gondolas glide past from small wrought-iron balconies.
Food and drink
Spread across the ground floor, the Felice al Gabrielli restaurant and adjoining K Lounge Bar are dressed in jewel tones, with hints of gold, Canaletto walnut wood, and marble floors that swirl underfoot. The cocktail menu at K Lounge Bar (named after Kafka, naturally) doubles as a history lesson, with each concoction referencing Venetian urban legends. The ghost that’s said to haunt Via Garibaldi? That’s the Fantasma, made with Cabira vodka infused with eucalyptus, cucumber extract, lime sugar, and bitter herbs. My favorite, the bubblegum-pink Sant’Elena, is a cross between a picante and a paloma, and named after the mother of Constantine, whose remains are said to be buried at the tail end of the island, lending the area its name. Who knew!
The main restaurant, Felice al Gabrielli (this time named after Kafka’s long-suffering lover), is helmed by Mirko Pistorello, who takes great pride in sourcing fresh regional ingredients from the lagoon and its surroundings. The menu is packed with Venetian classics like Rialto fritto misto and creamed cod with black squid sauce. Although the terrace was closed during our stay, it boasts unbeatable views of the island—stretching out to Giudecca, with Lido twinkling on one side and the Campanile spiking the skyline on the other. In the warmer months, you can expect light bites like cicchetti and salads served with classic wines and cocktails, perfect for golden hour.
The Corte Grande, where breakfast is held, may well be my favorite space in the hotel. What used to be an outdoor courtyard has been cleverly brought inside with a glass ceiling and has retained its original stone floors. Tables circle around a magnificent 14th-century wellhead adorned with sculptures of the archangel Gabriel. It makes the buffet of fresh fruits and Venetian pastries all the sweeter.
The spa
Due to the density of this historic island, it is highly unusual for Venetian hotels to offer anything more than a treatment room or two. So while Gabrielli's wellness offering isn't exactly sprawling, it is certainly significant. The spa is clad in sleek granite marble and features a large hot tub, a hammam, a sauna, and a multi-sensory shower. It is available to all guests from Tuesday through Sunday, or can be booked for exclusive use, though advance reservation is required either way. The treatment menu sticks to the classics: deep tissue, lymphatic, and relaxation massages. There is also a small but chic walnut wood gym.
The neighborhood/area
The hotel’s prime perch on Riva degli Schiavoni places Gabrielli in a strategic position—close enough to the must-see sights of the island’s northern sestieri while straddling Castello, the city’s considerably more local area. 10 minutes to your right and you’ll arrive at St Mark’s Square; 10 minutes to your left and you’ll arrive at the Biennale. Historically, the latter has been a particular draw for artists, some of whom would offer their work to the hotel in exchange for a room. The results line the corridors of Gabrielli, ranging from humorous sketches and intricate drawings to whimsical watercolors and heartfelt notes from the hotel’s former guestbook. The Arsenale vaporetto stop is right outside, but it can be more enlightening to skip the boat and head inland on foot instead. Just behind the hotel, you’ll find some of the island’s most under-the-radar bacari, where off-duty gondoliers prop up the bar over a spritz.
For families
While Gabrielli is certainly geared toward grown-up tastes, children are very welcome and receive a welcome kit upon arrival, alongside other thoughtful perks like mini bathrobes and slippers. Menus can be easily adapted for little ones, and if the young boy dining next to us one evening is anything to go by, the kitchen does a superb job. “That was excellent,” he informed the waiter on his way out. For family convenience, babysitting is available with advance notice. The hotel also features multiple connecting rooms specifically designed for families, which can be equipped upon request with cots, playpens, high chairs, strollers, and more upon request.
The service
Staff are warm, knowledgeable, and immaculately dressed in cream uniforms reminiscent of archival Valentino. The ownership group emphasises soft skills training across all its properties, a meticulous approach that is evident in the staff's swift anticipation of your every need. On our last day, for instance, no sooner had we exited the elevator than our bags were smoothly taken off us to be stored until our flight, without us needing to ask
Eco effort
Hotel Gabrielli’s sustainability commitments are woven into its recent renovation. Starhotels’ sustainability-driven initiative, “La Grande Bellezza—The Dream Factory,” supports the work of local restoration experts and is evident here in the sensitive and materially conscious restoration work. On the guest side, single-use plastics are eschewed in favor of glass water bottles and refillable dispensers in the bathrooms. Operationally, the hotel utilizes low-impact generators and energy-efficient LED lighting, reflecting the group’s commitment to reducing CO2 emissions and energy consumption across all its properties.
Accessibility
The city itself can be tricky to navigate for those with mobility impairments, but Gabrielli’s unique position makes arrival easy. All bridges along the canalside stretch of Riva degli Schiavoni are well equipped, and inside the hotel, elevators stop at every floor. Almost every room in the hotel is accessible for wheelchair users, as well as dining areas and the rooftop, though certain rooms are specially adapted with grab bars and lowered wash basins.
